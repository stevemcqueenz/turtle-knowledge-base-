## Text
<blockquote>
Today was a good day.

Vereesa Windrunner stood by the slit in her tent’s curtain, letting the light spill across her face. The sun - her people’s sun, the one they had once loved so dearly - pierced through the veil of plague-ridden clouds. For a fleeting moment, the camp outside looked almost like Quel’Thalas of old. Almost.

Shalandis Isle pulsed with life. Their new foothold teemed with movement and sound: soldiers unloading supplies, healers tending to the weary, commanders barking orders as fresh banners snapped in the sea wind. The Alliance had struck here once before, a bitter memory she pushed down, but this time the assault had held. For the first time in years, they had a sanctuary. After eight long, haunted years, she was home again.

Defenses rose swiftly, conjured from the ground by the finest battle-mages. To the east, Cenarion druids bent their will upon the poisoned earth, drawing blight out of the soil as if wringing darkness from a rag. The corruption still lingered, stubborn as a scar, but the land was healing. Slowly.

She pulled the curtain back again, gazing at the bustle. For a heartbeat, the sight carried her across the years - back to a summer morning in Quel’Thalas. She remembered standing in Silvermoon’s gardens, her sisters laughing as sunlight dappled the marble walkways, banners bright against the sky. The air had been alive with music, not the clatter of armor. The smell of blooming everroses, not salt and ash. She had been so young then, so certain that beauty like that could never be broken.

The memory faded as quickly as it came, leaving her with the sound of shouted orders and the sight of scarred land beneath scourged skies.

Shalandis was nothing more than a rocky speck on the map - an isle she had barely known before the Scourge descended. And yet, here it stood, transformed into a symbol of triumph. Against all odds, against years of retreat and despair, they had won something back.

Her lips curved into a smile. Finally, after so long, the winds of fate seemed to be shifting in her favor.

Her thoughts were interrupted by the sudden parting of the tent flap. Commander Braedin stepped inside without hesitation, the sea wind curling the canvas behind him.

Vereesa straightened. Her right hand. The steady pillar of the Silver Covenant. He had been the one to stand with her through the bitter years - after the first disastrous assault on Shalandis Isle, when defeat had nearly broken them; after the nobles stripped her of the title of Speaker, when she had stood alone in the councils of Alah’Thalas. Through every loss and every slight, Braedin had remained. Loyal, unshakably so - at times so much that even she found herself startled by the depth of it.

“My Lady,” he said, his voice soft as silk, yet steady as steel. “The Regency Council awaits you in the main pavilion.”

“Of course,” she replied, studying him as he stood framed in the dim light of her tent. Braedin was young - too young, some might whisper - for the authority he bore. Yet already he carried himself with the calm weight of command. Tall, broad-shouldered, with short platinum hair that caught the sun like polished steel, he looked every inch the figure of a Quel’dorei lord. A fine match for any lady of standing. She had even imagined, more than once, what he might mean for her cousin’s future. But not now. Not yet. She needed him whole, sharp, and devoted to the cause they carried.

“I need a few minutes,” Vereesa said at last, her tone firm but not dismissive. “I will join them shortly.”

Braedin inclined his head, bowing with that precise measure of respect he had mastered. Then he withdrew, the flap falling closed behind him.

Vereesa lingered a moment longer, letting silence settle back around her. The Regency Council. Their first gathering since Shalandis had been won. She drew a slow breath. This time, she felt hope stirring within her chest - not the fragile, brittle hope of survival, but something sturdier.

After all, it had been her hand, her people, her Silver Covenant, who had broken the Scourge’s hold here. Whatever the council’s squabbling, however the nobles of Alah’Thalas might sneer, they could not deny her this victory.

And perhaps, just perhaps, the tide was turning.
----

Vereesa pushed past the curtain of the grand pavilion, and for the first time in what felt like a lifetime, she entered not as an outcast but as a victor. For years she had been dismissed, her voice smothered by politics and pride, her authority stripped away by those who preferred squabbling to action. But now, now she held leverage. Now she had the means to forge unity from the splintered pieces of her people. No more whispered betrayals. No more knife-edged courtly games. What lay before them was not intrigue, but war. And she had enough allies to fight it.

The pavilion itself was little more than a larger tent, though its draped silks gave it an air of importance. Blue banners of Silvermoon hung heavy from the poles, their embroidered stars catching the lamplight. In the center of the space stood a massive wooden map of Quel’Thalas, carved with such painstaking detail that even the smallest ridgelines and rivers were etched in relief.

Around its surface lay the markers of their fractured world.

A banner of blue marked the Alliance forces: here, on Shalandis Isle, where their victory still felt fragile, and further south, where a second army lingered at the gates of the Eastern Plaguelands, holding the line with weary resolve.

Red tusked markers littered the east - what remained of the Amani. Scattered strongholds, clinging stubbornly to survival, and Zul’Aman itself, still defiant. Vereesa’s lips tightened. Let the Scourge grind them down. After all they had done to her people, she would shed no tears for their fate.

Purple markers had been placed with hesitation. Unconfirmed. Possible resistance cells - Quel’dorei who had endured within Quel’Thalas all these years. Rumors only, yet if even half were true, then their resilience was nothing short of miraculous.

A scattering of green stood apart. The Illidari. Loyal to the betrayer prince. Their motives remained an enigma - sometimes allies, sometimes rivals, always unpredictable.

White markers, small and few, bore the sigil of the Argent Dawn. Tirion’s followers, lending aid where they could, though most of their strength was consumed by the unending wars of the Plaguelands.

And then there was black. Black markers covered the map like a disease. Entire swaths of land claimed by the Scourge. Towns swallowed. Forests razed. Black encircled the Blackened Woods, black blotted Eversong, black pressed even against the gates of Silvermoon itself. At the heart of it all, the name burned like poison in her mind.

Dar’khan Drathir.

The traitor. The one who had doomed them all.

Yet she had not come here to argue over battle plans. Strategy could wait. Holding what they had gained, this fragile sliver of Quel’Thalas, mattered more than any map. And beyond that, she had come for something far greater.

She had come to ensure this victory endured. To make certain it was not remembered as a fleeting spark before the dark swallowed them again. She had come to reclaim her title of Speaker, to give her people a voice once more, and to weave them together into something whole. All it would take was five votes. Five members of the Regency Council. With the victory of Shalandis fresh in their minds, that should not be so difficult.

“Vereesa!”

The voice belonged to the council’s youngest member, Lady Farenia Rosehart. Her dark hair shimmered faintly in the lamplight as she rose, her expression halfway between delight and reprimand. “The hero of the hour! And late, as usual…”

Vereesa allowed herself a smile. Farenia was impulsive, sometimes even naive, but her heart was true. And most importantly, she despised Astalor. That alone made her useful.

“Apologies, venerable council,” Vereesa said, dipping into a graceful bow. “I had pressing matters to attend.”

“Of course.”

The reply came from Voren’thal, his voice flat as always. Age weighed on every syllable; here was a man who had seen centuries turn, who had endured too many councils and too many failures. His expression betrayed nothing. “Yet being summoned to a meeting and then kept waiting leaves a bitter taste in the mouth.”

Vereesa’s smile tightened. This was what she despised most - the endless posturing, the petty barbs. They had bled and burned to reclaim even this fragment of their homeland, and the first words spoken were complaints about her punctuality?

She drew in a steady breath, reminding herself of the stakes. This was not about wounded pride. Not about trading insults. She had to keep her composure, for the sake of everything they had fought for.

“Fellow members of the Regency Council,” Vereesa began, giving no heed to the earlier jabs about her lateness. “I have gathered you here for a reason. For today finally marks the day of our victory.”

She was cut off before the words could even settle in the air. A deep voice, sharp as a blade, sliced through her declaration.

Astalor Sunsworn.

Vereesa’s heart tightened. She despised him with every ounce of her being.

“Victory?” His tone dripped with scorn. He did not even look at her as he spoke, his eyes sweeping the gathered nobles instead. “You call this a victory? Victory will come only when meetings such as this are held beside our blessed Sunwell. Tell me, what victory lies in this moment? What have we truly gained? Hundreds have already died, thrown away, so that we might claim this… rock.”

Vereesa’s lips parted, ready to respond, but - as always - Astalor pushed forward, a man who saw the world as his stage.

“I came all the way from Alah’Thalas, eager to witness this so-called triumph your messengers praised,” he said, leaning heavily over the carved map of Quel’Thalas. His finger jabbed toward her, a dagger’s accusation. “Yet what I see here is nothing but a token. A foothold that exists only to soothe your wounded pride after years of failure.”

At last, he looked at her.

Vereesa faltered, not outwardly, never outwardly, but within. Astalor was… different. The usual majesty and haughty arrogance were dulled. His golden hair, once gleaming, looked lifeless, matted. His robes, once immaculate in crimson brilliance, now hung wrinkled and worn, as though he had come straight from battle rather than the polished halls of nobility. He looked tired. Concerned, even.

She had never been able to read him well, but this was something new. Something amiss.

“You couldn’t let it rest, could you, Vereesa?” His words came low, each one a stone flung in her direction. “You had to prove the isle could be retaken. But tell us - why? For what purpose?”

The questions weren’t truly questions. He had no interest in answers.

“Because you feel guilty,” Astalor spat. “And in that guilt, you’ve lost touch with reality. This is no foothold. We could just as easily have staged the reclamation of Quel’Thalas from the docks of Alah’Thalas and called it the same. What you have created here is nothing more than vanity. An empty gesture. One that brings our people no closer to salvation.”

Vereesa’s nails dug into the wood of her chair. Since when had Astalor ever spoken of their people with such conviction? Since when did he care for anything but his precious crown?

Rommath shifted uneasily, his jeweled fingers drumming against the edge of the map table.

“Indeed…” he said at last, voice measured, as though balancing on a blade’s edge. “This isle holds little strategic value. But even you, Astalor, must admit a gesture such as this is not meaningless. It will serve as a symbol for our people.”

Vereesa’s eyes flicked toward him. His mask of calm betrayed nothing, but she knew the truth buried beneath it. Rommath, Grand Magister of Quel’Thalas, still bent knee to Prince Kael’thas. Not merely loyal - he was in contact with the traitor, even now. Vereesa had kept the knowledge close, a weapon she might one day wield. For now, it was better left hidden.

She straightened, refusing to let the moment slip away.

“As I was saying,” she continued, her voice rising above the murmurs, ignoring Rommath’s careful intrusion. “We have achieved a great victory today. For the first time in a decade, we set foot once more on the soil of Quel’Thalas. Hard fought, yes. Paid for in blood, yes. But it was ours. Won by the determination and strength of our people and by the devotion of our allies.”

A ripple of approval moved through the pavilion. A few nobles clapped, some even cheered. Good. That was the sound she needed.

“While we are still far from total victory,” Vereesa pressed on, “this is the first step toward it. The first step to reignite hope in the hearts of our people. Just as the founding of Alah’Thalas once did. Tell me, Astalor - was that a waste as well? Was it folly to build anew while our ultimate goal remained the reclamation of Silvermoon?”

Her words cut through the air, sharp and measured. She took a deliberate step closer to the map, her gaze never leaving her rival.

“That city became a symbol of our unity, of our resilience. And so too will Shalandis Isle. Not merely a rock in the sea. But the mark of our return. The promise of vengeance.”

Astalor’s lips curled into a thin smile, the kind that never reached his eyes.

“Fine words, Vereesa. Stirring words. But words will not win us our homeland.” He straightened, pushing away from the map, and the room seemed to lean with him. “You compare this isle to Alah’Thalas? A poor comparison. That city was necessity - our people needed shelter, food, walls against the dark. It was survival. This? This is vanity. A performance staged to distract our people from the truth.”

He paced slowly around the table, his gaze sweeping the council, voice resonant, practiced. “We are weak. The Scourge is strong. Every life we throw away on gestures like this only proves it. Our people do not need hope. They need strength. They need leaders who can secure true alliances, who can rebuild an army worthy of Quel’Thalas. Not these hollow shows of defiance.”

He stopped, letting silence hang for a beat before turning to her fully. The fatigue she had seen in him earlier lingered, but now it looked more like a weapon - worn into his features for sympathy.

“Tell me, Vereesa, how long will this so-called foothold last? When the Scourge sends its full strength across the water, when Dar’khan himself turns his gaze upon this isle, will you still stand here and call it a symbol? Or will you lead our people into another slaughter, as you did before?”

A murmur spread across the council chamber. Some eyes turned downward, uneasy. Others fixed on Astalor, nodding faintly. He had struck where he knew it would wound: her failures, her guilt, the ghosts of the first assault.

But beneath the words, Vereesa sensed something more. Astalor’s vehemence was not merely obstruction. There was calculation in it. He wanted this victory diminished, her triumph reduced to ashes before it could solidify into power. Because if Vereesa rose - if she claimed the Speaker’s mantle - his own influence would falter.

Before Vereesa could answer, another voice joined him. Calm, measured, but heavy with the weight of centuries.

“Lord Sunsworn speaks truth.”

Voren’thal.

The old magister adjusted the folds of his robe, his pale eyes glinting in the lamplight. “This isle offers us little of value, save the illusion of progress. We must be cautious not to mistake sentiment for strategy. Symbols, as you call them, Lady Vereesa, are but fragile things. Easily broken.”

Vereesa’s jaw tightened. Of course. Voren’thal had long since abandoned any true independence. He had lived under Astalor’s roof in Alah’Thalas for years, enjoying his patronage, basking in borrowed wealth and influence. A loyal dog dressed in silks. His voice in the council was never his own - it was Astalor’s, polished with age and experience.

“Even if this isle is held for a season, what then?” Voren’thal continued smoothly, his tone as emotionless as ever. “Will the Scourge falter? Will Dar’khan abandon his hold? No. We will still lack the strength to strike at the heart of our enemy. Meanwhile, we waste soldiers and supplies to hold barren stone, while our true defenses - our new city, our surviving kin - are left vulnerable.”

He inclined his head toward Astalor, the deference subtle but unmistakable. “This council must not be swayed by speeches that tug at the heart. We must think with cold reason, or we will doom our people again.”

A few of the older nobles nodded, murmuring their agreement. 

Before Vereesa could respond, Lady Farenia sprang to her feet. Her chair clattered backward, the sharp sound cutting through the murmurs.

“Enough of this!” she snapped, glaring at Astalor and Voren’thal in turn. “Lady Vereesa led us to victory when no one else would lift a hand! While some of you sat safe behind your walls in Alah’Thalas, growing fat on feasts and gold, she bled for Quel’Thalas. You speak of reason, of caution, but I say you are cowards, too afraid to face the Scourge, too eager to cling to your comforts while others fight your battles!”

A wave of whispers crashed through the council chamber. Several nobles stiffened, their faces flushing with indignation. Even those who sympathized with Vereesa shifted uneasily. Farenia’s passion was genuine, her loyalty beyond question - but in her youthful fire, she had insulted nearly everyone in the room.

Vereesa closed her eyes for half a heartbeat. Stars above, Farenia.

Astalor did not waste the gift.

“Cowards?” he repeated softly, his voice carrying easily across the pavilion. He turned, addressing the assembled nobles with a wounded dignity. “You hear her. This is what Lady Vereesa’s allies think of you. That you are weak. That you feast while others die.” His gaze swept the room, catching eyes one by one. “Is this the unity she promises us? A unity built on scorn and accusation?”

Vereesa felt the balance tilt further, slipping from her grasp. Farenia’s defense, meant to raise her higher, had instead played directly into Astalor’s hands.

“Peace,” Rommath said, lifting one gloved hand. “This is not the place for insults.” His gaze flicked toward Farenia, and though his tone remained polite, the rebuke was clear. “Lady Rosehart’s passion does her credit, but passion alone will not win us our homeland.”

Several nobles nodded, seizing on the civility in his words as a welcome balm after Farenia’s fire. Rommath paused, letting the silence breathe before continuing.

“Shalandis is… not without merit,” he allowed, his masked face turning slightly toward Vereesa. “I will grant that victories, no matter how small, inspire hope. Symbols have their place. Yet symbols cannot hold back the tide.” His voice deepened, steady and authoritative. “We must remember: the Scourge does not falter when hearts are stirred. It falters only when met with true power. Resources. Armies. Strategy.”

His words sounded balanced, reasonable, yet Vereesa could hear the tilt, the subtle echo of Astalor’s argument. The message was clear: Shalandis might matter as a symbol, yes, but it was not enough. Vereesa’s triumph, when filtered through Rommath’s careful phrasing, was made to seem like an embellishment rather than an achievement.

Rommath spread his hands slightly, palms open in a gesture of conciliation. “Let us not mistake this foothold for more than it is. A first step, perhaps. But if we are to place faith in it, we must be certain it leads somewhere greater.”

Murmurs of agreement rippled again, steadier this time. Even those inclined to support Vereesa seemed content to drift toward the middle ground Rommath offered. One that, conveniently, left Astalor’s doubts standing firm.

“Well spoken, Lord Rommath,” Astalor said smoothly, seizing the moment. He looked wearier than ever, his voice low and heavy with solemnity. Vereesa almost believed it herself, the mask of a burdened statesman. Almost. But she alone saw through the act.

“I have… taken the liberty of investigating the cost of this assault.” His words dropped like stones into water, sending ripples through the chamber. “And what I discovered is terrifying.”

Vereesa stiffened. How? The numbers had been sealed, kept strictly within the highest ranks of the Silver Covenant.

Astalor continued before she could speak. “You know me well. Many of you have dined at my table, joined my feasts. You know I open my doors not only to lords and ladies, but to the less fortunate as well. I have always shared what I possess.”

A blatant lie. Vereesa’s hands clenched at her sides. He had never once shown such generosity to the poor, not without demanding something in return. But the nobles nodded, some eagerly, others with hesitation. They wanted to believe him.

“My fortune is not endless,” Astalor said, spreading his hands in a show of candor. “But I was among the fortunate few whose wealth was not trapped in Silvermoon. My gold lay safe in the vaults of Ironforge, and with it I have funded feasts, armies, and causes as I deemed just. You may disagree with my choices, but none can claim I have hidden them. I have been honest with you. Always.”

Then he turned to her, and the act fell away. The false weariness, the practiced humility, all stripped bare in an instant. What stared back at her were the eyes of a predator - sharp, merciless, certain.

“But Lady Vereesa…” His voice dropped lower, forcing the council to lean forward to catch every word. “She has no fortune left. No coffers hidden away, no vaults in Ironforge. Her inheritance, her family’s wealth - gone. Burned to ash with Silvermoon itself. All she has left is her name. A name she clings to.”

The air seemed to tighten around her.

“So tell us,” Astalor said, his finger stabbing toward her across the map. “How did you stage these assaults? Twice, no less. Twice we bled, once for failure, and now for this shallow triumph. From where did the coin flow, if not from your own purse?” His tone turned almost mocking. “Shall we hear it from your lips? Or would you prefer I explain it myself?”

Whispers swept the pavilion like fire catching dry leaves. Faces turned toward Vereesa, suspicion flickering in eyes that had cheered her only moments before.

Her heart pounded. He knew. Somehow, impossibly, he knew. Information that should have been untraceable now dangled in his claws, ready to be used as a noose.

“I was called a coward only moments ago,” Astalor said, his voice ringing with sharp authority. “Tell me, is it cowardice to refuse to bleed my own people dry? Is it cowardice to protect them from theft? It is easy, so very easy, for Lady Vereesa to cloak herself in heroism when the price she pays is not her own, but yours. Your sacrifice. Your blood. Your kin. Your gold.”

The words rippled through the pavilion, heavy and poisonous. A few nobles nodded with grim approval; others shifted uneasily, but none leapt to Vereesa’s defense.

Astalor smiled, the expression slow and deliberate, as though savoring the silence. Vereesa braced herself, expecting the final strike. But instead of pressing her further, his eyes drifted past her.

Toward Lor’themar.

Lor’themar had been silent all this time, his single eye cool and distant. An ally - or so she had believed. His silence now was deafening.

Vereesa tried to catch his gaze, searching for reassurance, for solidarity. But when his eye flicked up, it did not meet hers. It slid away, down toward the map, then to the floor. Anywhere but her.

Her chest tightened. No. Not him. He wouldn’t…

But Astalor’s smile widened ever so slightly, and in that moment, a sliver of dread burrowed deep into her. Something was wrong. Terribly wrong.

Astalor let the silence stretch, his eyes never leaving Lor’themar.

“Well?” he said at last, his tone deceptively soft. “You’ve been quiet, old friend. Surely you, of all people, have an opinion on whether this victory was bought honestly… or otherwise.”

The words were a knife slipped between armor plates. Vereesa felt her pulse spike.

Lor’themar’s jaw clenched. For a long moment, he said nothing, his gaze fixed on the carved map of Quel’Thalas. But silence was already an answer. The other nobles leaned in, the tension thickening. Finally, he exhaled, slow and heavy, and raised his eye to the council.

“It’s true,” Lor’themar said at last, his voice low but steady. “The Silver Covenant has not drawn its strength from generosity alone. Supplies meant for our people in exile… rerouted. Taxes owed to this council… diverted. Coin scraped from wherever it could be found, no matter the method. All of it poured into soldiers and steel - to pay for this campaign, and this victory”

The words crashed over Vereesa like a wave of ice.

Murmurs exploded across the pavilion. Shock. Outrage. Betrayal. Some nobles slammed fists against the table, others hissed whispered accusations.

Lor’themar did not look at her. Not once.

Astalor let the uproar build before raising a hand for silence, his expression that of a man who bore great sorrow for the truth he had “reluctantly” unearthed. “You see?” he said, his voice grave, tinged with false weariness. “This is no symbol. This is no triumph. It is a theft - a bleeding of our own people under the guise of valor. She has built her glory not on sacrifice of her own, but on yours.”

He turned then, his golden hair dulled, his robes wrinkled, but his eyes gleaming with predatory triumph. “What kind of future can we build upon deceit?”

The council chamber thundered with noise: some demanding answers, others calling for punishment. Vereesa’s throat tightened as she searched the sea of faces. Many looked at her with shock. Others with betrayal. And some with fear.

She wanted to cry out, to explain, to shout the truth: that she had done it because no one else would. That she had no coffers, no fortune hidden away in Ironforge. That every coin Astalor hoarded, every banquet he threw, every soldier he refused to fund - she had scraped from the shadows to keep their people alive.

But she couldn’t. No words came. Her throat closed, her tongue betrayed her, and the silence stretched heavy around her.

“I have known of this for some time,” Astalor said, his tone mournful but his eyes glinting. “Though I thought her merely greedy, not reckless. Yet it seems she could not live with her failure. And so she squandered what she took. All those resources… wasted here.”

He drew a long breath, steady and deliberate, as though preparing the ground for something greater.

“But alas, it is too late to turn back. We cannot abandon this place now. The isle must be held, fortified, for no ground can be given to the Scourge. And so…” He spread his hands, a picture of reluctant nobility. “Our people will need a strong leader in these dark times. Someone willing to shoulder the burden.”

For a heartbeat, his gaze fell on her. Vereesa’s stomach clenched. The council saw only a weary statesman. But she saw the truth in his eyes - the fire that had always burned there. Ambition. Pure, consuming, reckless ambition.

“I am ready,” Astalor said, his voice rising with practiced authority. “I am ready to take the mantle of Lord-Regent of Quel’Thalas. What our people need is not stolen coin, squandered in vanity, but true wealth. Wealth that can raise lasting armies, build lasting fortifications, and win us back our homeland.”

A hush fell over the pavilion. The moment he had hungered for all his life now hovered within his grasp.

“By the laws of the Regency Council,” Voren’thal intoned, his voice as ancient and cold as stone. “five votes are needed to confirm the title of Lord-Regent.”

The map of Quel’Thalas loomed between them, covered in markers of blue, black, red, and green - territories, enemies, allies. Yet in this moment, the only battlefield that mattered was this tent.

Astalor clasped his hands behind his back, eyes lowered as if he carried a burden too heavy to show pride. It was a masterful performance. Vereesa hated him all the more for it.

Voren’thal’s gaze swept the room. “We vote.”

The first voice was no surprise. “For Astalor,” Voren’thal said himself, as though casting his lot was merely acknowledging the inevitable. A lifetime of wealth owed to Sunsworn coffers had bought his loyalty long ago.

The second came quickly. Rommath’s masked face turned slightly toward Vereesa, then away. “Astalor,” he said, the word clipped and decisive. “For stability.”

Vereesa’s nails dug deeper into her palms. Of course. Kael’thas’s loyal servant would never back her.

“Lady Farenia Rosehart,” Voren’thal called.

The young woman shot to her feet, her dark hair swinging as she glared across the table. “Vereesa Windrunner. The true Speaker of our people, the true victor here. My vote is hers.”

Vereesa’s throat tightened, both in gratitude and dread. Farenia’s voice rang bold, but her earlier fire still lingered, turning allies into skeptics.

“Lady Miralin Wavesinger.”

The enchantress rose with fluid grace, her seafoam-white silks whispering against the floor. Her voice was soft, melodic, warm. “Our people need resources - and I know of no one here more experienced at acquisition and distribution than Astralor.”

“Lor’themar Theron.”

The name rang in the pavilion like the toll of a bell. All eyes turned to him - the ranger, the soldier who had bled beside her in the darkest of days. The one she had trusted.

The pause stretched, heavy as iron. Lor’themar’s one good eye fixed on the map, tracing its carved rivers and valleys as if the grain of wood might spare him from the decision he now faced. His hand hovered above the table, fingers twitching once before curling into a fist.

Vereesa’s breath caught. Say it. Say my name. He had to. He owed her that much.

Finally, slowly, he lifted his gaze. Not to her. Never to her. His eye drifted across the chamber, past the nobles, and settled on Astalor.

The silence roared in her ears.

“Astalor Sunsworn,” he said at last, his voice level, stripped of warmth. A soldier delivering a report, nothing more.

Vereesa’s heart cracked like the walls of Silvermoon. The silence of betrayal was worse than any insult, any wound. He had not raised his voice, had not spat her name with scorn. 
He had simply looked past her, as if she were already gone.

That made four. Only one remained.

“Lord Astalor Sunsworn.”

He closed his eyes briefly, as though weighed down by duty, and gave the faintest nod. “I accept the burden. My vote is mine.”

Five.

The tent seemed to sway. A murmur spread, first tentative, then swelling into applause. Some cheered outright. Others offered solemn nods. But all eyes turned to him.

Astalor stepped forward, his hand resting on the carved map, his voice rich with triumph barely concealed by humility. 
“Then let it be known. By the will of this council, I am the Lord-Regent of Quel’Thalas. And under my guidance, our people shall rise anew.”

Vereesa’s world narrowed to the pounding of her heart. She kept her face still, a statue carved from pride and rage, but inside the fire roared.

She had won them Shalandis. She had given them blood, steel, and sacrifice. And yet in the span of minutes, Astalor had stolen it all.

It was supposed to be a good day.
