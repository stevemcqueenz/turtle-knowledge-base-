# Sir Farabert of Stromgarde

## Description
Sir Farabert of Stromgarde is a knight-errant from the Arathi Highlands. Born into Clan Leder, he is a seasoned warrior who formerly served in the court of King Thoras Trollbane, as well as the Grand Alliance of Kalimdor. Today, he wanders the lands, claiming to be on a quest in search of dragons. 

### Appearance
Farabert is a handsome human male, approximately 25 years old. He has brown hair and a beard, ice-blue eyes, and chiseled features. He typically wears a practical set of composite armor, draped with the traditional red cloak of Arathor. Adorning his cloak clasp is a leaf mounted on a silver backing—a memento from Nordrassil. Farabert owns a horse and a mule to carry all of his equipment, though he travels without a squire.

Farabert's coat of arms retains his family's inherited blazon: *Gules, an eagle displayed Argent*. Following the Battle of Mount Hyjal, a wreath of golden oak leaves was added to the design, symbolizing Nordrassil under the setting sun of that day.

### Personality
Farabert is a man who embodies the classical spirit of chivalry. The harshness of the Arathi Highlands taught him that noble rights stem from duty rather than birthright, which allows him to work closely and naturally with people of all social classes. He is honorable, resilient, courteous, and strictly values his promises, while also possessing a sharp political acumen and a strategic mindset. In battle, his fighting spirit is described as "having the stubbornness of a dwarf."

Farabert has been an avid reader since childhood. Although this is not a highly praised trait in the Arathi Highlands, it has brought him immeasurable benefits and often allows him to present a humble and gentle demeanor.

Honor is the cornerstone of Farabert's character, but unlike his arrogant and belligerent peers, he does not view honor as more important than life itself. When a greater good is at stake, he is willing to cast his personal honor aside, granting him a more flexible moral code.

On the other hand, Farabert possesses a considerable sense of humor. He often wraps his harsh lifestyle in a romantic narrative, casting a poetic aura over his journeys. This frequently leads those unfamiliar with him to mistake him for being naive—when in reality, he is simply a man highly sensitive to beauty, both visually and spiritually.

### Abilities and Skills
An elite education in the Arathi Highlands has made Farabert virtually a master of the arts of war. From maintaining equipment and tending to horses, to the martial mastery of the sword, shield, and lance; everything from small-squad tactics to continent-spanning legion strategies are foundational skills imparted to him since his youth.

Furthermore, during the Battle of Mount Hyjal, he was exposed to engineering by the goblins. Realizing the immense value of this technology, he has since actively sought to integrate it into his new military theories.

Although incapable of casting spells, Farabert's extensive reading has given him an understanding of how magic works, and consequently, how to counter it. Similarly, thanks to his literary pursuits, he has forged friendships with many scholars and poets, providing him with a steady and reliable stream of valuable intelligence.

## Backstory
(**Note:** *Most of the public’s knowledge regarding Farabert’s exploits is derived from **The Lay of Sir Farabert**, composed by the bard **Perinya of Highvale**; the veracity of details is left to the reader’s own discernment.*)

### The Lay of Sir Farabert
  -I.**

O'er stony heights the eagles fly,

Where Arathor's old stones touch sky;

When Portal of great Doom was torn,

A son named Farabert was born.

The last in line, the youngest heir,

No lands or titles his to share.

Since peril haunts the highland glen,

This bleeding realm holds no spare men.

  -II.**

From sires of old, this law he kept,

While lesser men in comfort slept:

"The bread you break, you did not sow,

"The wine you drink, you did not grow.

"So let your honour not be birth,

"But deeds of arms to prove your worth.

"Protect the folk, be first in fray,

"A living shield, let come what may."

  -III.**

When two times seven winters passed,

To Stromgarde's halls he went at last,

To squire King Thoras, Fist of North,

And there his valor blossomed forth.

Most comely grown, with wit so keen,

A knightly grace was in him seen.

He shared the Prince's youthful spring,

But found more favor with the King;

  -IV.**

Where unshackled Horde in rage cry,

To meet the lancers' iron tide;

Young lightning pierced the shield-wall through,

Where Doomhammer his death-place knew.

The King then bade the youth to kneel,

Beneath the weight of royal steel;

To guard the crown, to hold the light,

“I bid thee now: Arise, Sir Knight!"

  -V.**

When Lordaeron to shadow fell,

And Prince did strike his father's knell,

King Thoras spoke with heavy heart,

"My kingly friend and I must part.

"Go forth, my knight, through shadow’s breath,

"To pluck his crown from halls of death.

"A brother's realm is sore betrayed,

"In south his glory shall ne’er fade."

  -VI.**

Alone he braved the gates of rot,

Where light and mercy were forgot.

A thousand deaths he turned to clay,

To win one step upon the way.

Where Prince had struck his father down,

Upon the floor he found the crown.

No thief had dared to claim the gold,

Where only rot and silence hold.

  -VII.**

He wrapped the gold in cloak of red,

To paths his king's decree had led;

Beneath a wide and southern sky,

To where the Lion's banners fly.

The restless ghosts did haunt his way,

With silver words and dark dismay;

But firm of will he would not hear,

Until he smelt the sea-salt near.

  -VIII.**

He found a ship of humble name,

Sir Farabert did hide his fame;

Till ocean swallowed sail and oar,

The base, the noble, rich and poor.

He lost his blade to salt and deep,

He lost his pride to ocean's keep;

But fast he held the sacred crown,

Against the waves that sought to drown.

  -IX.**

He woke upon a silken bed,

Where healing hands had cooled his head;

Beside him sat, with gentle sighs,

A noble dame with amber eyes.

'Twas Josephine, who walked the strand,

By Stillheart port's deserted sand;

For seven days she watched his breath,

And drew him from the gates of death.

  -X.**

With wit as deep as mercy’s flow,

She hushed the crown and mission’s glow.

No prying eye, no careless word,

To keep the secrets she inferred.

Through the long night, their whispers twined,

Of distant lives by fate designed.

As moon and star, with mingled grace,

Effuse their light in night’s embrace.

  -XI.**

As sunlight pierced the silent shade,

The Lady brought her late lord's blade;

"No knight without a sword may stand,"

She pressed the hilt into his hand.

No other gift could he bestow,

To make her name in honor grow—

"By thy fair name, I now decree,

Seven foul drakes shall cease to be."

  -XII.**

To Stormwind's gates of marble white,

His liege's charge fulfilled aright;

Beside the lighthouse, tall and grey,

Through many trials on the way.

He sowed the crown in silver sand,

Where tides do wash the Lion's land.

"O blameless may the father lie,

For deeds the son has wrought awry."

  -XIII.**

To Homeland came the victor knight,

But found no King to greet his sight;

The Prince upon the throne sat high,

But dared not meet his piercing eye.

The warrior King by baseness fell,

As rumors of betrayal swell.

He drew his sword before the throne,

"E'en Arthas made his treason known!"

  -XIV.**

Comrades of blood now choose their sides,

Where naked steel the hall divides;

Before the realm is torn in two,

He sheathes the blade he fiercely drew.

“Thou art his only living heir,

To break his heart I do not dare.

I swear no oath to tainted throne,

So guard the folk you call your own.”

  -XV.**

From Highland gates he rode apart,

And buried fealty in his heart.

No liege binds this wandering sword,

The wind his guide, the weak his lord.

Where suns no longer light the way,

The surging dead hunt living prey.

He shielded innocents in the strife,

And led them in the fight for life.

  -XVI.**

By shattered spires of wise decree,

He met the Daughter of the Sea.

The weary host pursued her quest,

For promise of a distant rest.

Sir Farabert held out his blade,

To beg a path for those dismayed.

With beauteous mercy through the foam,

They sailed the west and left their home.

  -XVII.**

The wind bore seeds across the main,

Until the knight kissed soil again.

This land of endless starlight blessed,

Was once by ancient elves possessed.

The sword was oiled, the axe was swung,

To build a home where briars clung.

But weary folk should bear in mind,

The shadows follow close behind.

  -XVIII.**

The Crown of Heavens gave its call,

Where demon hosts in darkness sprawl.

Farabert joined the march so true,

No other life his spirit knew.

The sons of men, the first defense,

Midst ancient cliff and forest dense.

The first of first, the sword to wield,

An eagle flies upon his shield.

  -XIX.**

The mountain shakes with hellish might,

As fire rains down and kills the light.

The iron walls are doomed to bleed,

They fight for time the world shall need.

Though ranks are broken, he took no rest,

Midst the Horde he stood the test.

Once nemesis, brothers in line,

Against the demon's dark design.

  -XX.**

Through hills of dead and rivers red,

As line by line the vanguard bled;

Sulfur stench o'er sacred land,

Beneath the Tree, the final stand.

The Great Defiler parts the shroud,

His burning eyes behind the cloud;

The bleeding Knight, he grinned in fray,

“That is the dragon first to slay!”

  -XXI.**

Upon the shattered, bleeding land,

As Titan's will drives mortal hand;

Against the doom he drove amain,

As eagle struck he scorned their bane.

Until the Spirits surged like tide,

In blinding white the Tyrant died.

A triumph rang through crimson sky,

Amidst the rose a knight lay nigh.

  -XXII.**

A distant harp from moonlit dreams,

A skiff drifts through the trembling gleams;

Silk mists caress the knight's face mild,

Elune'ara holds the battered child;

Violet maid at the prow sways,

A sidelong glance the stars displays.

When moonlight laves in haven deep,

The knight revives where ripples sweep.

  -XXIII.**

In grove the knight his days did dream,

And parting vowed by forest stream.

“If Star-kin falls to bitter strife,

By wind I'll come to share thy plight.”

At sea-girt realm a prize assigned,

But gold and land he left behind;

Nordrassil's leaf he took alone,

The sole reward he'd call his own.

  -XXIV.**

And so he journeyed far and wide,

With dragon rumors for his guide;

No lordly crest upon him gleamed,

But homeland colors wildly streamed.

Sir Farabert, the Highland's own,

A foe, a love to many known;

While evil dragons scheme to rend,

The tale of knights shall never end.
