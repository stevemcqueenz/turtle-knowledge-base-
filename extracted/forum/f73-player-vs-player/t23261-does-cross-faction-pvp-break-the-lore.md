# Does cross-faction PvP break the lore?

## Post 162603 by Silverleafeon (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2025-12-12T20:57:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=162603#p162603 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

One of the things that drew me to Turtle WoW was its dedication to quality. To staying true to the lore. To making me feel like I was actually stepping into a living, breathing fantasy world where my choices and my character mattered.

When cross-faction dungeons were added, I was initially hesitant, but I eventually convinced myself it could make sense. Adventurers far from civilization might have to put aside their differences to survive. A Human and an Orc, miles from safety, might temporarily cooperate to defeat a greater threat, a grudging truce of convenience. That kind of alliance fits within fantasy storytelling, and even in Warcraft lore, grudges can be set aside in the face of danger.

But PvP is different. Battlegrounds like Warsong Gulch, Arathi Basin, and Alterac Valley aren’t just random skirmishes. They’re expressions of the ongoing war between Horde and Alliance. Night Elves defending Ashenvale should not be fighting alongside Orcs; Humans in Arathi should not be standing shoulder-to-shoulder with Undead. Cross-faction battlegrounds break that narrative, undermining the sense of tension and stakes that makes PvP meaningful.

I’m not a hardcore PvPer. I enjoy battlegrounds from time to time, but PvP is part of a larger whole for me: it enriches the world, ties into quests, and reinforces the story. But with Cross-Faction battlegrounds, that experience will feel diluted and hollow. Imagine questing through Ashenvale as a Night Elf, defending your forests against invading Orcs, only to queue for WSG and end up fighting alongside those same Orcs. It’s immersion-breaking.

Yes, I could switch to Tel’Abim where PvP is the focus and cross-faction won't be an issue, but that’s not really an option I want to pursue. Moving characters or starting from scratch is inconvenient, and I don’t want to play on a realm where world PvP dominates every aspect of gameplay. I want a balanced experience: questing, dungeons, and the occasional battleground, all tied into a coherent world.

So, I’m curious, does anyone else feel the same way? Do you think cross-faction battlegrounds make sense within the Warcraft world, or do they undermine the story and immersion that make Turtle WoW special?

I don’t think this has to be an either/or choice, and that’s where I fundamentally disagree.

Turtle WoW didn’t grow because it chased pure gameplay efficiency. It grew because it offered a cohesive experience where gameplay, atmosphere, and lore reinforced each other. That’s the whole reason people were willing to accept slower pacing, longer leveling, and even inconvenience, because the world felt meaningful.

If lore truly “wasn’t worth it at the cost of gameplay,” Turtle WoW wouldn’t exist in the first place. There are already servers that optimize everything for queues, balance, and instant access. People came here specifically because this project valued immersion and world integrity alongside gameplay, not as an afterthought.

Long queues are a real problem, but sacrificing the faction war, one of the core pillars of Warcraft PvP, isn’t a neutral fix. It changes what battlegrounds are. WSG and AB aren’t abstract esports maps; they’re narrative battlefields. Once you strip that away, you’re not just solving a queue issue, you’re redefining the game’s identity.

And I’d argue that undermining that identity is just as dangerous to the long-term health of the server as long queues are. Players don’t stay on Turtle WoW because it’s the fastest or most convenient option. They stay because it feels like a living world with stakes and meaning.

So yes, gameplay matters. Queue times matter. But so does the soul of the game. Treating lore and immersion as expendable “nice-to-haves” is how you slowly turn a unique server into just another one.
