> **Deeno wrote: Mon Apr 28, 2025 9:19 am**
> ****PVP gearing****
>
>  The elephant in the room when it comes to pvp is gear disparity - a hot topic as postnaxx content rolls out. **My solution? Make battlegrounds restricted by item level.**
>
>  **How Would It Work?**
>  If a player’s item level exceeds the battleground cap, they cannot queue. If they swap to higher itemlevel gear while inside, they are kicked with a deserter debuff.
>
>  For example, if the item level cap is 78, there are two primary ways to calculate this restriction, each with pros and cons:
>
>  1. absolute ilvl cap (78 × 15 = 1170 total item level)
>  ✔ simple and clear
>  ✖ doesn’t properly account for weapons, disadvantaging players with dual-wield/mainhand-offhand/shield setups
>
>  2. average ilvl cap (total item level divided by used slots ≤ 78)
>  ✔ accounts for weapon differences fairly
>  ✖ creates an awkward meta where players might intentionally leave certain slots empty to lower their average item level
>
>  ![➡️](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/27a1.svg) the best approach: a hybrid system
>
>  To balance both issues, the best solution would be an absolute item level cap, but with weapons contributing as an average rather than a hard limit. This avoids the weapon disadvantage of the first system while preventing the empty-slot meta of the second.
>
>  (Additionally, this would require a UI update in the character tab, clearly displaying absolute item level to help players track their eligibility.)
>
>  **Why this is the longterm solution?**
>
>  Instead of constantly buffing pvp gear every time a new raid is released - a system that is not sustainable - or doing nothing at all, which would cause pvp gear to gradually become irrelevant as pve content progresses, this approach removes the need for artificial stat inflation.
>  - no need for new pvp exclusive stats like resilience
>  - pvp gear (including reputation rewards etc) can instead be designed to offer better stats relative to their item level
>  - rank 12-14 gear could receive minor stat buffs, but the main balancing factor would be item level itself
>
>  **The ripple effect**
>
>  **This system wouldn’t just balance battlegrounds - it would revitalize other aspects of the game:**
>  - players would need to optimize gear within a set limit, making quest rewards, crafted gear, and reputation items more relevant
>  - consequently rural areas and trade would become richer, as players seek out diverse gearing options
>  - higher item level pieces would still be valuable, but players could balance them out with lower ilvl gear, creating more diverse builds rather than just stacking raid bis

## Post 141223 by Thaladar (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2025-07-25T08:08:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=141223#p141223 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I am fine with PvE players bringing raid gear into PvP. They earned it and that is fine. The problem is that PvP needs easily accessible PvP gear to counterbalance it.

I would not even mind starting PvP gear being 30 to 40 percent weaker if it was easy to get. That way, raid-geared players would still have an edge, but skilled PvP players could still make plays and win fights. Right now, you are fighting backpedaling raid bosses casting Lightning Bolt or Frostbolt and two-shotting you.

The result is clear:

 * PvE geared players demolish new players
 * The team with the most raid gear usually wins
 * New PvP players are discouraged and often stop trying

This is not fun for anyone. I do not enjoy one-shotting green geared players, and I do not enjoy being one-shot by Naxx veterans either. My friends who have never PvPed before were shocked at how fast they died and did not queue again.

Whether it is resilience or another approach, we need easily attainable PvP gear so fights are about skill and decision making, not just who has the most gear. Some will say “I farmed scripted NPCs for epics so I deserve to destroy players without resistance,” but that mindset is gatekeeping and bad for the game.

Making PvP accessible means more people queue, more people have fun, and the whole game benefits. That is what we all want.
