# WSG: discussion on allowable geometry

## Post 175566 by Urthius — 2026-04-17T14:28:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=175566#p175566 | page 1 | era: post-1.18.1 -->

The geometry of WSG allows many "jumps" that have become part of the game, even being included in the [Wowhead guide](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/guide/classic-wow-warsong-gulch-advanced-map-strategies-wsg#jump-spots).

However, since TWoW isn't exactly Classic, the permissibility of jumps has been subject to [debate](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?t=23177).

The goal of this thread is to generate a **public consensus** on what is and isn't allowable in WSG on TWoW.

When I asked a GM, I received the following reply: "Use this simple rule: if place can be reached with normal walking or jumping (no walljumping), this place is okay to use."

My interpretation of the rule:
1. "Normal walking or jumping" means for all races/classes/genders **without speed buffs or slow fall effects**.
2. Alliance Fence Jump (slipping through fence) is **allowed**.
3. Alliance Graveyard Jump is **allowed** (a "walljump" to an accessible place, and devs seem to allow it).
4. Horde Graveyard Jump is **allowed** (a speed buff to an accessible place, and devs seem to allow it).
5. Alliance Top of Tunnel Jump is **allowed**.
6. Horde Top of Tunnel Jump is **allowed**.
7. Horde Fence Jump is **not allowed** (warned by GM, and not all races/classes/genders can do it).
8. Horde Caravan Jump is **not allowed** (warned by GM, even though it is theoretically a normal jump).
 - Presumably the same applies for Alliance.
9. Fence walking is **not allowed**.
10. Stump Jump is **not allowed**.
11. "Safespotting" (e.g. on hut) is **not allowed**.
12. Mage blink to inaccessible locations is **not allowed**.
13. Warrior intervene from bottom to top of graveyard is **allowed** (a class ability to an accessible place).
I view #3 and #4 above (the graveyard jumps) as debatable - devs seem to know about them and allow them.

The Wowhead article includes videos of the above jumps. Vega12 posted [some videos](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=162358#p162358) of other jumps, but these videos have since expired. Here's [another video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HY_7Navr6yo) of WSG jumps. And [another](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hCdzetaf1yY).

Ideally walljumping would be fixed, but until devs can devote their limited resources to patching it, community enforcement is the only option.

 **Edit**: changed graveyard jumps, changed warrior intervene, added Horde Fence/Caravan Jump.

“**WSG jumps were never intended, so they should be removed**” is a bad argument.

Almost every competitive game in history has high-skill mechanics that started as unintended, but became core parts of gameplay:

- Bunnyhopping & rocket jumping (Quake/TF2)

- Strafe-jumping (CS)

- Wavedashing (Smash Melee)

- Creep pulling & aggro tricks (DotA)

- Mutalisk stacking (StarCraft BW)

- Slope boosting in Titanfall 2

- Tap-strafing in Apex/Titanfall
If developers removed everything unintended, these games would lose the very depth that made them great.

WoW is no different.
Classic/Vanilla are full of emergent mechanics Blizzard never planned but players turned into real skill expression:

- Heroic Strike queuing

- Spell batching tricks (In 1.12 you could "jump" cancel the stun of charges/intercept, you still can vanish incoming CCs like death-coil or rocket-helm, you can still "target dummy" the hunter trap)

- wsg / ab / av jumps

- Countless PvP techs like LoS jukes and timing abuses
These weren’t designed — but they became core to how Vanilla actually plays.

WSG jumps are the same category.
They don’t make you invulnerable, they don’t break the map, and they have counterplay.
What they do is reward:

- mechanical skill

- map knowledge

- movement mastery

- creative flag-carrying routes

- coordinated counterplay
Without jumps, FCing becomes almost entirely a gear/gold check. With jumps, it has a real skill ceiling and enables even green geared players to pick up the flag — just like every other competitive game.

If “it wasn’t intended” was a valid reason to delete things, WoW would lose half of the mechanics that define Vanilla gameplay today.

Unintended ≠ bad.
Unintended is where most of the depth in competitive games comes from.

Was it ever intended that raids leave two-thirds of the trash mobs alive in Molten Core? Even Warcraft Logs—the largest parsing site—requires guilds to kill a minimum number of mobs just to make runs count.

Was it intended that players use Invis Pots to skip entire sections of Blackwing Lair?

Unintended ≠ bad.
Unintended is where most of the depth in competitive games comes from.
