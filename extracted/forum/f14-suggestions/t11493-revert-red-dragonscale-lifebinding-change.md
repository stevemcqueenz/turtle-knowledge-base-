# Revert Red Dragonscale Lifebinding Change

## Post 76077 by Shockhoof — 2023-11-05T22:32:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=76077#p76077 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

[This is referencing the October 30th, 2023 change - Reduced the spell power scaling of Lifebinding to 10%](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?t=11294).

I play a [Shaman](https://armory.turtlecraft.gg/#!/character/Shockhoof) and have been using the 4 piece Red Dragonscale set bonus since the set was created.  This change vastly diminishes the value of an interesting and rewarding gearing option for Paladins and Shamans, relegating the set to a pre-raid / early raiding option for both of these classes.  I suggest to revert the change and to allow late game healers an alternative itemization path.

For reference, Prior to the nerf, the set's Lifebinding HOT had ~85% scaling with healing power.  It has a 25% chance to proc on any direct healing effect.  At 85% scaling, your typical mid game Shaman's 700ish healing power meant that the Lifebinding HOT would provide ~850 healing over 12 seconds.  An end game, a BIS geared shaman using the set would have ~1100 healing power, causing Lifebinding to do 1175 healing over 12 seconds.

The set is well balanced as a pre-raid option for healers, providing very strong healing power, and a strong if inconsistent HOT effect with some stamina.  The fire resistance of the set is a small nicety in early raids you'd expect to use this set, MC and BWL.  Despite the set's complete lack of intelligence, MP5 or spell critical chance, Lifebinding's decent spell scaling allowed the set to retain value into the late game.  Healing Paladins and especially Shamans had a interesting gearing choice to make on whether to break their set bonus with traditional gearing itemization.

As a Shaman, the 25% proc chance means that across 3 hits of a chain heal, you have ~55% chance for the effect to land at least once.  This would offset some of the variance a 25% proc chance presents while raid healing, and notable healing across a raid during AOE intense encounters, especially during longer progession style raiding.  Without the MP5 or intelligence on other raid level healing pieces, using the Red Dragonscale set meant that users had to use consumes more aggressively and were at a disadvantage when doing mana intensive strategies like totem twisting.  This meant that there was now an actual viable gearing alternative which had interesting costs and benefits compared to typical Shaman end game itemization of full tier 2.5 and 3 piece tier 2, or combinations of Tier 3.

I started using this set during my character's early raid gearing, and would regularly use this set in dungeons and raids until I acquired my Tier 2.5 and 3 piece Tier 2 combination.  For dungeon healing or mana intensive progression healing, the set's strong scaling meant that I could use novel strategies like using extremely low ranked heals to fish for Lifebinding procs, allowing me to conserve mana in a way that the Shaman never had access to.  This is an interesting and refreshing way to experience healing and only viable if the coeffient is strong.

Once I had the classic Tier 2.5 and Tier 3 combo, I opted for the greater mana pool and stronger up front healing for most raid content, as this gave me more mana pool for totem twisting and greater up front healing.  The Lifebinding HOT is strong, however competes with the Tier 2.5's 5 piece bonus of faster chain heals, which equates to ~20% more healing over time.  Using Tier 2.5 or T3 with T2 3 piece gives stronger up front direct healing, which in better itemized raid groups is preferable, as HOTs lose value when raid healing is strong.  As mentioned above, T3, T2 and T2.5 have far better stat bonuses and actually increase intelligence, MP5 or spell critical chance, which means that continuing to use the full Red Dragonscale set you pay a steep price in mana pool and sustained casting.  Just prior to the nerf, I was going to experiment using the Red Dragonscale 4 piece with a combination of T2 3 piece and T3 2 piece, however I no longer have incentive to do this.

I strongly hope that developers consider reverting this change.  The set as it was presented a novel healing effect that allowed for new play styles, powerful healing for progression style raiding with a large cost to mana, MP5 or spell crit which made the set interesting and fun for a healer's itemization planning well into late game.

EDIT:
After some discussion on discord, wanted to clarify old and current coefficient.  It seems the dev that adjusted the effect referred to 10% healing power per tick of healing.  In the past, it was ~21% healing power per tick of healing.  The 85% I referenced in the post was total healing power over the HOT, the current nerf is 40% total healing power over the HOT.

## Post 76117 by Rramage — 2023-11-06T09:33:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=76117#p76117 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I play a healer paladin and I am also enthusiastic about the red dragonscale set, and wish to see its nerf reverted.

Like Shockhoof mentioned, it opens up alternative healing strategies for paladins as well as shamans. The lifebinding proc is a tremendous boon on high movement fights where my only other healing in motion is holy shock with its long cooldown. Being able to heal with more flexibility unlocks a more dynamic playstyle where I can be more present in the mix, running in to drop a hammer of justice on critical mobs, spend global cooldowns on actions such as judging light or wisdom on a target, contribute a consecration to aoe, or other things that there simply isn't room for when all you do is plant your feet, target the tank, and put a dippy bird on your flash of light key. Lifebinding also opens up raid healing as an option, where a downranked flash of light to top up a damaged character can proc a good heal over time to keep them in the fight. It's *fun*.
Red Dragonscale also comes with some setbacks:  Low stamina, total lack of intellect, no spell crit, no mp5.  Although the 4 piece proc is very good, maintaining the 4 piece set significantly lowers your amount of vital healing statistics.  Wearing Red Dragonscale commits you to a different playstyle than the traditional paladin healing gear.  Options are good!

Red Dragonscale also helps address the issue that paladin healing gear is a hot mess.  It's not until the very last raid of the game that we get a healing set.  Tier 1 is a joke for any spec, and while tier 2 is better it's still not a good healing set.  Ques has said on discord that there are no plans to introduce a T1 or T2 healing variant for paladins.  the ZG set and AQ20 set aren't healer gear, and T2.5 currently isn't healer gear although the planned healing version might be ok.
Meanwhile we're consigned to a mess of dungeon blues and cloth/leather off set pieces from raids.  A lovely teal dress from a dungeon is the best healing chest until AQ40, where it's replaced by another lovely teal dress.  The gear is a clown outfit that sticks out like a sore thumb amidst the other classes that start to look cool and iconic in their tier gear.  Leather butterfly wing shoulders and a teal dress don't fit the class fantasy of the paladin.  If we wanted to play a cloth wearing caster we'd have rolled priest.  The dragonscale sets have decent armor, look like real armor, and fit the class much better.  Maintaining Red Dragonscale as a viable gear choice gives us a choice in how we gear up and play, and choices are good!

Finally, I don't see any reason why this nerf was required.  Resto shamans already blow up healing meters with smart chain heals.  Paladins aren't racing ahead of other healing classes due to the ability to proc a HoT sometimes.  Red Dragonscale isn't great for PVP because of its low stamina and ability scores, and the HoT proc isn't strong enough to make a difference in pvp combat where HoTs get purged or dispelled or simply burst through. The only possible reason I can see why you'd want to nerf red dragonscale is because it's stronger than some early raid options, to which I'd reply: so give us better raid gear options!

Please revert this nerf.  It's unnecessary and only serves to hurt one of the very few, precious gearing and playstyle decisions that we get to make.
