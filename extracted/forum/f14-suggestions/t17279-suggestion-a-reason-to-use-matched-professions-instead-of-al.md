# Suggestion: A reason to use matched professions instead of alts for collecting matts

## Post 118004 by Fugus (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2025-01-26T17:25:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=118004#p118004 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I know on retail it was a huge thing where people would have their main who had the professions and then gathering alts  after they had established their accounts. My suggestion is to offer recipies that require you have both the profession and its matched to use.

Basically like this, all items mentioned bind on pickup, are not consumed on use and require more matts than the consumable versions of what they are based on as well as require you to know how to create the original recipe.

Alchemy + Herbalism = Endless flasks and potions (Includes the strongest healing potions as well as utility potions). The flasks persist through death and last until canceled or replaced. This profession would require the steepest increase in matts to create these variants and require you to go into Scholomance to use the Alchemy lab to create them with at least a 12 hour cool down between each one you create so only 1 created per trip. But being able to no longer have to farm for your own personal versions of those flasks or potions you have on tap would be nice. And if you dumped either alchemy or herbalism you would lose access to using these variants until you releveled those skills.

Blacksmithing + Mining =
 -Master Key that opens any lock.
 -Reusable versions of their weight stones, sharpening stones, weapon chains, shield spike. and mithril spurs.

Engineering + Mining =
 -Reusable versions of their trinkets that don't have charges so they can be reused. Does not break feral forms to use.
 -Reusable versions of their spawned pets but with a longer and shared cool down. Does not break feral forms to use.
 -A new item that turns turns simple wood and metal bars into a full stack of arrows and another that turns coal and metal bars into bullets. These crafted items are Bind on Pickup and endgame level.
 -Reusable lock pick except this one is named "Bent Spring" with a flavor text that reads "We wasted all those explosives to destroy locks when all we needed was this to open them...",
 -Reusable bomb but without any crowd control attached to it. Does not break feral form to use.

Tailoring + Enchanting =
 -Enchanted Gause which is a bandage that doesn't get consumed. Takes Cloth and Enchanting Essence to create.
 -Endless versions of Mana and Wizard Oils. Their descriptions would list them as enchanted polishing cloths.
 -Soul Gate (New Item): used to create soul gems. Is reusable and creates 1 stack each use with no material cost attached to it.

Leatherworking + Skinning (This one is hard because it really doesn't bring much utility to it to begin with)
 -The ability to turn softer versions of leather into a substitute for their harder level equivalents. Example: You could farm leather and turn it into an item that could be a substitute for a turtle scale that makes the same level of gear. (Thought of this because farming for mail materials generally SUCKS on leatherworking allow this to be used while leveling unlike the other things mentioned)
 -Trivially cheap armor kits to create for ones self.
 -It's own personal version of the Mithril Spur and Weapon Chains.

These are mainly off the top of my head. A good way to actually make it rewarding to keep your professions matching without being overpowered as all of these professions already brought these to the table except for the leatheworking ones or just removed mindless farming associated with certain classes. The most balance changing ones would be for alchemist and engineers not to have to farm every time they used their stuff but with a high enough resource cost to create it, it should be fine. Having and Endless Free Action Potion is good but having it require 2 black lotus and 2 oil blackmouth stacks and 40 strangle kelp to create, should more than balance it out.

TL;DR - last sentence

That's the thing I hated about WoTLK adding the stupid achievements system to the game. A friend of mine said he really liked it years ago. I asked why is that. The answer was something along the lines of "I like it when it tells me I've done something and I like collecting achievements". Which in my opinion stems from a psychological issue with a feeling of inferiority - you need some kind of "medals" for your "achievements" that others can see.

I see no reasons for achievements to exist in any game, they are quite stupid, useless and just add nothing to the game. The achievement isn't anything like acquiring a very hard to get piece of gear or being a certain level and having certain stats as it doesn't affect the game at all.

Achievements completely change the way the game feels and how players are playing it. Vanilla WoW is about that classic, old school experience of adventure, discovery, overcoming difficulties and connecting with other people. Old School games have a different merit system in the sense that they don't have one - your actual progress and actions in the game serve as your achievements, and achievements can be anything you want them to be. When you have predetermined achievements baked into the game, that's it and completing them more than once doesn't feel like the first time. My personal achievement/goal that I wanted for a long time was to have a level 60 character in Vanilla, because while I played for years, I always ran out of patience, will and determination to do it. I always lost interest and drive to do it and it kept bugging me for years, after I've done it on Turtle WoW, I no longer think about that anymore.

Which is why such things like "rewarding people for doing basically nothing" have no place in Turtle WoW or any other classic old school game for that matter. They come from the mentality people acquired from playing trash mobile games - you get rewarded every step of the way for doing basically nothing, it f*cks with your dopamine and you get addicted to being rewarded for everything you do.

How will having rewards for people using matched professions improve the game? I am beginning to be curious what problem does the OP have with this? Is he bothered that people aren't using matching professions or he expects that by having matching professions, it should be viewed as some kind of social status so when people see his character and say to themselves "oh, he has matching professions and he's getting some rewards for it, maybe I should do it too so I don't feel like I'm missing out on something. Now it seems like it delves into that FOMO psychology black hole - you start doing things, like with achievements, not because you want to, but because you've been made to perceive it as the optimal or meta way of doing things and you won't do things any other way.

On a fundamental level this is wrong and redundant and has no place in the game.

I personally never cared about achievements in the games. When they started all the stuff on retail, as a Tauren druid with a host of titles I could choose from, I was "Fugus of Thunderbluff" because I liked the sound of it.

As for my thing about matching sets, it isn't anything about achievements or feeling inferior or any of that. It's to balance out the game design where you are actually worse off as a character due to the setup.

As it stands, a druid with Alchemy and Engineering having an alt druid that is a Herbalist and Miner just leads with a superior choice. His main gets all the exclusives of both of those professions while his other can nearly be in greys and just run around farming stuff when needed.

As for how will having bonuses for people having matching professions improve the game? Easy, by actually giving them a benefit that doesn't change the balance of the game for not creating an alt just to be their mat collector for them.

Think of it this way, if there is no reason to give a benefit for matching them, then we should remove all the BoP stuff or profession requirements to use their stuff so you don't get any benefit for being an engineer over not beyond just being able to make the bombs or trinket yourself.

Face it, there is a major reason why people create alts expressly to farm, just like there is a reason many people gravitate towards certain races when they roll certain classes, because those races provide material benefits to the roll they are going for. Just like you aren't likely to see too many Warlocks, Mages or Priests who are Leatherworkers or Blacksmiths.
