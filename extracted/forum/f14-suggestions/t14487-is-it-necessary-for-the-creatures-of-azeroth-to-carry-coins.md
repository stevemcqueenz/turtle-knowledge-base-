Money in wow is kinda a mishmash of fiat currency and commodity money.

It has an intrinsic value, because it is made out of copper, silver and gold. These materials all have a value and money made out of these materials therefore has a value. It is not entirely commodity money though if you think about it. A gold bar can be sold to a vendor for 6 silver. If what gives wow money its value was entirely the value of the material used, you'd have to assume that a single gold coin was made out of 16.6 gold bars, which doesn't make a whole lot of sense if you consider the weight. The value of a gold coin therefore also has to come from somewhere else. A treasury or central bank must have been minting those coins in a way that made it impossible to copy. If you could simply copy a gold coin and create them from gold bars, the value of gold bars wouldn't be 6 silver (= 0.06 gold).

Now this leads to even weirder places, because you can use the same gold coin to trade with alliance and horde vendors alike. There's only one currency. So the alliance and horde both use the same money but are mortal enemies. How would they agree upon the amount of money in circulation? By definition, money needs to have the ability to store value. How would Thrall prevent Tyrande from simply minting more coins when the Nightelves ran out of cash and thus causing hyperinflation and devaluing all of the horde's money?  They'd need a neutral organization that both sides could trust to exclusively enforce financial stability.

Anyway. The coins have a value. Now a murloc can't just walk to Goldshire and buy some boots for some copper, why would he have money? Money would be as valuable to a murloc as a wallet with 500 Prussian franks would be to you. You cannot buy any goods with said money, so it doesn't have any value apart from the value of the material used to create the money. It doesn't have additional value as a fiat currency for a murloc.

But Furbolgs have money aswell. And Furbolgs do trade with both sides. So a Furbolg would sell you a potion for cash because he can rely on the fiat currency. He sells a potion to an orc for cash because he can use this cash to buy items from the next orc or a nightelf. Who else do Furbolgs trade with? Maybe they do trade with Naga. And cause Naga know they can use cash to trade with Furbolgs and therefore can trade with horde and alliance, cash has value for Naga aswell - value beyond the pure value of the material used. Who do Naga trade with? Maybe they trade with murlocs.

Why does a murloc have cash? Cause he knows he can trade cash for goods with a Naga, who then trades the cash for goods with a Furbolg, who then trades cash for goods with you.

So ignoring the cross-faction nature of Nord server for a moment.  Let's dive into it.

'A neutral third party that regulates and keeps track of inflation between factions'
Goblins!  Duh!  The goblins are in charge of managing and controlling inflation while encouraging cross-faction trading (via their neutral AH system) because they want profit from ANY party with the coin!  The 'Durotar Labor Union' is not aligned with the larger Goblin populations and are a 'radical splinter group' that hates the Alliance and Alliance allies.

Monsters get coin and items from defeating adventurers.  If we wanted to really go into this we'd have to remove all items from wolves or other 'non-sentient' beasts entirely and that would interfere with the gameplay experience.   It would require some alternate drops (furs, skins, teeth, organs, etc) otherwise players would have an even tougher time affording things than they do already (at least pre-60).  FF12 did this method and it seems ok but it is admittedly boring.  There's no 'oh wow I got a random green/blue drop' dopamine hit.

That said, FF12 did an alternative 'crafting' system (albeit in a janky way) of selling 'junk' items from beasts in order to unlock purchasable goods.  Implementation/testing of such a system would go far beyond the scope of the current content cadence, but I suppose it could be viable.  Turn in 20 murloc legs in exchange for a token, exchange tokens for green or even a blue item.

All interesting ideas but I don't see a need to complicate things at this point in time.  Either the tokens/gear are too easy to acquire and players are far better geared than they should be, or they take so long to acquire that the player outlevels the gear entirely.
