# Suggestion Regarding the New Cross-Faction Multiboxing Rule on Tel’Abim

## Post 170906 by Xeenar2 — 2026-03-15T19:34:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=170906#p170906 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

**The recently added rule states:**

> “Due to the uniqueness of the PVP environment, it is forbidden to multibox on characters of different factions. Any multiboxed characters must be of the same faction at all times.”

I understand the intention behind this rule, which seems to be preventing players from using opposite-faction characters to gain real-time intelligence during world PvP. In theory this could make encounters less predictable and reduce the ability to perfectly track enemy movements.

However, I have some concerns regarding how this rule can realistically be enforced and how it may affect the PvP environment on Tel’Abim.

 *1. Enforcement will be difficult to verify*

A player could simply log out of their main faction character, log onto an opposite-faction character to scout, relay the information through Discord, and then log back to their main when combat begins. From the server’s perspective this would appear as normal gameplay.

Guilds could also obtain the same information by having another player on the opposite faction report movements. In practice this achieves the same result the rule is trying to prevent, while technically not violating the rule.

 *2. Reporting may lead to frequent suspicion-based reports*

Because the behavior the rule attempts to stop is difficult to observe directly, players may end up reporting others based purely on suspicion.

For example, if a guild reacts quickly to an enemy movement, opposing players may assume cross-faction scouting or multiboxing is occurring. This could create a situation where GMs receive reports that are difficult to verify because the suspected behavior may simply be normal scouting or communication between players.

 *3. Limited impact on coordinated PvP*

In organized world PvP, intelligence gathering is usually done by multiple players rather than a single individual. Guilds frequently use voice communication to relay information about enemy movement.

Because of this, removing cross-faction multiboxing may not significantly change how intelligence is shared. Information will still travel quickly through voice communication once any player spots an enemy group.

 *4. Hardcore player hunting*

One scenario where the rule may seem relevant is when players attempt to hunt hardcore characters. Preventing cross-faction scouting could theoretically reduce the ability to track them.

In practice, however, these hunts are usually carried out by multiple players coordinating together. As soon as one player finds the target, the information is shared through voice communication, which means the rule may not meaningfully change the outcome.

 **Suggestion**

It may be helpful to clarify:
[*]What specific behaviors constitute a violation.
[*]How the rule will realistically be enforced.
[*]Whether normal scouting and information sharing between players is considered acceptable.

 **Final thought:**

Tel’Abim already has a relatively small PvP population. World PvP often happens because guilds anticipate each other’s movements, react to raid activity, or try to contest entrances and objectives.

 *This raises a broader question:*
Do we want world PvP to happen less frequently on a smaller server? And are we trying to prevent guilds from intercepting or contesting other guilds attempting PvE raids? These types of interactions have historically been a major driver of world PvP.

Clarifying the intention behind the rule would help players better understand what kind of PvP environment the server is aiming to encourage.
