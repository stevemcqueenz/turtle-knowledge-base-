# Ungoro Park Dungeon

## Post 94679 by Plainbear — 2024-05-13T00:22:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=94679#p94679 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

A group of overly greedy Goblins tried to use Dinosaurs and place them in a park in southern Ungoro to make money, and everything went bad before the park ever opened. They now need adventurers to take down the Arcanely enhanced Dinosaurs.

This would be a level 60 Dungeon with 5-6 Bosses, mostly Dinosaurs in destroyed wire cages in the jungle and a small ocean side area for a giant Hydra dinosaur.

I find that Ungoro need some support as it feel mostly empty and forgotten by non Dino skinning hunters. This dungeon could give a good source of leathers and use an often underused mob model of Goblins and Dinosaur.  Quest could include retrieving dino eggs from a science area, rescuing little child goblins and starting the Electric fences again.

Im not really into WoW modding for a few reasons:
 - the available resources are often outdated or hard to find
 - many tools are difficult to get working properly
 - the "community" can be pretty closed off - not very beginner friendly
There is definitely an economic aspect to it: if someone figures things out, they often set up their own private server and keep their methods private to maintain an edge.

That said, here's what i did:
 - (recommended) setup a virtual machine for safety
 - download a pre-built version of Noggit (blue) from GitHub (technically, you can compile it yourself, but the required third-party dependencies are not available the way they say it is)
 - find a clean WOTLK client from a shady website
 - connect noggit and the client

If youre looking for help, here is an invite to WoW Blender Studio: <https://discord.gg/KSkXHnxx>.
Once inside, check out the useful-channels section and follow links to other relevant servers (Noggit > Builds/stable-releases -> github).

If you want to mod with the TWOW client specifically, you'll need even more tools and workarounds. Honestly, it's probably easier to join the twow team as a QA or GM.
After just scratching the surface, I feel like a UE5 client remake is more than justified - but it's unlikely TWOW will ever opensource that…

**Devilsauric Park**

Deep within the steaming crater of UnGoro, where the earth still breathes as if the world has not yet decided what it wishes to become and where every shadow feels older than memory itself, a group of goblins once attempted to impose order upon something that was never meant to bow, where life always finds a way, believing that ancient predators could be confined behind steel and wire, that primordial instincts could be monetized, that the oldest ecosystem in Azeroth could be turned into spectacle and profit, and so they constructed cages, electric fences, viewing platforms and underground facilities, imagining crowds where there would only ever be silence and coin where there would only ever be consequence, preoccupied with whether they could and never stopping to think if they should, yet ambition built on arrogance rarely survives contact with reality and the park collapsed before it could ever open, leaving behind broken enclosures, sparking towers and a jungle that wastes no time reclaiming what never belonged to mortals in the first place.

But one of the greatest dangers within these ruins is not just the dinosaurs that now roam freely through twisted metal and shattered gates, nor the unstable arcane systems still humming beneath reinforced flooring, but a single dwarf who walks these grounds with deliberate purpose and cold certainty, a hunter who did not come for profit nor for redemption, but as the old rhyme would say, a hunter from the darkest wild makes you feel just like a child, because to him UnGoro is not a disaster site and not a failed attraction but the last true proving ground where predator and prey are stripped of illusion.

He calls himself Thargrim the Exiled, and on the stock of his rifle, beneath carved scratches that were not made by accident, the faint outline of a name remains visible to those who look closely, the words reading that the name “Nesingwary” has been scratched out, not erased but violently crossed through.

He does not present himself as a conventional boss awaiting challenge within a chamber, nor does he waste breath on long monologs, for he prefers the tension of uncertainty, appearing without warning as adventurers advance through broken pathways and shattered enclosures, marking one among them with silent precision and then retreating into the canopy, because he always aims for the head and he always chooses carefully, and in those moments of suspended breath when the jungle itself seems to pause and the air feels heavier than it should, a nervous goblin survivor mutters with forced laughter,

“You know who survived the last expedition? The priest. Only one left. Dropped to his knees prayin’ when the shots started. Lucky coincidence, huh?”

and the joke lingers longer than it should, because when the crosshairs settle and the silence stretches thin, whoever bears his mark understands that this is not random chance but calculated pursuit.

When Thargrim finally stands his ground in his own encampment among broken skull trophies and half burned journals, he speaks not loudly but with sharpened conviction,

“They said I hunt without honor. I say they hunt without conviction.”

and in earlier ambushes when he vanishes into smoke he leaves behind colder lines from the shadows,

“If it bleeds, it can die.”

or a simple and confident

“Anytime.”

before disappearing once more into foliage and steel.

Deeper within the park, behind reinforced glass and sealed metal doors humming faintly with unstable energy, lies a laboratory no one can enter, its panels flickering with dim arcane light and shadows moving behind the fogged interior as if something still grows below, and though no key responds and no lever turns, the presence of that sealed facility suggests that the park was never meant to stop here, that something larger was incubating beneath the surface, waiting for a future moment when the doors might finally open.

When at last Thargrim falls and the rifle grows silent, the old rail system of the park can be reactivated, for he used its elevated structure as a sniper vantage and carried the key upon him, and the ride that follows is not reward but reminder, allowing players to mount a restored cart and glide along fixed tracks through broken attractions, collapsed fences and silent enclosures, witnessing from movement the remains of ambition and the quiet dominance of a jungle that never needed permission to survive.

 **Mechanics and Gameplay Integration**

Within the dungeon itself Thargrim functions as a recurring presence woven naturally into progression rather than isolated to a single chamber, meaning that at specific intervals during advancement through trash areas he briefly appears from elevated structures or concealed vantage points, applies a visible mark to a randomly selected player to signal that he has chosen his prey, and then retreats into smoke or grappling movement while preparing a delayed high impact shot, reinforcing the narrative truth that he always aims for the head and subtly encouraging players to react without overt tutorial prompts, especially as environmental dialogue from goblin survivors hints that kneeling was the only reason a previous priest survived, thereby allowing the simple act of using the kneel emote at the correct moment to negate or survive the incoming shot, transforming a basic animation into a survival mechanic without requiring complex vehicle systems, collision detection or modern phasing features.

These ambushes occur multiple times but remain controlled in frequency so that they create tension without disrupting pacing, gradually escalating in intensity until the final confrontation at his encampment where the fight expands beyond single marked shots into a more direct engagement that preserves his identity as a precise and disciplined predator rather than an over engineered gimmick encounter.
