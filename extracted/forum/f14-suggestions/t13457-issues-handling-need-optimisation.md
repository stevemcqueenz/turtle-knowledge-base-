# Issues handling need optimisation.

## Post 90841 by Melg — 2024-03-20T03:13:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=90841#p90841 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Today i got this message from Turt Reynolds in discord: "Put short, I think it would be best if you take a break from the bug reports forum. After maybe some weeks after I've had the time to sort through the mountain of new stuff, when you see what fraction of reports of yours were actually helpful for our team and weren't instead closed as spam, maybe we can all reassess".
When QA team think of new issues as "mountain of new stuff" while this mountain was basically created by few peoples in span of like 4 days. Clearly something wrong.
Possible solutions:
 1) Introduce backpressure - add something like daily maximum of issues per account, it can also be gamefied, so when person already has many of issues closed and not rejected - it should give larger daily quota. (as a side note, github had nice backpressure build in, cause there you had nice report template, you could make your issue look nice, had formatting, inplace images, videos etc. which takes time to make, and makes you more disciplined about it).
 2) make QA team larger
 3) filter out some possible issues when something is new/unprepared. For example, if it would be announced, that current site with report system is "development in progress" and you DONT need reports on its bugs right now - i wouldnt created them. Same is for new content introduced in wow.
 4) make issue handling multistaged - for example on first stage you need only to mark to which categories this issues belongs, that could do gms/ other staff members, then QA team can slowly process rest with replicability and prioritization for fixes.
 5) do not encourage people to report issues if your staff cant handle them. Gms, public relations lead - all told me that can and should report issue, even when they are small and there is alot of them. But in reallity seems im not wery welcome with my reports.
 6) introduce canban workflow so you can actually notice choke points and streamline process from issue creation till its resolution.
 7) Introduce additional rules for issues creation which clearly expalins what feedback devs need from community and what is too much. As of today even separation between issue vs suggestion is wague and is not documented anywhere.
 8) improve search features on bug tracker, cause now some of issues can reappear just cause they cant be easily searched.
 9) Improve handling of old issues - cause now if you want your issue to be fixed, its better be on top of pile, so if reporter really want their issue to be fixed, and it was reportes by someone else 1 year ago and wasnt fixed yet, he is incentifized to reintroduce it as new issue into system. For example i saw behavior of devs that just observe new issues on tracker, pick something that they like/want to fix, and fixing it right away, completely ignroring pipeline of categorization and prioritization of issues.

Im not paid by twow team and i see this outcome of investing time (gathering issues from others, verbalizing them, reproducing it, filming videos as reproducible proof) with not welcoming environment quite upsetting. Even if shouldnt opened that many of them, i was never qualified worker on paid job to be accessed. Im just community member, which started to document ideas and issues i encountered in hope them to be fixed and our twow experience to be improved.

It's important when submitting issues in the interest of Quality Assurance, that you don't inundate the developer with opinions and issues specific only to the individual. A high quality bug meets the following standards:
 1. It addresses a specific problem that hinders the ability of the player to play the game.
 2. It provides easy to reproduce steps that lead directly to the resultant bug at least 50% of the time (bugs that cannot be reproduced are generally not worth reporting until they can be).
 3. It is clear and concise.
 4. It is not an opinion, but objectively a bug that needs to be fixed.
 5. If it is an opinion or quality of life issue and not a bug, it be labeled clearly as such.

The reality of game development and bug tracking, as well as having a limited pool of developers who can handle any specific bugs (and no, hiring more can often do more harm than good, or take months of training), means that most bugs get prioritized in order of severity. Crash bugs, Memory leaks, and other things that can lock up a computer are prioritized first, then crash and desync bugs, then anything that can render the game unplayable, then bugs that are inconveniences with work-around, then minor bugs, then finally... If they can get to them, quality of life issues, and lastly, opinions.

I think the issue you're seeing is that you believe your opinions are more important than addressing actual game issues. I advise you to consider seeking therapy for your issue:
<https://www.betterhelp.com/>
