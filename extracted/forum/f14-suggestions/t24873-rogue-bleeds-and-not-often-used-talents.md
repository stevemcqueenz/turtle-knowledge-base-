# Rogue bleeds and not often used talents

## Post 175429 by Wolfmanpaulx — 2026-04-16T07:06:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=175429#p175429 | page 1 | era: post-1.18.1 -->

Smoke Bomb, as one of the first Subtlety toolkit talents, is not performing as intended and is extremely rarely situational, which results in it being unused in most Subtlety builds.

Current state:
 35 energy, 8 yards radius, 8 sec duration, and a 20% chance for all targets inside to either be hit or to successfully hit.
It was originally intended as a PvP anti-caster defensive cooldown for situations where you are rooted, and Vanish or other root breaks (such as Improved Sprint) are on cooldown.

However, in practice, you can still get hit and pressured inside it to the point where it does not meaningfully change the outcome of a fight. Because of this, it often feels like the effect is not impactful enough to justify its usage.

On top of that, it has a 5-minute cooldown, which is quite high for a situational utility ability with inconsistent defensive value.

Proposed redesign idea:
 35 energy, 8 yards radius, 8 sec duration.

Instead of modifying hit chance inside the smoke, I would suggest shifting its identity toward a clearer offensive/utility window for Subtlety:

Increase damage of bleeds/DoTs by ~10% for targets affected by Smoke Bomb during its duration.
This would create a controlled 8-second pressure window where Subtlety can amplify Garrote, Rupture, poisons, and other bleed/DoT effects. Given that most DoTs tick roughly every 2 seconds, this would effectively enhance around 3–4 ticks during the window, which keeps the overall damage increase noticeable but not overwhelming, especially considering the 5-minute cooldown.

In addition, to preserve the original defensive intent of Smoke Bomb and improve its PvP usefulness:

Apply ~15% direct physical and magical damage reduction to targets inside the smoke.
This reflects the intuitive idea that visibility and precision are reduced inside a smoke cloud, making direct hits less effective and AoE pressure more effective. It would also help mitigate burst situations, while still allowing counterplay through positioning and control.

An additional talent that does not reflect current Subtlety tree usage, nor any modern Rogue build design (outside of some very old experimental “Rogue tank” concepts), is Blinding Haze.

Current state:

Blinding Haze reduces the energy cost of Distract by 5/10/15, and targets affected by Distract have their chance to hit reduced by 2/4/6% for 6 seconds.

In practice, this talent is rarely picked up by most players. The energy reduction is effectively negligible in real gameplay, since 15 energy is roughly one passive energy tick and Distract itself is already a situational tool used mainly for repositioning, crowd control setup, or pulling mobs safely. In most cases, Rogues already have enough time to wait for energy regeneration before opening, meaning this reduction does not meaningfully change gameplay decisions.

The additional 2–6% hit reduction is also extremely low impact. Even when fully talented, it is not strong enough to justify a 3-point investment, except in very niche scenarios such as large AoE trash pulls (5+ mobs), which is not something Rogue gameplay is primarily designed around due to having only limited passive cleave options.

Design context issues:

Looking at current Subtlety design, several core abilities already define the spec’s damage structure:

Rupture is mostly tied to Taste for Blood, which effectively incentivizes its use by converting combo point expenditure into a physical damage increase (~2% per combo point during its duration). Without this interaction, Rupture would have significantly less relevance in most rotations.
Garrote is often used by non-dagger Rogues mainly because there are limited alternative openers in PvE, especially since many raid mobs are immune or resistant to crowd control effects like Cheap Shot.

As a result, both bleeds are already somewhat “forced” into the rotation for mechanical reasons rather than purely synergistic design.

Proposed redesign direction:

A more meaningful direction for a talent like Blinding Haze would be to shift it away from minor utility modifiers and instead reinforce the natural synergy between Rogue bleed effects.

For example:

When Garrote and Rupture are both active on the same target, their interaction becomes amplified, increasing tick rate by:

 33% / 66% / 100% (based on talent points)

This would effectively reduce their duration by 1 / 2 / 3 seconds (based on talent points), creating a synchronized bleed pressure window.

Design intent:

This change would encourage deliberate stacking and timing of bleed effects, reinforcing Subtlety’s identity as a spec that builds controlled pressure through sustained effects rather than relying on flat, passive bonuses or low-impact utility modifiers.

It also aligns existing mechanics (Garrote + Rupture already being part of baseline rotations) into a more coherent system where combining them creates meaningful payoff, instead of simply being individually “required but uninspired” buttons.

My intention isn’t to slow gameplay down with setup, but to address two “dead spots” in the Subtlety tree and make existing tools interact in a more meaningful way.

Right now, both Smoke Bomb and Blinding Haze feel like low-impact picks:

Smoke Bomb is too unreliable defensively for a 5-minute cooldown
Blinding Haze provides negligible value in real scenarios

At the same time, Garrote and Rupture are already part of the rotation, but they don’t meaningfully interact—they’re used because systems require them, not because combining them creates value.

The goal of the suggested changes isn’t to introduce burst scaling or new multiplicative stacking, but simply to:

create a short, controlled window (Smoke Bomb) where Subtlety can apply slightly more pressure
and make bleed effects feel connected rather than isolated buttons

From a PvE perspective:

This wouldn’t meaningfully affect short-lived trash (where frontloaded damage like Sinister Strike will still dominate)
But it would provide more consistent value on longer encounters where bleeds actually have time to tick

From a PvP perspective:

Bleeds are already naturally limited by resilience and regeneration
So even with improved interaction, they remain sustained pressure rather than burst

Overall, the intent isn’t to push extreme numbers, but to turn currently underused talents into something that supports a clearer Subtlety identity: controlled pressure through coordinated effects, rather than isolated abilities with little synergy.

The whole “just stack hit to make Smoke Bomb work” argument ignores how the game actually plays and what Subtlety is built around.

Yes, on paper you can push ~19% hit and reduce the downside. But that means heavily warping your gear around a stat that Sub barely values, just to make a **5-minute cooldown** feel less bad for a few seconds. Outside of that window, you’re simply weaker:

 * less crit / AP / agility → worse burst and finishers
 * worse survivability → harder resets
 * no real gain to Sub’s actual win condition (setup → control → execution)

And the core assumption is wrong anyway — that Smoke Bomb creates asymmetry.

It doesn’t.

Even if you negate your own penalty:

 * casters will still land spells
 * warriors / druids / paladins still connect with yellow abilities and bleeds
 * several abilities are effectively guaranteed to hit

So the idea that “you don’t miss while enemies do” just doesn’t hold in real PvP.

Also, you do realize other classes operate under the same or even better baseline hit conditions, right? This isn’t some unique advantage you’re unlocking — it’s a self-imposed handicap to keep up.

From a practical standpoint:

 * In PvP, its usefulness is extremely questionable — most players don’t get real value out of it
 * In PvE (especially raids), using it is more likely to get you facepalmed than praised — 35 energy + 5 min CD for negligible impact is borderline trolling

And more importantly — we don’t even need this kind of defensive gimmick in Subtlety.

What the spec actually benefits from is:

 * better synergy within the tree
 * meaningful DPS / CC / CP / energy tools
 * something that reinforces its identity instead of fighting it

Even the old Dust design had far more practical use cases in both PvP and PvE.

At this point, if Smoke Bomb can’t be meaningfully improved without forcing players into awkward stat stacking, it would honestly be better to just replace it with the old Dust. It was simpler, but actually usable — and that already makes it a stronger design.
