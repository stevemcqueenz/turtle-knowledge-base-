# Tokenize T1/T2/Dungeon Sets; Token Regroups

## Post 167500 by Azinforum — 2026-02-05T09:30:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=167500#p167500 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

TLDR; Title
If TWoW team didn't agree Tokens were the best option, K40 wouldn't use tokens.

Proposal
- Tokenize Dungeon/T1/T2

- Use relevant materials (Frayed Abom/Shadow Skin; Fiery/Lava Core; Elementium)

- Token G1: Priest, Mage, Warlock

- Token G2: Hunter, Shaman, Druid

- Token G3: Rogue, Warrior, Paladin

- Make raid bosses, with Tier Gear drops, drop 3 items

- 1st Item is 1 of 3 Tier Groups, 2nd Item can be Tier Token OR item, 3rd Item is boss loot
Key reasons
- Experience the cool set effects provided by gear

- Smooth out progression

- Dungeon Sets are iconically old-school cool. Why do they have to be so insufferable to get?

- Give purpose to old mats like Frayed Abom, Fiery/Lava Core

- Make low level raiding to high level raiding experience more consistent. Why do MC/BWL not have tokens, but all others do?

- Some Token grouping is overly skewed
Why is the current system not-ideal?
- Take the 11% chance, meaning every 10-weeks you see a specific piece of tier gear. Meaning with 4 of class-x in the raid it would take 40 Lockouts to get people their item

- Have a 33% chance for a token shared with 2 other classes, that the item may not even be a good option for, meaning every 3 weeks you have a chance to roll against 6 to 10 others. Meaning it will take 18 to 30 Lockouts for everyone to get their item
Ideas
- Allow disenchanting of all tokens into relevant materials exchange (MC to Fiery Core, AQ40 to Idols, etc)

- If dungeon set is tokenized, remove BoP nature of Frayed Abom/Skin of Shadow
Problems for Execution (I am not a dev, so just guesses)
- Some work to make new Tokens, etc

- If bumping base raid-boss loot to 3 drops, which really should be done, likely requires tweaking of tables

- Tokenziation would mean new NPCs required. Re-using NPCs in BRD center probably fine?
Notes
- For things like AQ40, where there are only two groups, that system is fine for now.

- It is likely more painful to change it, and offers little improvement

1: No required items in addition to the tokens. Just flat token exchange. If the aim is to introduce a system to smooth-out progression at the lowest three set (arguably four) set tiers, it is counterproductive to immediately add complications.

 2: Vanilla loot tables and average times to become fully geared were always rooted in the subscription model AND the development time for the next tier of content. It was slow because a) Blizzard wanted the money from people needing weeks and months to gear and b) they didn't want players who didn't want to gear alts ending their subscriptions while waiting for the developers to release the next raid. Turtle doesn't have a subscription model, only a donation model. They can (and should) reassess EVERY mechanic in Vanilla WoW to determine whether it exists because the subscription model existed or if it exists because it's a good game mechanic in and of itself.

 3: Loot tokens allow for a reasonable increase in the number of tokens dropped. Any worry about higher loot competition has an easy solution: drop more tokens. The increase in loot competition that people argue tokens created in Retail was probably by design to increase subscription retention while giving the appearance of abundance as a bait and switch. Again, motivated by the desire for subscription money. TWoW doesn't have a subscription model, therefore there's no tacked-on loss by adding tokens or increasing token drop counts.

 4: The precedence for tokens has already been set with Naxx (though I've heard that system could use some adjustment).

 5: More gear being readily available at pre- and lower raid tiers means more geared players progressing to be available to tackle higher raid tiers.

Early MMO design—especially in World of Warcraft—was explicitly built around keeping players engaged for years. Systems like time gates, slow progression, and lockouts were tools to stretch content consumption so subscription revenue remained stable. If a game no longer depends on subscriptions, it makes sense to reevaluate mechanics that were originally built around that retention model. Even *if* TWoW created these servers as *exact 1:1 copies* of Vanilla WoW, without their extra content, the game and its population would still function differently from the population that originally played Vanilla WoW from Nov '04 to Jan '07 because there is no subscription model incentivizing players to make their time investment "worth the money." One could argue that true Vanilla private servers have *never existed* if none ever required a subscription with the same pricing (or, technically, inflated pricing to match whatever economic impact $15 a month had during those years).
