# Shaman talents - revamp idea

## Post 88830 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2024-02-26T19:07:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=88830#p88830 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!
Maining a shaman in this server. Mostly liking it but theorycrafting is my thing and I wanna write an article about how I think the tree could be balanced out. I always look at things from a PVE raiding perspective. Also considering leveling and PVP to a degree but my main focus is to theorycraft shaman talents in a way that they are, in my opinion, optimal. Not OP though.

I wanna start with resto because they are in a decent spot. They have the best raid-healing spell in the game and they also provide great utility. What I'd change:
 * Healing Focus is a 5-level talent, each level adding 14% chance to avoid casting interruption when a healing spell is done. My problem is that priests have the same talent with the same name, but they only need 2 levels to reach the 70% interruption avoidance. Because of this, would change the talent into a 2-level talent, each one giving 35% chance, just like the priests have it.
 * Nature's guidance gives 1% hit chance with melee and spells per talent (has 3 talents). Shouldn't be a talent on the resto tree. Removing it.
 * Healing way: makes healing waves better every time we cast a new one on the same target. Would add lesser healing wave into the talent since shamans very rarely cast the slow healing wave in a raid.
 * Restorative totems: makes mana and life totems better up to 25% with 5 talents. Would make them 50% better, with 10% added with each talent. This is a skippable idea but I think that both manaspring and healing stream totems are lackluster at the moment (manaspring is at least playable).

We freed up 5 talent spaces for resto this way.
My idea for these 5 talent slots is a talent which gives the target 1%...5% haste for 5 sec after being healed by a shaman spell which critted. It would further motivate the shamans to wide-heal while giving them an added, unique utility.

In short: removing hit chance talent, reducing the talents needed to prevent interruption while casting, allowing lesser healing wave to proc healing way, (maybe) buffing restorative totems and giving a talent which gives haste for healing targets if the heal was a crit.

I'd continue with elemental becaue that tree is the middle one based on difficulty (enhancement tree is a mess).
Elemental is a very good PVP choice but mostly lackluster during leveling and raiding. Threat management is bad, doesn't have a warlock curse for the nature spells, mana management is bad, too. Very bursty. Will try to theorycraft in a way that the end result will not be considerably stronger at PVP since it breaks balance.

 * Call of flame, increasing the damage of fire totems. Why? We have 3 damaging fire totems, 2 of them is melee range, the third one is a pull master. If anything, it should go to enhancement tree. Removing it.
 * Earth's grasp adds 50% health to stoneclaw totem and 20% added radius to earthbind totem. Earthbind totem is at least useful sometimes but the normal one fills its role perfectly. The taunt totem buff shouldn't be in the elemental tree either. Removing it.
 * Elemental focus gives clearcasting, but the Tesco version of clearcasting. Spells still cost mana. If clearcasting made the next two damaging spells free, mp5 reload would have kicked in in the meantime, allowing the elemental shaman to be better with mana management.
 * Elemental devastation offensive spell crits make melee crits more likely. This should be an enhancement talent. Removing it.
 * Improved fire totems buffs two fire totems which are melee range. If anything, should go to enhancement tree. Removing it.
 * Storm reach makes lightning bolt and chain lightning castable from further away. Would add shocks here so that elem shamans could have something similar to a rotation while avoiding being too close to the raid boss. Unless this change would break elemental shaman in PVP.
 * Elemental fury - would nerf this one a bit. It is basically a Ruin for elemental shamans but also gives double crit damage for fire nova and magma totems, which are melee-distance totems. Searing totem (the pew-pew pulling machine) is a maybe here but the others shouldn't be buffed by an elemental shaman in my opinion.

This would mean 10 less talent points in the elemental tree. How I'd fill it up:
 * A talent giving spell accuracy by up to 3% (3 talent points)
 * A talent which reduces threat up to 25% for nature damage (3 talents, mirroring priest's shadow affinity)
 * A talent making it less likely that enemies resist your nature spells by 6% (3 talents)
 * Improved elemental totems - a 1 step talent which adds 10% added threat reduction to tranquil air totem and increases the searing totem's damage by 10%

Elemental shamans would still lack the nature curse but I think adding it to them would make them way too OP in PVP. Adding something similar to a moonkin or to an assasination rogue would be interesting though. Since mana longevity is not that important in PVP, the main changes would be as follows: elemental shaman can shock from further away (optional, noted in the list that it can be dangerous at PVP) and enemies having a slightly lower chance or resisting their burst. For PVE though, ele could become a more mana-efficient caster with much less threat issue and some added penetration.

Enhancement. This is the hard one. Started as a tank build, became a melee DPS with tanking characteristics. The problem here is that tanking talents take up a lot of talent slots so people have to decide what to do with enhancement shaman.
I'll write two versions for it. In the first one shaman is handled as paladin's mirror, meaning that both can play 3 different playstyles. Healing, melee and tank for pala, healing, caster and melee for shaman. This version will remove the tanking talents and will try to make enhancement tree a melee utility build with decent damage and with an identity but without having them topping damage meters, like ever. In the second version I will handle shamans as druids and the enhancement tree will be comparable to druid's feral tree. This means less freedom and less identity though.

Enhancement talents for the melee version.
In short, I think that the tree has potential for a good melee utility build but the talents are all over the place and itn only results in a mediocre build cursed to swing Nightfall until the end of times. I'd add some damage and utility and would introduce the concept of reciprocity to this tree which would be used for two talents. It means a talent which always get applied to the shaman and to the target at the same time.
Let's check the list!

 * Ancestral knowledge. All shaman builds take this talent since it can be easily accessed. It isn't good though. Depending on build, shaman raiders usually have around 3000-7000 mana. This talent gives them 350 mana at best. Not really comparable to the druids' mana regeneration talent. Paladins have divine intellect which gives 10% more int. Every int is 15 mana and a slightly increased spell crit chance. Let's say that at best holy palas have around 400 int without the talent, granting them 40*15=600 mana and added spell crit chance. In both cases I checked extremely high values (7000 base mana vs. 400 base int). I would increase the added mana to 10%. It would mean 700 mana in the above example but would not give any added spell crit chance.
 * Shield specialization makes block chance and amount better. It is a tanking talent, removing it.
 * Guardian totems buffs stoneskin, windwall and grounding. From this stoneskin is the only needed one, grounding is useful as it is and windwall is useless anyway.
 * Enhancing totems buffs strength and agility totems. Would put stoneskin here, too.
 * Thunderhead makes lightning shield cheaper and castable on an ally. Would change thunderhead into an ability learnable by enhancement shamans which, for almost a doubled price, puts a lightning shield onto you and onto your target.
 * Ancestral guardian adds armor and dodge chance. Not very important for the DPS version, removing it.
 * Spirit shield makes shield better. Don't. Removing it.
 * Stormstrike - I'd slightly change it, adding you the nature damage buff instead of putting the debuff onto the enemy.
 * Bloodlust would work based on reciprocity. It is always a feel-bad thing for a melee character to use the ultimate on someone else. However, enh shamans have so low DPS at the moment that a bloodlust on them almost feels like a wasted bloodlust. Since enhancement shamans should never become a top DPS, my idea is to make Bloodlust a spell which targets the caster and the target at the same time.
 * Elemental weapons would be changed, too. As a passive, enhancement shamans would receive +1%...+3% melee hit chance as long as their weapon is currently enhanced. Other than that, rockbiter weapon would only get a +10% damage bonus instead of +20% but would also lose the added threat.

This means 15 free talent slots.
 * I'd give them a 5-step talent which gives them +5 weapon skill for the two-handed weapons.
 * I'd give them a 2-step talent which gives fire nova and magma totem +10% damage each, resulting in +20% damage from these totems.
 * Would put elemental devastation in this tree (3 talent slots).
 * Would make a new 3-step talent which reduces the next shock cooldown you cast by 3 seconds after a melee critical hit (cannot be stacked).
 * Would add a 2-step talent which recharges 2%...5% of your mana after a critical melee hit.

After these changes the enh shaman would be less of a white-hit machine because of the added weapon expertise. They would usually put down a windfury totem, stormstrike, bloodlust, forcing themselves to proc as many melee attacks s possible, fishing for crits. If a crit lands, mana gets regenerated and next shock feels immediate. If a shock crits, melee crit chance increases. In the meanwhile, flurry makes sure that the shaman has a bunch of extra haste. Enhancement shaman would try to maintain this circle while also using magma and flametongue totems for the added melee-range damage.

With proper gear this build would melee crit very consistently, meaning a gameplay of 3 second CD shocks, a constant state of flurry and always having enough mana for 'just one last shock'. However, this is all this build does besides putting down totems. I think it is manageable for PVP. Enh shaman was so bad there, we can give them more without them becoming oppressive. They lack any form of stun or any good form of CC (maybe frost shock into ghost wolf counts here). They would flurry and crit and crit but 1. they wield slow 2H weapons, 2. They lack real melee combat options. Compared to a rogue or an arms warrior, there are much less things an enh shaman could do even after the changes. No stealth or rushing in. Forst shock is basically harmsting. No shouts, heroic strikes, executes etc. I think, while this build would become kinda strong, it would also be a bit one-dimensional for close-ranged battle in PVP. PVE, the build would be decent and very interesting in my opinion.

The second version is different. Here melee DPS and tank are sitting together within one talent tree, making this a bit more problematic.
In this version I'd keep the changes from Ancestral Knowledge for a bigger mana pool. Would still remove Guardian totems like last time and in this version I'd remove improved ghost wolf and make it a level 60 shaman talent available for all. In other words the task should be done via 4 talent slots and the revamp of some existing talents. As a sidenote, for this theorycraft I imagine that TWOW developers add a final level of earth shock which actually taunts.

The problem with shaman tanks are the following:
 * They wear mail.
 * They keep threat badly, no taunt.
 * Aoe threat is a problem.
 * No defensive CD.
 * They use mana. Long fights don't favor them.

First I'd buff spirit shields. Shamans should never wear plate but they need the armor from somewhere.
 * Spirit shield amounts could be doubled, leading to 60% added armor from shields, instead of 30%.
 * In this version I'd rework Thunderhead in a different way. Thunderhead, if learnt, doubles the threat of each popping lightning shield while making them cost 20% less (just like now).
 * Here I'd rework elemental weapons. They by default make weapon enhancements better. I'd add that for as long as the shaman's weapon is enhanced, he'd receive +1%...+5% hit chance based on talent. Compensation for Nature's Guidance and a bit more to avoid the white hit stereotype. Here I'd make another change, namely to Rockbiter. With this talent it currently receives a +20% damge bonus. I'd switch this into a +30-50% threat bonus. Tanks would use rockbiter, melees would use another weapon enhancement.
 * Would buff weapon mastery here to give +15% melee damage instead of 10%
 * Stormstrike, Bloodlust and Enhancing Totems would work just like in version 1.

We have 4 talent slots to add.
 * I'd give them a 2-step talent which gives fire nova and magma totem +10% damage each, resulting in +20% dmage from these totems.
 * I'd make a 1-step talent in relation to this one, which doubles the threat generated by these two totems and would link the threat to the shaman.
 * Lastly I'd add a defensive CD (like a mini shield wall) for tanks.

This version of melee enhancement shaman wouldn't have the same kind of identity the last one had. They are a better version of their current self. Same utility, except for a 'double' bloodlust, less blank hits because they got +2% melee hit chance as long as a weapon enchant is on and got a bit more decent damage because I pumped up weapon mastery.
In exchange shaman tanks would be a working archetype. Much bulkier then before (a 3000 armor shield used to be 3900 armor on them, with these changes it is 4800 armor). Added threat output with a rockbiter weapon specializing via + threat. Added aoe threat via two fire totems and added a defensive cooldown.  To be honest I like this version less because this melee shaman is still half-baked while this tank is, while decent, still mostly  meme.
