# Leave some uncharted or unpopulated landscapes for keeping immersion

## Post 169259 by Sleeplust (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2026-02-23T12:44:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=169259#p169259 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

I would be happy to see possible expansion projects, like Stratholme Harbour, implemented in our game. My concern, however, is about the reduction of immersion due to overly exploited landscapes and a world map too crowded by reachable areas. I have posted that before as replies.

I believe that players do need isolating structures or landscapes that make the world look vast enough, like water and mountainous barriers preventing players from reaching the neighbouring region. A negative example is the blunt integration of Azshara and Durotar's playing areas in Cataclysm with a cross-regionalised Orgrimmar (not to mention the unrestricted flying mount), which in fact reduces players' sense of immersion in game. The Timbermaw intersection bordering Moonglade, Winterspring and Felwood and the troll village linking Arathi and Hinterlands regions are two other bad examples.

If enlarging the whole world in gameplay is technically impossible, a compromising method for avoidance of such immersion loss could be just building those isolating regions under a name like "xxx Ridges/Mountains/Lowlands" without large towns and highways, but a small camp or single NPCs like Nat Pagle. A laudable paradigm is Deadwind Pass, which is relatively small in game, but most players do not expect to climb and cross its surrounding mountains as they accept the evilly bleak tone of the region. Overall, the purpose is to maintain the immersion, making the world map free from over-exploitaion.
