I've already said this in a couple of places: The set-based gear systems in Vanilla's endgame dungeons and early raids were developed with intentional friction to serve a revenue-centric purpose and should be reevaluated, whether that solution involves tokens or not. Subscription MMOs are like toll roads built to recover construction costs. Time gates and grind systems slow progress so players stay subscribed longer. If the subscription model is removed, leaving those systems untouched is like keeping toll booths on a highway that’s already been paid off.

Early MMO design—especially in World of Warcraft—was explicitly built around keeping players engaged for years. Systems like time gates, slow progression, and lockouts were tools to stretch content consumption so subscription revenue remained stable. If a game no longer depends on subscriptions, it makes sense to reevaluate mechanics that were originally built around that retention model. Even if TWoW created these servers as exact 1:1 copies of Vanilla WoW, without their extra content, the game and its population would still function differently from the population that originally played Vanilla WoW from Nov '04 to Jan '07 because there is no subscription model incentivizing players to make their time investment "worth the money."
