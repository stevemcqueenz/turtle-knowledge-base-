# Class Changes 1.18.1

## Post 166747 by Nuninho32 — 2026-01-28T13:54:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=166747#p166747 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

This is in my opinion what needs to be fixed in each class:

PVE

Warrior

For Warriors, Fury completely dominates PVE DPS, which leaves Arms without any meaningful raid role. Prot works well as a main tank but feels weak outside that narrow position. Arms doesn’t need to overtake Fury, but a small PvE niche through better scaling or Slam quality-of-life would already go a long way. Prot could benefit from smoother threat generation rather than raw survivability buffs.

Paladin

Paladins are another case where one spec shines while the others struggle. Holy is strong and expected, but Retribution still has trouble justifying a raid slot, and Protection lacks snap threat and mana efficiency. Ret doesn’t need massive damage buffs—more consistent output and some small PvE utility would already make it feel less like a liability. Prot mainly needs smoother threat and mana handling rather than raw power.

Hunter

Hunters in PvE are largely defined by pet scaling, which makes BM the obvious choice while Marksmanship lags behind in sustained encounters. Pets also fall off harder than they should as gear improves. Giving pets better inheritance from the hunter and nudging MM’s sustained damage would help diversify PvE builds without changing the class fantasy. Traps also feel underused in PvE and could use more relevance outside of niche pulls.

Rogue

Rogues are effective in PvE, but the gameplay and spec choice are extremely narrow. Combat dominates almost everything, while Subtlety has essentially no PvE identity. This doesn’t need to be solved with damage buffs—PvE utility, debuffs, or synergy would already justify Subtlety’s existence. Smoother energy regeneration would also reduce how dependent Rogue performance feels on world buffs.

Priest

Priests are in a decent spot overall, but Shadow still struggles with mana in longer PvE fights, which limits its usefulness. Holy and Discipline also overlap too much in practice, leaving Disc without a clear PvE identity. Shadow mostly needs sustainability, while Discipline could lean more into shielding and mitigation rather than raw throughput.

Mage

For Mages, the current PvE reality is that Arcane clearly dominates. Frost and Fire are largely irrelevant in raids, not because Arcane is broken, but because the alternatives don’t scale or contribute enough. Arcane should remain strong, but Fire and Frost need help to become reasonable PvE options. That doesn’t mean Arcane nerfs—bringing Fire scaling up and giving Frost a small sustained-damage or utility niche would already improve Mage diversity a lot.

Warlock

Warlocks are effective, but their PvE gameplay is still overly centered around Shadow Bolt spam. Affliction scaling lags behind, Demonology lacks a clear raid purpose, and pets remain fragile. Improving DoT scaling and giving Demonology more meaningful pet-based value would diversify Warlock gameplay without inflating overall damage too much.

Druid

Druids show one of the clearest gaps. Restoration is solid, Feral is usable but punishing, and Balance remains severely underperforming. Balance mainly needs better scaling and stronger raid contribution through Moonkin aura. Feral tanking works, but threat and energy management can feel unnecessarily harsh compared to other tanks.

Shaman

Shamans are valuable in PvE, especially Restoration, but Enhancement relies heavily on RNG and Elemental struggles with mana in raids. Totem micromanagement also adds friction without much decision-making. Normalizing Windfury for consistency, improving Elemental mana economy, and reducing totem GCD pressure would significantly improve PvE feel without pushing Shamans out of their intended roles.
