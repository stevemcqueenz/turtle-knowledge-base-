# Fishing: A new and original study-friendly approach

## Post 111771 by Tzimy — 2024-12-08T11:12:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=111771#p111771 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hello all,
my name is Tzimy and I am an altoholic!   smiling_turtle_head
Since my university studying years, I had this bizzare and you might find interesting idea to have a "study" mode for fishing in WoW. While I was studying during exams, I loved fishing in various areas, listening to the environment (and sometimes the music). That calmed me down greatly and helped me focus. Because fishing is 10 secs max cast, I found a way (which is not legitimate according to the wow policy) to click automatically the fishing bobber when it moved, so that I did not have to interrupt my studying every 10 secs or so.

Having that dear memory and positive-in-real-life wow experience in mind, I am wondering if it would it be possible to create a new "study-mode" with long-timed casts (i.e. minimum 3, 5 or 10 mins depending on zone or tool used) which would give increased rewards according to the time it took to catch something? Like, for every minute passed, add 5 ordinary fish, or receive a rare reward, more valuable as the cast time increased.

Users will not know what the total duration of the fishing cast will be, so the longer it lasts, the better knowingly the reward will be. The anticipation will rise and excitement for something rare will increase. All this without interrupting your study, until the prolonged cast ends. Still, there is a necessary final user interaction: it will all depend on the reaction of the user to catch the fish in the end when the bobber moves (perhaps give a limited repeated bobber movements/sounds for the longer cast, if possible of course).

I believe that this will make fishing a much more challenging and immersive experience than it is now in the game, and it might actually help a lot of people in their studies!

Immagine after graduating, some of us can say, "Turtle-WoW actually helped me pass these lessons!".

Yours sincerely,
Tzimy
