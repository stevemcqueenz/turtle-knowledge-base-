Sap isn't a minute vs players. It is hard capped at 20 seconds, and can heartbeat earlier. It suffers from DR, so second sap is 10 seconds, third is 5, and immune after that, so at best it's 35 seconds for a full DR chain, which also means the rogue needs to stay stealthed on you and isn't doing anything else to contribute to the BG.

Also worth noting that sap and gouge share a DR, so it removes gouge from their toolkit if they full DR you, and even if they don't it'll make gouge last significantly less time.

For the movement speed, I would take the trade of removing it in exchange for having my 80% poison slow back. Right now crippling poison has the same slow % as every other slow on the market, so if someone is at range and you're both slowed, rogues cant catch up at all. Warriors have abilities to close the gap, paladins have freedom and dispel, and every other class has a myriad of ranged options, so your choice is 7% extra movement, 80% slow, or give us a gap closer like shadowstep. I'd wager, if you had to pick between those three, most people are gonna say "yeah, alright, keep the 7% movement speed".

As for deadly throw, firstly, it doesn't silence, it interrupts, important distinction. If you're just spamming instant casts, it literally does nothing to you as a caster when untalented. Secondly, the projectile speed of the thrown weapon is the same as normal, unlike TBC deadly throw which was really fast, so its super easy to juke, and very difficult to use to actually interrupt something important, as the spell is likely to be finished casting in the time you see it going off -> use the throw -> server registers you clicked the button -> throw animation winds up -> projectile travel time, and even if that does all line up, it has a shorter lockout than kick, at only 3 seconds, only marginally better than earth shock's 2 seconds for shaman, but a lot harder to land. As for the slow, that requires actually talenting into it, and having crippling poison in the offhand, and it is just a poison, so paladins, shamans and druids can all just immediately remove it, or anyone can just use antivenom to get rid of it.

All that said, I'm not trying to make it out like rogues have it that bad, but honestly, if you compare a vanilla pvp rogue to a turtle pvp rogue... We have a worse crippling poison, we have universal trinkets, so our CC is a lot more susceptible to being broken out of, and our sap is hard capped at 20 seconds, and in exchange, we have a clunky ranged interrupt that can apply our offhand poison, our blind is no longer a poison, and we move 7% faster.

Honestly, given all of that, I would say rogues are currently relatively weaker than they were in pure vanilla. I'd take 80% poison slow over 7% movement speed and deadly throw, and I'd take non-universal trinkets over physical blind.

Appreciate the clarifications on numbers. My point is about how it actually plays in BGs like WSG. Sapping someone off a push to force a split is already strong. As a healer, having a low geared rogue be on your ass the moment you ress with relentless saps esentially means that my team is not getting healed. Ofc, there are playarounds to this, but this is an extremely effective strategy in PUGs. Furthermore, you can also open on a different target while that first player is effectively removed from the fight. Add blind into vanish into re-sap and you create a very reliable stall that has almost no counterplay right now since blind is not a poison. Whether the hard cap is 20 seconds or not, in practice the chain buys more than enough time to break momentum and it feels oppressive.

On Deadly Throw, I get the distinction you are making, but Classic did not give rogues any ranged way to stop casts. Even if it can be juked and the lockout is shorter, it still adds a meaningful mind-game on top of a very high burst kit. Healers have to respect the threat of a ranged stop while also playing around kick, kidney, cheap shot, gouge and blind. That extra lever is not something rogues needed to stay viable.

About movement speed. Only rogues and druids get to choose their fights from stealth. Seven percent might look small on paper, but in practice it makes a big difference for picking targets, chasing, or safely disengaging after a kill attempt. On top of that, rogues already have an entire toolkit of control with cheap shot, kidney, gouge, sap, blind and more. Most classes do not have nearly that much CC or the same counters to slows, and most rogue targets are not mages with blink. If other classes are expected to rely on snares to survive, then their snares should naturally be stronger than a stealth class’s built-in chase tools.

I am not asking to delete the class. Even with changes, rogues remain top tier at picks, control and finishing. The issue is stacking utility on a class that already performs well without equivalent love for specs that struggle with mobility, like priests. If the devs are unwilling to make Rogues more on par with other classes, then the other classes need to get up to Rogue level strengt. Priests in particular have the worst mobility. Give them a simple, classic friendly tool like a short snare break on Fade or a minor movement burst after PW:S so they are not free food on rotation.
