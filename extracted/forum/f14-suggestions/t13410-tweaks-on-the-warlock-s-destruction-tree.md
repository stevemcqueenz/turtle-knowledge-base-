# Tweaks on the warlock's destruction tree

## Post 90319 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2024-03-16T11:49:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=90319#p90319 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!

I wanna propose some adjustments towards the warlock's destruction tree.
The main reason is to enable warlocks dealing a sustained amount of raid damage from a non-shadow source.
I think this is important because most raids have a school of magic as a main theme and many bosses and mobs are highly resistant to that school of magic.
MC and BWL hurts mages but they can just switch to another DPS build.
AQ hurts ele shaman and moonkin but they also have the option to play as a different build. It is not always easy, sometimes a caster has to give up on being a caster and heal or melee instead but at the end of the day, no class is fully excluded.
Currently TWOW can't really make a raid with a main shadow theme because warlocks, whatever the spec is, spend 90% of their time dealing shadow damage. There are also the spriests but they can always respec to be a healer (or embrace the memes and start smiting). If a raid ever happens where a main theme is shadow magic, warlocks are kinda locked out.

Note: The changes I propose have PVP effectiveness in mind. Warlocks are already beasts at PVP, specially conflag ones. I wanna tweak the destruction tree in a way that their talent enables a fire-based gameplay via a full destro build for PVE while not making them stronger for PVP.

Let's jump into it!

Warlocks in PVE have two builds (which touch the destruction tree) - to be honest, they have two builds for PVE altogether.
Both SM/Ruin and DS/Ruin goes into the same depth at the destruction tree, grabbing ruin and some other good talents and leaving everything else behind. There is no variety, warlocks have to be really rigid in spending their talent points.

The more frequent one, DS/Ruin looks something like this. SM/Ruin obviously has different talents at affliction and demonology but destruction looks the same.

![Image](https://i.postimg.cc/QNRfYKXn/1.png)

My goal is to tweak some talents here, enabling the fire-based gameplay. After the changes, a fire-based PVE build would look something like this:

![Image](https://i.postimg.cc/13bLZR1v/2.png)

 **The changes I propose ->**
Improved shadow bolt:
Currently: Shadow bolt crit increases shadow damage on the target by 20% for the 4 next non-dot shadow hit.
New: Shadow bolt crit increases non-dot destruction spell damage on the target... (same from here). This seems to be a nerf to SM/Ruin and DS/Ruin but it isn't really. This talent is mostly used to stack up shadowbolt damage and since shadowbolt is a destruction spell, these builds could use this talent the same way. Only difference is that they'd exclude some shadow priests from the party.

Improved searing pain:
Currently: Raises the crit chance by 10%
New: Raises the crit chance by 10% and reducing threat (at full talent, searing pain should have no added threat modifier).

Conflagrate:
Currently: If you have immolate on you, it can be 'exploded' for some damage.
New: The last tick of your immolate spells automatically end in a conflagration. You can't time it anymore which is a PVP nerf, but instead of a sometimes clunky talent, we have turned immolate into a very potent DOT for this build (together with improved immolate, emberstorm and the others).

 **The changes I'd consider ->**
Shadowburn:
Currently: Basically a shadow damage shock which costs a soul and puts a debuff on the target. If target dies while debuff is still on him, you get the soul back.
New: Hellburn. Same thing but would benefit from emberstorm since it's a fire spell now.

Removing the soul shard requirement from soul fire. It is not a good enough spell to have that extra requirement. Would even consider buffing it, giving you a soul shard after using this ability. After all, 1. the talent's name is soul fire 2. warlock raid rotation is extremely minimalistic. Motivating the player to sometimes press an additional button leads to better gameplay experience and adds some complexity (when to use it, when not to).

 **How the current PVE-builds would be affected?**
Barely. They are focused on shadow-bolt spamming so they need the DS or SM for the added shadow damage. It is highly unlikely they would go deeper into the destruction tree. The only difference is that improved shadowbolt debuff would only proc on shadowbolts realistically.

 **How these changes would affect PVP destruction lock?**
Conflagrate would be considerably weaker for them - in PVP you usually don't want to wait until immolate gets expired on the target. In exchange improved shadowbolt procs would be beneficial for all destruction non-dot spells, including searing pain and shadowburn/hellburn and if shadowburn change is considered, it would receive a 10% damage buff because of emberstorm.

 **What this build would lose in PVE?**
Compared to SM/Ruin:
 * Affliction spell radius.
 * Higher chance that your affliction spells get resisted.
 * Weaker curse of agony ( it counts since mob debuff limit has increased).
 * Lack of SM making shadow spells considerably weaker.
 * Slightly smaller life tap because life tap deals shadow damage to you.

 **Compared to DS/Ruin:**
 * Slightly squishier - no demonic aegis, not necessarily fully into demonic embrace.
 * No master summoner for real quick summonings (f.ex. a lightning fast voidie sacrifice for survival).
 * DS and every possible bonus it has, inclusing smaller life taps if you sacced a succubus.

 **What would this build give you compared to the others?**
 * An active, phaseshifting imp for party stamina buff just like SM/Ruin since you dont have DS.
 * Emberstorm being the 'SM of fire spells'.
 * A maintained fire-damage output via searing pain.
 * Shadow bolt turning into a utility spell if your spell crit is high enough for debuff procs.
 * Big dot which doesn't require you to lose a gcd just to activate conflag. Every immolate is a menace at PVE.

 **How this build would play like?**
 * You summon your imp and give cookies to everyone who hates tea.
 * For a small amount of mobs, you put up your assigned curse and immolate (maybe corruption).
 * You start spamming searing pain - if your crit chance is high enough, you consider casting some shadow bolts if shadow bolt debuff is not up.
 * Shadowburn/flameburn if there is no time for a last searing pain.
 * For big packs: rain of fire by default, hellfire if you have balls made of steel.

 **Disclaimer1:** I think that making searing pain spammable if you go deep into destruction tree is okay. It is a spell with a short casting time but also because of this, it only receives a fraction of the +spell damage you have. You lose 5% fire damage if you compare emberstorm to DS imp sacrifice but your high base crit chance gets even further increased for searing pain by 10% for added damage.
 **Disclaimer2:** This build would not be optimal to tank Twins because of the lack of extra threat from searing pain.
 **Disclaimer3:** I have considered changing emberstorm (from +10% fire spell damage to +10% destruction spell damage) but it would break the meta. Shadowbolt is a destruction spell and DS/Ruin would have the option to give up on bigger lifetap and the usage of corruption for a +25% shadow bolt damage buff by DS succubus + emberstorm, if they do this:

![Image](https://i.postimg.cc/5NVvS6jJ/3.png)

 **Disclaimer4:** The changed improved shadowbolt would also add complexity to the warlock gameplay. Imagine a raid having SM/Ruin, DS/Ruin and this kind of conflag warlocks. Since the first 2 builds usually spam shadowbolt, they would be on the duty to apply the debuff. Debuff gets applied. Searing pain has much less damage than shadowbolt, so conflag warlock, whose shadowbolt is by default weaker, starts to avoid the debuffed target if it is possible. Also, if we assume that shadowburn gets changed to hellburn, conflag locks have to keep an eye on mobs with low health and improved shadowbolt debuff on since their 'shock' is bigger since hellburn is a fire spell. Only in case of a bunch of minmaxers but still, added complexity to warlock raid gameplay.

What do you guys think?
