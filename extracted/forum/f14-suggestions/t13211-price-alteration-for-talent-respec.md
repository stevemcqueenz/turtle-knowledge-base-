# Price Alteration for Talent Respec

## Post 88572 by Natuaduck (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2024-02-23T15:10:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=88572#p88572 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

**Let me preface before people respond with "skill issue" and "it's not that much":  I personally do not have money issues because I've been playing WoW servers for ages now and I have a 60 as is.**

 *HOWEVER* On behalf of the countless newbies to Turtle the price of talent changing is quite high. A jump from 1g to 5g is already a lot as it is considering the value of gold in vanilla vs later expansions.

- Even a change as simple as 50s to 2.5g would be better.

Cheaper respecing allows personal experience with testing out build instead of "just googling it."

Cheaper repeccing wouldn't effect the people who have more than enough gold that 5g means nothing to them.

Cheaper respeccing would only benefit the people that want to utilise it most with experimentation OR switching specs from pvp/pve depending on situation.

Cheaper respeccing doesn't take away or effect the economy in any way as there's already huge gold sinks in the form of consumables for raids and pvp content.

Cheaper respeccing doesn't take away any challenge. The game, leveling, and time invested is what the real challenge is in classic. Having to trek to a main city is already a time investment itself.

Cheaper respeccing is quite a minor and almost unnoticable change that, if even done, a majority of the playerbase wouldn't even notice that it happened in the first place.
Every argument against this has been unhinged and met with some pretty cross negativity wherein people project their own abilities on new players for not being "as good as they are".

Gold sinks only work if the money is taken out of the economy, like respec cost, epic riding and repairs. Buying raid consumables in the auction house is not a gold sink. I agree with the points made by Hctwowfan and Inovatu.

Also note that specs are not as important on low levels as they might be in retail. There's nothing stopping arms/fury warriors from tanking or enhancers and ferals from healing etc, except for player squeamishness. Part of the vanilla experience for new players is figuring out how to make stuff work and that includes using the full toolkit of a class regardless of allocated talents.

This whole spec = class role in a dungeon thing came up after WoW was canceled in November 2008, afaik.

However, something like a reasonably tuned "undo" button might be nice for people who realize they've made a mistake immediately after or even during talent point allocation.
