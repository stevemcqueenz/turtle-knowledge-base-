# Addressing rare loot frustration in WoW Vanilla raids: Memories token system proposal (round 2)

## Post 167969 by Manolothegreat (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2026-02-11T15:40:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=167969#p167969 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

Hello, fellow adventurers!

I’m revisiting this suggestion nearly two years later because the underlying issue still persists: extreme RNG on rare raid loot can significantly undermine long-term motivation and raid stability.

I’d like to propose a system that preserves the spirit of WoW Vanilla while introducing a more predictable progression path for the rarest items.

 **The Concept: Boss Tokens**
The idea is to introduce a raid-specific token system tied to final bosses in end-game raids. Here's how it would work:
- When a raid defeats its final boss, it drops a *Memory of [Boss Name]* (e.g., *Memory of Ragnaros*).
- The token functions as normal loot and can be rolled or assigned via Master Loot.
- Tokens can be exchanged at a lore-appropriate NPC (e.g., Chromie) for **legendary components only**, not finished items or regular gear.
- All crafting materials, quest chains, and requirements remain unchanged.
The purpose is simple: introduce long-term bad-luck protection without trivializing legendaries.

 **Why this is needed?**
Legendary prestige should reflect commitment and guild value, not pure luck.

Take *Atiesh* as an example. It is typically granted to one of the most valuable and trusted members of a guild. When you see someone wielding Atiesh, you immediately recognize that player as a core contributor who earned the guild’s investment.

In contrast, you often see players in blue gear holding Thunderfury because they hosted a “bindings hard-reserved” pug and got lucky. The same pattern appears weekly in global chat with countless Kara10 Scythe HR runs. In these cases, RNG determines ownership, and not contribution. That undermines the symbolic weight of legendary items.

 **How It Would Work**
This system would not replace existing loot drops but would act as a supplement to alleviate frustration from bad RNG. Players could still get their desired loot the traditional way, but tokens provide a safety net for those who are persistently unlucky. Example structure:
- Thunderfury Bindings: Each binding could cost 20 *Memories of Ragnaros*. That would translate to roughly 8 months of consistent raiding for both bindings, which feels fair considering the weapon’s prestige.
- Eye of Sulfuras: A cost of 20 *Memories of Ragnaros* seems reasonable
- Scythe of Elune quest item: This item is currently gated behind a 2% drop chance from a 10-man dungeon with a 5-day reset. Setting the cost at 20 *Memories of Mephistroth* would provide a fair long-term path. Clearing Karazhan 40 twenty times remains a meaningful challenge for any guild.
- Atiesh Splinters: The current system works well. No changes needed here.
Costs should be intentionally high, representing many months of consistent clears, ensuring that dedication, not shortcuts, leads to completion.

With this system, you can be confident that the MVP every guild has, the consistent, reliable player who shows up every week, will eventually obtain the legendary they are working toward, even if they suffer from prolonged bad luck. Effort and contribution would ultimately prevail over randomness.

 **What This Achieves**
- Preserves legendary prestige.
- Rewards long-term guild commitment.
- Prevents multi-year droughts caused purely by RNG.
- Aligns legendary ownership more closely with contribution rather than luck.
This system does not speed up acquisition. It simply replaces infinite variance with structured, time-based progression.

Legendaries should represent value, trust, and sustained effort, not lottery wins.

What do you think?
