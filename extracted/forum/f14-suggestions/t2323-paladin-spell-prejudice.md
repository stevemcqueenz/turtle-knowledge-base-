# Paladin spell: "Prejudice"

## Post 14606 by Awen — 2021-12-16T19:49:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=14606#p14606 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Imagine a spell called "Prejudice". It would be a cooldown based self buff that would temporarily enable the usage of your spells against any opponent like they were a demon or an undead. It would utalize already existing spells like "Exorcism" or "Turn Evil" and it would go well with the class fantasy. In lore holy magic is heavily dependent on one's will, so it seems logical to me that the restrictions on these spells are coming from one's perspection. Being a religious zealot can change that perspection.

PS: If the name "Prejudice" is problematic, it could be named "Dies Irae"

## Post 14753 by Merikkinon (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2021-12-23T15:31:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=14753#p14753 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

The false premise about paladins is that the way they think and view others is truly 'righteous'. Whereas its rather well estabilished that prejudice is a real trap for many of those who feel they worship 'in truth', and especially those that think they are the arbiters of such truth.

If they have to be truly righteous and just - and we're saying they ARE - then we have a problem because priests are downright filthy and not bastions of 'light' quite often.

I don't think this is something that should be done any time in the near future.

But I do think it would increase the depth of the game if the classes were a lot more conceptually representative of traditional lore (not just WoW lore) than they currently are. Truthfully, it's just my opinion that the orig developers didn't do a bang-up job on conceptual class development at all. Lots of damage numbers, lot of spells with appropriate names, but not so many with conceptual angles to them (and caveats for using). However, Exorcism and Turn Undead are examples of good conceptualization.

Btw, this would be better served as a talent than an ability (spell) itself. It would indicate a 'buy-in' by the Paladin into that form of thinking, or behavioral tendency. It would just affect certain spell usage.

It is my understanding that is the what the OP was encouraging. It seeks to give the Paladin the 'chance' to be prejudiced, to let zeal override his judgment and persecute, yet at the cost of danger to himself. The technicalities of it are one thing, the concept however, is strong. The game has zeal being 'good'.  ALL good. I am pretty sure history is replete with the horror and pain of zeal.

We have 'balancing' going on in 1.16. Obviously it is more about 'power balance' than conceptual development. Perhaps the former is needed (that, to me, has always been the complaint about balance tree - "Not Viable For Raiding"), but does that have to exclude conceptual development of classes? This may or may not even be a value to the development team. I don't know. And if not, so be it.

But it's an argument or conversation worth having, imo.
