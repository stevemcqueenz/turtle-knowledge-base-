# Random traits of mobs

## Post 43642 by Xudo (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2023-04-28T15:56:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=43642#p43642 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Discussion about altering properties of mobs in world lead me to idea (which is pretty common in different games, like PoE, Diablo, Battle for Wesnoth).

Mobs could get random trait, which will alter its properties.
For example Razormane Battleguard <https://database.turtlecraft.gg/?npc=3114> could get:
+10% damage and be named "Strong Razormane Battleguard"
+10% hp and be named "Sturdy Razormane Battleguard"
+100% money loot and be named "Rich Razormane Battleguard"
+(level * 2) to all resistances and be named "Resilent Razormane Battleguard"
+100% armor and be named "Armored Razormane Battleguard"
+50% damage at night and -50% damage at day (simultaneously) and be named "Nocturnal Razormane Battleguard"
he can be immune to slow and roots and be named "Unstoppable Razormane Battleguard" with elite status
list can be quite long.

This way mobs will be harder and players will know how exactly he will be harder before engaging.
Mobs also will be very varied while being the same.

Changing the Names would actually require making whole new mobs on the spawn table. That is a lot of labor. However, there are two ways to do this with varying level's of ease.

One makes less database work but more scripting work and the other, the opposite.

Method one is to make ~8-10 abilities that are self buffs and one ability that "Casts a random spell from a list". Each mob would manually have the "Random Cast" spell added to their ability list. That spell should be instant cast, with a 1 second delay (the parameter that makes mobs wait an amount of time before using an ability). Give the ability a 60 min CD. Make each of the random buffs non-typed, visible on the mob buffbar, and last 60 minutes.

Method two requires making an invisible mob that creates an aura, or buffs mobs randomly in an area, with similar effects to the method above. It would require more ingenuity and in-environment testing, but less database entry labor than the other method.
