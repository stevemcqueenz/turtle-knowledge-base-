# How to Extend Progression Without Invalidating Old Content

## Post 29076 by Nahath — 2022-11-13T05:21:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=29076#p29076 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Almost every RPG creates the same mentality in players: "If it's too hard, come back and do it when you're more powerful." and in the case of MMOs, you can add, "or have someone else carry you through it."

I'm not entirely opposed to this, but by creating a few situations where it doesn't apply, you can add enormous gameplay and story value. Rather than talking too much theory, I will explain through example.

 **The Basic Idea:**
Suppose Deadmines got a couple changes. The first is that VanCleef is working with a business partner who flees when the players enter the dungeon...unless every player is level 18 or below. Then they are so overconfident in their safety that the business partner sticks around (complete with some very nice loot).

Besides that, VanCleef loves dueling. If a player who is level 24 or below is able to solo the dungeon, he brings out his special dueling blade, which will get added to the loot when you kill him (note: I have no idea if 24 is the right number here. Adjust to whatever makes it super-hard but not impossible).

 **Making it Better:**
You probably look at that and think "Kind of cool, but it really wouldn't change much." So here's how you give it serious impact. One of the drops in the above steps is a bind-on-account crafting component (I know BoA isn't in Turtle Wow, yet, but I suspect it could be added one way or another). This component is actually used to build awesome level 60 gear. Implement that type of thing throughout leveling dungeons, and they become major hotspots of dedicated play, rather than being just another way to level. The excitement for starting new alts would be huge.

For level 60 content that gets outdated as people gear above it, you can still do something similar: ex: special rewards for 2-manning Stratholme or for killing Ragnaros within a limited time frame.

 **Making it Even More Awesome**
A good way to make people excited about these items is to focus them on horizontal progression--the items that drop or get crafted aren't necessarily stronger than Naxx or AQ40 gear, but they allow players to do things that are otherwise impossible or at least unrealistic. You could make Shaman gear that's so good for tanking that it's finally reasonable for a shaman to tank a raid. Or a skill book that permanently increases mount and/or swim speed. Lots of opportunities here beyond cosmetic.

 **A Controversial Opportunity**
This kind of change could even put an increased level cap on the table if Turtle WoW ever wants to move to any of the expansions.  It would give people reasons to freeze XP at 60 and still run the vanilla raid progression.

Let me know what you think.

If the only thing you want out of leveling is that one item at 55, yeah, that feels pretty bad. But that's the rare case. Most people are interested in having more characters but leveling alts is widely viewed a chore with no payoff until you hit 60. If leveling an alt instead contains a series of interesting challenges (because most players would be looking into other things to get in your leveling process--not just that one reward), then leveling that alt becomes an interesting and varied experience. I would expect that the number of people who would see an exciting opportunity for an alt would be far higher than the number who would see a grind more boring than the level 60 grinding that they're already doing. Clearly we have a difference of opinion on that.
