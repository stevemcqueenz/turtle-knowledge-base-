# New ideas for Twow proffessions

## Post 99054 by Qorvulth (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2024-07-09T23:09:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=99054#p99054 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

So i got here by thinking wouldn't it be neat if we could some how make coals... for steel , after all having a cost just to buy it is quite the sink for us steel producers.

My idea , as you might know ... are usually revolving how do we incorporate idea that doesn't gear toward self sustain but rather specialized what one want to do.

So two new professions comes to my head... Lumberjack and carpentry.

Lumberjack just chop down trees and shape them into what ever shape they need to be in.   Taking some inspiration of engineering here ... you could make it do the wooden frames , wooden plank and so on.

Carpentry is responsible for further refinement of stuff , such as furnishing guild base ( aka im thinking after 2.0 comes ) and making base weapon hilt and maybe adding in socket like holder gems for the upcoming jewelcrafting to have.  bow crafting speciality and wand maker speciality can come from this.  I would argue that carpentry to also have infusion of making trinkets too.

For the mining side , They gain access to smelt wood into coals.   Now here's where my idea goes wild ... what if lumber could be smelt into different grade of coal and those coal are use to smelt different bars , so like copper may need only a grade F coals but a steel require Grade A coal to smelt it.

I find this idea to not make it harder but make it a bit more job streamlined and more effective for the market to be more independent for players to tackle.   At the moment we are quite stunted by the coal cost even if we use exalted coal price , its still a baseline price that we have to adjust for rather than being more open and freedom from vendors

I don't know the exact amount of different items a blacksmith, tailor or leatherworker can make. Checking the database for "plans" gives 200+ items, checking for "pattern" gives 350+ items. The plans and patterns that are taught by the profession trainers aren't even included in that number.

I'm having a hard time coming up with more than 10 items a carpenter could craft in turtle wow, so I guess it'd be impossible to come up with 200+. I don't think carpentry would work as a primary profession. Maybe as a secondary profession. But you wouldn't want to add a gathering prof for a secondary prof. Secondary profs are a hit and miss kinda. Everyone can pick them without the downside of blocking you from picking something else. That leads further down the path of everyone being able to do everything.

Also don't understand why you'd want to add grade f coal to smelt copper. You don't need coal to smelt copper. Neither in wow nor in rl. You exclusively need it to create steel from iron. So for a single item craft. There's no need to be able to craft it yourself. By that logic, why can't a tailor make their own thread? Why can't a miner/blacksmith create their own fluxus? It's ok if you have to buy it. A herbalist can't create spices either. Not everything needs to be craftable.

Lumber cutting would have to be a secondary profession in this case as a gathering system and carpentry or woodworking a primary profession.

Some ideas for items woodworking could make:
Weapon hilts: could be used in blacksmithing to craft weapons,
Woodworking could also produce bows, wands, staves, spears (polearms)
Maybe with some engineering pieces a sort of trojan horse pet that would act as a single target walking wooden horse and deliver an aoe explosion after reaching the target.
It could also be used to craft arrows logically,
craft traps
craft a glider that would be an on use item that practically grants you slow fall effects for a limited time.
craft a lot of cosmetic items
maybe a boat that would let you traverse continents if you use it near the ocean (shore to shore at will) (don't know how this would realistically work, but it would make crossing continents a lot less place dependant).

Tents would make more sense in woodworking then and survival could be scrapped, torches would also fit into this profession, it just seems like an expansion on an already existing profession conceptually.
