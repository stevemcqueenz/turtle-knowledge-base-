Hear me out on this one. This would make some sense in lore and gameplay for some of these races to allow Paladins.

Orcs make sense lore wise because since Orcish Invasion of Azeroth from the Dark Portal, Orcs have been exposed to Human cultures and their world entirely in fact. You would think that after years upon years of being in Azeroth that there is bound to be Orcs who became influenced by Human Paladins, Priests, and Clerics and converted to the Light and attempted to share this with their brethern much like how Arcane magics were introduced to them. As Tirion Fordring mentioned once: "I have seen Orcs just as honorable as knights and men just as vile as the Scourge."

Trolls make sense due to what we seen in future expansions of WoW with the Zandalari Trolls. Why can't this part of the Lore, at least this aspect, be implemented? Some could argue that if Trolls be Paladins then why not Taurens. Tauren never made too much sense to me to be honest and honestly Trolls didn't either until I played some of the newer expansions regarding the Zandalari Trolls and their Paladins. And if that doesn't work for Trolls then what I said about the Orcs being exposed to Human society and influence...could be applied to Trolls as well to an extent because Trolls, mainly Amani Trolls, have a seething hatred for Elves and Humans alike to the point of never associating themselves with whatever power or magic they have regardless how strong it is. But that's the Amani. Not the Darkspear or the Zandalari.

Now Night Elves do make absolute sense in all technicalities because there has been at least one Night Elf Paladin in Warcraft. If Night Elves can have Priests and Priestess of Elune, then why not Paladins who follow Elune as well and gain their power of the Light from her in the same manner? And if that doesn't work, then perhaps influence from the Humans and Dwarves rubs off on the Night Elves in their bid to fit into a world that is foreign and new to them since their reawakening. Not to mention that Night Elves as a playable race has one of the smallest selections of classes among all the races as well. And Paladin and Mage classes would work well as wonderful additions for the Night Elf race both lore and gameplay wise.

But these races are my suggestions for the Paladin class to be considered in this game. Paladin class is my favorite class and its my mainstay class of all of WoW.

Is this a joke thread? Lorewise (at least, Warcraft RTS and Vanilla), Paladins were literally developed in the Alliance for the purpose of specifically fighting against the Orcs and Horde. Paladins aren't really a "class" that any random person could be. You have to specifically be a member of the Church of the Holy Light, which is exclusively an Alliance church (Note that just believing in the Holy Light doesn't make you a member of the Church). Just believing in the Holy Light is not enough to be a Paladin. It's a highly specialized order within the Alliance that upholds both the Church of the Holy Light's and the Alliance's values. An Orc warrior who just believes in the Holy Light isn't going to become a Paladin, in fact he probably wouldn't even gain abilities that would make him comparable to a Paladin.
It would be like if some Korean farmer trained with guns and promised to uphold the law and insisted he was a New York City cop without ever setting foot in NYC and without ever joining the NYPD.

Night Elf paladins **COULD**  make sense as a weird exception, but realistically the amount of Night Elf paladins who could exist would be so historically low that it would cheapen that point by allowing players to be Night Elf Paladins. Hell, even Gnome paladins, as silly as it sounds, are more plausible than Night Elf Paladins would be. Keep in mind the reason Dwarves can be Paladins in Vanilla and High Elves can be Paladins in Turtle is because they have been allies with humans for ages. Night Elves and Humans have only known of eachother for five whole years by the time of Vanilla, they've lived on separate continents for 10,000 years with minimal to zero interactions. Night Elves are only in the Alliance on a technicality, compared to the other Alliance races the Night Elves operate mostly independently of the Alliance.
However a Night Elf """Paladin""" who pulls their faith from Elune technically would not really be a Paladin. You could kind of make the class mechanics work but it would not be a Paladin.  Paladins in the WC2/WC3/Vanilla timeline are specifically a Human order (with dwarves and high elves thrown in and you could make a case for the odd gnome or odd night elf).

The Light =/= The Church of the Holy Light
The Light is a force/magic/belief while the Church of the Holy Light is a Human organized religion revolving around that force/belief.

Anybody who believes in the Holy Light CAN become a priest of the Holy Light even if they don't join the church or its numerous orders. Paladins on the other hand are a specialized force within the Church of the Holy Light. There ARE "evil" or "villainous" Paladins, such as Arthas or the Scarlet Crusade, but again, Arthas and the Scarlet Crusaders were former members of the Church of the Holy Light, not random Joes, and, because what they believed they were doing was right, they retained their Paladin abilities. However, simple belief in the Light and belief in upholding righteousness will not magically transform you into a Paladin. It's what keeps a Paladin bound to doing what they believe is the right thing. An Orc Paladin would have to somehow be accepted into the Church of the Holy Light, an Alliance organization that, time and time again, has proven itself to consider the Horde it's enemy. Do you understand what that means? An Orc Paladin would literally have to be accepted into the ALLIANCE to even be considered for training. The odds of that ever being happening are so astronomically low and I doubt the Orcs in Orgrimmar would be happy about accepting back an Orc who trained to become a special class of holy warrior designed as a countermeasure to the Horde.
