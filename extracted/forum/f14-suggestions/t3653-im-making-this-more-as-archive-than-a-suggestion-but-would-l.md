## Post 22136 by Smersh — 2022-08-12T16:00:00Z

Hello,
I  want to suggest a thing, although suggesting things for classes is a bit late now that class changes are done. Suppose I decided to make this post more for myself than to be seriously considered, because that's just how it goes. Written down thoughts are better, even if forgotten somewhere in dark corners of the forum.
My grasp on intentions of each spec:
 **This part is needed for me more than for you, in it, you'll see my idea of what specs are and what they should stem to be in the future, since spoiler alert blizzard have not upheld its initial ideas into future, so feel free to just skip to the suggestion itself.**

In my opinion, it's pretty obvious how specializations became a thing and how exactly the talents between them were spread.

First, they split up the class into three of its main aspects. If classes include more general hybrid things, splits were easier, as you could just put three roles in different trees like with druids, with more obscure classes you had to split them based on other arbitrary gameplay mechanics, for example mage got split into three spell schools mage can learn spells from, while warlock got into summoning, damage over time and cast spell split, which don't really feel like equals like in mage case.

That created a problem in which some trees had to have additional things sprinkled in from a bin of ideas. That returns us to rogues, the split into three parts for rogues was a bit difficult because rogue isn't that wide of a concept, and so they used rogue core game mechanics as the difference, but quickly realized that they had to sprinkle more and more here and there.

 **Assassination** is based on the idea of finishing moves, attacks that are supposed to be the last ones used in combo to finish off the enemy, hence the name, the tree was supposed to have everything related to finishing moves and speeding up the process of obtaining combo points, with sprinkled in later ideas being the crit focus like in seal of fate and cold blood talent that probably was born when they tried to think what would be a fair trigger to additional combo points and poisons. Poisons are a bit of a special case because they were always meant to be part of the rogue toolkit, but only assassination really fits the idea of poisoning an enemy, thus it's a perfect match.

 **Combat**  is very blatant, it is based on moves that are used to get those combo points, moves like sinister strike, backstab are the tree's bread and butter, a problem they encountered with this tree was the fact of just how much weapon mastery things they wanted to give to a rogue, whole dodge and parry swashbuckler style to really create the agile warrior kind of feel, so the additional gimmicks of the tree turned out to be same as for warriors arms tree, but themed around agility and swiftness rather than slow and steady 2 handed weapon counterparts. In the end that tree turned out to be too strong, bloated with powerful combat based abilities that you couldn't really include into either of the other two, since the concept of a subtle fighter nor of an assassin trying to spam finishers fit with things like, energy rushes through your had due to high burst of adrenalin in your body. I suspect that most of the time in rogue development was trying to make two other trees comparative to this one in usefulness potential and as we can see they did not succeed.

 **Subtlety** is a third and last tree, many people tend to think that it's a support tree and utility tree, but there haven't been any intentions like that made by the developers, as they stated, they wanted one spec to feel like a meaningful choice not a place for free cc for pvp which at that point wasn't even realized well. This tree is based on abilities you use in and right before you come out of stealth, so-called openers. You can see both in 0.9 version of the talent tree and this one the huge focus on things like ambush, garrote, sap and alike. In 0.9 the themes thrown in this case were a duo of ghostly strike and set up. With other talents that seemed a bit out of place being preparation and deadliness, but deadliness was just a blatant attempt at making the tree be able to hold on its own at least a little bit better. And preparation kind of also fits, as it's a way to reuse stealth through vanish, while giving all the cooldowns back for you to reopen or do whatever else you wanted.

And all this on paper sounds nice and dandy, but here comes the next issue. The initially designed talents turned out perfectly well for the game that was a leveling simulator with a couple of dungeons. The issue arose once pvp and pve endgame was realized. But that's a separate discussion entirely. In short, trees were designed around three main aspect classes, all aspects work in regular dungeon and leveling environment but can't hold up in upscaled to 40 man content because a lot of things became straight up useless or else they would be too overpowered with some few examples being transformed into raid mechanics in some encounters, like ability to disarm traps in suppression room.  Making it work is impossible, and pretending like it isn't is weird, blizzard took two expansions of tailoring the game to raids to fix it and still there are specs like beast mastery and subtlety rogue that doesn't work in endgame pve. So today I'm not trying to talk about that, but rather about one thing I know turtle have focused on for the last few years of its existence.

The suggestion itself:
There is an ability that bugged me so damn much ever since I joined wow as a game. I started playing it seriously in legion and then went to check out vanilla and throughout all this time I have never ever been excited by it, I always felt like it was so out of place. Like, Shadowstrike from legion? Badass. Improved ambush? Hell yeah. Shadow step from tbc? Really cool. But hemorrhage was always just, this doesn't feel right, and I sort of grew into forgetting it even exists because it just always clashed with the idea of subtlety so much. Is it an opener? Nope. Is it something you use in stealth? No. Is it based on thievery or snatching something, or preparing for an opening? No, no, no. It was always just a weakening bleed that isn't a dot, like literally every other bleed in the game. And so I got this idea randomly, what if hemo was never meant to be part of subtlety, how did such an ability came into being in such an unfitting spot and why did blizzard were fine with it and just moved on keeping it as is. And as it turned out I was right, it was never a subtlety ability. In fact, it wasn't even an ability, it was a passive that worked like this:
![Image](https://i.im.ge/2022/08/12/OMJa9m.Screenshot-2022-08-12-175530.jpg)

And it clicked. A talent that worked of crits and functioned like deep wounds, like all the other bleeds in the game, it was just a way in assassination tree to boost the damage of your crits, like one of ignite. The prerequisite to hemo in 1.12.1 version of the talent tree in 0.9 was also there. Improved rupture. Which made perfect sense, rupture is a finishing move that doesn't have it's improved talent version now, as it was rebranded to be an easier sell for a subtlety tree. What I think must have happened here was this talent was reworked into an ability effect of which was to buff your attacks just like the previous variation did without being a passive somewhere in between 0.9 and 1.1 patches as assa had only one active ability cold blood, but as it was made developers felt like it no longer fit that well into the tree that was about finishing moves and crits, simultaneously all trees were finalised to look prettier, as in first versions they looked robust and not exciting with talents and arrows flowing all over the place unlike the 1.12.1 version that looks more like a table than a spontaneous talent clumps. So hemo just had a really bad time to be reworked, it was kicked out of the tree together with a most useless looking talent, improved rupture, and added to subtlety that in between 0.9 and 1.1 came through a process of merging many talents and looked rather empty. Making the final look of 1.1 version of talents much more acceptable. It feels like this ability, moved abruptly, was destined to get back to assassination eventually but for whatever reason they decided to keep it, even the out of place improved rupture, they decided to make it work, they connected two abilities and renamed the rupture into serrated blades adding an armor penetration effect to it as they probably thought would make subtlety more powerful as it was one of the underperforming tree, later fully abandoning idea of armor pen based subtlety and adding deadliness as the powerhouse of the tree instead and the rest is history.

What I think turtle wow can do with this situation is to restore the historical injustice. It's obvious that hemo in its current form no longer is an assassination ability for most of the players, as they are used to seeing it and using for pvp or whatever else reason. The ability is objectively decent, even if it doesn't allow for subtlety to stand on its own in much of anything, but by that same argument it also has no place in subtlety tree either. I think it needs to be reworked slightly. Tweaked to be more about crits or more about combo points, which I mean it is already slightly tweaked that way, as the cheapest combo point generator, it's the best at getting combo points quickly which is in fact what assassination is about. I think it needs a bit of a boost in this direction to fully become a good ability for assassination, a tree that is lacking a capstone talent that is worth taking. Because vigor must go, it just isn't used for anything, no purpose or interest in players. After all we might have seen already what fully realised hemorrhage would look like with tbc expansion and it's capstone talent for assa called Mutilate, with its theme slightly altered to give a boost to dagger based spec that lost it's niche with less powerful backstab compared to the amount of health mobs and players had. Just taking out hemo would be silly, serrated blades would have to go with it. My best bet would be to separate the two halves and keep armor pen talent where it is, while putting improved rupture in second or third assa talent tree. The both would need to be slightly stronger since they don't acompany each other anymore. Lastly, that would mean subtlety would lose a talent and a pretty good one. I think best option is to either keep it as is, since subtlety is already filled with some of the most pointless talents in the game, or instead give something new and interesting. I thought of maybe a highly thematic improvement garrote got from tbc that in addition with the dot silenced the target for 3 seconds. Garrote in real world is a method of strangling someone with a cable, so being silenced is such a perfect thematic fit while also giving usually overlooked garrote a niche that is needed and is interesting. It just makes so much sense, but it seems players kind of don't care about that, so perhaps there is needed a much stronger or more interesting option, I honestly have no clue.

In any case, I doubt this post with cause anything, but it is interesting to think about how abilitities that randomly end up in places do so. It was certainly interesting to me while I was looking for possible explanations, so I hope this post made some things clear to some people about my view. I really hope that if big turtle wow class changes are ever going to happen turtle devs won't fall into same mistake as wow developers did by adding up new themes into each tree without refigning all the old cool gems that didn't got refinment and just got forgotten and repurposed, because in the end all this method leads to is a mess that requires reworks over and over but now people expect this new theme they particularly liked to be fully represented in some way later on.
