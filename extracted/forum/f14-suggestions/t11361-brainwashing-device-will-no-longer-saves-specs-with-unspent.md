## Post 75109 by Xudo (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2023-11-01T06:35:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=75109#p75109 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Giving 3rd spec for additional gold is actually good idea. But where is the limit? Is 10 saved specs is enough? May be 15?

I used to play warrior protection. I never changed my spec for years because it worked fine all this time. I was perfect in my niche role.
And I think that forcing single player to support more than 2 different specs like druids and shamans are used to do is bad for players. You need to stack all kinds of consumables for all your specs. You need bank space for gear for all your specs. You need to adjust your keybinds to all your specs individually. You lose muscle memory when you change your role.

Server is supposed to encourage leveling.
Right now people can level single druid and shaman and (attempt to) play all kinds of roles endgame.
You won't need to level any other character if you have druid. Mage? Nah, I can spec boomkin for 5g. Priest? Nah, I can spec resto for 5g. Rogue? Nah, I can spec feral DPS for 5g.

With infinite specs you need less people in your guild roster, because your people can fit any role.
If you need 2 druids: resto for healing party and feral for melee party in your raid and you have 3 universal-specced druids in guild and only 2 druids online, you can pick them both and spec them whatever you need. If you need 2 same druids, but you have 2 feral druids online, then you need to recruit for backup resto druid and gear it. Dualspec actually solves this problem, situation is illustrative.

Other problem is PvP. If speccing from PvE to PvP spec don't cost you anything, then you easily bring your t3 stuff in battlegrounds and have fun there killing people who didn't progressed that far yet. You don't have to choose between PvP and PvE talents because you can switch anytime.
