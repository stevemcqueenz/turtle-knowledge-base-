![🧠](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f9e0.svg) Why this change would improve the game:
![💼](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f4bc.svg) 1. Inventory Management Becomes Less Frustrating
Constantly running out of space because you can only stack 10 or 20 items per slot interrupts the flow of gameplay. Players spend too much time juggling inventory instead of playing the game.

![⚔️](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/2694.svg) 2. Frees Up Players to Actually Use Their Items
When items are limited by low stack counts, people tend to hoard them “just in case.” With bigger stacks, it’s easier to use your poisons, powders, and runes as intended—more often and more creatively.

![🧪](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f9ea.svg) 3. Makes Crafting & Combat More Dynamic
Whether you’re brewing potions, enchanting gear, or prepping for a boss fight, it helps to have enough resources on hand. High stack limits allow deeper strategy without forcing constant trips to storage.

![💀](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f480.svg) 4. Souls and Other Unique Items Deserve Breathing Room
Items with story or gameplay significance (like souls or essences) shouldn’t clutter your whole inventory. Letting them stack makes it easier to enjoy collecting without feeling punished.

![🪙](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1fa99.svg) 5. Less Micromanagement = More Immersion
Inventory stress kills immersion. Letting players hold more of what they earn lets them focus on story, combat, and exploration—not sorting 50 versions of the same powder.
