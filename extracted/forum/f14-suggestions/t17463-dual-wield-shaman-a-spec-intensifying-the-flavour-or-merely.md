# Dual-wield shaman, a spec intensifying the flavour or merely a product of balancing?

## Post 119218 by Sleeplust (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2025-02-07T06:40:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=119218#p119218 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I know the title seems a bit too big.

Looking back to the year 2000's game Warcraft III, where (orcish) shamanism is first introduced as a mainstream faith or religion in game, the shaman unit wears a cloth or leather attire with a claw (like a big glove or Balrog's claw in Street Fighters, presumably wolf's) worn on one hand. Other units or heros who bear resemblance with shamans or exist as a "subclass" of shaman, including orcish farseers (such as Drek'thar and Thrall) and furbolg shamans are never seen fighting with dual-wield weapons. If we consider witchdoctors and shadowhunters as shamans, again, they do not dual wield.

The only connection between shamans and dual-wielding combat is probably dual-wielding heros mountain kings. The question is "Are mountain kings actually shamans?" I personally do not think that mountain kings should be taken as shamans. In the game World of Warcraft: Cataclysm, which was released in 2010, newly available dwarf shamans' combat style is strongly similar to mountain kings', but that was how dual-wielding shaman was created and we should not take that as a proof.

The only answer I find is that dual-wielding shaman is only a product of balancing mechanism, just like how Draeneis became relatives to Eredars and how night elves became human-faced trolls. They are never intensifying the "flavour", but destroying it.

The better choice to be made able to dual wield is druid. Druid of the Claw of night elven faction is the only non-hero humanoid unit in four main factions of Warcraft III who dual wield weapons (bear claws) and melee.
