There are so many variables in the mix now, where fixing one thing could break another thing. I agree with those two goals, as a Warrior main I've gone on at length about my dissatisfaction with a number of changes with respects to design ethos and spec identity, but in a show of good faith I'll say the buffs to Arms' bleeds is a good move in many ways (namely in mechanical identity and spec fantasy, but also eliminating the noob-trap aspect of Rend). There's definitely changes to be found in the patch that are steps in the right direction.

> There were some trees which didn't really do these. Personally I'd have barely touched properly working trees but some needed a revamp:
>  * Hunter's survival tree was a random set of talents and mostly worked as a shop where caster hunters bought some talents. Hade no identity at all.

Hunter as a whole, but especially Survival, was undeniably in a pretty half-baked state at launch, but the original tree still has a focus on traps, melee utility and crowd control. This is an idea I'm gonna keep coming back to but this feels like more of a number tuning issue than one of core design. A lot of the less invasive tweaks and changes that were made I'm in favor of. (but please just give Counterattack back, I don't want it)

> * Demonology warlock. Most of the tree buffed your demons but by far the best talent rewarded you to sac it (similarly to now but at least DS is nerfed and it's easier to get).

I'd argue that plays into the identity of Demonology being the spec of studying and exploiting demons perfectly fine, that contrasts with Hunter's bond to their pets and distinguishes the two pet classes in both mechanics and flavor.

> * Holy pala is arguable. It was a perfectly working build but when I tried it I felt like it's a 'Tesco priest with mana hack'. You had a guy usually in cloth standing back, looking like a priest, healing like a priest except for the much smaller array of heals. But melee healer in it's current form is not really an answer. Definitely gives a unique identity but it just doesn't work at many places.

Paladin's a whole can of worms to open here, but the Big Blue Dress thing with Holy felt more like an issue of itemization at the time. There just wasn't enough plate and mail gear that rewarded Holy enough to compete with healer robes, which is an issue that can be addressed in loot tables instead of talent trees. I'm not strictly opposed to the idea of a melee healer, but the current implementations feel a little heavy-handed.

> * Enh shaman. Was it a tank tree? A melee tree? Both? Pre-changes you were either a bad DPS as an enh or you just stayed a healer - with bloodlust. I think enh tree needed a revamp, too. While I'm a huge fan of shaman tanking, enh becoming both a tank and a melee DPS is unfair, shamans basically became druids, even if the tank is a bit iffy sometimes. I'd have preferred enh becoming either a 100% valid melee DPS or a 100% valid tank, not a weird amalgamation of '80% valid builds', even if LFT needed more tanks. Sacrificing class design and talents to solve the LFT tanking problems is a disservice to the related class.

It was supposed to be both, like Feral. The difference being Feral's talents affected both forms to identify them more as a true hybrid. The original idea was that Shaman is the offensive counterpart to Paladin's more defensive identity, while both were supposed to act as tank/heal/dps hybrids at most stages of the game, Paladin got more robust tanking options in comparison while Shaman had better damage options in the form of range and burst. That can get a little weird for me because while I hold the original design of the game in high regard, I question the decision to have a tank spec designed to fall off before endgame (this is something original blizzard devs have admitted to in interviews). Enhancement's main identity has always been the melee portion of Shaman's abilities, with Earth representing their defensive powers and Air representing their melee offense. I think this again comes down to tuning, I'm a believer that Shamans should be able to tank in most if not all settings and I don't think it's a bottom-up overhaul away.

> * Prot tree for warrior. I just hated that taking the tanking talents if you wanted to tank was actually bad for you (except for the must haves at the top). Random DPS talents from fury being way better for tanking than the complete lower half of the tanking tree felt like... Well, felt really bad.

I said it was gonna come up a lot but this feels like another numbers issue, a portion of the lower talents that are just CC are there for general utility and PvP, but the non-CC talents just didn't have the numbers to feel very worthwhile. I don't think it was ever a problem with core design, and I don't think it ever needed a complete overhaul. I can only speak for myself but all I really wanted out of this patch was re-tuning and tweaks, not a fundamental redesign of every spec.
