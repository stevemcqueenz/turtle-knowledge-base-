## Post 23475 by Talrassan — 2022-08-26T02:13:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=23475#p23475 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

+1

I think there's a few different merits in changing some or all of the stuff produced through engineering to be useable either untrained or, as this post suggests, through mastery of something like the survival profession.

As a roleplayer with interest in PvP, engineering has always bothered me. I can only either choose to handicap myself arbitrarily through roleplaying my characters the way I envision them, or choose to give up roleplay to be on an even playing field with others in PvP.

All other professions' products you can buy and use without ever training them (for the most part). How come I can use a crafted piece of gear without knowing how to make it, chug down a potion without knowing how to brew it, use an enchanted weapon with no knowledge of enchantments, but not throw grenades without the ability to craft them? This isolates engineering in a way none of the other professions are, making it a de facto mandatory pick if you value efficiency even slightly.

But is this really necessary? You can already sell what engineers create, there's just no real market for it since everyone who can use it can also craft it. As an engineer, you can pay other players to gain their professions' advantages, but you can't pay engineers to gain theirs. In that sense, it's a profession akin to first aid, yet it takes up a main profession slot.

For these reasons, I, too, would welcome a change to the requirements of using engineering's consumables and gadgets.
