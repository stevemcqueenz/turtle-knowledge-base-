# Paladin dungeon tanking

## Post 161680 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2025-12-05T14:37:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=161680#p161680 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

Hey!

This will be a more controversial one, but I think someone has to bring up the subject.
I'd propose changing the power curve of prot paladins.
At lategame, they could be as strong as they are at the moment.
They have a whole talent tree for tanking and paladins deserve having a fully functional raid tank option.

But the problem is **dungeons**.
I also have a pala, so I can experience the thing first hand.
Used to be joking to friends whenever I logged in as the dworf that for this dungeon I bring the demigod.
But usually, I heal nowadays.
And whenever I healed a dungeon, basically anywhere between Deadmines and Black Morass, I felt a huge power gap between a pala tank and anyone else.
Gearing made it worse, level 40-60 had maybe the biggest gaps.

Let me explain it.
Sure, prot palas cannot range pull unless they learn engi for that or at least use EZ throw dynamites.
But that's one disadvantage you can always circumvent really easily.
There are also niche cases when prots face unique problems.
Probably they are the tanks most screwed by fear and mana burn can really hurt them.

 **But in exchange:**
 * They have an extremely consistent threat on ANY amount of targets (pick a number between 1 and infinite, assuming healer can keep up). While everyone else (besides shamans) has to be careful about pack sizes, pala could pick up any number of packs. After a prot pala run, having anyone else as a tank just feels slow and being dragged out.
 * Survivability is extremely strong. Consistent blocks all the way, 10% damage reduction without any downsides and a 5 min defensive CD means that yes, you could pull those 2-3, maybe 4 packs together and don't die.
 * Mana. Each tank has a form of finite resource. Shaman tanks go oom really fast if they overreach, bears and warriors need to build up the resources within the fight. Wisdoms (plus the free hits, plus the items giving extra hit) result in a pala tank which, if played well, never has to drink. I remember prots running into 3-4 packs, spamming abilities and finishing the fight with 80%+ mana consistently.
 * Utility. Next to having nigh infinite resources and being uncapped for threat, prots give excellent utility for the team. Blessings, aura, wisdom procs for mana-using melee.

 *Again, I don't want prots to be weaker at raid content in any form.*
But during leveling, they shouldn't be this much better than the alternatives.
So I propose keeping the power curve lower for a while before eventually reaching the same level where it currently is.

 **There are many reasons for which I propose this:**
 * It's a bad design when you internally sigh every time the tank isn't a pala.
 * It kinda discourages other classes to queue as a dungeon tank. Pala efficiency has created an expectation in the DPS and messages like why are you pulling so few or why can't you keep threat on each of those mobs, noob lol are bound to happen.
 * It gives a hard reality check to new prot pala players when raiding environment is reached and suddenly they are not special in the role.

 **If I had to rephrase:**
Dungeons are usually built based on the concept of trash packs interrupted by not too hard bosses.
The pala's ability to have good threat on any number of mobs at a time (consec) makes it capable of tanking several packs at once.
A bear or a warrior would lose threat left and right.
A shammie might not, but without a def CD or block+ all over the roof, they would die super quick trying to replicate that playstyle. And they would go oom during this.

This is a huge inherent advantage for dungeon tanking.
