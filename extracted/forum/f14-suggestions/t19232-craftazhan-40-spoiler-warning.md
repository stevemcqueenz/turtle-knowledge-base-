# Craftazhan 40 (Spoiler Warning)

## Post 130664 by Piacrt (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2025-05-12T03:53:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=130664#p130664 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

The new raid, Karazhan 40, has almost nothing for professions. And by almost, I mean the only thing that was added was a new healing food quest, which is nice, but a bit underwhelming.

Being a half-tier raid, we can look at AQ40, another half-tier raid, to compare.
- New gear via a reputation for resist gear

- Bosses have a chance to drop new, powerful enchantments

- A catchup raid that adds new tiers of spells for all classes
We get a couple of these boxes checked with Karazhan:
- New gear (no rep needed) for resist gear

- A catchup raid (Kara10) that drops upwards of 1 recipe for many professions (or a whole set for Blacksmiths and a few enchants)
Most of the items from Kara10 that were made for crafters were clearly statted for new players. Towerforge, the goggles, etc. A bit underwhelming. Some things are really neat though like the Smelling Salts and Quickness Potion. The invocations are underwhelming, but at least the Dodge one gets some use and the Arcane Resist one is a must for those shiny new resist sets.

With Karazhan 40 mostly being made up of small gear upgrades for many classes, it feels like something is missing. We climb the tower, with a library of knowledge surrounding us, blue dragonkin, and ultimately, a portal to another planet. And all we get to learn for crafters is a salad recipe. Amazing.

Kara40's surrounding content was certainly built to resemble other raid tiers but falls short due to the gap between Kara10 ilvl and Kara40 ilvl.

It seems to me that professions were forgotten about for the most part for this raid, so my suggestion is how can we improve this?

This is a very difficult topic imo.

I completely agree that
 1) Professions are mostly left out when it comes to K40 and it's kind of a shame.
 2) The legendary library of Karazhan still feels underused and could provide more knowledge.
 3) Enchanting could use some new stuff.
 4) Gemology is weirdly missing the entire raid progression that Goldsmithing has.

But yet I still heavily disagree with your proposed additions. Why?
A) Exclusivity is a problem on a server with thousands of players. People can't really handle it. We saw it when Enchanted Gemstone Oil was bugged and people started paying hundreds of gold for 1 Oil. We see it from all the complaints because people think they are entitled to a Scythe drop and struggle with the 0.85% drop rate.
If it's an AQ40-style 1% drop from an Outland boss whoever gets the recipe stands to make tens of thousands of gold and become the new public enemy number one. Loot RNG should not have this much of an impact on a server this old. Especially when the circle of people who have access to Outland is still under 15 guilds (as far as I know). This would just feed those who are already ahead of everyone else.
As much as it sucks that Gemology just dead-ends in pre-raid dungeons - I think it would have sucked even more if there had been a hugely important gem recipe that was a 1% drop from MC (like the weapon enchants). So I actually like the new paradigm. It seems deliberate and careful that new recipes that allow you to create non-BoP stuff pretty much only come from vendors and quests, ie they are widely available to even the playing field on the markets.
The Salad recipe works because everyone can pick it up, even from a cleared ID. Imagine how stupid the market manipulation would have been if the recipe was a 1% boss drop? Or if it required limited resources from Outland.

B) The powercreep is real with your proposed additions to enchanting and gemology. Especially considering it's in their nature to be applied to other people. So the powerspike they provide would not be limited to K40 - it would seep into everything from pre-raid to PvP. Since CC2 TWoW already has a massive problem with making raid progression before K40 a complete joke. We should be careful not to add too much to that.
I could see this working if the effect was much more limited - say "+AP/SP when fighting demons" that kind of stuff.

I think they over time learned some stuff. The ES/K10 recipes that filled gaps (+nature/arcane damage to gloves) were better than the ones adding new bonus stats. The Gems were all made widely available with 1.17.2. The K40 recipe comes from a quest that everyone can just pick up. I think this is a lot better than to add uber exclusive super powerful recipes that are cornered by whoever gets insanely lucky among the top 5% of the server.

Yes, it misses some magic. It would be awesome to get cool new recipes. But I think it's better this way.

Yeah, that's why I mention they might have learned something from the ES recipe. Also take note that the AQ40 recipes are 1% from Twin Emps and blue quality, while Formula: Eternal Dreamstone Shard is also 1% per weekly lock-out and of legendary quality (yes, the initial drop rate was lower, but it was buffed pretty quickly to 1%). So this tells you something how they think about AQ40-style recipes. Those kind of drop rates are for legendary stuff, not for common enchants that virtually every raider would want.

I think the server age is precisely the problem. It changes things a lot. You have to balance a new raid like K40 around groups in full BiS because the development cycle was 4 years since full Naxx farm, not 4 months like on retail. And you have to balance the economy around 10k players of which some have vast riches and others are just starting out. It's different when a fresh server hits AQ40 opening, but also there's nothing wrong with not repeating the questionable choices that Blizzard made.
Professions deserve some more cool recipes, but AQ40 is probably a bad blueprint.

Yeah, ilvl requirements are an interesting approach (like I mentioned making enchants K40 specific), but where do you draw the line? The least disruptive requirement would be something like ilvl 90+. But at that point you might as well buff those items directly instead of making people jump through an additional hoop. But there's something there, some clever design to avoid general powercreep from a new tier of enchants. Just has to be very carefully considered. But as much as cloaks are an underserved market right now, going form current melee BiS +3 Agi straight to +15 agi (like you propose) doesn't really feel like a carefully considered adjustment. I think non-tradable BoP enchants are a good option to liberally powercreep while limiting the broader impact, but obviously that does nothing to boost professions. I don't have a good solution either.
