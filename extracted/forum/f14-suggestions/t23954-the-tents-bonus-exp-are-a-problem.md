Early MMO design—especially in World of Warcraft—was explicitly built around keeping players engaged for years. Systems like time gates, slow leveling and progression, and lockouts were tools to stretch content consumption so subscription revenue remained stable. If a game no longer depends on subscriptions, it makes sense to reevaluate mechanics that were originally built around that retention model. Subscription MMOs are like toll roads built to recover construction costs. Time gates and grind systems slow progress so players stay subscribed longer. If the subscription model is removed, leaving those systems untouched is like keeping toll booths on a highway that’s already been paid off. Even if TWoW created these servers as exact 1:1 copies of Vanilla WoW, without their extra content, the game and its population would still function differently from the population that originally played Vanilla WoW from Nov '04 to Jan '07 because there is no subscription model incentivizing players to make their time investment "worth the money."

One could argue that true Vanilla private servers have never existed if none ever required a subscription with the same pricing (or, technically, inflated pricing to match whatever economic impact $15 a month had during those years). If money is involved, it is always a motivating factor in peoples' behaviors. I think tents adequately, as they are, reevaluate the paces of leveling that are available to the playerbase. They're there if you want them, you pay nothing but your own time if you don't interact with them. It's like driving through the old abandoned toll booth and miming like you're still paying. You don't have to, nobody's forcing you to, and you can't force anyone to do it with you. Think of that one gnome player from the Vanilla era that asked his entire MC raiding guild to wait for him while he toggled on the slow walking animation and strolled from Stormwind all the way to Blackrock Mountain because it was "more realistic," and, yes, he kept the walk animation on while in MC, too.

If you're having a hard time finding dungeons, the only solution you have at the moment is pinging /world and hopping in the dungeon finder at the same time, and while you wait, maybe give thought as to why dungeons and raids required the numbers that they did in Vanilla Era. Are exactly 5 players needed for all dungeon-based experiences to work and be fulfilling for various people across all MMOs in existence? Would a dungeon or raid system fail if it and its content was designed (and correctly worked) around other numbers, including a flexible range? What if raid sizes were chosen by the original development team not because their size was selected to reflect a desired difficulty, but because the average speed that the population could complete gear progression in a raid designed for 10 players just happened to be the speed that Blizzard was comfortable providing from a subscription-retention standpoint and THEN the difficulty was balanced as a 10-man raid?

I think it's always important to ask oneself why universally appied friction is jumped to as a solution when it can be adequately applied by the individual on a case-by-case basis without harming the desired experience of others. That's why /pvp is a *choice* in a PvE or RPPvE realm and not the baseline like in a PvP realm (which you still *chose* to participate in if you create a character one one).
