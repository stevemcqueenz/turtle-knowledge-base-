TBH I don't like tents either. I don't particularly care either way about the amount of rested XP they give, but I don't like how they twist the purpose of rested XP.

In my mind, rested XP is supposed to be a bonus to help players who haven't played in a while or who don't have as much playtime as their friends to be able to catch up. It can also serve as an incentive to play multiple alts and bounce between them while levelling.

TWoW's tents basically eliminate those purposes altogether. Since you can now get max rested XP in the space of a couple of minutes, it changes the nature entirely from a nice-to-have bonus, to just another chore you do. It would literally make the game better if they took them out entirely and just doubled XP rates to compensate, as now you can spend more of your time doing things that are fun instead.

So... yeah, I think they should make it so they A) do not stack, and B) only give an amount of rested XP that is the same as (or slightly less than) what you'd get sitting in an inn. IMO, logging out in an inn should be the default, and people should only use tents if they're in the middle of something when they need to log out and don't want to port home.

At the same time, I think it'd be great if they added a secondary type of rested xp that has the same effect, but doesn't count towards the rested xp cap. If you had both rested and this secondary type, you'd either get 3x xp, or be able to get rested past 30 bubbles. You'd get this secondary type from being well fed, to make cooking a little nicer to have.

Edit: They could also give survival an advanced type of cooking fire that lets players share meals and gain slightly improved benefits from being well fed from those shared meals as long as they eat/rest for at least 2-3 minutes, with stacking benefits from having multiple fires like how tents work now. This way you'd still get that benefit of having players come together in groups to sit around and socialize for a bit, but without destroying the purpose of rested xp or turning it into the afk chore that is the current tent implementation (since the costs would be lower, people could use them in the wild, and since the time needed to sit around would be lower, there'd be less incentive to afk).
