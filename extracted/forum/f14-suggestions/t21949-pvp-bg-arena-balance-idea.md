# PVP --- BG/arena balance idea ^-^

## Post 152103 by Ryitzeelalala — 2025-09-17T14:48:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=152103#p152103 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Firstly, huge shoutout to the incredible Turtle WoW development team—thank you for delivering what is, in my opinion, the finest classic WoW server experience to date. The 1.18 update is absolutely fantastic, and I sincerely hope you’re able to navigate through these challenging times (especially with the Blizzard lawsuit  crying_turtle  ) and continue to grow stronger.

Since the PvP update in 1.18.1 hasn’t arrived just yet, I’d like to humbly share some thoughts on how battleground and arena balance could be approached.
How about introducing a fully equalised PvP environment? By that, I mean once you enter a BG or arena, all players—would have **identical base stats** regarding of their race & class. All external gear and consumables would be **disabled** (yes, you read that right!). Instead, upon entering, each player receives a set amount of resource points.

Then, in the preparation area(like a seperate new room in WSG/Arathi Basin), there could be NPC vendors selling **temporary gear**—armour, weapons, alchemy potions, engineering gadgets, trinkets, you name it—all purchasable with those resource points. Think of it like Dota: players spend their points during the prep phase to kit themselves out. To keep things clean and development-friendly, these items could be made usable only within the BG or arena (e.g., special AV bandages available only there!). There could even be a bank NPC to help players store unnecessary outside gear and free up bag space.(Please make these vendor NPCs large and visually distinct—like an armory rack or a large quartermaster's table—to prevent players from struggling to click on them in crowded environments like Alterac Valley.)

To ensure fairness while still respecting character progression, the only external advantages a player could bring into this new mode would be the skills and abilities they have already learned on their character. Furthermore, for bracket-specific balance (e.g., 10-19, 40-49), all players would have their stats normalized to the maximum level of that bracket. For example, everyone in a 10-19 battleground would be scaled to the power of a level 19 character.

While this wouldn't create perfect balance (as a level 19 will have more abilities than a level 10), it would be a massive improvement over the old system where both level and gear created imbalance. This approach should also be relatively straightforward to implement.

Additionally, during the match, players could earn extra resource points through kills, assists, capturing objectives, scoring, etc.—which they can use to “shop” mid-match back at the vendor.

This way, your balancing efforts could focus primarily on tuning those BG/arena-specific items—say, axes having higher burst damage, swords being more consistent, and so on. You could even introduce BG-versions of rare items like Tidal Charm replica(so we don’t have to fight tooth and nail in PvE for unbalanced advantages), or maybe even powerful artefacts inspired by Dota (why not a Divine Rapier-like item?).

On the topic of PvP gear(usable in open-world) progression, I also have a suggestion:
With(or without) the above system in place, players could earn tokens from kills, assists, and wins in BGs and arenas. These tokens could be exchanged for epic gear usable in the open world—but with **a weekly cap** of, lets say, 1-2 items (similar to raid lockouts, to slow down gear acquisition).

The gear could be released in tiers: for example, you start by unlocking Tier 1 PVP pieces. To upgrade to Tier 2, you’d need to use your T1 gear plus some PvE materials and additional PvP tokens—again, with weekly limits. This way, you can even phase the release of higher-tier PvP gear with content updates.

Stat-wise, these items could be slightly less powerful in PvE than their raid equivalents but offer unique PvP advantages. And, please considering add more funny-effect trinkets which only exchangable using pvp reward tokens.

For the sake of choice, the old (if unbalanced) BGs and arenas could still remain available, but perhaps this new system could be introduced as a separate, optional game mode that players can queue for.

To fit the game's lore, these two modes could be named:

 **Hero Mode**: You enter the battleground with everything your character has earned throughout your journey, fighting for your faction's glory.You may face well-prepared champions from the opposing faction adorned with awe-inspiring gear in challenging battles, while lesser-equipped opponents will stand little chance against your might.

 **Soldier Mode**: You enter as a rank-and-file soldier of your faction, drawing standard-issue gear from the quartermaster at the starting area and earning the right to requisition powerful, rare temporary supplies from the armory based on your performance and contribution in the battle.

Once again, thank you for all your hard work. Wishing you all the best!

Given that WoW's class talents and abilities are already quite complex, the PVP temporary-gear system shouldn't be as vast as Dota's. Instead, it should be streamlined, more akin to Counter-Strike, where players in the preparation phase primarily choose between armour, weapon, and a few extra consumables.

 **For example:**

A Plate Armour Vendor could sell just three sets: an aggressive DPS set, a defensive tank set, and a set tailored for healing/support. For simplicity, each "set" could be a single item (e.g., a "Battlegear Chestpiece") that provides the combined stats of a full armour set, with their stats adjusted for type and scaled for each of the 7 level brackets (10-19, 20-29, etc.), resulting in 3x7=21 possible items, and automatically applies a unique visual appearance.

A Cloth/Leather/Mail Armour Vendor works the same as above.

A Jewelry Vendor could sell rings and necklaces, primarily to allow for fine-tuning of stats, with items similarly scaled for each level bracket.

Two Weapon Vendors: One could sell standard weapons (all 15 in-game types, excluding fishing poles ofcuz), with their stats adjusted for type and scaled for each of the 7 level brackets (10-19, 20-29, etc.), resulting in 15x7=105 possible items. A second vendor could sell special weapons available only at level 60, featuring powerful, unique effects at a high resource cost.

A Trinket Vendor could offer various unique trinkets (base PvP insignia, engineering gadgets, offensive/defensive trinkets, tactical items), each with a minimum level requirement to restrict use in lower brackets.

An Alchemy Vendor could sell potions (standard alchemy pots, special items like herbal tea, and even powerful flasks at a high cost), also with level requirements.

Two General Goods Vendors: One could sell class reagents (candles, vanishing powder, seeds, etc.) for simple gold (to avoid complicating the resource point economy). The other could sell engineering gadgets (like grenades, like rocket helmets or speed-enhancing boots with no stats, only function) and ammunition for hunters at no resource cost to prevent them from being unable to shoot.

Furthermore, the initial amount of resource points granted to each player at the start of a match should be sufficient to purchase at least: one full armour set, 3-5 core weapons (e.g., a 1H weapon, shield, 2H weapon, and ranged weapon for a Warrior), 4-5 different trinkets, and a stack of grenades plus 4-5 stacks of potions.

Powerful temporary special weapons and trinkets should be priced so that they can at least be purchasable within a single match. Extremely powerful items could even be designed to drop from special events within the BG itself, just similar to the system you implemented in Sunnyglade Valley.

@Zvyrhol, thanks for the reply—I appreciate you sharing your perspective.

While I understand where you're coming from regarding gear progression being a core part of WoW, I have to respectfully disagree when it comes to instanced PvP. In my opinion, gear disparity should primarily matter in PvE or open-world environments. Battlegrounds and arenas, however, shouldn’t be dominated by gear advantage alone; instead, they should emphasize player skill, coordination, and tactical decision-making in a fair competitive setting.

That said, my suggestion isn’t really about making everyone “the same.” More skilled players would still gain an edge—by securing kills, assists, and objectives, they’d earn extra resource points, allowing them to access better temporary gear and consumables during the match. So, truly experienced PvP players would still have a noticeable advantage from the start, not because of pre-farmed gear, but because of actual combat experience.

And regarding the "progression of your character"—earning tokens from fair BGs and arenas to exchange for powerful gear you can use in the open world is itself a very rewarding form of progression. It just shifts the focus from grinding to performing.

This approach could solve 2 big issues:

 1. It prevents situations where gear power creep leads to “one-shot meta” or extreme snowballing—where whoever attacks first simply wins or god gear player just deleting ppl. With this system, global damage/healing adjustments wouldn’t even be necessary; balance could be fine-tuned through the temporary gear itself.

 2. It allows for comeback mechanics. If one team is falling behind, players could invest in powerful situational items (think of something like Blade Mail in Dota) to turn the tide.

I absolutely agree that gear-based progression should remain meaningful in world PvP—that’s part of what makes outdoor risk/reward engaging. I also think the old-style unbalanced BGs should still be available for those who prefer them. But especially with the upcoming rated arena system in 1.18.1, offering a separate, opt-in mode with equalized footing could help ensure that competition is based on skill rather than who brings the most Fossilized Potions or best-in-slot gear.

After all, without some form of balance, arenas might just become a contest of wealth and preparation—which isn’t really what “competitive” should be about.

Let's be honest: even putting class imbalance aside, the current state of gear disparity is like showing up to a chess tournament where your opponent has an extra row of pawns just because they've played more matches—or like facing a boxer in the ring who gets to wear brass knuckles because they once beat up a black bear together with his/her fellas in a PvE event. A player with superior skill but inferior gear stands almost no chance.

While this kind of advantage might be acceptable in the open world—where you can roleplay as an all-powerful warlord...  However...  should a 20-APM, room temperature IQ keyboard-turning Pally/Shaman(plz..) really be able to effortlessly crush a highly-skilled player(with 'grass' gear set, just analogy..) in WSG, only because they've grinded better gear? That doesn't seem like a healthy or fun competitive environment

9/22nd new thoughts regarding BG rewards, PvP gear redesign, and rank adjustments.
 ------------- Separator -------------

 1. BG Rewards & World Impact
Under the proposed Hero Mode (original) and Soldier Mode (equalized gear) system:

Soldier Mode:
Players earn PvP tokens based on performance (kills, assists, flag captures, wins, etc.).
 ***This system actively incentivizes participation and rewards skill—players cannot afk or leech if they want to earn rewards, and more skilled players will naturally acquire tokens faster, ensuring their effort is directly reflected in progression.***
These tokens can be exchanged for PvP gear usable in the open world. However, this mode does not yield traditional PvP reputation badges (e.g., 3 for win/1 for loss, which could be turned in for specific bg reputation/honer).

Hero Mode:
Traditional PvP reputation badges (3 for win/1 for loss) remain unchanged. Additionally, a weekly faction competition could be introduced:

The faction that turns in more badges each week gains a world buff in zones linked to specific BGs (e.g., Alterac Valley → Alterac Mountains, Arathi Basin → Arathi Highlands, Warsong Gulch → Ashenvale, *Sunnyglade Valley → Tanaris* ←if there's badges added for this one).

Buffs could include minor movement speed, mount speed, or XP bonuses, benefiting both PvE and PvP players (e.g., faster gathering, leveling, or travel).

Weekly PvP quests (similar to PvE raid weeklies) could also be added, rewarding PvE consumables and PvP tokens—exclusive to Hero Mode to attract diverse players.

 2. PvP Gear Redesign
I strongly support the idea of revamping existing PvP gear (as discussed in [[viewtopic.php?t=21299](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?t=21299)]). Here’s a concrete proposal:

Remove all current PvP gear (R7-R10, R12-R13 armor and accessories) from vendors. Retain only PvP insignias and tabards.

Reintroduce gear with revised stats and tiers:

PvP Tier 1 (≈ T1 raid level): R7-R10 gear + existing cloaks/accessories.

PvP Tier 2 (≈ T2 raid level): Newly added set.

PvP Tier 3 (≈ T3 raid level): R12-R13 gear + new cloaks/accessories. (which leaves Kara40 equip still the best in unfair PVP senario, but provides PVP players a chance to fight against em)

plus some interesting trinkets ofcuz...

Acquisition via tokens:

Assuming ~25 tokens per BG (15 for kills/assists/objectives + 15 win/3 loss bonus), set Tier 1 gear costs to ~250 tokens (≈10 BGs per piece).

Implement a weekly cap (e.g., 2 Tier pieces per week) to control progression speed.

Tier 2 and Tier 3 gear should require previous tier pieces + additional tokens + PvE materials (e.g., crafted items, gold) to upgrade (similar to T3/Kara gear upgrades).

Resource sink for "PvP nerds":

Sell temporary consumables (temporary food, water, basic reagents including temporary Blind Powder, temp Light Feather) for gold(with a not-super-cheap price) inside BGs as I mentioned before. to incentivize PvE activities for sustainable PvP gameplay (like buying supplies in Sea of Thieves).

 3. Rank Adjustments & Incentives
PvP ranks should remain but decoupled from gear progression. Instead, offer officer privileges:

Higher ranks (up to R14) grant discounts (up to 50%) on consumables and general goods in main cities and BGs (faction benefits for military service).

Honor gain should be faster in *Hero* Mode than in *Soldier* Mode to incentivize participation in the unbalanced format.

Add unique cosmetic appearances (e.g., transmog sets, tabards, mounts) for high-rank players to maintain prestige and engagement.
