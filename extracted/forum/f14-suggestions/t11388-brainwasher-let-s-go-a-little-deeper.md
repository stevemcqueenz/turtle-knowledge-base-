# Brainwasher: Let's go a little deeper

## Post 75288 by Nahath — 2023-11-01T18:02:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=75288#p75288 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Almost all of the conversation about the brainwashing device has essentially been the same points in different words (i.e. it's convenient and saves time. Here are some examples). I'd like to go a little closer to the root on this:

First, it's worth noting that every argument for making respeccing easy/free could also be made for changing your class and race with equal ease. That doesn't mean the arguments are wrong--just that they need a little analysis to understand what's really going on, so that's what I'm trying to do here.

One of the fundamental trade-offs in game design is what I call "intrinsic story" vs. convenience. Intrinsic story is the feeling that there is something real about the world you are in--that when you kill a giant fire god in game, you get at least a sliver of the same feeling you would get if you were actually a great hero doing that in real life.

Much of intrinsic story is built on things that are difficult or inconvenient. For example, instead of flight points, Guild Wars 2 has teleport points (which are also much more numerous than WoW's flight points). As a result, the world feels less real because you spend so little time actually traveling through it. We don't love traveling, but doing so adds weight to the world.

One part of that intrinsic story is character identity. If you can change anything any time, then your character has no identity: You're an amorphous blob that becomes whatever shape you want in the moment. Of course, changing anything any time is incredibly convenient, and so we are looking at that fundamental trade-off. How important is character identity vs. how important is convenience?

When somebody talks about "talents actually mean something", they're in effect saying "Let's move toward stronger character identity: stronger intrinsic story". There isn't a clear right answer for everybody, but Turtle WoW chose to lean toward stronger intrinsic story as compared to retail WoW that has consistently moved toward convenience. And for many of us, that's what we wanted.

So, given all that, should they revert the changes to brainwashing device? Based on my personal preference, I wish they had never introduced the device in the first place, so reducing its effectiveness moves the game in a direction I like. However, once a convenience is introduced, it feels like a betrayal of your player base to take it away--especially if that convenience was purchased with real money. If the devs had moved on this as soon as the one-talent-point exploit was discovered, I would have been fully on board. But when it's been around this long and people have developed their playstyles around it, I'm not so sure.

I hope this post at least gives more understanding of the devs and players who like this nerf--even if I didn't actually change any minds.

You can keep peeling that onion and before you know it you start questioning the meaning behind why we play in virtual worlds and value virtual items and virtual achievements.  (you can of course go even deeper and start applying the same logic to "real" world achievements, but lets not go there)

Ultimately, we are really here for the very real human and community interactions.  Everything else is just a gateway and conduit to that value, and is ultimately accessory and filler.

To an extent, illusion and collective self-deception is the only thing that makes this whole thing work.  Every once in a while something will JAR you a little and you will "wake" up to the transient and valueless nature of the virtual world.  That puts you in a position to weigh the community and relationships value you have against the very real opportunity cost of time invested into virtual/valueless achievements.

Nerfing items, nerfing strats, homogenizing and generally devaluing things players have worked for all JAR us a bit.  I guess the question is whether the community and relationship value is strong enough to weather it.  Looks like T-wow is going to find out.

I wish T-wow would continue to focus on new content, art, and community building.  They need to remove from their team all of the members who are focussed on "the way things should be", purity tests, and the correct "value" of an item, etc.  Or at least rein them in some, and stop all the jarring changes and nerfs.

I think the true spirit of "No changes" wasn't purity of design, rather it was about leaving things in a state that reduced the "jarring" effect, and allowed community focussed facets of the game to persist longer.
