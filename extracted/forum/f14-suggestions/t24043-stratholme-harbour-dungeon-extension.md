Suggestion to add the harbour section to Stratholme as part of future dungeon expansions. As a port city, this would be a lore accurate addition that goes all the way back to WC2 and was even added in WC3 Reforged (see below). The geography of this area suggests the vanilla wow team may have had plans for the harbour originally.

![Image](https://i.imgur.com/ZCLsRDi.png)

And here is the real world size of Stratholme relative to Allah'thalas. I've marked the approx. area where the harbor could be added. The blue arrow shows the most logical city street from which to build out from.
![Image](https://i.imgur.com/1o9hkuU.png)

With that said, Stratholme apparently has much higher elevation than Allah'thalas, as shown below. So a long set of stairs down to the harbor would probably be required, similar to Stormwind.
![Image](https://i.imgur.com/GdUhfvk.png)

As a final point, if this expansion is added, it would be very cool if an uninstanced version of the harbour could be accessible to anyone who happens to swim from the shores of Allah'thalas or east from Eversong Wastes. Possibly having an addition instance portal to the city from here. This would help increase immersion with the open world.

The expanded dungeons from twow has been some of my favorite content so far, so I know they could do this justice.

Hello!

So, you said that these are innovative ideas, but actually, they are not. The harbor has existed in Stratholme’s concept since older Warcraft maps. It was simply never fully realized in WoW. So this isn’t about innovation, it’s more about restoration.

About your concern regarding “overly exploited landscapes,” I think that term needs clarification. Many instances in the game can technically be accessed from outside without using the portal. That alone is not exploitation. If you’re referring to players skipping parts of an instance by jumping over mountains or climbing boxes, that’s not a design philosophy issue — that’s a collision and boundary issue, and it can be solved easily with invisible walls or proper terrain blocking.

Now, since we’re talking about a harbor specifically, that actually opens two very strong design possibilities:

The harbor could function as a questing zone in the open world.

The instance version could represent the same location at a different moment in time — effectively allowing players to interact with the same place in two different narrative states.

Then it works more a immersion deepenner.

And you mentioned players needing isolating structures to preserve the feeling of vastness. Where exactly do you think a harbor fails to provide that? A harbor, especially one built below elevated city walls, naturally creates separation through verticality. If anything, it reinforces scale.

Regarding your Cataclysm example — I see it differently. Impassable mountain walls used as hard borders have always been a design shortcut. They compartmentalize zones artificially instead of creating meaningful ecological transitions. Ecotone zones, elevation shifts, cliffs, and layered terrain create immersion. Empty, unclimbable mountains do not. Verticality adds depth. Classic WoW severely lacks that in many places. Mount Hyjal is actually a good example of how vertical scale can enhance immersion rather than reduce it.

About your final paragraph — I’m not entirely sure what you meant by the “small room for expanding.” Are you referring to physical map space, or to lore constraints?

If it’s physical space, then the solution is vertical development, not horizontal expansion. A harbor carved into cliffs below Stratholme would not crowd Ala’thalas — it would visually separate them even more.

If it’s lore space, then the harbor is already lore-accurate. The question is not whether it fits — it’s how well it’s executed.

I completely agree that implementation must be careful. But that does not mean avoiding development
