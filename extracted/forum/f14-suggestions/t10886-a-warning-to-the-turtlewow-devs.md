# A Warning to the TurtleWoW Devs

## Post 70833 by Saltypete — 2023-10-14T21:20:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=70833#p70833 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I have to preface this post with a little bit of background information. I work in crisis management as a certified expert and have been doing so for ten years. Crisis management doesn't just involve telling others what to do when a crisis happens, but also involves warning others when I can see one is about to happen. The latter is one such thing I want to talk about today. Normally I charge about 60 USD an hour for this, but I like this game a lot and would rather it didn't implode, so this will be free.

I love Turtle WoW and I love most of the work your devs have done — the quality of Gilneas is equal to the zones present in baseline vanilla WoW, for example — and the majority of GMs that I've seen have been polite and reasonable. The advice and criticism I'm going to give comes from a place of understanding that your team seems to genuinely care about providing the best vanilla+ experience possible. There is no animosity whatsoever. Also, I'm not a mage main.

With that said, I'll begin. Lately I've noticed some very concerning nerfs to farming in dungeons — particularly solo farming. The difference between the various Dire Maul farms a year ago and now is very obvious; both in the drops from the lashers and from the non-existence of arcanite within the final thorium veins in jump runs. While nerfing the drops may be understandable to some degree and from certain schools of thought, the most alarming change that indicates a dangerous shift in design philosophy has been the buffing of the crocodiles in Zul'Gurub.

The buffing of the crocodiles indicates a certain hostility towards the idea of people being able to solo-farm in dungeons. I have seen one GM say (in a manner unbefitting a professional representative of a company¹; see this post: [viewtopic.php?t=10584&start=44](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?t=10584&start=44) ) that this was due to safespots. I have a hard time believing this, and I think he may be misinformed; as there are videos out there guiding people on how to do the farm without safespots. And, if, indeed, safespots were the issue, they could simply be removed as the ones in Zul'Farrak were. Adding the charge and the immunity to slow was far too specific a change to be merely about safespotting.

This shift in hostility towards dungeon farming is understandable, but misguided. From what I've been able to gather, the intention seems to be to force players into the open world to engage with others in the interest of "fairness." This will not work in the way you think it will. I have spent the last ten years of my life thinking about the way that people think for a living. I assure you: this will not work in the way you think it will.

I have seen companies attempt similar things before; restricting one portion of their product with the idea that this will force consumers who have little interest in engaging with another portion of the product to engage with it. Initially, there is some success in this. But the success is always short lived, and the percentage of the consumer base who engage with the part of the product they didn't like is always miniscule.

Think of your own personal experiences. Imagine a game with both a singleplayer mode and a multiplayer mode. You like one mode, but not the other. You've surely played one such game in your life. Let's say for sake of experiment that in this case, you dislike the singleplayer mode, but like the multiplayer mode. If the devs were to remove the multiplayer mode to force you to play singleplayer; would you? No. You might dabble in it now and then, hoping to recapture the feeling you had in the multiplayer mode, but you would ultimately just stop engaging with the product. Or imagine an album with three songs you like, and the rest you dislike. If the artist removed those three songs off the album on Spotify, would you listen to the other songs? No. You would stop engaging with the product.

A similarly extreme situation has taken place on Turtle WoW. The open world is simply too populated for the average person to waste their time trying to farm it. Simply ride around, and you will immediately comprehend the truth of this statement. Trying to farm Felcloth in Azshara is nigh impossible for some classes that don't have a long-range instant tag ability; and even for those that do, it's still very, very unpleasant. And this is not unique to Azshara. Winterspring might as well be a moshpit during the hours when the average person is able to play the game.

This kind of competitive farming in the world appeals to some players. Some people enjoy jostling for mob tags, being the fastest, perhaps even engaging in world pvp to assert their farm rotation, etc. For many other players, however, it is not only unappealing; it is considered a toxic and actively negative experience.
As per the statement three paragraphs above, consumers will avoid engaging with portions of a product that they find unappealing. In extreme cases, this results in total disengagement with the product entirely. In other, far more insidious cases, it results in them engaging in behaviors that allow them to bypass that one particular part of the product that they don't enjoy; while continuing to engage with the part(s) that they do enjoy.

This brings us to the end result of this negative, hostile shift in design towards solo-farmability. It will not result in more players farming the world — the world is full, and the players interested in this kind of comptetitive farming are already doing it. It will not result in a more positively active community. It will not result in a more lively world. It will result in a more negatively active community, with mafias forming to monopolize certain lucrative farms (see: Devilsaur mafia in Classic, scout bots for Black Lotuses, Dragonwatch and other world boss coalitions that preceded it in Turtle WoW.) These mafias can be broken up, but it will require considerable resource investment, and they will never stop being formed. The best way to stop a mafia from forming in a circumstance such as this is to create an atmosphere that does not encourage their formation.

But, much more dangerously for the health of the game, gold buying will become far, far more prevalent if this design philosophy is not stopped. This is the true end result of forcing people to engage with a portion of the game that they intensely dislike when the circumstances are so extreme. People will either quit playing, form mafias, or buy gold. I have never seen a single instance of positive behavior from a consumer when forced to interact with a part of a product they didn't like under such extreme circumstances as these. I don't think I need to get into why gold buying is **massively** negative for the health of a server, so I won't bother.

I clarified earlier that I have never seen a single instance of positive behavior from a consumer when forced to interact with a part of a product they didn't like under such extreme circumstances. I must clarify further: this is not something as simple as forcing people to group for certain quests. It's easy enough to ask for help from a friend, quickly find a partner, or just level up enough that you can do it solo. Some people may not enjoy being forced to group up, but the circumstances are so minor that they're willing to accept it — and, really, if they're playing vanilla WoW, they probably do enjoy grouping with people; even if they need some prodding to put in the effort.

Pushing consumers to engage with open world farming or group dungeon farming will result in the average, casual player bearing almost the entire weight of the negative changes, with the more dedicated audience being left mostly unaffected. People who are willing to level alts to 60 solely for farming, or who have well-geared, competent guild members willing to duo/tri farm with them at almost any moment due to the sheer size of the raiding guild, or who have the ability to come online outside of peak hours², are not going to be as affected by the hostile design shift in solo-farmability as someone who has a main with dungeon blues and can play two to six hours a day, at most.

The average player who can spend two to six hours a day playing will not spend those precious hours fighting bots and gold farmers and dedicated players for whatever scraps they're able to gather up if they are forced to. They will stop playing or buy gold. As of now, they are not necessarily forced to do this. But, if the game continues to change in this direction, they will be; and it will be disastrous. To assuage concerns of those who may think I have a bias as an average player, I do not. I am a core in a guild that has Naxxramas on farm. I am one of the players who stands to suffer the least from these changes; not leastwise because the game is essentialy over for me and I don't need to farm nearly as much as I did before.

Some players approve of the nerfs to dungeon farming. These players largely exhibit "crab in a bucket" mentality, and their concerns, while worth listening to, should be weighed with extreme caution; as their mentality, if heeded to in its entirety, will engender hostility between the consumers and resentment for the devs.

One concern I hear from people who support this change in direction is that they find it unfair that people with certain levels of gear can do things they cannot — such as a raid-geared tank being able to two-man certain things with a raid-geared healer that two characters in pre-raid blues cannot. This concern is based in envy and should not be listened to. Getting more out of a video game when you put more effort and/or time into it is the basis of any video game that aims to repeatedly draw in consumers — which is to say, any video game that wants even a modicum of success.

Another concern — and one far more nuanced and interesting — is that of players whose classes cannot farm in a way that others can. A core idea of vanilla WoW is that every class is better at doing something than another class, and in general, the concept of "equality" in regards to class design is antithetical to the vanilla WoW design philosophy. However, it is true that warriors are uniquely disadvantaged when it comes to their ability to farm. In the open world, they have no long-range instant tag ability. Charge, as fast as it may be, is not as fast as fire blast or arcane shot. In dungeons, they have extremely limited aoe and no self-heals. This concern is largely based off the concept of "equality," which would destroy the design philosophy of vanilla WoW.

Because the trade-off, of course, is that warriors dominate group content with their damage and survivability. It is not equal, but it is fair. They excel in one place, and fall behind in another. Attempting to force equal behaviors from inherently unequal classes will ironically result in less fairness; as certain classes will have their downsides made less pronounced, while other classes will have their downsides made more pronounced. This is where the hostility between the consumers and resentment towards the devs will begin to fester. These changes are not, as has been stated, in the interest of fairness. They are in the interest of equality, and attempting to force equality in an inherently unequal game will make it less fair.

Players who find it unfair that a mage can do what they cannot — such as one person who claimed mages can make "too much money" — are free to level a mage themselves. Warmode, tents, self-boosting, unlimited account creation, and a lack of subscription fees are all features of Turtle WoW that allow one to level an alt to 60 with great speed. If they don't want to do this for whatever reason, then that is a problem for them to deal with; not the dev team. Again: getting more out of a video game when you put more effort and/or time into it is the basis of any video game that aims to repeatedly draw in consumers.

Some players may tell you that they should not have to level a mage to 60 just to farm gold. They don't have to. Any class can solo-farm gold in dungeons somehow. Some slower than others, of course, but that's the nature of the game; and I would strongly caution you against attempting to change said nature. If this hostile design philosophy towards solo-farming in dungeons is not reversed, or at least abandoned quickly, the consequences will be very, very negative for the server. I've seen similar situations like this before, and it never works out well. Thank you for reading.

¹ The Turtle WoW team is technically volunteer, but remember that money is made from donations. You may be volunteer, but with the size of your servers, the dev team, and the GM roster, interacting with your consumers with the professionalism expected from a company — albeit a very small one — is highly recommended.

² Even outside of peak hours the open world is still packed, just slightly less so.
