# Allow Cross-Faction Interactions on Tel'Abim (PvP)

## Post 74150 by Jolikmc (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2023-10-27T06:40:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=74150#p74150 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Yes, you read that right.  I'd like to see cross-faction interactions (re-?)enabled on the Tel'Abim PvP realm.

Before you immediately shout "**This defeats the purpose of PvP, you *noob*!!**" and raze my thread for heresy, here's a "Cons and Pros" list for my proposal.

Cons
- This *somewhat* mitigates the functionality and purpose of the automatic "[faction lock](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?t=10966)" when the population is significantly heavier on one side or the other.
- This will probably have **no effect** on a majority of players wanting to make and play Alliance Paladins.
- As is currently the case on the ("RP-")PvE realm, this *does* allow an opposite-faction player to "curse you out", which may add to one's stress.
 - On that same note, this also means that players from opposite factions *can* "stalk" each other across the world via Friends List, as is also the case on the ("RP-")PvE realm.
- **"This defeats the purpose of PvP, you *noob*!!"**
 - To be more specific, this arguably defies the lore of the Alliance and Horde being in a state of "Cold War", or being outright hostile toward one another.
Pros
- This *somewhat* mitigates the "faction lock" issue as Shamans can join Alliance parties and Paladins can join Horde parties.
- No separation anxiety!: friends can be in the same parties and guilds *regardless* of faction.
- It's more fun to trash-talk your opposite-faction opponents when you can understand each other.  (;
- This actually **doesn't** prevent people from engaging in World PvP, much less Battleground PvP.  Even players in the same *guild* can still kill each other and do Battlegrounds, if they want to.
- This doesn't really affect the lore as PvP is purely a game mechanic.  Lore-wise, the Alliance and Horde *are* un an uneasy kind of "Cold War" with isolated land or resource conflicts popping up.  It's actually *more* lore-friendly for, say, an Orc Warrior and a Human Paladin to become allies and possibly friends.
 - Additionally, the Common / Orcish language split was purely a game mechanic.  Lore-wise, every sentient race on Azeroth *should* know "the common language".
So, yeah.  That's my proposal.  I can't imagine this is going to be very popular, but I just wanted to get this out there.  It's definitely easier than shoe-horning a Shaman or Paladin class into the opposite faction, at the very least!

I beg to differ.
Horde and Alliance are indeed not in a Total War, but neither are they merely in a Cold War.. rather, something in between.
They are definitely hostile towards each other, have conflicting interests on all fronts, may it be resources, territory, revenge, worldview differences etc.
Whenever their paths cross, they engage in a battle, if both sides are confident enough in their chance to win.
Diplomats exist on TWoW, and the factions can show compassion towards each other on ocassion, individuals can go against the interest of their faction/race (but that would be considered treason).

When I play as an undead, I'm a Forsaken, not an entity existing in another world that is controlling this fake character in a fake world, but a Forsaken, that fights an Alliance Human city guard or a Scourge necromancer, not "mobs", the same way I fight an Alliance Human Paladin or Dwarf Warrior, not "players".
That is the fundamental principle of immersion in a role, or roleplaying in an online game; reducing the gap between the player and the games world.

No-Cross-Faction adheres to this principle.
Still, the name of the new realm reveals that there is this mindset embedded, that faction conflict is somehow not an inherent part of the lore and a reality on Azeroth. Why, just because these factions happen to be roles players can assume? There are more factions in the world than Alliance and Horde, there shouldn't be a distinction.
