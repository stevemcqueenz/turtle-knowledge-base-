That's a cool idea, but does it really make sense ?

The representation of the hunter in World of Warcraft is more that of a ranger, an archer, a survivalist who sets traps and hunts in the jungle or forest.
I doubt that this kind of profile would be particularly effective in the water.

On top of that, their connection with the wild is mostly tied to their hunting environment, and the closest thing to a marine animal as a pet would be something like a turtle or a crab…

They can also take on the role of a marksman of course, but underwater that seems pretty ambitious to me.

Maybe it's just me, but I'm not buying it.

Name can be adjusted to be more appropriate. For example, I don't think Dwaves naturally see any cheetahs in their environment, and I'm not sure if they even exist in warcraft lore, but is a reference to the general speed increase. I used "Seal" just as an example, and since it's also a mammal, but "Shark" may be the most appropriate, since they exist in Warcraft, and are close to pretty much every starting area or capital in the game. So surely every hunter would be knowledgeable of the creature.

You also mentioned survival, and swimming is absolutely a survival skill. In lakes and ponds, a hunter may use a fishing rod, but at sea(accessible by every race), you may want to swim deeper and use a harpoon to catch your pray. As such, a stance most appropriate for swimming will be necessary, but your focus on form opens you to a dazing attack.

At the end of the day, your trainer is the one teaching you the aspect. So even in the off-chance that your character hasn't been to the sea once by level 20-30(depending on when the ability is set to be learned), your trainer is the one coaching you on how to utilize this aspect
