# A dive into Multiboxing

## Post 14510 by Overtyped — 2021-12-11T20:31:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=14510#p14510 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I've recently come across a certain person playing a full party, and that person took the initiative to ensure to me that he isn't using any outside programs. I believed him and still do, but that doesn't mean it doesn't bother me.
It was quite impressive, he was able to seamlessly heal, use warrior abilities, mage abilities, without any break, and I want to explain why it's easy to do.

I'm going to now use autohotkey as a placeholder, because i don't literally want to make a real guide on how to multibox, because I am against it. Autohotkey is much easier to detect, but the tools that multiboxers use can be undetectable, because these programs hide under the cover of system processes, completely indistinguishable.

So using "AutoHotkey" You can make 1 script that targets each wow window, and when you press "1" it will press "1" in all windows, this technically is not injecting, so I imagine it's very difficult to prove they are doing this, and you'll see why soon.

I want to explain what Turtle Wow can, and likely can't see. If Turtle wow has the anti cheat tools, it's easy to detect injectors(These are programs that directly communicate with the games code) and then there are anti cheat tools that are morally obscure, but can look at current running windows(This doesn't include everything, and can't unless they want a lawsuit, and is why anti-cheat is basically worthless to stop anything. EX: anti cheat is such a joke, that even changing the name of something like "autohotkey" to a common system process is enough, and that's because they can't prove it's AutoHotkey without breaking the law) but programs like "autohotkey" aren't even needed.

Without using any special program, it's possible to bind keys to do multiple things. In this event, the only thing turtle wow can detect, is how many keys are being pressed, and when, but even this is easy to resolve, you simply set human-like delay timers on the keys, for example, you press "B" and then set the parameters for when the key will be pressed. You can change delay timers to be within a range as well, and in doing so, you can completely avoid any pattern.

EX: you press 1: then, within 100ms-1500ms(Or whatever you set it to) it would then press a key in each window based on this delay, how would turtle wow be able to detect a pattern here when there isn't one? They wouldn't be able to, and that's why multi boxing is so hard to prove.

I am someone who has done this in the past, so I can tell you from experience, you can absolutely stay within any rule, while still being able to multi-box effectively.

But... let's say Turtle Wow can detect things like "autohotkey", (which I doubt they can, and ill explain why at the end) Then--

You can use bindings instead.
If you bind "1" to wow tab 1, bind "2" to wow tab "2" and so on, then by pressing 1, you can also make that character in wow 1 use the abilities in bar 1, such as frostbolt, or whatever. For healers it's a little different, all you have to do is use a ingame mouseover macro for whatever window he's bound to. All perfectly legal, because it's 1 key per 1 ((("""INGAME"""))) press.

You might think that it would be difficult to constantly press all 5 keys to keep all the frostbolts and whatnot going, but no it isn't, the mouse also counts as a key, by binding "mousewheel up" to one dps, and "mousewheel down" to another, you just roll it up and down, and easily keep 100% frostbolt/shadowbolt uptime, no brain power or autohotkey required.

All you would have to do after that, is minimize all clients except your leading character's and keep them tiny, so the constant ping-ponging between characters wouldn't be jarring, and now, with simply 5 buttons, you are able to solo all dungeons in the game with no major downsides.

Of course, this has limits, multiboxing 5 characters in this way is very very easy, but multiboxing anymore would be strenuous, unless you can grow more hands.

Still, i want to say this is all conjecture, if turtle wow was able to see "autohotkey", which is a program not directly interacting with the servers, and renamed as a normal computer process, then it would also mean they can see and directly open every program, and every file/folder/game/credit card number, on your computer, which they likely can't, I'm just showing that it's as easy as breathing to get around ((""ANY"")) possible method.

Even if something is technically within the rules, should people have this kind of advantage, is this the vanilla spirit? It's absolutely possible for this person to do dungeons at 60 on his own, and farm gold way faster than any other person could, but just because it's within the rules, should it really be allowed? Who needs those gross inefficient friends, when you can literally spawn your own friends?

When I go into the world, it's a bit jarring to see a group of people following one, while nuking everything in their way without issue. I would like to ask others if they really like seeing this out in the world, or competing with it for mobs. What kind of effect would they have on the economy when something like righteous orb farming is now technically soloable by 1 person controlling many? LFG is out of the equation, they are the group, and don't need you.
