**Azeroth Treasury Bonds**

 **Core Concept:**
Azeroth’s National Bank issues Treasury Bonds, a voluntary system where players temporarily or permanently remove gold from circulation. This mirrors real-world central bank operations: gold is “parked” or destroyed, stabilizing the money supply and slowing inflation. The appeal is economic impact, prestige, and subtle recognition — not game-breaking new rewards.

 **Bond Types:**
 - *Short-Term Bonds (Soft Sink):* Gold locked for 30 days, reducing circulation during high-inflation periods. Options include:
 - *1% Treasury Bond:* Pay 100G, get 101G at maturity. Safe, guaranteed profit for cautious players like Tharok who want passive gains without risk.
 - *Venture Co. Risky Bond:* Pay 100G, 80% chance of 105G, 15% chance of 80G, 5% chance of 0G. Appeals to gamblers who chase high rewards.
 - *Variable-Rate Bond:* Pay 100G, return (100–103G) adjusts based on AH price index. Savvy players buy when rates are high (e.g., 1.2% for 101.2G) to maximize gains.

 **Mechanics:**
 - *Denominations:* Bonds sold in 100G, 500G, or 1000G amounts, catering to casuals and whales.
 - *Maturity Dates:* Short-term bonds lock gold for 30 days; long-term bonds are permanent. No early withdrawals, ensuring reliable sinks.
 - *Issuance Cycles:* New bonds released weekly, with limited quantities (e.g., 5000 bonds per cycle) to drive demand and prevent over-hoarding.

 **Dynamic Tuning:**
 - Rates adjust via AH Price Index (tracking flasks, herbs, ores). High inflation (e.g., +5% AH prices) → more bonds offered, variable rates drop (e.g., 0.6%) to keep gold circulating. Low inflation (e.g., -2%) → fewer bonds, higher rates (e.g., 1.2%) to lock gold.
 - Risky bond default odds may tighten during inflation spikes (e.g., 7% chance of 0G) for stronger sinks.
 - Devs tune issuance and rates like a central bank, balancing gold supply to curb inflation without crashing the market.

 **Outcome:**
Azeroth Treasury Bonds become the cornerstone of gold stabilization: a trusted, repeatable way to soak up excess liquidity, protect quest reward value, and preserve the Classic economy — all while staying fully in-theme with the original game.

I have to separate these questions into segment if you will. So here goes.

> What is inflation in WoW?

Every quest you complete and gives you reward in term of item or gold, is inflation, every item, you have not bought on auction house, but received from a dead NPC and sold at a vendor is inflation. (You have to destroy the item, meaning selling it to a vendor to generate G's out of thin air). If a large amount of players are questing and killing mobs, the amount of money introduced into the economy grows, when these players need repairs and post items at auction houses that's when the money is getting destroyed, therefore called gold sink, or deflation.

> What is circulation?

Amount of golds being exchanged directly between players, be it via auction house or direct trade. If I have 100G, and I buy some bracers from Leatherworker, who uses this Gold from sale to buy rugged leather from a skin farmer, who then uses this gold to buy crusader enchant from enchanter, for his new weapon that he just recieved from a BWL run yesterday, so he is able to kill beasts quicker to farm more rugged leather, that 100G UNTIL SPENT at vendor for some item (therefore destroying that gold at a vendor, for something non-tradeable) is in *circulation*.
