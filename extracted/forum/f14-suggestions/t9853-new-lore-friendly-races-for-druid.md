With the addition of Gilneas, I believe that human druids are a reasonable addition to the roster. Gilneas has always had deep roots in druidism with many harvest witches once inhabiting the area, and with Gilneas in ruins not only will a much higher influx of Gilnean refugees be expected to flock to the Alliance, but as well the desperate refugees will likely need to turn to their ancient ways to survive the trek to safe Alliance lands, akin to how many Draenei turned to shamanism out of necessity as they became broken. Unorthodox, yes, but with the Scourge and the Forsaken both being prevalent in the area of Silverpine, it's certainly a possibility that some Gilneans succumbed and were eventually freed from the Lich King's grasp, and nature magic, unlike the Light, doesn't necessarily reject Undeath.

A lot of people are throwing shit at you, but I'll respectfully tell you the basics of Druidism here in WoW and why none of the things you're talking about are allowed:

Druidism is a custom unique to the Night Elves, or so it was until WoW's release, when they made the Tauren Druids just to balance the scales.

 1. Humans are a civilized race, built on faith in the Light. They can't be druids because simply... where did they come from?
 2. Humans originally practiced a very archaic form of shamanism and magic, which was further enhanced by the teachings of the High Elves in the war against the Amani. From there, the arcane side of humanity emerged. By now, humans probably don't even know they were once shamans, and they have no need to become one again, being able to be mages, paladins, or priests. 3. Trolls are only shamans, the faith of trolls is based on the Loa, they have no way to honor Mother Earth or Elune, which are the foundations of the druids, moreover, there is no race of troll that is known to have been druids, since they are always based on: Magic, Daemonism or Shamanism.

Finally, and quite separately, I'm a bit lacking in the lore of the Worgen, but I can tell you that here they are not druidic creatures or anything like that. They are creatures that were summoned from another dimension by the Archmage Arugal using the Scythe of Elune, and for the most part, I've seen that they are warlocks.

I read into it myself a little, but when it comes to the WarCraft RTS Games, the WarCraft RPG and then to WoW, things might become a little complicated:

We have a standardized version of the Druid here in WoW, but when you read through the different druid variants, you might quickly notice, that druidism (according to the RPG) doesn't always comes down to shapeshifting. But the shapeshifting is a big part of what the WoW-druid is.
From what i learn there and when we draw parallels to the Wildhammers, it seems like we have the variant of a "Lone Druid", which is more like a D&D Druid who talks to animals and have animals as companions (in Wildhammer case: Gryphons). When we break it down to the dwarves as playable race, we could fit those descriptions into the Hunter class from the game.

It's rather tricky to get lore and game mechanics aligned to each other.
(I made a statement about priests somewhere in the forum where i mentioned, that we have a pretty standardized form of the priest here on Turtle and that the priest actually "should" be something different, depending on the race/faith.)

So from my understanding the Wildhammers "Druidism" is a very basic one that wouldn't align with the druid we have in the game. I would even go so far to say, that Nightelves and Wildhammer Dwarves might have a different concept of druidism(?).
Regarding shamanism i wasn't able to find much, but from what i got this far, i assume that the Wildhammer concept of "shamanism" wouldn't align much with what we have in the game either.
