Retail is generally used to refer to the current version of WoW hosted by Blizzard (ie: Dragonflight), but I believe the relevant change in this case started in Legion and has stuck around since then.  Basically another way to refer to Modern WoW.

It might be difficult to understand the perspective if you're not familiar with Retail, but the basic idea is this:  Post-Legion, if I'm fighting a mob and someone else walks up and finishes it off, we would both get credit for the kill (we're effectively auto-grouped for that mob).  This might sound harmless enough, but over time it turned Retail leveling into a free-for-all:  Players began commonly butting into others' fights to finish up their quests as quickly as possible, and the only way to avoid unwanted 'help' while soloing was to avoid other players entirely.

I don't expect you to agree with me that this is a problem, but I think it comes down to different playstyles.  Some folks want all the bonuses they can get in order to minimize downtime and get to max level ASAP.  Others would rather take their time, complete all available quests, maybe turn on some challenge modes and generally smell the roses along the way (frequently Solo).  This suggestion would basically be forcing a Retail style of play on everyone, and there's a lot of us who specifically play Classic to avoid that.

One final thing:  It might be easier to think of Solo players as playing their own personal "Solo Challenge" (similar to what the "Hardcore Challenge" started as, before it was coded into the game).  I don't mind tossing some heals or grabbing adds if someone asks for help with an elite quest, and I'm happy to wait my turn if someone else is already waiting for a spawn, but I'm always going to try something solo first before grouping for it.  Also, hard pass on the random no-whisper group invites (rude, IMO).
