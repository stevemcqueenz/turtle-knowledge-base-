# Novelty vs Nostalgia

## Post 5139 by Gifted (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2020-02-27T15:54:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=5139#p5139 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

What I like most about Turtle WoW is how the changes the server has made in the past was mostly to add new content (novelty) to the existing game world (nostalgia). Things like the survival skill, the raceway, and custom world bosses really make Turtle WoW a unique and novel experience while still staying true to the nostalgic experience of vanilla. I'd like to encourage this trend to continue.

Lately I'm seeing a lot of suggestions about changing the actual game world (nostalgia) rather than adding or opening access to new content (novelty). I'd really like to discourage this. Things like giving classes new abilities that affect gameplay balancing. Things like changing drop rates and making BoP items into BoE items.

Here's a few reasons I don't agree with the novelty over nostalgia approach:

- Balancing Issues - Blizzard with its vast resources struggled to balance the game. I can't imagine how hard it would be with Turtle Wow's resources.
- Nostalgia Issues - I like the add on content approach because it can be safely ignored if players want to stick with the original game content. Changing gameplay mechanics with new abilities or altering class balances can't be ignored. Part of the nostalgic experience was the fact that the game isn't perfectly balanced.
- Recruitment Issues - I think this is the most important factor to consider. I've tried some of the heavily customized private wow servers (like the 'classless' server) and been intrigued at first, but ultimately left once the novelty wore off (sometimes just a day or two later).
I'd much rather see the Sands of Time instance open up than a lot of the suggestions I'm seeing that sacrifice nostalgia for novelty. It would be an **AMAZING** recruitment tool to keep growing the server population. And I think it stays in-line with Turtle WoW's current balance of novel new content to explore that doesn't alter or take away from the nostalgic vanilla wow experience!

> **Gifted wrote: Thu Feb 27, 2020 3:54 pm**
> - Balancing Issues - Blizzard with its vast resources struggled to balance the game. I can't imagine how hard it would be with Turtle Wow's resources.

The thing about this is that even with the content added, it was not as though there were no existing material to draw both inspiration and resources from. We have some very good and very bad changes of later expansions which I believe we could cherry-pick to improve the overall experience of this game, especially aligning with the goal of a slower and immersive experience in the game world. No player should feel the need to reach max level to enjoy a particular style of gameplay, nor to feel viable.

I strongly agree we should maintain the "nostalgia" however, and I do not think these improvements ought to be done at the expense of the feel or challenge Vanilla poses over later expansions. I don't like the Quality of Life content which overall makes the game feel like it itself is breaking the fourth wall and encourages players to skip through the game. However, I think Vanilla lacks some of the rounding out of classes and playstyles of later expansion. I strongly believe the warrior leveling experience would benefit from merely the Rend talent change in TBC of a 70% improvement at 3/3 compared to Vanilla's 35%. This is not even factoring in the changes in WoTLK to Rend mechanics incorporating weapon speed, and the base damage being nearly doubled without the talent. I am continuing to read up on changes introduced in other expansions to inform my opinions on this.

I do not believe changes should be done at the expense of "nostalgia" or to remove the immersion and challenge of the game world.
