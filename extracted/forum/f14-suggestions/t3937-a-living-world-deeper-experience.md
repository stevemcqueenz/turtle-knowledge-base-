# A Living World, Deeper Experience

## Post 23829 by Sapiverenus (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2022-08-28T15:02:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=23829#p23829 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

This is a long one.

Players should be necessary to stop certain factions/ forces from growing stronger or to stop the opposing faction's NPC groups in the World. Such as an easy enough open world quest for a level 15 in Silverpine. . . . slowly growing into a 16x level 15 or 4 man level 35 or solo level 50, 20 minute mission in Silverpine.

Raid bosses the cause of invasions and catastrophe in the world that progress. . . yes, enemy factions can WIN and the playerbase as a whole LOSE. A FAILSTATE to the game!

And more consequential player Deaths! NPC deaths! Scripting and scripting.

 **EDIT**: *I like the Chromiecraft idea of capped leveling, slowly progressing the cap. 10 levels every 3 weeks; reset every 6 months with your character locked until the server cap progresses once more to allow for it [hence you make a new character].*
Most quests should require grouping. But not all.
Gear should be mostly crafted and have accurate visuals, NO transmog.
Random enchanted gear shouldn't be dropping for the most part; especially not from a boar or bat.
Healing should be severely nerfed, including bandage.
Threat should depend on what a mob finds threatening, such as a tanky mega damage dealer being threatened by the healer keeping the party alive. Or simply hating Warlocks because they're evil, suspicious, and painful.
CC should generate good threat.

Exhaustion. . . sit at the fire and eat&drink a shit ton (depending on level). Or suffer reduced XP gain (down to 75% my first proposition), reduced regens, and inverse haste. . . After resting & downing nutrients, get 125% Rested XP rate for a certain amount of XP and a slight improvement to regen and haste. Taking a lot of damage, using a lot of mana or energy or rage will all increase the rate of exhaustion. . .
Stamina reduces Exhaustion rate of gain and increases Rested XP max and health regen max, Spirit removes Exhaustion and adds Rested quickly while resting and improves mana regen max.

Creatures should spawn slowly, spawn a bit more from more secluded areas into less secluded areas, avoid settlements a bit more due to hunting, run from players when hunted often, and drop a bit more of their body parts.
Hunters should be particularly good at hunting by using preparation and/or ranged abilities and tracking them down long-term; otherwise creatures should be too quick and their 'aggro range' [the point at which they notice you] much too great. Some creatures should simply be too difficult to kill, such as Rares/ Elites. Some creatures will charge right at you.

XP rates in general should be .5x or less, **using abilities and combat itself should generate some XP, kill XP should be much lower or not even a thing for half of the classes, much less XP from simply turning in a quest, and XP gain a bit different for each class. MODIFIED but not strictly 'realistic'.**

World progression. . . . Scripted world progression With a 1.25 - 2.25x boost to XP gain for however many XP bars or levels after it's done. These should generally be infrequent or extremely limited. **Most leveling should be regular leveling.**

Content difficult enough you can't just grind to the rind. . increased aggro range and modified chase time depending on mob; and much greater pull radius. Some mobs simply retreating to get friends. . . some running away because they're wild animals and don't want to confront you. Some mobs patrolling in groups after someone made an attempt to take them out. . . or ambushes on you the player or whoever they can find (sometimes waiting a while to move out, or going right away).

A version of stealth for everyone; which amounts to moving slowly, disabling attack on activation, disabling nameplate, and removing tooltip so there's no indication you are hovering or clicking on an NPC or player. With 'camouflage' which amounts to some color change with a random pattern or noise built in to own some bots and get a jump on players. Some should be too advanced at this point but there's a lot of lazy noob bot makers.

 **Money and items** should be more difficult to come by, and random vendors should not have infinite money. If a player sells something useful to the vendor it should just be up for sale if they have no reason to hold onto it. . . not all vendors should price their goods the same either [there's good reason for NPCs to be crooks or saints, but generally not to an extreme].
This ties well into professions, XP loss on death, gear loss on death, slightly lower loot power, a bit more sensible and important crafting, long gathering times for mining and higher quantity requirements for alchemy, and the simple concept of having to soulbind or remove soulbinds from valuable gear.

 *Removing things just to add several other things that contribute to the vision of an online World. Without needing to reach end-game. . . as the design goal. Just fun to play, and every level of player useful and relevant to level 60 players.*

 *Part of that is by having less of a gap between level 60 and level 15 power, and important world progression happening across Azeroth. Small problems can grow if someone doesn't keep it in check when it's a weak problem. Not the same thing daily, but weekly or whatever is appropriate; and actual world progression and open world scripting. . . more dynamic problems such as elusive bandits.*

Some professions should provide some XP -> Warrior Mining increases strength/stamina and melee tool/weapon use -->>> *hence they gain a tiny bit of XP.*
Mages enchanting/disenchanting get XP based on its difficulty just makes sense --> *but greens should be rare and there should be some way for enchanting to work without a previously existing enchanted item lol*.

[+10% more XP for dealing the killing blow, up to +10% more XP depending on how much damage you take before something dies, and a little little XP for simply taking damage if you've been dealing damage as a Warrior ];
[+14% XP from quests that benefit your faction or race or fits your spec, and up to +6% more XP from healing/boosting allies in combat before the mob dies as a **Paladin**];
[and in general a tiny infrequent amount of XP from simply using your abilities or doing class things like taking damage as a melee fighter. . . up to a limit of 100% of your XP from other sources such as quests, professions and kills but not over that amount.]
And up to 2/20 mini-bars of XP from using class abilities on a Training Dummy per level; and 4/20 mini-bars of XP from Dueling. Training Dummy slow af though.

Different craftable Training Dummies that will lose durability and break; heavy solid training dummy for heavy weapon, light dummy for light weapons; and some weapons simply destroy most materials. If the material is too tough then it loses durability and hence Damage too quickly though. Most gear not very repairable but can be recycled; hence use a training weapon.

Mobs and players affected by and affect weapon types differently. Stone Golem requires a Blunt weapon. Vine Lashers require a Sharp weapon. Blades chip against stone golems. Goops might slow down your swing when using a mace or hammer. Crits and Rogues in particular bypass armor through openings and weak points often creating bleeds; Maces and Hammers can deal Crushing Blows that can bypass armor and stun. Bit of the usual but with more interaction. Cutting is flat armor reduction, Blunt is % armor reduction. Keep the rogue class fantasy alive though. . . some sort of poison for elementals, a big hammer and crit 3x in a row against the giant stone golem's ankle 'bone' or over their body's 'leyline'.

 *Not hyperrealistic*, it's just playing your class. The pace of the game should be slower overall but fuller and more interesting.
 *Following the idea of the World being the Main Character, and You being a small part of it:*
A daily repeatable hunting, growing, or fishing 'quest' for some measely money and reputation to feed the people of your faction would help merge RPG and Progression.
It should be repeatable up to the point where the server's players have provided too much food. . . thus a daily server-wide limit and an in-game locale limit. Not every shanty needs 1000 lbs of meat 'daily'. The existing crop growing without the Un'goro requirement would be used in this, or for alchemy quests [not that anyone knows how to grow such herbs].

A weekly repeatable blacksmithing or leathermaking 'quest' written separately and depending on the area for some measely money and reputation to help troops, guard, or anyone at all. Sometimes a person just needs a set of leatherarmor. Up to a low local, server-wide, NPC limit. Et cetera. Each profession has its use to the world.
If content is more difficult thus requiring: group and/or lone skill and/or preparations; it will make for a more engaging and social experience given it's balanced right with content where it should be. There's nothing immersive or triumphant about abusing the blind, deaf, and dumb mob AI as it is. It's a weak point of the game. A big reason people like PvP is the greater complexity of combat and more substantial person-to-person interaction.

 *What's immersive: Struggling to grow or streamlining to the finish?*

Most players don't learn how to play optimally or use every ability even if they'd get a lot of use out of them, and so they cause problems for dungeon & raid groups. . . . hence they may not even dungeon or raid.
Anyway, If dungeon and raid content is to get more interesting, reactive, and intelligent/dynamic mechanics/AI then the playerbase need to learn how to work in a group and use their class. Having others tell them to "use your damn X!!" after the second wipe is better than a singleplayer leveling experience and tents when it's an MMORPG and suppose to be a Big Epic Fantasy World.

I definitely don't want buffs for classes though. More of a lean into their class identity, yes.

Some content should be both necessary, difficult, and necessary to progress or keep hell from breaking loose. Imagine unchecked forces leading to roaming parties hunting players and NPCs of your Faction or both Factions down. . .

Experience and some or complete Gear Loss on death [bank some crafted gear or drops] would make this type of stuff more impactful and exciting to overcome, but I think that requires a bit of nuance. 5% XP loss and delevel is not consequential with current XP gains, and losing easily replaceable gear (such as with skinning and leatherworking) isn't a big deal right now. With the suggested mechanics though. . . 5% XP loss and delevel, and loss of items on death (though reclaimable, or perhaps they are stolen. . .) is impactful.
So add a soulbind enchant to the game! For seriously good gear.
And add a tracking gem you can hide well in your armor if a rogue helps in trade chat; but requires a twin tracking gem to use! Call it "Twin Affinity Gem" or "Twin Affinity Enchant"; the latter applicable to a separate item.

And items can be retrieved from one's body or be 'recovered' and held by an NPC in some settlement if they move through an area such as through a scripted mission/ raid on an unchecked quest location; or when hunting in the plains! If they care to take it that is. If it's soulbound, they may just drag your body back to camp!

Last idea: Players help gear NPCs. Most places should have OK mediocre gear though. Maybe no shoulders and bracers though. . . and these NPCs will be scripted to skirmish a little for zone control. Gear requires repair though. . .
With good local reputation or proof of Rank or of participation in something reputable, you can probably get some items cheap if you die (up to a weekly limit).

If **Rogues** are not Sketchy Cheaters and Underworld Leaders, *a crisis*.
If ****Paladins**** are not Crusading and Civilization Leaders, *a crisis*.
If **Mages** are not Arcane enchanting Thermostats and Intellectual Leaders, *a crisis.*

Undead Orc Tauren Goblin Troll
Human Gnome Dwarf Night Elf High Elf

If they're not acting like themself, carrying out tasks in whatever part of the world they exist in, then things should get worse. Hell. . . there should be a FAIL STATE FOR A WORLD; basically instead of Instances and static questing, stuff like the Scourge can literally WIN.
IMAGINE THAT.

Hell, many fail states should exist. That would be some serious shit.

Thinking about it, a lack of Warriors should lead to the most severe of Crisis. But they need resources, utilities, transport, et cetera. PvE and PvP content should be Warrior content. If Warriors aren't taking care of threats then "the darkness consume us all".

Thus the game could be a dynamic between multiple war fronts and a fragile civilization trying to support its troops and the logistics involved with doing that. Or it all goes boom.
If all goes to shit the world could be impossible to level in and NO MORE RESPAWNS. Imagine a server going cold after 4.5 months. . . gotta wait till July! ha. Imagine actually needing the resources from Warsong Gulch. Needing to fight to the death for it. Slowly getting necessary resources after winning.

Some minor others major; 'content' should require the assistance of particular classes. They have a specialty.
Professions should require the contribution of certain classes.
The world requires some upkeep from locals, and sometimes that means 30 minutes invested in one location or simply being around to help when surprise attacks or events unfold. Micro and macro scale.
Chores aren't fun but with slower power creep and lower power ceiling, it's simply a dynamic world that some random level 60 can't trivialize.

 *Scripting shouldn't generate a bunch of events or chores based on what classes are in the area or server population. The content should be fixed but well designed; when areas fail, things fall to more defensible positions and leadership begins to become more active. Quests become narrower but interconnected (as in a war or disaster). There should be a Tug o War element to it all.*

By 'scripting should be fixed' I don't mean NPCs can't see an opportunity for a group to complete something in the area and thus prompt them for aid. I mean that the 'power creep' of the ENEMY should make coherent sense.

 **With leveling capped every 10 levels for 3 weeks at a time, harsh death consequences make sense. More people are grouping and more people are having to play smart, learn the game, explore, improve, et cetera to overcome adversity.**
