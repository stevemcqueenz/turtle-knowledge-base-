# PLEASE DEVS, DO NOT FALL INTO THE TRAP OF PVP GEAR.

## Post 148558 by Mactnite — 2025-08-27T03:39:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=148558#p148558 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Please avoid making PvP gear more powerful or introducing new sets. It's a poor solution informed by the skinner-box mindset that has consumed the entire MMORPG genre. PvP gear enforces a grind in arenas and battlegrounds to become competitive, rather than rewarding skill expression.

For a competitive and enduring PvP system, you should consider the Guild Wars 2 model. They have battlegrounds and arenas where, upon joining, everyone's level, stats, and gear are normalized. Everyone competes on a level playing field. This is the most competitive and inclusive way to implement BGs and Arenas. It also makes balancing classes easier for the team, as they won't have to account for gear disparities.

World PvP would also benefit, as raid gear would remain the best gear. This keeps world PvP accessible, allowing players to be competitive without being forced to grind arenas or BGs. It remains an asymmetrical form of combat where power disparity from PvE gear can be expressed, without being unbalanced due to who grinded PvP and who did not.

Putting PvP gear behind a grind only makes it less interesting and less fun for those who don't want to live in BGs and arenas, but might like to participate occasionally or engage in emergent world PvP.

Please. Normalize levels and gear for arenas and battlegrounds. It is a better solution. Anyone can try it and play it this way, and it will be far easier to balance classes for PvP, which will also improve world PvP. Provide cosmetics and titles as rewards for grinding. NEVER create a separate PvP gear system.

You developers have done so much to reject major retail changes that undermine the classic experience. Please consider that you can also reject the retail gear model here and try something different, a proven concept, to solve PvP gear disparity problems.
____________________________

My idea is as follows:

Any character level 10 or higher can queue for arenas and battlegrounds.
 *Upon entering a match, all players are boosted to level 60.
 *All players are given standardized gear, stats, and a full set of max-rank skills and spells.
 *This includes a standard set of consumables (potions, food, drink, etc.).
 *A player's chosen transmog/visual overrides will appear on the standardized gear.

Inside the preparation area, several NPCs will be available:
 *A Trainer NPC to reset talents for free. These changes are not permanent and will revert when leaving the instance.
 *A Trinket NPC who provides PvP trinkets for free.
 *A Weapon Master NPC and a Ranged Weapon NPC who offer all applicable weapons for free. Players can take as many as they need.

Players are given a set amount of pre-game time to equip gear, set their talents, and prepare.

In arenas or other round-based formats, players can reset their talents and gear between rounds as needed.

With gear and stats normalized, cross-server matchmaking becomes perfectly viable and balanced.

Alright, I've read through all the responses. I appreciate the discussion, even the disagreements. This is a complex issue and it's clear people have strong feelings. Let me try to address the main points that have been raised.

First, to those like Crake and others who brought up the crucial distinction between normalization and homogenization: you're absolutely right. This is a key detail I should have been clearer on. The goal isn't to make every Warrior or Priest an identical stat block. The idea is to create a level playing field where skill and build choices matter, not gear. A good system would allow for stat distribution choice, maybe through a PvP interface or different sets of normalized gear you can choose from. You could build for max stamina, crit, spell power, or healing. The point is to remove the power grind, not the ability to customize your character for PvP.

Now, to the folks like Idontcareatall and Cheruscan who say things like "PvPers just want to stomp noobs" or "go play a MOBA." This is the kind of black-pilled, defeatist attitude that has gotten us into this mess. I'm not interested in catering to people who quit the second the game becomes fair. I'm interested in building a system that encourages more people to actually try PvP and stick with it. The current system isn't some sacred, hardcore paradise; it's a barren wasteland dominated by a small group of geared players farming everyone else. That's not a community; that's a predator-prey simulation. And telling me to "go play Guild Wars 2" is missing the point entirely, I want to play World of Warcraft, I just want its PvP to be healthy and not a dysfunctional afterthought.

To Zvyrhol and the argument about new players being "overwhelmed" by having all their abilities at once in a BG... come on. This is a 20-year-old game. The classes are not that complex. We're not asking a new player to theorycraft from scratch; the game could easily provide a simple, pre-made "recommended" talent template and gear set they can use with one click. The current system, where a new player has to somehow grind against unbeatable opponents for dozens of hours just to start having a fair fight, is orders of magnitude more overwhelming and discouraging. My system lets them jump in, learn by doing, and actually have fun from their very first match.

Alcilis, I hear you on the "Vanilla spirit" and the time investment of PvP. But "Vanilla+" means being willing to improve on the original, not being chained to its every design flaw, especially ones that have proven to be catastrophic failures. And let's be real, as Manletow and Crake pointed out, nobody enjoys the grind. They tolerate it for the reward. My system doesn't remove the reward; it changes it from a statistical advantage that ruins the game for others to prestige; titles, cosmetics, mounts, things that show off your dedication without breaking the game.

Finally, to the suggestions about just tweaking numbers or adding a stat like Resilience (Untakenusername, Hammerfall): we've seen this movie before. It doesn't work. It doesn't solve the grind; it just moves it. It creates a separate PvP gear grind, splitting the player base and ensuring you still have to farm for weeks before you can compete. It's a band-aid on a bullet wound.

The current model has failed for 15 years. It's time to try something new. A normalized system isn't about copying other games; it's about learning from what actually works to create a healthy, competitive environment within WoW. Let world PvP be the wild west where gear and time investment rule. Let instanced Battlegrounds and Arenas be the balanced sport. This is the compromise that actually gives both crowds what they want.
