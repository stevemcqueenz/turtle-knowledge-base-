# About crafting revival

## Post 173390 by Alexianon — 2026-03-31T11:20:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=173390#p173390 | page 1 | era: post-1.18.1 -->

Dear Community,

In a [previous discussion](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?t=24573), I proposed replacing BoP with BoE to bring back the rationale for trading. But even if all items become BoE, crafting will remain "second-class"—items are inherently weaker than raid items, and their production is, in most cases, unprofitable for the artisan.

My idea: change the crafting principle itself.

Instead of fixed stats, crafted items should have ranges of stat values and, for example, a chance for an additional magical property. This would create a natural economy where price is determined by rarity and quality, not artificial restrictions.

What this means:

- Crafting ceases to be unprofitable. One successful "benefit" pays for the costs of several unsuccessful ones.
- Long-term demand emerges. Even a raider might want to improve their equipment through crafting.
- Crafting becomes an alternative to raiding, not a "service."
- Combined with BoE, a market for unique items is created, where price is determined by rarity and quality, not artificial restrictions.
The transition from deterministic to probabilistic crafting is one of the most promising ways to revive craft professions.

Sincerely

A healthy, varied economy with multiple ways to progress is, in my experience, a better defense against RMT than rigid restrictions.

I want to explain why, in my view, improving crafting would actually reduce RMT rather than encourage it. Let me use my own experience as an example.

I'm a blacksmith and jeweler. I rarely run dungeons — I focus on crafting and quests. Every day, I buy 500–1000 gold worth of materials and turn them into finished goods for the auction house.

Take the Hateforge Cuirass. Last week, I noticed the price was stuck at 55–60 gold, with almost no sales. I started a price war and gradually brought it down to 30–33 gold. Now I sell 1–2 cuirasses every day. My profit didn't suffer — it just shifted from high margin + low volume to low margin + high volume. More importantly, the item is now accessible to players who couldn't afford it before.

So ask yourself: does lowering prices encourage RMT? I'd argue the opposite. When gear is affordable through normal gameplay, players have less reason to buy gold.

Now imagine the reverse scenario. If I wanted to, I could buy up a lot of the consumables before raid nights and drive prices through the roof. Would that discourage RMT? Of course not. Inflated prices push players toward illegal options — exactly what happens now under the current system.

My point is simple: a healthy, competitive market with multiple players trading and crafting naturally regulates itself. When crafters can compete, prices drop, gear becomes accessible, and the incentive to buy gold decreases. Artificial restrictions don't stop RMT
