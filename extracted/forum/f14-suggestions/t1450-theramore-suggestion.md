As with many other settlements in WoW we have now is one general problem; they are all not literal but mostly symbolic, just like as most maps are done for the game.

I believe that the whole world scale is way too much off and looks like it's heavily downscaled. Why? Well, it's kind of a complicated matter here since computers back then were much less productive and Blizz, as they were and still are, had to appeal to the majority of the potential player base.

Therefore, most settlements are crudely represented as small villages at most, cities are much more detailed, yet still lack way too many buildings and limited to the brim. Only the most necessary stuff, nothing extra.

Of course, if we'd have a much more modern version of the game with fewer restrictions and limits then we could expand the world and fill it with much more details and perhaps even improve it according to the game's lore. Alas, as wide (some might think) WoW we have now, there's simply not enough space and areas would be way too crowded (npcs and buildings) for players to navigate.

Another proof of the downscaled theory is that with each patch Blizz shipped they made more and more wide areas with it and always added more and more flight points to the game. Of course, that's part of their marketing idea and possible way to control and ensure the income from each expansion… Business … nothing else, baby.

## Post 8682 by Roxanneflowers (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2020-12-12T20:26:00Z

The one thing about Theramore that never really made sense to me is that it is so sadly lacking as an actual fortification.  Yes, there are high walls and towers with cannons on them ... but there are no gates that can be closed and barred.  There's just open gaps in the walls where an enemy force could rush in all too easily using force of numbers.

The gap in the wall on the landward side has no closing gate that I can see, not even a portcullis, and the bridge from the land to the island of the "fortress" is made of stone, so you couldn't destroy it by fire (although with Jaina Proudmore in residence, she ought to be powerful enough to destroy it magically if need be).

Likewise on the seaward side, there's just this huge wide open gap in the walls from the docks, big enough to unload entire shiploads of enemy troops through for a bumrush into the outer bailey.

Even the central tower doesn't have any particular fortification defenses to it other than being on a rise with a ramp leading up to it.  There's no signs of a portcullis to drop down to prevent access to the interior.

I know that what the artists were going for was a Cannon Tower look for the place, but in terms of siege warfare, just looking at the place it's hard to believe that a large enough force would have much of a problem overrunning Theramore from either the landward or seaward approaches.  Yes, there's plenty of cannons around (and sailors who would know how to use them), but a rush of enough fighters would overwhelm those defenses in pretty short order just by exploiting the gaps in the walls.
