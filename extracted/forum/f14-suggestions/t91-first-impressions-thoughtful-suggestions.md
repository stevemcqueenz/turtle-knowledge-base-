# First impressions/Thoughtful suggestions

## Post 553 by Zest — 2018-10-07T21:37:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=553#p553 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I'm enjoying reliving my first MMO experience. And so far, I've had a great time with this server. Here's what I've found that poses a danger to the current model of Turtle WoW, because I'm worried there could be a large fall off of playership unless a few changes, both big and small, are implemented into the server design.

First off, the lower experience rates are wonderful for a few reasons, (which was the whole theory behind it to begin with to be sure): it discourages senselessly grinding out mobs, rushing content, ignoring the community, skipping ahead, etc. But there is a drawback to this as well. Unless there are helpful people around doing the same quest, or you are playing a Warlock or Hunter, many times quests would be otherwise impossible, because you are under leveled for the content. It's frustrating, because what do you do when A: grinding mobs is inefficient, laborious, defeats the purpose, and B: moving on to another beginning zone is very time consuming, complicated, and even dangerous to do. I know it's "Turtle WoW", but as you'll find (or have found already) while leveling, a certain kind of juggling has to take place. It goes like this -- as you acquire new quests, they send you along to more difficult content upon completion, which is great, except you have not advanced fast enough to meet the difficulty it demands. So either you form a group and do the quests you need to do and then disband, or you're forced to seek out another zone. So you leave zone A for zone B, return again to A to finish quests that are now orange/yellow rather than red, only to seek out zone C (if there even is such a thing) in short order. Now that C is complete, you can move back to B, so on and so forth, etc. This is awesome, because it requires a different play-style, but it can also be frustrating, because it only adds even more walking to the game, which can be tedious. While grey quests awarding experience might be slightly helpful, the mobs, nor the rewards offer any hope of upgrades, which are in constant demand. So, here are a few solutions I've thought of so far:

 1. First, a simple way of fast-traveling between the starting zones around Azeroth. Way-gates, portals (or "Tortals"), you get the picture. So Valley of Trials ---> Mulgore.

 2. Increase the limit of Hearthstone "Homes" from 1 to 3 for short durations. (Homes 2 and 3 will expire after a few hours, and Hearthing to one won't pop the CD on the others.)

 3. This is the most radical, and appealing solution, which I think could be quite a draw to many players across the board, because it would be quite the novelty on the private server scene. Allow all quests and dungeons, towns and zones, to be available cross faction. So Horde could acquire quests in Goldshire; Alliance in Kalimdor, etc. Wailing Caverns and Deadmines would be open to all. If someone selects "PvP", they will be attacked on site in these areas by NPCs, etc, as normal. But if friendly, they will go on their way unharmed. I especially like this idea, because I absolutely love some of the Alliance areas, such as Dun Morogh and Loch Modan, but simply don't have the time to roll on both factions. It would also make the world feel more populated, because you would see large numbers of opposing faction milling around in your turf. Which strategically, could be pretty cool. (Mutual combat can be activated at any time.)

 4. Lastly, BoE, and BoP equipment below a certain level could be account bound, so you may reuse gear you acquire on your main to use for your alts. Sort of like those "Heirloom" items that came into play in later patches. Would add entertainment value to the leveling process. Planning ahead, and recycling equipment. Since it will take so much time to reach 60 under the current format, it would make the trek slightly more forgiving, which might encourage players to return to lower level content more frequently. Given that there is a constant need to group because of the challenge faced due to a chronic lack of experience, such a mechanic couldn't hurt. This would also increase the value of certain lower level items that are BiS for some time, since they could be used both to level and to eventually twink. And should I be lucky enough for a Tigerstrike Mantle or Assassin's Blade to pop, I might consider utilizing them immediately rather than waiting for a high payout at a future date, because the item would be very useful for multiple play throughs. And just think of what you could do with a single bank where all your gear is stored, even across the faction barrier. So my retired mail and plate from my Orc warrior could go to a Dwarf paladin. This would also raise the premium on equipment found in dungeons that nobody in a group can utilize, because it could always be put to some useful end.

Misc. complaints and concerns that will probably go unfixed (ranting to the wind here, feel free to stop reading):

So far the scripting is phenomenal. But one thing I've noticed from my memory of vanilla retail, is that the mob density is slightly higher than it should be, and the re-spawn rate seems too quick. This is hard as a warrior who is under leveled to boot. Pull two mobs, you die. In a hard spot and try to flee, you should've escaped, but now three or four scorpions are sniping at your heels. Don't remember so many marauding mobs everywhere, but it's been over ten years, so who knows.

More to come. Back to the long scrabbling trek across the beach. My only question is: will there be water on the other side of this slightly slanted desert plain, or is it just more or the same?

Thanks for all the feedback guys. It seems like we have a split camp. I think part of the problem, of course, is that we're all here for different reasons. Some are more into the RP aspect, others into the simple joy of vanilla leveling. Keeping it blizz-like as possible is obviously very important to some. But I disagree that implementing some of the suggested changes could potentially alienate a large part of the population. Let's at least agree on one thing, our principle concern with playing this game is just that: playing. Enjoying the ride. But as this is an MMO, and an old one at that, we have to keep our world thriving. Part of the draw here, was the reduced rates, the theme, and the role-playing aspect. All of which, have been a smash hit, and it would seem that these changes (yes, changes) have achieved their intended aims. A less toxic, albeit small, but therefore much more mature and friendly community. Having to utilize all the areas is also a great consequence of the reduced rates, I am in complete agreement. But as with any private server, the looming danger is that one day the player base will immigrate elsewhere.

My main contention is this: a few quality of life enhancements could only keep people active, and draw others in. Playing across the lines of our respective allegiances simply doesn't break the lore. In fact, it is more in tune with the story behind the World of Warcraft. I always took issue with the Horde-Alliance dichotomy being a little too two-dimensional. Let's not forget that Arthas was once the Paladin Prince of Lordaeron before he became the Lich King. Grom Hellscream, sacrificed himself to thwart the very demonic forces that imbued his being with the preternatural ferocity that allowed him to conquer the Burning Legion's grasp over his people. Not to mention Illidan, Kael-thas, or Sylvanas. Thrall and Malfurion probably matched a few spliffs at some point. Idealistically, at least, they're very similarly oriented. Oh, Sargeras too. (The story of the fall is not unique to the bible apparently.)

On a more practical note, we are all time-restricted. And given the nature of the long walk ahead of us all, I've no qualm with spending the time, but I would be thrilled to know that my efforts would always be moving me forward in some way. I would like to start a Druid, but thematically, and for my own imaginative reasons, I would only be willing to play a Night Elf Druid. But unfortunately, I don't want to invest a lot of energy into a secondary character if it won't somehow benefit my main. So I will probably wind up playing as a Troll hunter or rogue, so I can take up alchemy to keep my warrior alive. If there was a cross-faction mechanic, I could either bank my equipment and supplies in a shared cache, or simply be able to level across all zones. Given the small, friendly, slow nature of our community, I don't see why this would be insulting enough to those enjoying themselves for them to actually leave. If you're uncomfortable killing your own kind, or moral ambiguity is simply not your cup of tea, then don't. But the Blackrock Orcs are not part of the Horde. Nor their dwarves part of the Alliance. Not all elves are friendly with another. It's just personal preference. PvP versus PvE. If you're not into risking your own hide to shed some of the enemy's blood, then don't. It's that simple. But if you're more of an Illidan, Arthas, Hellscream or Kael-thas, (think chaotic-neutral) small betrayals are meaningless smudges which are easily shadowed out by vengeance, power, and personal freedom of choice. I think such opportunities would bring many new characters into our world. More stories to cross paths with.

At the very least, merge the auction houses. Expand the quest-log from 20 entries to 30. Small quality of life stuff like that could make all the difference, and would carry very little risk of upsetting or alienating anybody.
