# Suggestion: Multiboxing Rules

## Post 40186 by Pureavatar — 2023-04-03T20:12:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=40186#p40186 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I know, I know, but I love multiboxing and I love Turtle WoW so hear me out. As far as I can understand, the issue with Multiboxing has to do with the economy and server stability. I'm desperate to come up with a better solution than the current MB'ing rules so here's my idea.

Glyph of Many Faces
Players who wish to multibox are given a unique usable glyph similar to warmode or turtle mode which they may use on a limited number of characters they control (10?) or it creates a copy which can be given to other characters who then use it.

Once glyphed these characters become part of a single glyph group with the following restrictions.

 1. Glyphed characters may only trade/sell/email other characters registered to the same glyph. They may not use the Auction House at all.

 2. Characters within the same glyph group may quest in the world in groups of two. May run instances in groups of five. No raids and no PvP.

 3. Only one character from a glyph group may join with players outside that glyph group at a time.

 4. In order to add a new character to the glyph there must be an empty space. If 10 characters are already registered then one would have to be permanently deleted to make room.

 5. 30 day time limit on how often characters may be deleted from a glyphed group.

 6. Player possibly can use turtle coins to purchase additional glyphs to create additional glyph groups. Not to expand the one they already control.

 7. No third party programs may be used to assist in the control of the characters. This is the same rule which is in place now although if the players can't send their resources to other players maybe it isn't needed.

The idea is to allow MB'ing players to interact, quest, and run dungeons with other players while also prevent them using multiboxing to dump tons of gold/resources/materials into the economy. It would also create a database for the devs to reference when investigating possible rule violators and, I would imagine, make enforcement a bit easier simply because anyone who is MB'ing without being part of a glyph group can be assumed to be violating server rules.

I'm sure it isn't perfect and there are probably things I missed but I have been chased off of three servers now because MB'ers got swept up in the effort to stop exploiters. Enough that eventually MB'ing just wasn't allowed anymore. Since that is exactly what Blizzard did with their servers to little effect it doesn't make sense to keep trying the same thing. There has to be a better option than more and more restrictive rules. Maybe embracing it the way Turtle Wow has in the past but with a careful eye towards growth and server health is a better way.
