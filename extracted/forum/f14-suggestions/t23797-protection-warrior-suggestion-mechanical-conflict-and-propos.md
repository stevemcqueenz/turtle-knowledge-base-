# Protection Warrior Suggestion – Mechanical Conflict and Proposal

## Post 166870 by Morkahja (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2026-01-29T20:44:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=166870#p166870 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

Protection currently has a core contradiction: the better you mitigate damage, the less rage you generate. Dodge, parry and reduced incoming damage starve rage, directly harming threat generation. This makes optimal defensive play feel wrong.

Block at least grants some rage, but it’s not enough. The system rewards taking damage and punishes avoidance. That incentive structure is backwards.

Proposed changes:

> **Rage on Avoidance** (Protection talents)
>
>  Gain 1 rage when you successfully parry or dodge.
>
>  Or perhaps
>
>  Gives you a 10/20/30% chance to generate 2/4/6 rage when successfully parry or dodge.

Block keeps its existing rage and remains the strongest defensive stat.

This aligns rage generation with defensive success, not damage taken.
> **Block Retaliation**
>
>  Successful blocks deal minor shield-based damage or threat to the attacker.

 *Reinforces shield gameplay and gives block a clear identity beyond mitigation.*

> **Unyielding Presence**
>  While in Defensive Stance, you and your party takes up to 5% reduced damage based on your current Rage.
>  At 100 Rage, this effect is increased by an additional 1%.

New Protection talent: party damage reduction scales with the warrior’s current rage.

Up to 5% damage reduction as rage fills.

At 100 rage, gain an additional +1% bonus (total 6%).

 *Creates a real decision: spend rage for threat, or hold rage to protect the group.*

Why this works:

Defensive play is rewarded instead of punished.

Threat generation scales with skillful tanking, not reckless damage intake.

Rage becomes a meaningful resource, not just fuel to dump.

Adds depth without increasing raw damage or survivability inflation.

Right now, mitigation competes with threat. It shouldn’t. Rage should flow from mastery of defense and, at its peak, let the warrior become a stabilizing force for the group.

You missed the core argument.

Commenting on language or AI translation is irrelevant. Not everyone is a native English speaker, and clarity of ideas matters more than prose purity.

The issue raised is not lack of tools or player knowledge. It is a mechanical contradiction:

In Vanilla and Classic alike, mitigation and avoidance directly suppress rage generation. That means the Protection specialization is mechanically punished for investing into its defining strengths. A spec should not become worse at its role the more it specializes into it. That is a design flaw, not a skill issue.

Listing stance-dancing, Bloodrage usage, Charge tricks, weapon upgrades, or deliberate mitigation downgrades does not address this contradiction. Those are compensations for the problem, not solutions. Saying “take more damage to get rage” is precisely the issue being criticized.

Avoidance vs mitigation terminology is understood and not the point. Whether avoidance was intentionally designed to give no rage does not automatically make that design coherent or immune to critique. Intent does not equal correctness.

The druid comparison actually supports the argument: druids were later given rage-on-avoidance specifically because rage starvation from avoidance feels bad in practice. Warriors having more buttons does not invalidate the underlying mechanical tension.

If you disagree with the proposal, argue why rewarding defensive success with controlled rage or group mitigation would harm balance or gameplay depth. Explaining how to brute-force rage through damage intake is not a counterargument to the design issue being raised.

Well, I think the Turtle WoW team has the goal of allowing players to fulfill their playstyle fantasy. They also aim to make each talent spec a viable experience.
What we can see is that they have already taken big steps toward a better Protection Warrior experience. Everyone who doesn’t see or understand this should compare the Protection Warrior in Classic and Turtle WoW. The dev team has taken major steps in exactly the direction I am proposing in this thread: making mitigation a good source of resources. See Shield Specialization and Concussive Blow. Even Defensive Tactics can be counted as a talent that allows players to even out their rage generation while wearing mitigation gear.

The announced changes for the next patch are also pointing in the same direction, so everyone who disagrees with the idea that mitigation should not equal rage or threat starvation might want to think it over and align with the direction in which the class spec is being developed.

My changes do not contradict the design direction chosen by the dev team; they are a complement to what they are trying to achieve.

When we look at the Protection talents, most of them are meant to make the Prot Warrior tankier and increase mitigation. There is no reason to believe that the original design philosophy was the one mentioned above, that “mitigation should starve resource generation and threat.” The design of Defensive Stance and the Protection tree is meant to sacrifice damage for mitigation. The same goes for all mitigation gear: the sacrifice is always damage. We can see that Prot gets talents like Defiance specifically to mitigate the threat loss caused by lower damage.

The point I am trying to make is that when I want to focus fully on mitigation, as the Protection talent tree suggests, I cripple my experience, while the other talent trees synergize well with the gear decisions I make. This is not by design; it is a flaw, and I think it should be further worked on, just as it already seems to be according to the preliminary changes coming in the next patch.

What this is about is immersion in the Protection Warrior experience. As a tank, I do not care about my damage output, and I don’t think tanks should deal high damage. I love mitigation.
I love seeing resist, dodge, parry, and block. I love timing blocks and Shield Slams to push my block chance as high as possible. I love reducing incoming damage and attack speed with my abilities. I am not interested in dealing damage myself; I am interested in giving my group the best possible platform to deal their damage and cast their heals.

That is why I would like to see dodge and parry gain more importance in deep Protection: to make them super meaningful stats and mechanics. I would also love to see block, parry, and dodge have a chance to automatically trigger Revenge without a cooldown; I think that idea was excellent.
