# Profession thoughts and ideas.

## Post 165490 by Fugus (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2026-01-18T02:24:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=165490#p165490 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

When it comes to the professions, almost all the stuff is Bind on Equip so you can trade or craft them for others without too much issue and the overall stuff isn't even that strong compared to what you can get without it.

Then you have Jewelcrafting which makes all this stuff not only sooner than you can find them normally but also really good stuff that you can only get by being a Jewelcrafter because they are Bind on Pickup.

Then combine that with the fact that you don't even need the gathering profession on that character and can just throw Engineering on him for the utility as well (As long as they aren't a feral druid....) without any issues so long as you have the gold or gathering alts to get the stuff.

So, my basic idea is to add actually irreplaceable level stuff to the gear crafting professions but make them Bind on Pickup and require the accompanying gathering profession with it to use.

Honestly, I feel every profession should have some uber quality bind on pickups that you can only get by having matching professions to use. So if you dump the professions, you lose access to the items as well.

So...

Skinning/Leatherworking
Mining/Blacksmithing
Mining/Engineering
Mining/Jewelcrafting
Tailoring/Enchanting
Alchemy/Herbalism

Have the best of the best for each of the primaries require the matched set to craft and use.  Otherwise, it gets increasingly hard to justify being anything but an Engineer, Jewelcrafter, and/or Enchanter on your main character with everything else being done by alts. Or just being an alchemist because at least those are things you need to make more than once.

There is a fundamental clash of ideas of what professions are meant to do.

At current, with the exception of engineering and to a lesser degree jewelcrafting and an even lesser degree blacksmithing, professions do not translate to characterpower.
At least in the meaning, that you personally need to have said profession to benefit from the spoils of said profession via trade with other players.

At present, it allows me and my group of friends to meet all the needs we could have by splitting professions based on the needs of the group.

By giving you powerful rewards for not just one, but potentially combos of professions, you very easily create scenarios where said professions are strictly optimal for specs or classes.

Engineering is undoubtably the strongest profession for aoe tanking for any shield-wielding tank variety.
If we say for the sake of argument, specifically dragonleatherworkers with second profession of skinner can create a piece of armor that gives anyone they heal a healing over time effect (think red dragonscale set but in good)

If those profession bonuses strive to be all somewhat equally useful for any combination of professions, you have 45(!) possible combinations of professions to take care of and try to all make equally valuable for the individual player.

If the aim is not to have them be equally powerful, then why does it matter what you pick, its the game punishing you to pick anything but the optimal option. A feeling, everyone who doesnt pick engineering is already familisr with. Minus maybe the fact that outside of fights that require a shittob of sappers, the impact of engineering in pve is largely irrelevant.

Having the profession and offering its services is the benefit of having a certain profession. Adding direct combat power to the profession is forcing players hand and limiting their choice of profession to whatever is "the right choice" for their class.

Right now your performance is equivalent with 8  out of the 10 profession, with jewelcrafting allowing you to craft ridiculously expensive bop pieces that at best allow you to skip mc loot. Only engineering being an outliner but kinda paying for it by being a profession that fails miserably at the whole economic aspect of a profession to make money.

Adding more profession locked stuff is very likely to make the dread of taking the "wrong" profession more impactful, rather than less
