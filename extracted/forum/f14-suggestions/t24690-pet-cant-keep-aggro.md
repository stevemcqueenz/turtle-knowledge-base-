Which dungeon matters massively. The pets rely majorly on growl for their threat. if you don't have the talent bestial precision specced for some reason, your pets hitchance with growl goes to the dumpster fighting anything higher than lvl 60. For the same reason, if your pet is underlevelled you would deal with a similar issue. (But since you have growl r7, thats not the issue)

Secondly, check if your pet actually casts growl on cooldown. Some pets (particularly when they know claw) have a tendency to dump their focus on other abilities, leaving not enough to spam growl on cd. Similarly, you also wanna check if your pet also knows cower. And turh it off if it does.

Lastly, the pet is not meant to be a rock solid tank holding everything without its hiccups. Feign death, giving your pet a second to close the distance before you start blasting and other small optimisations go a long way to keeping the mobs attention focussed on your pet rather than you.
Wows threat has a quirk where if someone has a mob, anyone else has to work extra hard to take it from them. so once you rip threat, your pet has to actually deal 110% of your threat before the mob focusses on the pet. IF you start blasting before the pet has ever engaged the mob. You are likely griefing yourself

## Post 173893 by SvenS2 (Bug Report Enthusiast) — 2026-04-03T14:27:00Z

Around your 50s, the pet starts to drastically struggle keeping up with aggro. And this is for BM, the other specs notice that shift even sooner. However, I'm on the stance that individual Hunter playstyles should have individual better threat reduction. For context:

In melee you have Disengage which scales horribly and has a very high manacost for rank 3. In the open world, your best bet is to weave in Rank 2 disengage hits and stay in Aspect of the Viper.

Ranged on the other hand benefit from the higher threat pull cap, but if you're a MM hunter, it doesn't really matter, you'll easily pull threat from your pet. Annoying, but your best option is to again, stay in Aspect of the Viper and auto-shoot, and use the occasional steady or arcane shot when the mob is at low HP.

For BM, you have to let your pet fight the mob alone for a few sec to gather enough threat, and then you join the fight. Intimidation also helps a lot with that. Fast attacking pets help with this playstyle, and do a better job at maintaining threat, despite the lower damage.
---
I've made threat reduction proposals for the hunter in other threads, but I'll share them directly here.

 **Beastmaster**

- Unleashed Fury also increases your pet's threat generation while in Aggressive mode by 8/16/24/32/40%
 - Just add pet aggressive mode to your pet attack macro, and you'll benefit from the improved pet threat generation.
 - This won't interfere with dungeons or raids, as the pet is kept in passive mode.
 - This also won't affect new or bad hunters, which keep their pet in defensive mode (i.e. won't unintentionally steal aggro from the tank)
 - This change can help with pet tanking in leveling dungeons, if your party can't find a tank(as per usual).
---
 **Marksmanship**

- Add a talent or add the following effect to an existing talent:
"Reduces the threat you generate by 15/30% while at least 10yd away from the enemy."
 - Only 2 talent points for a big chunk of threat reduction.
 - Distance requirement maintains the ranged hunter playstyle, meaning melee still rely on Disengage
---
 **Survival**
- Improve Disengage scalling and reduce manacost per level.
 **Current**
Rank 1: 50 mana; –140 threat
Rank 2: 100 mana; –280 threat
Rank 3: 150 mana; –405 threat

 **Proposed**
Rank 1: 50 mana; –200 threat
Rank 2: 75 mana; –500 threat
Rank 3: 100 mana; –800 threat

 - Melee hunters deal significantly more damage now, and don't benefit from the ranged threat pull cap, which is why Disengage needs to reduce drastically more threat.
 - Current Rank 3 of disengage, despite costing 50% more mana, provides only a 36% threat reduction increase... This is so bad and backwards, considering late game you scale exponentially more, and thus require higher threat reduction means.
 - The steep 150 mana cost also makes it a pretty bad option, since Survival has a lot more things to cast here in turtle wow (More melee abilities and traps), so it needs to be pulled back, even with the much increased threat reduction. Which is why I think 100 is the sweet spot.
