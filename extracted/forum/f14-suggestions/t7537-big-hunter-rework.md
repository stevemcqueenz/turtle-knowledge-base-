# Big Hunter rework

## Post 48184 by Crazyhelix — 2023-05-31T02:29:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=48184#p48184 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I welcome fans to read other people's fantasies.
In this topic, I would like to propose changes to the hunter, and not pointwise, but in the form of a ready-made solution with a clear visual model. Follow the link to see the project of these talents. There is a script on the page that starts viewing examples of talents every minute (it works unstable but…).

**[embed: https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1r07lIcRQvV2XZQruHGQSKED_tGMD95ZpFZewkz6brgU/preview#gid=1989203322]**

Of course, talent alone is not enough here. The basic problems of the Vanilla hunter have not gone away:
Beastmaster - low pet survivability, low pet damage, lack of interaction between actual talents, a huge number of frankly weak talents and, in general, an extremely boring rotation.
The capabilities of the pet practically do not differ from the hunters of other development branches in a qualitative way. Add additional pet talent points to the talents of the lower floors, then the BM pet will be able to buy more passive skills from the trainer (especially true for additional resistances). In the current edition, this is +120 pet points for the BM branch.
Need to add avoidance 2: Reduces the damage your pet takes from area of effect attacks by an additional 75% (best design here i think to add this ability like Passive skill on BM branch that available on 56 (mb 60) lvl).
My edit adds attack speed talents (including a modified Enhanced Aspect of the Hawk) to the hunter, which, combined with MM's Go to the Throat talent, should make it necessary to use fast ranged weapons to maximize speed and crit procs, providing pet with a significant influx of tone, which will allow the latter to use abilities much more often, which ultimately will lead to the necessary increase in the damage of the entire specialization.
Survival - if we evaluate the talents themselves, then it is obvious that the surv had to develop as a melee fighter with the support of a pet. But Blizzard came to this only in the Legion. However, within the vanilla-BK-LK line, the concept of a hybrid damage dealer seems to be quite organic: melee / ranged / traps and all with the support of a pet. Making a Surv a full-fledged melee fighter without making radical changes to spells / talents is simply impossible (and there is no need). But a hybrid is possible.

The main missing link is the ability to set traps during combat. It is in this case that the concept will play for real. For this to work, you also need a system of traps from WoTLK (20 sec CD, 60 sec active, only 1 can be placed at a time). Melee combat will complement the damage, and not be its basis, but in order for there to be synergy between different combat distances, new mechanics are needed. In my version of talents, the Master of Tactics talent is responsible for this implementation.
Marksman is the hunter's only working spec. But .... only in numbers. There is a severely limited version of working talents that allow MM to deal quite good damage. But firstly: the rotation is disgustingly boring, and secondly: there is no variability, because all other talents are just rubbish (in fact, it's a miracle that it is in this branch that the hunter has the opportunity to grow in talent levels with the least loss in quality - that's all MM's merit). At the same time, it is this branch that is most affected by the Trueshot added on the turtle, because in fact, this is just an auto shot booster, and MM has the strongest auto shot. It gives some damage per mana boost, but Trueshot does nothing else! Only allows you to scale the final damage from an auto shot from a hunter's mana. This is a very bad innovation, because does not fix broken mechanics. It should just be removed. In my version, there is a change in mechanics, and there is also an increase in previously trash talents. Now you really want them, and sometimes you just need to take them! But there is also a "but" - such talents will give a noticeable increase in damage output compared to the current MM, and this is too much. The problem is in the mechanics, not in the naked damage. Therefore, the Damage from the MM's TOP abilities: Volley and Aimed Shot must be reduced at high levels (from about 35+) by about 20-25%.
Other implied changes: Give Multishot +1 target to hit at levels 4 and 5. Separate the CD of the Arcane shot and the Aimed shot. Decrease the cooldown of the Volley to 45 sec (it is possible to add at least a small scaling from the attack power).
And finally, the pets themselves: they need to make their characteristics more diverse and give them special abilities by class (how did this happen later, I don’t remember when - cataclysm or already Pandaria? But add ONLY class abilities), because now useful pets can be counted on the fingers of one hand.
As a result, the overall model would look something like this:
Surv has the highest damage potential, but only if he implements a combination of ranged combat + melee combat + traps. Due to the same bundle, it is able to impose a "viscous fight" on melee opponents. Interesting spec for leveling and intense pvp.
Marksman remains a turret (first of all, a raid one), only now it makes sense in speed, in order to “shove” more Steady’s for the Aimed shot CD and get a more bonus. Also there are useful buns for both the raid and general pve.

BM remains the most autonomous spec, but now there is a choice between contact (due to Go to the Throat - it will definitely be used in PVE, but in PVP it is not clear) and remote (the concept has not changed, just the pet has been fairly strengthened). BM's leveling potential is also increasing.
All these changes create more interest in the previously underused hunter specs (due to their weakness), change the balance of power within the class, which will definitely attract interest from the players. Which talent alignment will give more damage in raid PVE (mobile encounter or dummy?) is completely unclear! And in PVP? In BG, open world or Arenas (I hope they will appear in a full version)? It's impossible to predict right off the bat. One thing is for sure: SUCH a hunter looks much more interesting and at the same time the idea of its classical implementation does not break.

P.S. examples of talent are not some final calculated product. This is just a "guess" for an example.

Some quick thoughts about your project:

Does the mouseover for scatter shot work properly? I get "righteous fury" there, but I have an idea what I'm supposed to see there. Also, "improved predator aspects" should probably include the wolf.

I fully agree about pets deserving some love.

What's Legion? I thought they canceled WoW altogether in december 2010, after seeing how they had butchered their own project with WotLK...

Traps: First of all, they are already useable in combat with FD, but that's one thing they changed in TBC and I think that was a good change back then. I'm not sure why you're referring to WotLK, however. Traps were perfect in TBC and I don't remember any significant changes in WotLK. What am I missing?

I don't know how useful your version of "master tactician" actually would be. I mean, it's a 6-second buff that requires the hunter to either enter or leave melee range, crossing the deadzone all the time. Toss in the wolf aspect in it's current state and things get frustrating and it gets only slightly better with your version of Readiness. It's another story in PvP, of course.

How about this instead:

a) Replace Master tactitian with something closer to the TBC version: "all attacks have x% chance to increase crit chance by y% for z seconds". Or toss that one out completely for something similar to "expose weakness" that works for melee and ranged attacks. This would buff melee hunters but would also support a (my) range-based survival playstyle where traps and melee skills are used to get out of melee asap while melee dps is mostly a sidenote.
b) Make traps useable in combat. Period. Same for wyvern sting. They did that in TBC and it was a good change. No need for Readiness in that case. Alternative: "Aspect of the spider": allows to set traps in combat.

Speaking of melee hunters: is mongoose bite actually useful in its current state?

How about we replace "improved slaying" with "improved tracking" and increase damage against tracked targets? Fits the survival theme and I think they did that in a later expansion. Makes the talent less situational, too.

Lonely Strider: I can see what you're doing here, but how good would this be with the vanilla minrange? Might need some work, like "also reduces minimum range to 6m" or so.

Conclusion:
Some interesting ideas. There's a lot of TBC stuff in there. TBC was the good expansion where the big flaws in class design were fixed, so taking ideas from there is a good idea. As always, numbers need to be checked. As long as talent changes make classes more interesting to play or make some specs "good enough to be played", they're probably a good thing. Raw power buffs (which you don't seem to be aiming at), however are not. Easy games are boring games, regardless of the number of keys you can  press.

Having played a survival hunter for many, many years now on various tbc and vanilla servers (I've found my way to turtle due to the lack of good long-lived tbc servers. I'm staying for the slow and steady attitude ;-))  I dislike the idea of "the melee hunter", especially with people asking to replace "my spec" with that one. However, upon closer inspection, I see that your talent trees would - with just a little tweaking - allow me to play basically "my old hunter" without any significant changes, while still offering improvements to the melee guys.

I don't see your project in its current state as perfect, but you're definitely on to something here.
