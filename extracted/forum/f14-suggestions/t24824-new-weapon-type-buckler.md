# New Weapon Type: Buckler

## Post 174759 by Azkaul — 2026-04-10T22:57:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=174759#p174759 | page 1 | era: post-1.18.1 -->

The Buckler: A Renaissance Shield Begging for Implementation
![Image](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcRTpWaBYSrNgbc18fQ8rHRrd-BbQd1Bd0boYw&s)

WHY
I've thought about this for a long time, as early as back in Vanilla, and I've always wondered why Shields are just "Shields", whilst Swords, Axes and Maces are 1h and 2h variants. Now I'm not asking for two-handed shields... At least not in this post.

I've long felt that there should be a middle ground between the "your damage kinda sucks now" choice of using a shield over DW or 2H builds, and playing buckler-rapier builds in games like Dark Souls and Elden Ring have only enhanced my hunger for this playstyle and aesthetic, long after the release of Vanilla WoW. **A middle ground that would allow for a fair increase in survivability without committing to full-tank status, whilst also being able to deal decent albeit fairly reduced damage.**

The Aesthetic of The Buckler fits perfectly into the "Magical High-Fantasy Renaissance" World of Azeroth, a world with rudimentary firearms, cannons, naval combat and exploration, pirates and fantastic clashes of cultures.

WHAT
Bucklers are a fantastic piece of late Medieval and Renaissance weaponry, evolving from early Roman influences like the Parma (pictured below)
![Image](https://deepeeka.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/ah6129_standard_bearer_s_parma_dia_24_inch.jpg)
Roman Parma

The intent of the shield is clear at first glance: This isn't your standard Legionnaire's hulking "Scutum" shield, designed for lines of big sweaty 'Joe Rogan's' to form impenetrable (albeit heavy) turtle-shells out of, and then shimmy towards European Barbarians, before giving them a good stabbing with a Gladius.
It's a shield designed to deflect strikes rather than absorb them, and then allow the wielder to give their opponent a good bell-ringing with the metal nose where possible.

In short: **The Buckler is a hybrid shield that is a trio of deflective, protective and offensive.**
Naturally its raw block ability is a shadow of what a proper shield can achieve, but its ability to enhance parry opportunities plus its nature as what is effectively a large set of knuckle-dusters with a tiny shield attached makes it a fascinating opportunity for some expanded class abilities.

HOW
Anyone familiar with shield-using classes has likely noticed quite a large number of shields are actually tiny and in many cases even literally refer to themselves as "[something] Buckler of the [whatever]", these Shields largely already exist in the game but thus-far simply lightning-rod to the base role of "Shield", and all that goes with it.

My proposal for the role of bucklers is this:
- Shield: Significantly reduced block amount, probably 25% of normal shields. Maybe as low as 10%
- Parry: Increased Parry Chance by a small amount (should not be enough to simply replace the damage mitigation of the block nerf, otherwise it's just exactly as good as a hulking great shield)
- Weapon: Acts as a weak off-hand fist-weapon. Likely with a 20% damage nerf on a raw off-hand weapon
- Riposte: Doubles next auto-attack speed after you parry.
Some thoughts on potential scaling;
- Agility could increase parry chance by some % per point whilst using a buckler, making it a more viable tanking alternative.
- The nullification of crits and crushing blows could be reduced or removed entirely for this item

WHO?
My initial thoughts are that this new weapon would be used primarily by **Warriors, Shaman**, and **Rogues** who have a medium-depth-investment in the Combat Tree.
Although there's some historical instances in which Clergy (**Priests**) may have used these with maces (as they were unable to shed blood), so it may be worth putting it deep into the Discipline tree as well.
There is also potentially an argument for **Hunters** to use it as well, as I'll explain in 'The Aesthetic'

Paladins are hulking great titans of The Light and so *do not concern themselves with the dashing finesse of such devices*, although I could be convinced otherwise.

THE AESTHETIC
The Aesthetic of this is simple; the lightly armoured musketeer, switching from gun to rapier and buckler in close combat, the dashing rogue fencing his way through adventures, the warrior loose on the field of battle with a buckler and a rapier running through the enemy.
![Image](https://i.ibb.co/nM511ZkW/ZZZZ.png)

Lore-wise, this would be a cultural import from the fallen Gilnaeus, who are heavy users of muskets, rapiers, bucklers and Renaissance-to-Victorian aesthetic weaponry.

In each instance they prioritize finesse over the kind of raw 'tankiness' associated with The Legionnaire, The Man-at-Arms or The Medieval Plate-Armored Knight.

 - **For Warriors**; this implies light dungeon tanking with increased threat generation that meets in the middle of out-and-out Fury Dual-Wield tanking, with additional utility in levelling and world exploration.

 - **For the Rogue**; it's a chance to add a whole new fantasy role that I believe Combat Spec was always destined to offer, rather than what it is; a scuffed up Fury Warrior. Again, with world-exploration, off-tanking, and potential pvp use.

 - **For the Hunter**; they can act as true Musketeers did, tactically switching (much in the same way Warriors do now) to close combat when the situation calls, and this scales amazingly and synergizes with their existing high parry abilities, making them a reasonable threat up-close, and a danger if you end up facing a flurry of high-speed attacks from them after a number of parries.

 - **For the Shaman**; smacking enemies in the mouth with element-infused fist strikes in the off-hand whilst deflecting blows, giving a fair alternative to pure 2H enhancement builds, and an enhancement tree that could be modified to give this hybrid style of combat more interesting synergies (e.g. pop a Shield unit on parry, Block and Parry chance scale equally to make them more tanky and so on).

Something that I thought of is this. Based on the codifying of "Vampirism" as a stat, Turtle isn't above adding new attributes to gear to encourage more varied gameplay. So, let me propose a new stat: Counter.

Mechanically, this would give you a bonus to your next attack after foiling an enemy attack. Mainly meaning parry, but I think expanding it to dodge wouldn't be unreasonable either. It could be as simple as a 1-1 bonus to your Str stat. So with a buckler, you'd get a worse shield, but whenever your opponent fails to hit you, you do more damage. Essentially trading reliable defence for unreliable offence (which can be made more reliable with the right build). A middle-of-the-road option in between using a proper shield and dual wielding.
