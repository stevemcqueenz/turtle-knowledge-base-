There are two opposing approaches to the issue of class availability in games.

 1. Your character is an individual. They have their own background, which may differ from the traditional way of life of their race, so, for example, in D&D, your drow could follow the path of a druid.
 2. Your character is part of a society. They are bound by the history and traditions of their people, so, for example, in WoW, your orc does not believe in the Light and cannot be a paladin, but can be a shaman.

Yes, WoW adheres to the second approach, and the TWoW devs respect this. That's why every new race-class combination is justified by some lore reason. For example, dwarves became warlocks after gnomes settled in their city. Or tauren became priests (as I understand the lore of 1.18.1) after uniting with a tribe that reveres celestial bodies.
