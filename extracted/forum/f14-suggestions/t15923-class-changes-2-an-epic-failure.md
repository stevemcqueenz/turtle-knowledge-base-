>  For 10-19 ability to dualwield from lvl 10 means only second enchant on your offhand weapon. Additional 15 agility or crusader is actually very cool.

This isn't about game balance, this is about design philosophy. What level you learn spells/abilities and the order you learn them in was very intentionally chosen. Level 20 is the milestone in which your class's kit starts becoming truly fleshed out, where the identity of each spec begins to show itself and become apparent in practice. Dual Wielding is one of the defining abilities of Fury, and Fury is the playstyle that 20 begins to introduce to Warriors, just like how Druids learn Cat Form to get their melee damage and shapeshifting fleshed out, and how Shamans learn Water as an element which expands their healing at 20.

Level 10-19 is about defining the primary roles and functions of your class. Rogues learn Dual Wielding at 10 because you will never see a Rogue without two weapons, dealing damage is one of their core functions. Warriors learned Defensive Stance and Taunt at level 10 because they're the only non-hybrid tank. Tanking, while an optional playstyle, is a core part of Warrior's identity, even if you're playing Warrior as DPS you're still likely to throw out an occasional Taunt to protect your party. Dual Wielding for Warriors is just one avenue for their DPS playstyles since you can go Arms or even 2h Fury, it isn't as essential to Warriors as a class like it is for Rogues. To give Warriors Dual Wielding as soon as they can put their first talent point into Fury just reeks of the "jump right in" design philosophy of Cata and Retail. There's a lot of buttons and different playstyles for fresh players to learn, and the original devs were careful to slowly and gradually introduce them all to new players while tying this mechanical progression into the progression of power fantasy.

This obviously isn't my only issue with CC2 for Warriors, and while a singular change like this might not seem like a big deal at first glance, it carries very bad implications for the Turtle Devs' respect and understanding of the game's original design.
