# Night Elf Paladins.

## Post 67424 by Ironfoe101 — 2023-09-28T15:15:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=67424#p67424 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Paladin: Priest in a can.

No, this is not a post in any capacity about how Night Elves could, would, or should follow the Holy Light.  Instead I will outline my opinions on what actually makes a Paladin, and how the concept can be applied to any class/race combo that has access to priests.  I will do my best to cover why access to this class can be reasonable, but due to misconceptions of what Paladins are and their history I’ll be breaking them down both lorewise and mechanically.

THE LORE

The Origins of the Paladin

Paladins are an extremely young concept within Warcraft’s history.  They are only about 20 years old at the start of Vanilla World of Warcraft’s story, not existing at all during the first war.  It was only after the devastation of the first war, and before the second that the Knights of the Silver Hand were formed by Archbishop Alonsus Faol.  Faol took some of the surviving Northshire Clerics and taught them how to defend themselves, along with Knights of Lordaeron who he instructed in the ways of magic, and formed the modern Paladins we know today.  This was not just to be a fighting force, nor simply to add healing to the front lines, but to be a source of hope.

So we have three things that we can break down that I hope most would agree that a Warcraft Paladin is.  They are trained in the ways of war, some of their ranks are pulled straight from Knights.  They are practitioners of magic, a mixture of healing, buffing and smiting techniques.  Lastly their source of magic being the Holy Light.

Knights are where the iconic warhammer, mauls, and plate armor come from for Paladins.  During the first war Knights were described as using huge maces, and even during the second war Knights who did not become Paladins were wielding Plate and Warhammers.  This gives us a solid basis in what makes up the martial part of Paladins, and what they need to be able to do in physical combat.

Clerics of Northshire make up the other half of the disciples that make up the ranks of the Silver Hand.  They were spell casters that supported the Alliance during the first war, and were nearly wiped out as a result.  Never having a reason to need more to defend themselves over their long history until the invasion by Orcs, their squishiness was their downfall.  This is a good example of what the spell casting part of Paladins draws from, while never receiving the full suite of spellcasting it is still good inspiration on what some of those expectations may be.

Which brings us to what is probably the most important aspect of a Paladin in warcraft, their source of magic.  For you can have a more casting focused Paladin, or a more combat oriented, but their displays of Holy Magic will always be iconic.  But what exactly is the Holy Light, what makes it special?
I think no one would contest the statement that the Holy Light is Divine magic, but its source and nature may not be so agreed upon.  Back into the past we have the Warcraft 1 manual which states that Clerics have the ability to channel the ‘spirit of humanity’.  Warcraft 2’s manual states that Faol ‘tutored them (the knights) in the ways of magic’.  Warcraft RPG Book (2nd Edition) states that divine magic isn’t something granted by divine beings, but is an effect of one’s faith (whatever it may be) and how it manifests in a tangible way to affect the world.

Until the Naaru were introduced during The Burning Crusade era lore, the Holy Light has never had a specific deity or power associated with its abilities.  The mixture of conflicting faiths in how to follow the Light, along with how its practitioners are often at very severe odds does not lend itself well to the idea that it is a granted power.

What I’m trying to get at here, is that the Holy Light in itself is just magic, divine magic, and it isn’t more unique than that.  A Paladin’s role in their culture, society, and the battlefield is unique as no other race has fielded an equivalent.  A divine spellcaster, in heavy armor, trained and encouraged to go into melee.  There is nothing to stop other races that have priests, to do something similar with their own.

A Troll Warrior trained in the ways of the spirits/Loa, or Undead who uses their faith in the Forgotten Shadow to bolster their own strength, along with their allies.  A Sentinel and Priestess of the Moon work together to emulate and better integrate into the Alliance military by fielding their own divine warriors that focus their efforts into the thick of things.  These all could be racial equivalent of Paladins that could be represented by the class in the game.

BUT WHAT ABOUT MECHANICS

I’ve made my arguments regarding what makes a Paladin.  Now if Night Elf Paladins were to be implemented (which was my entire point of this post) how would they work?  Would it be Night Elves following the Holy Light?(No)  Would you need to reskin their abilities to be more arcane/lunar based?  Rename all their abilities because it’s too much like Humans?

But you already have a class that shares the same skill set, same names, same effects AND quests yet derive their powers from different faiths.  Priests.

The Loa, the Forgotten Shadow, The Holy Light, Elune, vastly different in their cultures, their followers and yet save for a couple unique spells they share the same spells and talents.  The later levels have quests that are common and run odd themes yet are the same despite the core differences in Priests between the cultures.

Is it that far fetched to have a Night Elf Paladin with unique intro quests, without having to change core gameplay?  Night Elves already have the base ingredients to a Paladin.  The Priestesses of Elune are divine casters, and already share a close relationship with Sentinels.  While Night Elves do focus more on ranged combat historically, they now are part of an Alliance and would it be that strange for them to experiment with battle tactics taken from their allies?  Especially if you consider Paladins at all successful in the last two wars.  High elves already are teaching certain tactics to Humans for them to be hunters, why not Humans showing the strength of a frontline faith anchor for battles?

Their starter Paladin quests could give them their own flavor.  Just how Human Paladins are/were Knights of the Silver Hand, Night Elf Paladins could be something unique for their own order.  Champions of Elune, Elune’s Light, Knights of the Moon, something to distinguish their order away from Knights of the Silver Hand.  Ursal the Mauler, or Vrlus Vilehoof could be the target for the Holy Strike Quest.

I know my argument could easily be used for Troll Paladins, or Undead Paladins and you know what?  I completely agree, except it’s my understanding that the class/faction line is not to be crossed which is why I have stopped at Night Elves.  I thank you all for your time reading, and hope you all have a nice day or night.

They are Priestesses of the Moon, do worship Elune.  However they do not use arcane or nature magic.  Arcane and divine magic are very different, and we know for a fact that Elune's priestesses don't use arcane magic because that is strictly forbidden in Night Elf culture.  Starshards for Priests, and Moonfire/Starfire for druids may do arcane damage, but both are sourced from divine magic.  While both Holy and Nature magic are divine in nature, Priestesses of Elune came before Night Elf druids so they do not use Nature magic.

Additionally in the quest 'Doling Justice' which is a Vanilla quest Tyrande refers to herself and the temple as 'Servants of the Light', which is not to say in any capacity that they are related to the Holy Light, but they worship the Light of the Moon.  There is a quest reward that is 'Light of Elune', WC3 voice lines of "By the Light of the Moon",  "Goddess Light my Path".   Priests in WoW have Holy Fire, Holy Nova.

WoW shows that Night Elves using Holy Magic isn't too far of a gap to jump with their suite of Holy magic, considering they have 1 actual moon theme spell.  They are numerous references of Light already within their culture, including Tyrande herself.  I don't think the fact that Paladin has an in game Holy Theme is too much of a stretch for Night Elves.

Besides, a lot of this isn't how Night Elves are, or were, but how they may progress as the Vanilla wow story unfolds.  There was only 1 Human Ranger before, Nathanos, yet we're able to progress the story of High Elves sharing the skills and Humans adopting a new style.  Night Elves are not longer only in their own forests, where ambush tactics make perfect sense and no real need for the same kind of front line that Humans used in the past.  They've joined into an Alliance, they're fighting in a much wider and diverse world.  They can adapt, change, and work outside of their normal ways in the story.
