# Profession Revamp – A Tiered, Endgame-Centered Crafting System

## Post 141575 by Wilsonsds (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2025-07-26T14:11:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=141575#p141575 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

This proposal reimagines professions in a way that gives them long-term value, identity, and meaningful progression. Instead of reaching 300 skill and becoming obsolete, each profession evolves through **specialization stages**, unlocking niche power and high-end relevance along the way.

Every profession follows a clear, tiered structure that ties progression directly to player choice, PvE content, and economic collaboration.

---
 **![📐](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f4d0.svg) Core Design Principles**

- **Identity**: Every crafter and gatherer becomes unique based on their specialization path.

- **Endgame Purpose**: Professions matter throughout raid tiers — not just at level 40 or 60.

- **Player Interdependence**: You will need other crafters and gatherers to reach your goals.

- **Raid Integration**: Dungeons and raids drop materials, unlock recipes, and fuel crafting.

- **Replayability**: Alts can specialize in different branches, encouraging diversity.

- **Craft Value**: Crafted gear is slightly stronger than the equivalent PvE drop — it’s worth using.

---
 **🛠 Crafting Professions – Specialization Structure**

Crafting professions (like Blacksmithing, Tailoring, Leatherworking, Alchemy, etc.) now follow a **multi-stage path** of specialization. As you progress, your crafting power becomes more focused — but also more powerful within that niche.

 **![🪓](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1fa93.svg) Example: Blacksmithing (but applies to all crafting professions)**

|  |
|
| **Stage 1** | 1–125 | Generic recipes (common & uncommon gear up to level 20). Rare drops fill missing item slots for twinks. Optional white gear sets every 5 levels for *Vagrant Challenge*. Uses 25% less material. |
| **Stage 2** | 126–175 | First branch: *Armorsmith* or *Weaponsmith*. Unlocks rare recipes up to level 40. |
| **Stage 3** | 176–225 | Specialize by theme: *Physical*, *Magical*, or *Elemental*. Gear up to level 58. |
| **Stage 4** | 226–275 | Focus on a specific gear *slot* (e.g., Helm, Shoulders). Pre-BiS gear for that slot. |
| **Stage 5+** | 276–300 | Tiered progression: gear slightly stronger than Tier 1, 2, AQ40, Naxx, and post-Naxx. |
 **![⚠️](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/26a0.svg) High-end recipes are BoP** (bound to player) and acquired via *questlines and PvE drops (BOP crafting items)*.
 **Crafted items are BoE**, allowing them to be sold or traded.

---
 **![⚗️](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/2697.svg) Examples for Other Professions**

- **Tailoring**: Choose between *Magic Weavesmith* (creates special cloth used by others and accessories like flying carpets, gliders, parachutes) or *Fashioning* (makes powerful magical sets ).

- **Leatherworking**: Choose Leather or Mail paths. Follows same structure as Armorsmith.

- **Alchemy**: Specialize in Transmutation, Potions, or Elixirs. Later ranks grant unique cooldowns, procs, or powerful multi-use cauldrons.

- **Enchanting**: Path splits into Magical vs. Elemental focus, then into Armor/Weapon(wands and staffs)/Utility.

- **Engineering**: Starts with Goblin vs. Gnomish. Later opens into gadgets, explosives, firearms, transport, trinkets — each with unique recipes and item slots.

 *Each specialization has unique materials, some of which are required by other professions. This ensures interdependence.*

---
 **![🔁](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f501.svg) Material Interdependency & Crafting Economy**

At higher tiers, crafting recipes require **rare materials** that:

- Drop from raid bosses;

- Are refined through specialized professions;

- Or must be traded across professions.

 *Examples:*

- A Tailor may need an Alchemist’s Mystic Resin;

- A Weaponsmith may need Engineering’s Precision Core;

- A Leatherworker may require Blacksmithing’s Runed Rivets.

This creates **cross-profession collaboration** and encourages player-driven markets and guild synergy.

---
 **![🌿](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f33f.svg) Gathering Professions – Progression After 300**

Gathering (Herbalism, Mining, Skinning) remains *unchanged until skill 300*.
Once maxed out, a new **specialization path** becomes available.

 **![🎯](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f3af.svg) Three Branches:**

- **Quantity** – Increases the number of items gathered per node.

- **Quality** – Adds a chance to gather rare materials (e.g., Black Lotus from Plaguebloom).

- **Recycling** – Allows dismantling of crafted items to reclaim valuable ingredients.

All gatherers begin with:

- *Quantity Rank 1*

- *Quality Rank 1*

- *Recycling Rank 1*

This grants standard world gathering power and low-level recycling.
 **Specialization effects only activate once the gatherer reaches skill 300.**

---
 **![📘](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f4d8.svg) Progression Through Raid Content**

To unlock higher specialization ranks, gatherers must collect **specialization books** dropped by bosses in dungeons and raids.

|  |
|
| UBRS | Any boss | Step 1 | 100% |
| MC | Ragnaros | Step 2 | 80% |
| BWL | Nefarian | Step 3 | 60% |
| AQ40 | C’thun | Step 4 | 40% |
| Naxx | Kel'Thuzad | Step 5 | 20% |
To learn Step 2 in Quality, you must already have Step 1 in Quality — and so on.
Books are **soulbound** and **account-limited**.

---
 **![🔁](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f501.svg) Recycling Details**

Higher recycling ranks allow breaking down higher-tier gear:

- Rank 1 – pre-raid gear

- Rank 2 – Tier 1

- Rank 3 – Tier 2

- Rank 4 – AQ40

- Rank 5 – Naxx

This creates a **valuable player service**: old raid gear becomes recyclable for rare reagents.

---
 **![🌋](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f30b.svg) Raid-Only Nodes**

Some herbs, ores, and skins will exist **only inside raids** and require appropriate *Quality ranks* to harvest:

- Molten Flamepetal – Molten Core

- Shadowbone Ore – Naxxramas

- Bloodhide Behemoths – AQ40
This gives gatherers **real endgame presence** in PvE progression.

---
 **![🔮](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f52e.svg) Final Thoughts**

This system transforms professions into **long-term, endgame-relevant choices** that reward planning, effort, and collaboration.

It makes professions:

- More fun to level and master

- More relevant in every raid tier

- More impactful in economy and community

- More distinct and specialized

It also solves the classic problem of “hit 300 and forget,” while avoiding power creep by keeping gear only **slightly stronger** than raid drops of the same tier.

---
 **![📜](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f4dc.svg) Disclaimer**

This concept is *not* meant to be taken as rigid or definitive.
It is not set in stone.

 **This is just a conceptual foundation** — a brainstorm, a skeleton, a creative direction.
The goal is to provide ideas that *better designers and developers* can refine, discard, or transform into something much more polished.

Let’s open the discussion. Let’s build something together.

---
 **![😔](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f614.svg) The “Too Late to Matter” Problem**

Have you ever worked your way up to 300 Armorsmithing, dreaming of crafting powerful gear for your guild, only to discover there are already **five other Armorsmiths** in your roster?
And to make matters worse, **the guild leader is one of them**?

So when that Naxxramas recipe drops, you know exactly what’s going to happen:

- It’s going to the guild leader.

- Then to their alt.

- Then to their friend's alt.

- Then maybe, just maybe, to you — weeks later.

This system solves that.
With specialization, niche identity, and interdependence, every profession path matters.
You won't be just another Blacksmith — you'll be **the only Elemental Armorsmith who crafts the best bracers with elemental resistance and good stats**.

That’s what gives value to your time.
That’s what makes professions meaningful again.

Some ideas:

- **Ore Powder** — created by smelting or crushing low-level ores. Used in Alchemy as catalysts or stabilizers for transmutations.

- **Salt-Cured Leather** — Leatherworkers can treat hides using Salt + specific Ore Powder (e.g., Tin Dust for flexibility, Iron Dust for durability). Result: high-resist leather components.

- **Arcane Metal Dust** — used by Enchanters to bind effects to armor or modify reagents (like creating hybrid dusts).

- **Conductive Paste** — crafted by combining Copper Dust + Herbal Extracts. Used in Engineering for trinkets and charged devices.
These kinds of systems add **horizontal value** to low-tier gathering and create *interdependence between professions*. A miner isn’t just farming for a Blacksmith anymore — they might supply Alchemists, Enchanters, and Leatherworkers as well.

 **Hey! Appreciate your thoughts — and yeah, I think we’re kinda aiming at the same target, just taking different paths to get there. ![😄](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f604.svg)**

I totally get the appeal of BoP near-BiS items to give professions that personal impact. But the idea behind this proposal is actually the opposite:
 **Let’s make professions connect people — not isolate them.**

When the item you craft is **BoP**, it becomes just for you.
Cool, sure... but then what? You max two professions, craft your own gear, and you’re done. You can’t help your guildmates. You can’t trade. No one needs you anymore.
It starts to feel like... Retail. ![😬](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f62c.svg)

And we’ve seen this before:
 **Back in TBC, Blizzard did exactly that**. Gave crafters powerful BoP gear.
What happened? Tons of players felt *forced* to drop their chosen professions — professions they picked because they fit their character’s identity — just to chase the meta.
People became resentful, disconnected, and lost motivation to play.

 **Any system that pressures players to abandon who their character is, just to stay “optimal,” is a design red flag.**

Instead, this system proposes:

- **Crafted items are BoE** — so you can sell, trade, or swap with other specialists. If I make plate bracers and you make plate belts, we can help each other. You don’t need to do it all — you *shouldn’t* do it all. That’s the fun.

- **Recipes are BoP** — and that’s where the progression happens. You earn the recipe by doing PvE content, questlines, raids, etc. Only people who’ve reached that point in the game can craft that item — it keeps the sense of achievement alive.
So yeah, someone who maxes two professions will still be able to craft two powerful items for themselves — but those items are **BoE**, meaning they can also share, trade, or make gold with them. And if someone else wants to follow the same path, they totally can — even on an alt.

It’s not about handing out free BiS — it’s about building a profession network that’s alive, relevant, and fun to be part of.

 **TL;DR:**
BoP items = solo game & forced choices.
BoE items + BoP recipes = social game with real progression.

And that’s where the magic happens. ![🔧](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f527.svg)![💰](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f4b0.svg)![🌱](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f331.svg)

 **Thanks, Bigsmerf! Really thoughtful reply — and a lot of your ideas fit perfectly with what I’m aiming for. ![😄](//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f604.svg)**

I totally agree that gear with hybrid effects, procs, and utility bonuses is where crafting can really shine — and your examples (Blood Drinker sets, party-wide buffs, support gear, debuff weapons) are **amazing**. That’s the kind of identity-based design that makes professions feel alive.

That said, I do want to be transparent about the core goal of the proposal:
 **Yes, crafted gear is meant to be slightly stronger than raid gear from the same tier** — not to outshine it right away, but to serve as a *second step in the raid progression curve*.

Think of it like this:

- **Raid loot is your entry point.** It gets you into the content, helps you survive, and builds your core setup.

- **Crafted gear comes after** — once you’ve done the raid, gotten the recipes, gathered the rare mats, and coordinated with other professions. You’re rewarded with a piece that’s *just above* the power curve, enough to justify the work.
Without that slight stat incentive, there’s a risk that crafted items — even if cool — end up as “fun side things” no one really uses long-term.

What I really love, though, is that your idea of **gear with unique effects, support potential, and hybrid flavor** blends perfectly with this vision.

So here’s where I think we meet in the middle:

- **Crafted gear is slightly stronger than its raid equivalent** — but not because of raw stats alone.

- **It offers unique utility, theorycrafting value, and alternate build options** — for those who love going beyond cookie-cutter BiS lists.

- **And it's unlocked through real progression** — raid drops, PvE content, specialization paths, rare mats, etc.
 **Also worth noting:** the proposal already plans to include **hybrid gear with procs and special effects** — it’s just not fully detailed yet.
But through the specialization system, players would eventually unlock thematic and niche recipes — including those cool off-build items you mentioned.

So your idea doesn’t conflict with the core proposal — it actually fits perfectly into how profession progression was envisioned from the start.
