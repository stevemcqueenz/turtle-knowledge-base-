# "Friction" in WoW

## Post 133564 by SvenS2 (Bug Report Enthusiast) — 2025-05-29T21:21:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=133564#p133564 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I've been thinking on what makes vanilla feel vanilla for a long time, and after Shroud's vid and Max's reaction to it, I think it's a good time as any to share my thoughts
Shroud's video
[embed: https://www.youtube.com/embed/pSd4HSaWTig]

Max's thoughts on the topic
[embed: https://www.youtube.com/embed/ovZ5J5oqhTk]

Below I go over what I mean by friction and what parts of WoW can be counted as that. I'm putting it in spoilers as it's rather long, but feel free to give it a read as it gives some context to the following paragraphs. **Also, for the rest of this thread, I'll use friction, grit and immersion interchangeably.**  In the videos they're talking about extraction shooters, but the concept of friction can be applied to many games, especially MMOs and more so MMO RPGs. Friction can be interpreted in numerous ways. In the videos it can be interpreted as intentional limitation on accessibility, quality of life or applied vagueness as to how things function or what is most optimal. Each game genre has its own type of friction, with the biggest shared one possibly being asymmetry(levels, characters, abilities, items, etc...).

Well what about RPGs? One of their "frictions" is the inclusion of activities that place you in the shoes of your player avatar, a sense of real life events being possible in the game and their following consequences. To give a proper example, talking to a civilian. In some RPGs you get multiple dialog options with diverging paths. In others, you don't even have options, you need to type in your dialog, like "I want to buy" etc... These can feel tedious, especially the latter, but they can help immerse the player in the respective game. Another RPG example would be mana regeneration. In some RPGs, your character needs to perform a specific task, like channel or meditate in order to regain mana. In Everquest for, your character needs to sit down in order to mediate and regain mana. Such an inconvenience, but at the same time such a fascinating immersion building addition. And a much larger one being consequences of your actions. Wrong dialog box or killing of an NPC can in cases drastically change the course of your playthrough.

Now what is a game? That can be defined in a lot of ways, but one of the best fits is: "A solo or group activity where a goal needs to be achieved within specific conditions". The goal itself can be reaching a set number of points, good timing, speed or accuracy, and plenty of other variants. The conditions are the more interesting part as they are the rules for the game, the thing which balances it out for all the players.

We finally reach World of Warcraft. My perspective on what makes vanilla feel like vanilla is exactly that RPG immersion aspect. Content can be enjoyed mostly solo, but there are also challenges which you're not meant to complete alone. Elite quests and spread out areas out in the world give out a sense of danger and mystic. It's the toughness of the enemy that gives them value, and not necessarily the gear they drop or quest chain they're part of. This form of grit makes the world feel much bigger than it actually is, despite you seeing less of it.
That is not all however. Even though you're killing giants, dragons and all sorts of monsters, the game feels grounded and not blazing fast. I don't partake on RP unless it's forced by the game, but I admire players that place their own limitations in order to achieve their specific immersive goal.

Now, I'm not saying that quality of life changes are bad or shouldn't be applied going forward. Absolutely not. If gameplay and balance is hampered too much, nobody will enjoy the experience, no matter how thematical it is. But I absolutely think that non-gameplay related RPG elements is what is going to help this server continue to feel like Vanilla+ and not something else.

---
With all of that being said, I think we should explore flavorful "friction" to WoW in order to maintain or improve upon its RPG feel. I'm going to share some of my own suggestions, but please feel free to toss in your own ideas.

> This is just at the top of my head. I'll add more or edit existing proposals as time goes on.
>
>  ****Survival Challenge****
> 1. Player needs to "/wave" NPCs in order to talk with them.
> 2. You only regenerate out of combat HP/MP when sitting.
>     1. Any form of in-combat passive regeneration will continue as normal. Such as those obtained from talents.
> 3. Your character will periodically become hungry and/or thirsty
>     1. Failure to feed or quench their thirst will result in a debuff
>     2. Debuff is removed after eating and/or drinking
> 4. Your character must "/sleep" in order to normally log off
>     1. Failure to do so will result in a debuff when logging back on.
>     2. Debuff is removed after visiting an inn/rested area. Subsequently logging off while inside a rested area will not apply the debuff.
> 5. Your character recovers twice as much health/mana while eating/drinking. (we gotta give some benefits given the sitting downside)

I have played less than an hour of EverQuest, but I remember in there you have to write /hail in order to initiate conversation with an NPC. In WoW this was made simpler by allowing interaction with RMB. The idea in EQ is cool, but I think the way WoW handled it is way better and more straightforward. Since I don't come from EQ to WoW, I don't know how EQ to WoW players feel about this in particular.

Regarding this "friction", I think Vanilla is good in this way that a lot of things aren't explained and you have to learn them as you play along. What I think can be done to increase it in a fun way is to add horizontal progression to the game.

Things like expanded weapon skills with some kinds of passive perks you get when you reach a certain threshold.

Quests that completing them awards permanent passive perks or abilities.

Quests that are repeatable or randomized so you can go back to doing them again, even if they yield no XP the second and each subsequent time. If you want to experience a zone you like on a high level character, you can wait for the quests to reset after 48-168 hours or something and repeat them.

Quests that are randomized - each reset has different objectives - one time you have to kill 12 bandits, the next time it's collecting 7 bandit bananas and 5 bandit weapons. Another quest is for killing 12 wolves, the next time is for killing an elite wolf and collecting 6 hides and 6 teeth or something like that. Or it can be completely random - take you to an entirely different part of the zone and have you complete a completely different objective.

Maybe something like a debuff can be added based on character level to add some kind of a challenge to completing the quests or something like that.

Gear can be upgraded further through professions, similar to how Asian F2P MMOs did it - you collect ingredients and go to an NPC and you can upgrade with a chance of failure to have items get grades, like +2, +5, +14 and so forth.

I can see how people can be opposed to the above suggestion since it can change the power drastically, but all the class changes they've made on Turtle WoW are more or less like this and most people don't mind them, only a few very close-minded players are upset about them, because they refuse to adapt.

Another thing can be to have something inspired from Star Wars Galaxies where players can take quests which are individually randomized for each player and after completing them, they learn unique abilities that are permanent. Imagine you're an adventurer and you are bored and exploring places on the map where nobody goes and you find a hidden shack with a hermit inside and the hermit gives you a series of quests and in the end you learn something like a modest healing ability with 5-10 min cooldown. Wouldn't that be cool? And if other people ask you where you got it from, they can't find the same hermit on the same place, but they can find other things that other players can't. Such randomization can encourage people to travel the world more rather than just quest in popular spots in popular zones and then camp in big hubs and just travel to dungeons/raids and otherwise you can't see characters going to remote places of the map, because they have no reason, because exploration isn't rewarding other than you can walljump your way to some place and have a tiny dopamine rush that you've arrived at a new place and after that - nothing.

Even adding unscripted events like NPCs spawning and traveling the world and engaging in activities and players can interact with them one way or another can also be something to make the world feel more alive. Skyirim although being a stupid game (unlike Morrowind) had such events - you travel the read and you see some Imperials escorting a prisoner and you can choose whether you want to let the prisoner escape or not and other similar encounters, they did not have some unique consequences, but it was an attempt, although poor and lazy, to add some life to an open world.

I really stand behind this that Turtle WoW can be expanded sideways in various and unpredictable ways to make every level in the game an interesting adventure with new things to encounter when you least expect them.
