I am a bit torn on the issue myself.

"design, that dictates you need X profession for Y spec" being good or bad is subjective.

I see both sides of the coin.

Having bloodvine and similar BoP makes it so that Engineering cannot be a requirement for minmaxing even more so than just the crit being there. Do u want sappers or do u want best dmg gear is now a legitimate question.

On the other hand limits the brainwashing device, as u can change spec, but we definitely do not want to have it also change your profession (now do we)! And you kind end up coupling spec and profession.

Pros and cons.

This is subjective.

From my point of view the coupling is not that bad. Minmaxing is not required for Turtle content - u can clear all content as a caster w/o ever using bloodvine, u can clear all raids w/o sappers. If desired to achieve (minmax every detail) - make it more difficult.

## Post 28679 by Ugoboom (Bug Report Enthusiast) — 2022-11-07T20:41:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=28679#p28679 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Its indeed subjective, but tbc and wotlk leaned into this design. vanilla didn't, and this is clear. Tailoring only really has this one item as an outstanding issue, and the only real offender was engineering, whose gadgets were meant to be weird oddities that really didnt make a difference when it came to leveling. The designers of engineering as a profession really didn't take into account how the game changes when 20 to 40 raid members all go engineering and start sappering in unison with voice comms. They thought the crit bonus on bloodvine would be an extra nicety to reward tailoring self-crafters, and only the ultra rich who could afford. They really didn't think bloodvine 3set would destroy gearing progression as it becomes very feasable for everyone to get the 3set and switch professions on a whim. They really didn't mean for it to be that powerful, for evidence of this, look at the tanking and leather sets that sit along bloodvine.:Utter garbage that were really only designed for people who only did ZG maybe 2 times and weren't going to raid much further.

This is why it makes sense to go back to what vanilla really started out as, with professions being moneymakers and RP flavor, and not having them be responsible for player power. There's many ways to fix this but the easiest by far is to start with removing restrictions where it makes sense, and where it doesn't instead add alternatives, like with engineering player power.
