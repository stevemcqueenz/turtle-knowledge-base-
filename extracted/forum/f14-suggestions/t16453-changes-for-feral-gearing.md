# Changes for Feral gearing

## Post 112262 by Shambakriger — 2024-12-11T16:08:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=112262#p112262 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

DISCLAIMER: The goal of these changes is to create more flexible gearing for druids as well as a less bloated talent tree with more meaningful choices, while still embracing the identity of Feral as a distinctly hybrid spec. I am not a numbers guy and I have not tested out any of this numerically, I do not wish to necessarily buff feral druids, but to give them a direction. All numbers stated here are completely subject to change, especially those involving set bonuses and talents.

Bears reach both armour and hit cap quite early in their gearing, making late game gearing an annoying task of trying not to overcap on overly abundant stats.
My fix to this is the removal of 2% physical hit from the Natural Weapons talent, 1% hit staying through the addition of +5 weapon skill while in Cat or Bear Form as well as complete removal of the armour cap for bears.

Baseline Change:
Armour cap removed for druids exclusively

Talent Change: Thick Hide
Increases your armour contribution from items and reduces the damage received from critical hits by 3/6/10%

Talent Change: Natural Weapons
Increases the damage you deal with physical attacks in all forms by 3/6/10% and increases your Claw weapon skill while in Cat or Bear form by 1/3/5.
Also increases your chance to hit with spells by 1/2/3%

Talent Change: Feral Adrenaline
Removed (This talent served little purpose other than bloating the feral tree, in my second version I’ve proposed a rework instead)

T3:
For the T3 feral set I've completely removed all +defense and moved most of it to +armour. Defense feels lacklustre as a tanking stat for druids as we don't benefit from 2 out of the 4 effects. Removing defense also allows us to explore other, more unique avenues of itemisation
The other stats have stayed somewhat the same, some of the strength has been changed to agility and +hit has been moved around to make sure that T2 and T3 never have +hit on the same pieces, allowing for easier mixing of the two sets. My goal isn’t to make 9/9 BiS, multiple of the pieces have better alternatives for both mit and DPS and would only be equipped for situations where you’d want the 8/9 set bonus. For Cat some of the pieces are designed as straight upgrades (Head) while others function as a sidegrade with higher mitigation (Hands) and others as alternatives to current BiS but with +hit (Shoulders), some are just straight up bad for Cat (Chest, Boots, Ring)

Set Bonuses: (and thought processes)
 2pc: Increases your chance to dodge by 2% (a minor stat increase that works both in Bear and Cat form, same as the original T3)
 4pc: Swipe now hits an extra target (this would be massively strong for all AOE and would help incentivise wearing 4pc T3 even when just DPSing, if it proves too strong it could be replaced by any similar benefit to swipe. It was placed as the 4pc bonus to allow mixing it with T2 3pc bonus for an optimal AOE set. This was changed from the original to differentiate it from the Warrior set)
 6pc: Any missed attack in Bear Form now generates 10 rage, and your finishing moves in Cat Form now refund 30 energy on a dodge or parry (just a small buff to rage generation while in bear, and a 30 energy refund would smooth out missing a finishing move when powershifting a lot. This was changed due to the original bonus being very small and only benefitting bear)
 8pc: Your barkskin ability infuses your fur with magical energies, reducing the damage of the next five incoming spells by 30% (this is a nerf to the former set bonus as I believe having a 10%DR tied to a set bonus is too strong and therefore confining in gearing choices, my idea instead serves as situational BiS on certain encounters and also specifically exists to save bears against KT frostbolts. I haven’t thought of a Cat set bonus for this one as 8/8 is more situational mit BiS for bears and makes little sense for cats to wear)

T2:
Feral T2 is close to being perfectly itemised, I would in the same vein as I’ve done here move all the +defense to +armour and add set bonuses for Cat Form as well, but apart from that I would not change anything. Feral tier sets should be a mix between mitigation and damage, skewing slightly more toward mitigation. Gearing for Cat should be defined by wearing certain amounts of tier sets for the set bonuses, and then wearing more “glass-cannon” off-pieces. Making Cats wear a mix of mitigation and damage, simply because it’s their BiS (think of Ghoul Skin Tunic) is a great way to incentivise hybrid gameplay.

Some notes on Cat gearing:
Cats suffer from some of the same problems as bears, namely overcapping on abundant stats and having no real valuable alternatives. This is another goal with the change to Natural Weapons. Allowing cats access to weapon skill greatly increases the value of their white hits and therefore the value of haste, this is compounded by cats who choose to powershift less thereby securing more uptime of Tiger’s Fury. These changes would allow alternative gearing paths for cats more focused on staying in form and would help differentiate the two playstyles even further. I’ve chosen to somewhat ignore T1 and T2.5 as I believe creating purely Cat-focused “glass-cannon” sets misses the point of Feral druids entirely. I might propose a rework of them later when I have the time, as I do believe having feral tier sets that deviate from my previous formula could be interesting.

Some notes on talent changes:
Rather than trying to address bears' newfound squishiness only through itemisation, I've chosen to also do it through a reworking of two talents. I've proposed two different versions here, one featuring more changes than the other.
At the moment Bears biggest issues are strings of crits and crushes, rather than trying to remove them through +defense or active mitigation I've instead added talents that help bears better "soak" up the damage
The first and most minor change is the addition of 10% reduced damage from physical critical hits to the Thick Hide talent. This coupled together with the removal of armour cap might just be enough to fix the problem of bears squishiness in later raids.
If Thick Hide being changed does enough for mitigation, I would advocate for removing the talent Feral Adrenaline as it serves little purpose at the moment and bloats up the talent tree.

A second, more extensive version of changes:
,In case more mitigation is needed, I propose an overhaul of Feral Adrenaline instead of removing it, making it useful for both Cat and Bear Form.

Talent Rework: Feral Adrenaline (requires 3 points in Thick Hide)
When suffering a physical critical hit while in Bear Form you only take the base damage as an intitial hit, and the critial multiplier staggered over 2/4/6 seconds  This effect can only occur once every 6 seconds.
Additionally, while in Cat Form your finishing moves grant you a 5% increase to damage, stacks up to 1/2/3 times. This buff is lost when leaving Cat Form

This is a mechanic somewhat copied from the Brewmaster monks of later WoW expansions, but I think it fits as a distinct form of mitigation unique to druids, and fits the fantasy of being a giant bear, soaking up big critical hits. The added benefits for Cat Form exist to encourage switching between both play styles, even if leaning more heavily into bear mitigation talents. The talent is tailored for staying in form rather than powershifting, this is to allow more bear focused builds where you might drop the 5 points in Furor, but still retain power in Cat Form.
This talent might be wholly unnecessary in case the removal of the armour cap proves to be a big enough boost to mitigation, which I suspect it might. In that case numbers could be tweaked either by removing Feral Adrenaline and disregarding this rework, or perhaps through nerfing other aspects of Bear survivability like my proposed 10% crit damage reduction through Thick Hide.

Concerns and feedback, a short answer to some questions posted on the TWoW Druid Discord:
“This would buff bears too much”
This might be the case, and if so I’d still argue for nerfing both these changes and other aspects of feral accordingly to reach a fitting balance. The main idea behind these changes is to take bear mitigation in a different direction that feels better to gear for, for gear progression, and for the general theme of the class (answer by Tephus)
“This would buff cats too much”
Giving cats +5 weapon skill will be a big boost to damage, especially for non-powershifters. It might be enough damage to warrant a nerf elsewhere, in that case I would make sure that the powershifting rotation still comes out on top as I believe the highest skill rotation deserves the highest DPS.
“Removing armour cap is too fundamental of a change”
I’d argue that it’s way less fundamental than a lot of the other CC2 changes, and that removing caps will become a necessity in post-naxx content. Just imagine what the bear-focused T4 will look like with the current stat distribution not being able to give us any more armour?
“Instead of removing +defense we should look to make it more useful for bears by altering the scaling or adding additional effects”
This is a fine point and one that I wouldn’t be opposed to, but for me it should come after the removal of the armour cap as gearing around the cap is only going to become more and more of a nuisance as we go further into post-naxx progression.

I’d love to hear your thoughts and feedback!

Some thought on the recently announced balance changes for feral druids dec 20th:
I believe the devs original intention was to create an alternative to powershifting but that failed since the original Open Wounds build still benifitted massively from powershifting. The new change to Ancient Brutality, letting ticks from DoTs grant +energy, is a nice incentive to actually stay in form. It seems like the Vampirism was removed from the talent, it was mostly useful for soloing and AoE but its weird to nerf an already not great bear talent.
Open Wounds change is nice, having it simply tied to active DoTs is a lot simpler and more manageable. The wording atm implies that the damage bonus will be as large from having a 1cp Rip up, not sure if that's intentional but it would allow for easier weaving of Ferocious Bite into the rotation.
Nice to see the FB talents finally getting buffed a bit, my prediction regarding the bleeds playstyle is that it will be intended for bear MTs who don't want to powershift. They'll be able to skip Furor and instead pick up all Bear mitigation talents in the feral tree. I think it's important to keep the bleeds playstyle at no more than 90%DPS of Powershifting, or we risk homogenising the class entirely.
Atm there are no listed changes that would benefit powershifting cats, except for the change to FB scaling on Feral Aggression. We are still quite far below a lot of other classes on DPS, so I hope that we'll see some powershifting buffs if cat DPS continues being this subpar. I still believe that granting Ferals +5 weapon skill through the Natural Weapons talent would help a lot.
For Bears we mainly saw nerfs, Swipe was admittedly a bit too strong perhaps, but it did feel amazing going into Bear form wearing full Cat gear and just swiping away. Frenzied Regen buff was needed as the original nerf was way too severe. Feral Adrenaline is no longer completely useless, but still seems like a very niche talent, if they intend to keep it i'd follow the same route as Ancient Brutality and add some Cat synergy to it as well.
I dislike the Savage Bite change as it makes Bears already slow gameplay even slower, I'd much rather that the threat modifier was dialed back a bit to reach a similar result. Bear catch-up threat is still very bad and missing a 9sec CD Savage Bite is going to feel horrible.
I still stand by all of my points in the original post, excited to see how these changes play out ingame.
Thanks to the team for doing everything they do
