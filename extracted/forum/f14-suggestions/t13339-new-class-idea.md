# New class idea

## Post 89690 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2024-03-08T13:26:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=89690#p89690 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!

I was reading older articles in this topic and I want to share with you my take on this. This will be long.
As far as I have seen, this topic has two main approaches: adding a new class or adding two new classes, both of them being faction-specific.
The former is easier while the latter gives more flavor but can also cause balance problems. Since the main server has cross-faction, PVE is not really touched by it but this can cause a PVP nightmare. Currently we only have shamans and paladins locked behind factions and seeing the madness of retri in PVP, this can lead to balance issues.
I will take the optimistic approach and assume that in case of big balance problems (either based on individual class power level or based on PVP faction imbalances), those will be handled by the team. I still believe that retri will be handled for PVP (while some aspects of paladin can easily be pushed in PVE - specially for prot) and I assume that if balance goes wrong anywhere, TWOW Team intervenes eventually, so I go with 2 new classes for this article.

The first thing I want to check when creating a new class is the number of classes available to each race. Optimally the new class shouldn't be available for those who already have a bunch of playable classes and should focus on those who only have a few.

Number of available classes per race:
 * Human -> 7
 * Dwarf, High Elf -> 6
 * Gnome, Night Elf -> 5
 * Troll -> 7
 * Orc, Undead -> 6
 * Goblin -> 5
 * Tauren -> 4

So these new classes have to have a specific flavor based on which they are not suitable for humans and trolls but taurens surely get it. Optimally the horde class should be tauren + goblin while the alliance class should be night elf + gnome.
This makes things a bit hard though because if I follow my heart I go by witch doctor and bard. But excluding trolls from witch doctors and humans from bards is a crime.

For horde I'd go with monk. Stealing from retail is not very creative but it has a weird fit in my opinion on several levels. We don't need to steal the whole idea of the monk from retail but it can be a good starting point.
Disclaimer: introducing monks doesn't mean introducing pandarens.

 **What would being a monk mean to an Azeroth which lacks the pandas?**

Lorewise I'd imagine these monks as energy users, just like rogues and cats. These beings of Azeroth have decided to hone their hand-to-hand combat skills. While out of combat, they frequently meditate to seek their inner wisdom and to raise their awareness of the world around them. Aquiring a flow, a 'one-ness' with their environment allows them to perform some actions others would consider magic, just by alternating the flow of the world around them.

 **Why wouldn't the monk class fit for:**
 * Orcs -> Orcs are way too hot-headed and choleric to find the truth through meditation. The more spiritual and calmer exceptions like Thrall reach their truth based on the traditional elements and shamanistic teachings.
 * Undeads-> The undeads' soul would not have the health and intensity to merge with their environment while reaching the inner peace through the flow.
 * Trolls are extremely spiritual but when they reach towards inner wisdom or when they seek higher truths, their culture determines where they go: a troll in a spiritual journey can go to the way of spirits, the way of the holy light or towards the way of the demons. For them, any other route is unknown.

 **Why would it work for:**
 * Taurens -> Taurens can be shamans, just like orcs and trolls but they are different in a way. Their spirituality relies on harmony and peace which can be manifested through shamanistic or through druidistic ways. Their mean principle is peace and balance for which they may take whichever path that resonates with it. Being a monk is indeed a way towards peace.
 * Goblins -> This is a weird take but hear me out. Certain members of the Durotar Labor Union started to feel fatigue in their work and soon their inventive spirit started to fade. They found less and less joy in the stereotypical goblin way of living and as a radical solution they started to travel to find a place where they can find the spark which got lost in the meantime. Eventually they found it in Mulgore, learning from tauren masters. The goblins, being goblins, left the place after a while and with they newly-found spark they are businesmen at day, sages at the night. They implemented the way of the monk in their lifestyle to avoid burn-out.

 **What would a monk do?**
I'd rework retail here, another druid- or pala-like 'I can be anything' is too much in my opinion for TWOW. Since they use energy, while I'd allow some magic-like abilities to them - I don't think they should be near as adept at magic-related things as mana-users. I'd exclude healer from the options. In my vision, a monk can be a tank or a melee DPS.
I really want another viable tank to TWOW anyways. In dungeons you can see 3 of them (warrior, druid, pala), at later raids 2 (warrior, druid) and at Naxx usually just a warrior - if we don't count gigachads like Shockwarden of course. Adding some variety to the raid comp with a new tank is always a fun thing to do in my opinion.
We could also get rid of the weird build names retail has. Brewmaster etc. This class is not defined by being a drunken bear after all.

Sample general abilities:
 * Single target stun with decent damage on a 1 min CD
 * Parry automatically launching a counterattack. This autoattack doesn't cancel any talent which requires casting time and was in progress by the monk
 * Dazing attacks
 * Rolling movement with +40% movement speed in-fight, but only towards a party member (can't be used to run away)
 * Focus - party-wide agility buff

 **Build-wise I'd imagine them as per below:**

 **Tank build ->**
 * No shields, leather armor
 * High dodge, increased parry, no block
 * Parries being followed up by counter-attack for threat generation and damage
 * Talent for damage reduction and to negate crushing blows
 * Better at single-target tanking, having some cleaves but no aoe attacks
 * Instead of a shield wall effect, a high CD talent which drastically increases dodge for some time.

Sample abilities:
 * Self-buff, giving your melee hits +x% threat
 * Talent negating crushing blows and reducing the damage taken from critical hits
 * Talent to give +x% armor after gear
 * Increased parry and dodge chance
 * Big CD ability, drastically increasing dodge chance for 5 sec
 * Adding them a taunt
 * Upon dodge giving a 'confused' debuff to the enemy which gives a slight haste penalty for some seconds
 * A shout-like ability which decreases enemy crit chance (slightly)

 **DPS1 build, the more support-oriented one ->**
 * Focuses on the flow with the environment, specializing in those 'almost magic' feats I mentioned earlier
 * Great at helping allies with some quick-to-expire buff, including added hit chance, haste or crit chance (big variety but each fades after 10 sec)
 * Despite being a DPS class, some its melee attacks heal nearby allies (like a shadow priest but weaker healing ratio)
 * Decent on single-target-DPS but lacks cleave or aoe
 * Fun idea -> This build could kick out the curse from someone as a way of decursing
 * DPS-wise I'd put them in the shadow priest-cat area because of the added utilities (cat should be buffed tbh)

Sample abilities:
 * Decurse kick - an aoe, non-dmaging kick which kicks out the curse from any raid members within 5 yards. Gameplay-wise you can roll fast to each group and groundhouse-kick the curse out of them (5 sec CD)
 * A talent which adds healing to the melee damage, based on a ratio of the damage dealt
 * Specific attacks on CD which give short-duration party buffs. Let's say that you have 8 punches like this. They share a global CD of 30 sec but each of them has a 3 min individual CD. Meaning that you can use one every half minute but you can't use the one you just used for 3 minutes. This build would mostly revolve around the buffs it gives to the party this way. Each buff has the duration of 10 sec. These buffs are party-wide.
 1. Added melee crit chance punch.
 2. Added ranged crit chance punch.
 3. Added agility punch.
 4. Added health renegeration punch.
 5. Added mana regeneration punch.
 6. Reducing threat after damage and healing punch.
 7. Added hit chance punch.
 8. Strength buff punch.

 **DPS2 build, pure DPS ->**
 * Limited utility but great self-utility
 * Great at single-target-DPS and decent with aoe. Has no cleave
 * DPS-wise I'd put them at rogue DPS-level

Sample abilities:
 * Added damage to melee skills
 * A double melee-attack on a 30 sec CD
 * An aoe kick
 * Changing the roll ability enabling to roll towards enemies too and dealing damage when they crash with them (but adding CD to it)

Monks would have added damage with staffs and fist weapons.
Monks could wear cloth and leather.
Monks use energy.
Monks use relics.

Disclaimer: monk kicks deal the same amount of damage as normal melee hits.

 **Gnomes and Night Elves -> The Hybrids**

Once upon a time there was a gnomish scientific center in Ashenvale which analyzed the local plants and animals. The night elves helped them since the gnomish method didn't include hurting the living. The elves were happy because the gnomes' intellectual way helped them further understanding nature while the gnomes were also happy since the local flora and fauna was interesting. This idyll was short-lived though as a curse was casted upon the beings who worked there. Some of the local spirits of the forest were against this research, considering it a sacrilege. Those affected by the curse started to behave more savagely and had sudden bursts of rage. The society still accepted them back and the Alliance started to work on a cure, up until a day when the cure research facilities near Ironforge were attacked by gnolls. Several hybrid showed an exceptional power while fighting the enemies back and some of those started to consider themselves blessed, not cursed. Upon seeing what a hybrid is capable of, some of those who were cursed decided to stay this way and fight for the Alliance in this savage state.

 **What would a hybrid do?**
Let's say that we have 4 roles: healer, caster, melee and tank. If we count in monks, horde currently has 1 faction-specific tank (monk), 1 faction-specific caster (shaman). 3 faction-specific melee (monkx2, shaman), 1 faction-specific healer (shaman). From alliance, paladins can be tanks, melee or healers. To have a balance here, I'd advise hybrids to have 2 melee specs and 1 caster spec.
A hybrid is a class using rage, even for casting. They buff themselves up and rush to the opponent, except for the caster archetype which uses his rage to funnel damaging spells upon the enemies.

Sample general abilities:
 * Self-buffs, which play a central role at a hybrid's lifestyle (15 minutes duration)
Disclaimer: Hybrids have a limit on the number of self-buffs they can apply at once. Let it be 2.
 * Self-buff for melee crit chance
 * Self-buff for spell crit chance
 * Self-buff for bonus rage after dealing damage
 * Self-buff for haste
 * Self-buff for armor
 * Self-buff for movement speed
 * Self-buff for melee hit chance
 * Self-buff for spell hit chance
 * Melee cleave
 * Charge-like ability
 * Shock-like short ranged nature and frost type spells, which cost rage
 * Having the 'nature warlock curse' for 3 minutes which they can apply with a critical melee hit, this is automatic once the talent is learnt

 **Build-wise I'd imagine them as per below:**
Melee1 build, the more support-oriented one ->
 * Lower DPS, akin to shadow priests and cats
 * Their main gameplay is buffing the party with the most applicable buff and putting up nature curse besides occassional shocks and Alliance zugzug
 * Cleave can crit allowing putting up the curse on several opponents at once

Sample abilities:
 * Using any of the above self-buffs as party buff. They can change the buff anytime for the rage cost
 * A nature damage-based weapon buff which reacts to the 'nature curse'

 **Melee2 build, the more DPS-oriented one ->**
 * Cannot buff the party but having stronger self-buffs
 * Damage is similar to rogue
 * No aoe

Sample abilities:
 * Enabling a 3rd self-buff slot
 * Melee crits enhance self buffs, for example turning the 2% crit chance one into 3% for 10 seconds
 * Big single-target hit talent which can only be used after a cleave
 * Hitting themselves with an immediate auto-hit, receiving double the rage from both the attack and from the damage taken. 30 sec CD
 * Melee crits giving them twice the rage they otherwise give

 **Caster build ->**
Rage-using caster, I have to elaborate here.
As a caster you don't wanna get damaged so your initial rage generation comes from talents. You'd have 2 ways to build up damage for a fight. One of them is an aura you can cast on yourself, slowly doting you until you cancel it. You get double the rage for the damage you'd normally get. There would also be a baseline spell with mediocre damage (requiring no initial rage), building up your rage. Once you have enough rage, many spells become available. Some give buffs to rage generation, allowing you to use better talents and deal more damage. But the best talents you can use have drawbacks, for example adding you a debuff, halving your rage generation for 5 seconds or giving you a -30% spell damage debuff for the same amount of time. As a caster with rage you can go infinite in a long raid boss, no need to refill. But you also come with disadvantages - slower build-up towards the good spells and using the best spells makes you rage-starved for a while. Also, you want to rush pulls to keep up rage.

Would allow for them decent spells with low-rage requirement so that trash clearing doesn't feel like a base-spell fest but most encounter would start with 1-2 cast of the rage-generating, base range attack.
Their biggest disadvantage would be the low DPS at fast-cleared packs because they don't have the time to build up. Other than that, long fights would see them having serious highs and lows in damage - after using an ultimate-like ability, the debuff they receive takes them back for some seconds. Not dispellable.
Their strengths would be never going dry (this alone is not enough, an arms warrior never goes dry in Naxx but no sane person would recruit him), having high damage spikes after the build up and having some potent but unreliable debuffs. Debuffs are applied with crits so this build gets the more reliable the better-geared it is. But again, putting up a debuff, specially if a fight is short is not consequent. If they don't hit with a crit, the debuff doesn't come.
As a unique debuff - to avoid them being too strong with high-level gear and spamming big spells infinitely, they also receive a unique debuff: above 30 rage they receive a spell hit chance debuff (1%) which increases by 10 rage points, which means a 7% hit chance penalty if the build really overdoes it with Naxx gear and just spams the big spells. The gameplay of this build would be to build-up enough rage to cast a high-variety of damaging spells while keeping the rage in control to avoid unnecessary misses.
Disclaimer: When starting to cast a spell with a rage, we have to be sure that we have enough rage when the casting time finishes and we actually cast the spell. Starting to cast a 20 rage-cost spell at 21 rage and finishing it with 19 rage work as an interrupt on yourself. You still got the remainder of your rage though.

Sample 'spells':
 * 2 sec cast time, no CD - deals (low-medium) damage
 * 1 sec cast time, 5 sec CD, 20 rage cost - deals (high) damage, gives back the invested rage if the opponent is below 30% HP
 * 2 sec cast time, 5 sec CD, 20 rage cost -  deals (medium) aoe damage
 * 2 sec cast time, no CD, 10 rage cost - deals (medium) damage, gives you a (low) passive rage regen for 10 sec
 * 1 sec cast time, 3 min CD, 50 rage cost - deals (really high) damage in an area but gives you a spell damage debuff of 30% for 5 sec
 * instant, 3 min CD, 20 rage cost - your next spell crit gives a buff to you, allowing your next 5 damaging spells to decrease the CD on all of your abilities by 1 sec
 * instant, 1 min CD, no rage cost - giving you a passive rage regen for 10 sec, can be used out of combat, too
 * instant 3 min CD, no rage cost - your next spell is guaranteed to hit and gives you 150% of the rage you'd normally get
 * instant, 5 rage - you lose 1% of your life every 2 seconds and receive double the rage amount for the damage taken. Can be cancelled anytime.
 * instant, 1 min CD - lose all your rage, regain half that many life (percentually -> losing 20% of your ragebar restores 10% of your life)

Hybrids would have added critical hit chance with staffs and maces.
Hybrids could wear cloth and leather (nature fantasy).
Hybrids use rage, they need to build it up.
As a memento of their closeness to nature and sympathy towards the goal of the druids, hybrids use idols.
