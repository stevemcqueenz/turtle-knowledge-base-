# Language Skills and Abilities

## Post 41546 by Khalthul — 2023-04-14T16:55:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=41546#p41546 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hello Turtles,

Being that we are a roleplay server, advanced in our diplomacy with Alliance and Horde to come together and fight world-ending enemies abroad... I feel it somewhat uncanny, though convenient, that we all speak the same language. I write this as a Horde player primarily, so it is in that perspective for the most part, but I do give the Alliance its due.

Essentially I think it would give an extra dimension of immersion if you had the choice to speak from a set of languages that you have learned since birth or from prolonged experience and reputation with another race.

Say, if you choose Forsaken as your race, you would automatically be equipped with the skill of [Orcish] and [Gutterspeak], and have the ability to toggle either of those Tongues similar to "Find Herbs" or "Sense Demons." If you are a Warlock, you could perhaps acquire the demonic tongue, [Eredun], which only warlocks who know [Eredun] will be able to understand you *(and maybe Curse of Tongues doesn't effect Warlocks, but that is a whole other matter*).

An Orc who is not well-versed in [Gutterspeak] is a normal occurrence. But the skill can be gained by one's reputation with Undercity. The more an Orc or Troll or Tauren who works for the Forsaken, the more they would naturally develop that ability (or so that would be the explanation). Or, at the Forsaken's discretion, he or she can choose to speak in Orcish, but still have entrusting side conversations with their undead brothers and sisters without any tribal Horde ears prying.

When someone becomes a diplomat in order to activate the Cross-Faction side of Turtle WoW, they are automatically equipped with the [Common] or [Orcish] tongue depending on their opposing side. This would be the easiest solution for universal communication between Horde and Alliance for them to both possess the ear for [Common] and [Orcish], but in speaking they can choose what tongue to speak in.

What I want to avoid with this idea is a mandatory gathering of all tongues, and thus I wouldn't want to see every character grabbing languages from a trainer just to yield the same universal result we have currently. I think if there are any problems or divides in communication, /party or /raid chat is universally understood as they are. /say or /yell is in the chosen tongue of the speaker.

That means if you are an Alliance player, a Human for instance, and you are participating in the story of a Horde caravan of tauren, trolls, and orcs, you would understand the [Orcish], but the sidebar [Tauren] and [Troll] conversations would escape you, yet it would not keep you from the whole experience, instead it would add to the immersion. That is the whole of my argument in adding a system like this.

...

If we want to go a step deeper, in a more realistic, but more difficult-to-implement solution... it would be to add a lingua franca language that Horde and Alliance diplomats use when trying to speak to each other, aptly named [Azerese], so as to preserve the integrity of [Orcish] and [Common] communication and culture. A Dwarven diplomat, for example, who works with the Horde would know [Dwarven], [Common], and [Azerese], but not [Orcish]. From a more advanced roleplaying standpoint, this seems to make the most sense to me, but would not be without its own set of complications. A [Translator's Aura] ability might help, in allowing their known languages to be automatically understood to those who don't know, but seeing those everywhere you go would be a bit annoying, and it would take away the cultural exclusivity that this post is trying to promote.

Thank you for your time in listening to my ruminations,
