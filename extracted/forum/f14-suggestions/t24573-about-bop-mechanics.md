# About BoP mechanics

## Post 172920 by Alexianon — 2026-03-28T17:49:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=172920#p172920 | page 1 | era: post-1.18.1 -->

Dear Community,

My goal is to highlight a number of fundamental mechanics that, in my opinion, conflict with the idea of ​​an open world and a healthy economy. I hope this will spark discussion.

 1. Bind on Pickup (BoP) kills the open world and trade
The "bind on pickup" mechanic was introduced in 2004 to encourage players to join raids, extend the lifespan of content, and create a sense of mass participation—hundreds of players simultaneously in raid zones creates the impression of a living, vibrant world, which was important for the first massively popular MMO to gain traction. However, in the context of Turtle WoW, which has added many new features and positions itself as an alternative to the official classic, BoP becomes an artificial limitation that:

Kills trade. The best items (raid and quest items) cannot be sold. A merchant, no matter how wealthy, will never be able to buy them. This defeats the very idea of ​​economic freedom.

It creates a raid monopoly. The only way to obtain top-tier gear is to raid. Players who don't enjoy raiding (or can't schedule them) are left behind. Crafting is merely a temporary "catch-up" for them, not a fully-fledged alternative.

It contradicts the spirit of an open world. In an open world, players should have freedom of choice: raid, farm, or trade. BoP takes away this freedom.

Item rarity should be regulated by drop rates (and, consequently, price increases), and its decline should be controlled by wear and tear and the release of new items.

 2. Crafting is "second-class."
Crafted items are inherently weaker than their raid counterparts. They are only needed as temporary gear before a raid or for those who can't obtain the raid version. Craftsmen are becoming "service" for raiders, needed only for consumables. Crafting as a standalone aspect of the game doesn't work.

 3. Absurdities in Blacksmithing
Items that are bound during creation. The blacksmith spends rare materials but can't sell the resulting items. This turns crafting into "self-service" rather than a business.

Items that require the Blacksmith skill to equip. A blacksmith selling such an item is forced to find a buyer who is also a Blacksmith (which limits the market to a minimum). If they want to wear the item themselves, it's more profitable for them to forge the item themselves rather than buy it at auction.

 4. The crafting economy is broken
The auction house has an absurd situation: the cost of crafting materials for an item can be, say, 370 gold, but the item itself is listed for 600, even though it's obviously weaker than its raid counterpart. This is a consequence of artificial scarcity (rare recipes locked in raids) and minimal demand. As a result, the seller inflates the price to recoup costs, while the buyer sees an unreasonable price tag and doesn't buy. The market doesn't work.

Conclusion
Turtle WoW is a unique project that has already done a lot to develop the classic game. However, the economy and crafting remain in the state they were in 2004. This is unfair to players who want to develop as artisans and merchants.

I'm not offering ready-made solutions—I hope they will emerge through discussion. But I believe it's important to acknowledge the problems.

The best medicine for the market and the economy is self-regulation, not rules (mechanics). I consider replacing BoP mechanics with BoE to be the most urgent solution.

Sincerely

Zvyrhol,

I've only been playing WoW for a few months, and I encourage you to take my opinion as an unbiased one from a new player. Of course, I understand your arguments, and to a certain extent, I agree with you.
The thoughts I've written are what I didn't like and, in my opinion, can be improved while maintaining a balance between the gameplay itself and the game's appeal. The main things I didn't like were item binding and, as a result, dead professions (it's essentially possible to play without them altogether, and buy consumables with gold, which has nowhere else to spend in the late game, meaning a small number of crafting players are needed).
For example, if a legendary item with a BoP mechanic has a 1:100 drop rate, you can change this rate to 1:10,000 with a BoE mechanic, which will automatically adjust its price (for example, to a level that makes it unobtainable without guild support) and open up new options for players. This was a hypothetical example, but it's entirely possible to calculate drop rates mathematically without disrupting the server economy, and, if desired, to automatically correlate them, say, based on the number of players and other conditions.
I didn't quite understand the idea about P2W play (you meant gold grinding for items or RMT). I'll comment on real money. In any case, there's no protection against unfair play. Yes, buying gold with real money doesn't make much sense right now. But there are people who provide raid escorts for item grinding, or who you can give account information to, and they'll independently obtain these items in raids, etc. (most likely, a number of other services are also available for purchase). So, this problem currently exists with the BoP mechanics (as with any other multiplayer game). Moreover, BoE is creating a legal channel for trade, which could reduce the demand for illegal gold, since players will be able to earn money through crafting and trading, not just farming.

I disagree with you here.
BoP mechanics don't offer a choice, but rather impose a single, possible path for the late game. In the BoE system, achievement doesn't disappear: you still have to accumulate valuable items (gold), which in WoW also requires time and skill (it's just a different path).
For a player who doesn't enjoy raiding (or can't schedule it), "adventure" becomes a chore, and the open world, crafting, and trading are also adventures that don't make much sense at the moment.

To summarize the above.

I'm not saying that abandoning BoP is a panacea. Rather, I'm suggesting we consider how to make this game world better. And I'm convinced that a balanced solution can be found that will improve the gameplay rather than destroy it.
