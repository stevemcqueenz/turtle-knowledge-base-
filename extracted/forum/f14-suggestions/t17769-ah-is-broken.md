# AH is broken.

## Post 121145 by WoWentheusiast — 2025-02-27T18:11:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=121145#p121145 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

The Auction House is one of the key features of TurtleWoW, designed to create a vibrant economy where players can buy and sell goods to their hearts' content. However, as many of you have observed, the system is now showing signs of severe strain, and it’s important to discuss the root causes of this problem.

The crux of the issue lies in the inherent greed of a few players. These individuals are manipulating the market for their personal gain at the expense of the broader player base. This has led to inflated prices, scarce resources, and an overall decrease in the accessibility of essential items for many players.

This systemic problem begins with market manipulation, a strategy where a small number of players buy out entire categories of items, creating artificial scarcity. They then relist these items at exorbitant prices, making it difficult for average players to afford the goods they need for their in-game progression. This practice not only exploits the game's economy but also diminishes the enjoyment and fairness of the game for countless others.

Additionally, there's the issue of gold farming and selling, which has become rampant. Some players and external entities farm gold extensively and sell it for real-world money. This influx of gold into the game skews the economy, giving those willing to spend real money an unfair advantage. This gold is then often used to further manipulate the Auction House, exacerbating the problem.

Furthermore, the lack of effective regulation and oversight by the game's developers allows these practices to continue unabated. While there have been attempts to curb these activities, the measures taken so far have proven inadequate in addressing the root causes.

The result is an unbalanced economy where the rich get richer and the average player struggles to make ends meet. This not only affects the in-game experience but also the community's overall morale. Players are left feeling frustrated and disenfranchised, which can lead to a decline in the game's active player base.

To address these issues, a multi-faceted approach is necessary. Firstly, stricter regulations and monitoring of the Auction House activities must be implemented. The developers need to identify and take action against market manipulators and gold sellers. This could include temporary or permanent bans for repeat offenders, as well as more sophisticated tracking systems to detect suspicious activities.

Secondly, introducing price caps on essential items could help to prevent exorbitant pricing and make goods more accessible to all players. While this may not entirely solve the problem, it would be a step towards creating a more balanced economy.

Lastly, fostering a community-driven approach to economic fairness can make a significant difference. Encouraging players to report suspicious activities and providing incentives for fair trading practices can help to create a more equitable environment.
