## Post 14347 by Lahire (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2021-12-04T08:01:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=14347#p14347 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Shadow Hunter is a WC3 hero but he is already clearly in the game : troll priest and shaman are analogous to the Shadow Hunter. The class design was voluntarily made broader than WC3 heroes (for ex. the war class represents Blademaster, Mountain King and Demon Hunter).

The designers' intent was : a class too narrow is quickly boring, and you don't feel different enough from other players if you are all playing Illidan or Muradin. RP feels worse. So they created a layered and flexible class personalisation system, which incorporates : race choice, class choice, talent choice and gear choice. The idea was : from the articulation of these 4 systems, you can express your identity as a player, or mimic one of the heroes of WC3 if that's your fancy.

The broader class, then specialized slowly via talents and gear, is also essential to produce the feeling of adventure and progression vanilla is known for. You begin as a lil undefined "orc warrior", you end as a musclebound Blademaster ; for example. If you are a "hero" from the beginning, this progression is lost. That's why the Demon Hunter class in retail struggles to feel right. It is too powerful, too iconic, too acrobatic from the start, and then never really evolves. It feels linear : it feels bad. It's just a power fantasy, not a RPG fantasy.

By suggesting narrow classes, iconic hero classes from WC3, players go directly against this design intent. WC3 Heroes are too narrow to permit the RPG feel the designers were seeking. I understand the attraction (each player wants to be the hero of the story ofc), but from a designer perspective, it's not what vanilla was aiming for.

You should know the game before adding to it.

There are some hard-coded features that make adding to the game difficult. Like the maximum of six classes per race.

You have Trolls: Warrior, Shaman, Hunter, Rogue, Priest and Mage - 6
Orcs: Warrior, Shaman, Hunter, Rogue, Warlock and Mage - 6
Undead: Warrior, Hunter, Rogue, Mage, Warlock, Priest - 6

Dwarves: Warrior, Paladin, Hunter, Rogue, Priest, Mage - 6
High Elves: Warrior, Paladin, Hunter, Rogue, Priest, Mage - 6
Humans: Warrior, Paladin, Rogue, Mage, Priest, Warlock - 6

Those races can't have classes added to them. Adding a completely new class is far more difficult than adding existing classes to the races. You have to balance the class against the old ones, implement a working talent tree and ranks of different abilities WITH unique casting animations. Unless you are a whale able to invest thousands to both stabilize the server, finance the whole process and then continue financing the business and provide the technical abilities I don't see how that can happen. Limit your dreams to what you can achieve yourself or finance, look out for hard-coded client limits and act accordingly.

For reference the rest of class slots:

Night Elves: Warrior, Hunter, Rogue, Druid, Priest - 5
Gnomes: Warrior, Hunter, Rogue, Mage, Warlock - 5

Goblins: Warrior, Hunter, Rogue, Mage, Warlock - 5
Tauren: Warrior, Hunter, Shaman, Druid - 4

If you want to remain in lore and fix the imbalance of class options between factions some people and I once found enough evidence that mage for Tauren is funnily enough the most lore-friendly option since Grimtotem Geomancers and Pyromancer Loregrain are representing that class and those abilities ingame.

Adding a new class is far more difficult both financially and technically AND can't happen where there are already six classes.
