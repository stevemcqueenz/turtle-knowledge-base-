Let me expand on this a bit.
Everyone probably knows the story of how Blizzard suddenly realized *sarcasm* that statistically, people generally prefer characters in games that *suddenly* look like humans. And they're heavily biased toward Alliance players, so they urgently need to introduce blood elves for the Horde, lol.
Although it would seem the solution is right before our eyes.
The Forsaken are dead humans (I've always found it amusing that, for example, the spirits of the Horde heroes (who can now be seen at the gates of Orgrimmar during the seasonal event) have undead models, while in quests, the spirits of dead humans look like... *suddenly* ghostly humans). Give players the opportunity to choose which game model they prefer, just like they later did with the worgen. Also, giving them a sprint speed without a mount, which the tauren initially had but then scrapped (though I think it's implemented in yours?), plunges us into the crazy inner workings of this game's development.
My point is that WoW is a hodgepodge of different, often questionable, decisions. Like the constant confusion with models, where Sylvanas is literally a dark elf, lol. Or in the ruins of the ancient elven empire, there are statues of perfectly good high elves, who, in theory, acquired their appearance much later, having already sucked from the sunwell.
Basically, the idea is that you don't have to follow in their footsteps and learn from their mistakes. You need to learn from their mistakes.

I'm truly convinced that the option to choose a human appearance for the Forsaken is a good choice. It will attract new players to this faction. And let's be honest, in a game where everything revolves around gear, the Forsaken's clothes look like they were buried in them and are almost indistinguishable. Just go to the donation shop and click on the items offered to the Forsaken. Only the helmets are distinguishable. Everything else is simply pointless and not worth the money.

To save me the trouble, I'll say a few words about the ruins of Lordaeron. When Blizzard designed the faction capitals, it seems they completely failed to understand how important it is for players to have a place where they can show off their new mount or the item they've earned. Undercity, by design, is simply the worst place for meetings, using the bank, and the auction house—I tell you this, loving it with all my heart.

This problem could be solved by introducing a truly small plaza in the city on the surface, with quick access through a side gate. A bank, an auction house, a tavern, and outdoor seating. No new textures needed; everything is already in the game. And the Horde would have an alternative to the (overcrowded) Orgrimmar, for those who don't appreciate its brutalist style.

And finally, I beg you, if you decide to continue making new locations for the Otreshiye, don't use the style from the original game. Words cannot describe the shock my colleagues and I, who had championed this faction for years, felt when the Brill Tavern was taken from us, turning it into some kind of birdhouse.

And finally, one last thing. This is more lore-related, but it's actually both a question and a suggestion.

Regarding the Forsaken lore retcon.

When we played classic WoW as the Forsaken, three facts immediately hit us:

 1. You are the Chosen One. The "Will of the Forsaken" skill literally points you to this. You are one of the few who was able to regain free will (with the help of the Dark Lady, of course) and escape the Lich King's control. You are almost certainly somewhat insane, after everything you've been through (roleplay it), but you are actually a pretty cool person.

 2. The Light has abandoned us. The vast majority of us were once members of the Church of Light. But now the Light doesn't answer us; in fact, it's literally burning us.

The fact that priests can cast light in the game is a mechanical concession, just like the fact that, although we breathe longer underwater, we still suffocate, even though we don't need air at all, not even at all. And the fact that anti-undead skills don't work for us (though in some early versions they seemed to work, according to old-timers).

A new religion is being formed, the Cult of the Forgotten Shadow. Join it or seek other paths, the choice is yours.

 3. We are doomed. Absolutely. The Forsaken faction has a strong aversion to necromancy, as necromancers are (former or current) members of the Cult of the Damned, i.e., their worst enemies. Without necromancy, we cannot replenish our numbers, and therefore our extinction is a matter of time. Live with it (lol). Dead horses as mounts and Apothecaries stitching together abominations kind of added to the whole thing. And then there's the quest where we establish contact with a lich (lol what?!) who doesn't look like a lich.

We got this from in-game quests, which were quite controversial, and from the Warcraft tabletop roleplaying game, which was later declared non-canon, to our surprise, bewilderment, and horror.

And then came everything Blizzard did to our faction, yes.

Actually, the question is: How do the Turtle WoW administration view the Forsaken? Where can I find out more?
Frankly, I'm already confused by the surviving Lordaeronians in the mountains, calling themselves the "Lordaeron army" and wearing a cloak with the Lion of Stormwind. Well, anyone who played Warcraft III knows perfectly well what happened to the Lordaeron army. The Scarlet Crusade is made up of living people, largely thanks to the secret intercession of the Burning Legion, as I see it.
In those same mountains, the quest giver says something like "we wanted to swell our ranks," referring to a certain cemetery. So, does that mean they're already resurrecting the dead? Without waiting for all the Wrath of the Lich King fun and the Valkyr expansion?
