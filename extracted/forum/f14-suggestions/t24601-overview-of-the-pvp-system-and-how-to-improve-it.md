# Overview of the PvP system and how to improve it.

## Post 173079 by Runbunnyrun — 2026-03-29T20:47:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=173079#p173079 | page 1 | era: post-1.18.1 -->

After discussing PvP on the Discord as well as in game with other players, I have decided to summarize the issues with the new system, hoping that it will reach the Turtle Team. The shortcomings are numerous, and in my estimation all stem from the same root causes: a lack of understanding, direction, and resources allocated to PvP development.

There are 3 major problems that need to be addressed if this game is to ever reach its potential, but before I expand on them, I will start on a positive note, the one redeeming quality that has every player coming back and hoping improvements will be made: class design is quite good and the changes made have greatly improved the feel of most specs.

Now, the negative specs:

 1. The grind is unsustainable.
Rank 14 has always been a very intense grind and the system was supposed to address that, making gearing more approachable to a large chunk of the playerbase. Has it succeeded? No, not really.  It takes around 20 hours to reach honor cap.
The weekly cap is 20'000 honor and the total cost of the tier 3 pvp gear is 520'000. That's 26 weeks of grinding, exactly half a year.

While this kind of timeline is understandable for PvE gearing which can be done by simply raidlogging for a few hours each week, it is less so for a system which requires a 20 hour commitment every week. For many of us, it will make all other in game activities out of the equation; to reach honor cap until one has all of the PvP gear, one must BG, only BG, forever BG, 20 hours a week, for 26 weeks.

 2. The gear itself is a mixed bag. This point has multiple subpoints.
 2a. For some specs, the stats distribution is great and so are the set bonuses and for others, not so much. Here's an example: Immolate cast time reduced by 0.2 seconds.
This is bad, straight up, simply a previous set bonus made by Blizzard in the early days of Vanilla when they didn't know how classes would truly functions, recycled and printed on a brand new set. Feral druids, however, enjoy a much needed reduction in shapeshifting mana cost and a movespeed increase in cat & travel form, which while not necessary, is a tremendous benefit and fits the spec's gameplay aswell.
Many sets do not even attempt to mitigate some of the specs' weaknesses specifically for PvP.

 2b. Some set bonuses show a clear lack of understanding of PvP. For instance, the Resto Shaman's tier set reduces the duration of interruptions, which is very strong and more importantly coherent for a hard casting healer. It also, however, gives a CHANCE to resist silences altogether. In PvP, adding RNG to such a key ability as a silence, often the main window to kill a healer, is very unwise. RNG is inevitable, it is a rpg after all, but adding unnecessary RNG on such a core element of gameplay displays a chasm between the developpers and what the PvP playerbase asks for.

 2c. The gear is often not good enough in PvP. PvP gear should be the best gear for PvP, just like PvE gear should be the best for PvE. There can be some overlap, tier 3 PvP gear should be sufficient to clear a raid such as BWL, and someone in T3.5 shouldn't feel powerless against someone wearing T1 PvP gear -- but the best gear PvP has to offer should be the best gear for PvP, plain and simple.

 3d. There isn't even resilience (reduced dmg from crits & dots) on any piece. This has been the de facto PvP stat on Turtle, even implemented on Arathi PvP gear... Why not add it on the PvP tier set, too?

 3. There aren't enough PvP pieces. Rings, trinket2, belt, necklace, cloak... All of these pieces are missing. Of course there's always the reputation gear, but let's be real, it doesn't hold a candle to endgame PvE gear. Not even close.

All of this leads me to believe that the PvP rework was rushed, made in whatever short amount of time was left between all of the PvE content and the release date for the patch itself. An afterthought, forced into a patch that had to be pushed on a specific date. It also leads me to believe that there is no developper whose job is to design all things related to PvP, and that whoever has time from the PvE team will be tasked with handling PvP as well.

Now, the solutions:
 1. Hire a developper whose entire job will be to design PvP. None of the current team currently has that skillset. PvE and class design? Great, you're doing a tremendous job. PvP? Not so much. Find your match for PvP, and Turtle WoW's PvP will be better for it. I unfortunately don't believe significant progress will ever be made should this not done.
 2. Add a PvP stat. While lacking finesse, this is THE tried and tested way to ensure PvP gear remains the best in PvP. It makes balancing PvP without affecting PvE, and vice versa, a lot easier. I'm personally a fan of PvP Power to have leverage over both damage inflicted and damage taken, but merely reducing damage taken from other players would work as well.
 3. Communicate with the playerbase to ensure more coherent systems, including gear and tier set bonuses for each specs.

There are actual suggestions I could make on a more technical level, but this will always be insufficient, as long as the developpers themselves do not understand that which they attempt to balance and improve.

Thank you for your time.

Great post, I want to elaborate on it a bit...

I mostly disagree with the changes. The new system isn't the improvement it's being sold as, and I say that as someone who ground for almost 5 months under the old system and got backpaid four weeks worth of effort despite putting in three to four times the work that would have been required under the new system. So forgive me if I'm not jumping for joy at the changes.

The Grind...
The 26 week figure being thrown around is only true if you play one spec on one set. Druids, paladins, priests, shamans all have two or three viable sets depending on role. The real timeline for a committed player is far longer than the post lets on.

The Gear Stats and Set Bonuses...
If this gear is meant for PvP it needs to actually reflect that with reduced base stats and increased PvP oriented stats. If the goal is a hybrid system that eases people into PvE too, then split it, three pieces tuned for PvE and three for PvP, so both audiences get something real out of the grind. And if the team isn't sure how to balance set bonuses, maybe run a short in-game survey after BG matches asking players what feels like it overwhelms their ability to perform. Stop guessing and let the players give you something to actually work with.

The Missing Gear Slots...
Adding more honor-gated pieces for rings, cloaks and necks sounds nice until you do the math. Without raising the honor cap or seriously cutting item costs, it just means longer sessions and less life outside the game. You can't expand the gear pool without reworking the economy around it.

The old system had its problems but at least the effort you put in felt proportional to what you got back. Right now that isn't the case, and dressing it up as a player-friendly rework doesn't make it one.

Very disappointed
