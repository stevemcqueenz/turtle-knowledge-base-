# why raiding is the true wow class design killer

## Post 102614 by Caos (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2024-10-06T09:55:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=102614#p102614 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I've seen a lot of people in this server arguing over and over how pvp killed WoW and made all classes basically the same but I don't think that is true, at least from the open world and bg pvp perspective.
The real issue are raids. The issue is that in raiding everybody is specialized in doing a very specific job (tank, heal or dps), and thats against some of the WoW's classes design. So obviously classes that consist of only doing and taking damage (aka warrior) are very good for this type of content.

So what's your balancing plan? you wanna make pallies hit and tank like warriors while also being able to heal, dispell, buff, buble , ress? Or the retail way where every hybrid just takes a dps spec and loses all their support potential, essentially making hybrids 3 separate unrelated classes in one.

People here hate retail so much so they probably gonna make pallies and druids hit like warriors. But why would a warrior or rogue get in a raid if a druid or pally hits the same and they can ress, offheal and overall give much better support.

The best way is probably just being able to get better gear through different routes than raiding (crafting, open world, pvp ..), or nerfing raiding gear overall so the rest of the gear is not much worse, and leaving each class to be good at the things they are designed to be good at.

Vanilla usually lets hybrids be hybrids with some taxes which Turtle alleviated to a degree.

Was thinking about this.
I think that hybrid classes should be 'width'-based while 'a pure class' should be 'depth-based'.
I think that a hybrid's DPS tree should have a comparable output to a non-hybrid's.
But in exchange, non-hybrids should have a much wider area to customize how they wanna play the 1 role they are capable of.

A DPS class can have many different damage profiles. Single-target based, cleave based, aoe-based, CD-reliant pumper, consistent hitter, big damage upfront etc. A class with 3 melee DPS options for example should be able to choose among many damage profiles and set up the class as he wants to play it.
Imo a hybrid's DPS, while outputting comparable damage numbers, should be restricted in this. ''This is your DPS spec. It is designed for CD-based aoe damage with some sustained single-target. Take it or play another role.'' - For example.

tldr: imo hybrid power level should match non hybrid but non-hybrids should have real depth for the 1 role they can do.

We all grew up in societies that favorize competitiveness and makes us interiorize a strive for elitism and merit. Blizzard had succes because it's game was designed arround those concepts.

 **On the matter of DPS meters :**
Tools such as DPS meters were only the logical step because it was convenient. You could still calculate your or your mates theorical DPS without any addons. If you want to restrain players behaviors, you would have to redesign the whole game to make numbers irelevant (including meta-numbers such as chronometers (speed-running)). If you somehow manage to do that, the game will be something else entierly and I would bet that peoples will leave precisely because competitiveness and merit are **a part** of why it's succesfull in the first place.

 **On the matter of Hybride tax :**
Class identity can mean more than one thing. for example If you see it as in a esthetical thing, then you could normalize all numbers and retain it. Such view would bring players to play druids because they like it for the spells looks, the mechanics, the lore, and all things that can't be quantified because it's tied between a individual and the game.
Another example, If you see it as a role performing thing then you can't normalize all numbers and retain it. Players with such view would typically play whatever class is the most performing at a role they likes. If you suddenly makes a class being able to perform all roles, then you are neantizing class identities from a role performing point of view.

 **On the matter of PvP and PvE :**
Balance issues are a symptom of the game design and not it's cause. You have to constantly rework your balance amongs classes if you still have power creep as the reason to keep your players playing. That’s why the game is only balanced at max levels and gears. Doing balancing for those specific conditions reinforce the strive for performance and elitism. If you want to have a better balance, you need to reduce the delta of the conditions at which that balance apply. In facts, that means reducing scaling through stats (you can instead have more items that modify how you play, for example, the druid's helmet enchant).
