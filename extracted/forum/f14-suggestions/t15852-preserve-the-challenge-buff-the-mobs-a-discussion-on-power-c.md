# Preserve the Challenge: Buff the Mobs! [A Discussion on Power Creep]

## Post 108012 by Dannyp92 (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2024-11-15T09:50:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=108012#p108012 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Greetings fellow adventurers and Turtle WoW enthusiasts!

As many of you have noticed, the recent class changes have made our characters stronger and more dynamic, which can be exciting. However, these changes have also widened the gap between player power and the strength of the environment, contributing to a phenomenon known as power creep. While it's fun to feel powerful temporarily, an overly trivial game world diminishes the challenge and sense of accomplishment that many of us cherish. This is especially true for Turtle WoW, a server celebrated for its focus on the leveling journey and an immersive open world.

Why This Matters: The Essence of Vanilla WoW:

Vanilla WoW occupies a special place in our hearts because it struck a balance between challenge and exploration. Unlike modern iterations of the game, where leveling is often treated as a chore to rush through, Vanilla made every step of the journey meaningful. The dangerous open world, where mobs could overwhelm the unprepared, was a core part of this experience. Turtle WoW, as a "Vanilla+" project, carries the responsibility of building upon this legacy while staying true to what made it unique.

Combat Modernization: A Double-Edged Sword:

I understand and appreciate the desire to modernize combat mechanics and make classes more engaging to play. However, if players grow significantly stronger while the world’s mobs remain static, the balance is lost. The world itself—the heart of Vanilla's charm—risks becoming too easy, predictable, and, ultimately, less rewarding.

A Call for "Monster Changes" to Match Class Changes:

If we are making our classes stronger, why not make the monsters stronger too? This doesn’t mean simply giving mobs a huge HP pool and turning them into time-wasting sponges. Instead, I propose a more nuanced approach to bring the world’s challenge back in line with player power:

 - Slightly Increase Mob Damage
Start conservatively, perhaps with a 5% increase to their damage. This would make players more cautious in pulling multiple mobs while preserving a fair and balanced experience.

 - Adjust Aggro Ranges
Increasing aggro ranges slightly could restore the tension and danger of traversing the open world. Warriors and other classes can currently handle multiple mobs too easily, and this adjustment would reintroduce the need for strategy.

 - Introduce New Abilities for Mobs
Instead of just buffing stats, give mobs exciting and thematic new abilities. For instance:

 *Some Wolves could apply a stacking bleed.
 *Some Undead could have a chance to slow players with a chilling touch.
 *Some Bandits might randomly use a smoke bomb to reduce hit chance.
Adding randomized abilities within mob categories could spice up gameplay and keep players on their toes.

 - Enhance Mobs in Thematic Zones
Specific zones could feature more advanced or specialized mobs, providing a greater sense of progression and danger as players move through the world.

Balancing Fun and Challenge:

This isn’t about making the game brutally difficult—it’s about maintaining the delicate balance that makes Turtle WoW special. A "Vanilla+" experience should retain the thrill of venturing into a dangerous world where preparation, strategy, and awareness are rewarded. By keeping mobs relatively strong compared to players, we preserve the heart of what makes this game stand out.

Final Thoughts:

I recognize that changes like these would divide opinions, just as class changes have. However, if Turtle WoW continues to evolve without addressing the growing power gap, it risks alienating those of us who love the game for its meaningful challenges. At the very least, I believe this topic is worth discussing with the community.

How do you think we can keep the world dangerous and engaging while embracing new class designs? Thoughts?

IMO classic vanilla had the perfect baseline difficulty level.
