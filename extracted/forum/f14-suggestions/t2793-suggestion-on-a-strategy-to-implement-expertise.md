# Suggestion on a strategy to Implement Expertise

## Post 17435 by Ugoboom (Bug Report Enthusiast) — 2022-03-08T05:29:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=17435#p17435 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Dodge and parry are two classic mechanics inherited from DnD and other RPGs. In low stakes and casual environments, they add quite a nice bit of variety and flavor to gameplay. However, in challenging high end content such as naxx and the new raids being developed by turtle staff, dodge and parry serves only to make tanking an inconsistent and frustrating experience. Blizzard quickly quickly realized this problem and added expertise in TBC and eventually removed the stat altogether in later xpacs. Ask any person who regularly tanks aq40 and naxx, and ask them their thoughts on getting parried and dodged at the start of a pull. They all hate hate hate it. It's one of the reasons vanilla has a high tank turnover.

So, adding in Expertise would allow high end geared players to significantly lessen if not remove their chances. However, adding in +expertise gear spells and server calculations is a lot of work. thankfully, expertise is already in the game, via something called Weapon Skill. Currently, weapon skill is so rare as a stat, that people don't really go beyond 8 and usually just 5. **However, there's nothing stopping us from adding lots of gear with heaps of +skill.** I think this is a rather elegant solution to many many problems facing endgame raiding, from RNG to gear design.

The details:

Same level mobs have a 5% chance to dodge, parry, and be missed, so +50 weapon skill is needed to hit expertise cap. +3 lv mobs have a 6.5% chance on those 3 mechanics, so +65 weapon skill is needed to hit expertise cap. having two caps will encourage minmaxers to build trash and boss sets which increases complexity and engagement, just like spellcasters currently are encouraged to do.

However, do note that the 5% and 6.5% also include chance to miss. This means that reaching expertise cap will automatically hard hit cap 2hand users and soft hit cap dual weild users. Fret not, for this allows for an opening to diverge 2h and DW gearing, because right now, gearing for both is nearly identical, and this is a shame.

With +10 and +15 on multiple slots of gear, hitting expertise cap is possible. However, weapon skill also has another benefit that has not cap: 25 skill = 1% crit. Gear would need to be itemized with this in mind, so if there's a ilvl 90 glove with 30 str and 1% crit, a new ilvl 90 glove with 30str shouldn't exceed 25 skill. This kind of hard cap vs soft cap design is great, as it has people spend the most time simulating and considering whether exceeding a soft cap is worth it, or by how much it's worth it.

Concerning tier: For warriors, this could be whats needed for designing the missing DPS warrior tier sets (which need to be added if other offspecs are getting their missing tier sets). Offpieces would suddenly not be so bis, and dps warriors would have to consider their options, especially if new tier has set bonuses that offer further skill. For rogues, offpieces with skill might have them consider the benefits of going for expertise, versus the powerful benefits of set bonuses. More interesting gearing all around.

Implications of this change:

- Tanks will be able to consistently get threat starting a fight. As pulls will become far more consistent, this will allow tanks to more easily tune their gear for threat versus mitigation, and will allow dps to predictably learn how long they need to wait, instead of guessing or watching some addon's meter
- Edgemasters will be strongly nerfed. Players looking to skill up won't be so harshly price gouged by this unfarmable drop, this terrible bit of game design
- Racials that add +skill will be strongly nerfed. Players will feel way less bad about playing the race they like the look and RP of
- Fresh 60 dps and tanks will be able to get their mandatory 5 skill, faaaar far easier. It's still possible to be a noob and completely discount skill and miss items, but knowledgeable players will not be so harshly set back by bad RNG on select few items.
- Tanks will do a bit more threat, meele dps will do a bit more damage. (There are separate concerns facing spellcasters that can be fixed too, to help them keep up with this)
- 2h dps, which are specs currently underperforming: 2hfury, enh, ret. Will be strongly buffed in that they can completely ignore hit as a stat. This also allows designers to add new bis items for these, without just straight up invalidating 1.12 gear.
- DW fury warrior will be somewhat buffed... from its top position even higher. This is probably the main concern.
- Ferals will be left out of the fun. However, if +fists is modified in the code to also buff the +feral combat skill, which i believe is the weapon skill cats and bears use in computation, then bam, ferals get the benefit too and will have strong reason to scoop up +fists gear. This would also put them in the "will be strongly buffed by this" camp as 2H specs, as cats can drop hit for more dps stats and bears can drop hit for stronger mitigation or threat. Also, instead of +fists, +feral stats would be fine too, but it's a bit silly to add class specific stat benefits to what is usually class unrestricted gear.
I really think this is the best way to make vanilla tanking and dpsing less painful due to RNG that simply is not fun on pulls where that RNG can easily wipe you, due to no fault of your own. Getting this critical form of RNG out of high end raiding will be a strong boon to proper difficulty design elsewhere in the game.
