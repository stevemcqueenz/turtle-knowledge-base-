# Goblin Priests, Shamans, and the problem with race/class design

## Post 73895 by Eleeejah — 2023-10-26T10:57:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=73895#p73895 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I like TurtleWow but I really dislike the liberties taken with the way class and race combinations are handled. TurtleWow isn't just about new content and lore, it is about balance too. The same thing can be said about class and race combinations and is the same outlook Blizzard had on them when designing the game back then. It seems like lore reasons are the only thing now and those lore reasons are up to personal interpretation. Vanilla WoW had 6 healing combinations for each faction, alliance and horde. The Horde still has six. The Alliance has eight.

To start with my reasons are that these are the original Cataclysm combinations from when Goblins were added. It can be perfectly assumed that priests and shamans were around on Kezan for the relatively short period of time in between Vanilla and Cataclysm. There is no reason they shouldn't be there regarding their lack of existence yet. Most of the new combinations were to support already existing or new lore, and because there was no new lore regarding Goblins and shamanism or the light, it can be assumed it was already there as opposed to Tauren Paladins or Troll Druids being new combinations as a result of new lore.

It's not like Goblin's were given these combinations in Cataclysm for just balance without any supporting lore. Goblin shamans UNBALANCED the 3 paladin, 3 shaman equivalent. Goblin shamans lore is that they seek to capitalize on the elements through making deals with them and such. The elementals in Azeroth are living, breathing beings you could certainly make deals with. You might not agree with this lore, but your opinion on it being stupid or not doesn't invalidate it as a lore reason. Beyond that, using the elements for power DOES NOT require respect. Dark iron shamanism exists independently from the wildhammers, and they enslave and punish the elementals in their city using this power. There are many reasons to have them, while the only reason not to is completely subjective.

The same thing applies to priests. We often equate goblins to being the opposite of gnomes and super technological orientated beings, though they are not. The peak of goblin society and everything it revolves around is greed and money. Their technological focus is just a byproduct and tool for getting richer. The same thing can be said about their use of the light. Using the light doesn't require you to have holy ulterior motives, IE scarlet crusade, and the light itself has been shown not to be a black or white, good and evil parallel to the void. It makes perfect sense for some goblins to focus on the light as a tool to gain power, and the church of our world having such heavy links to land, power, money for centuries is COMPLETELY something goblins would do.

There is absolutely no reason other then people's subjective opinions NOT to have goblins be these classes. It is, at the end of the day, the people in charge of this' opinion that matters, but I don't think it's ok when their reasoning is completely arbitrary. There should be consistency here, and no gnome hunters or whatever else if the reasonings for not having these combinations exist. Beyond that though, I think it is bad when these arbitrary decisions come without any concern for faction balance. The Alliance having 8 healing combinations to the Horde still having the Vanilla 6 is not something you should see in a supposedly "more balanced" vanilla. The Horde had one more shaman then the Alliance had paladins, and they had one more priest. Giving the goblins back these classes would perfectly fix the balance both in equal number and class balance back to what it was.

tldr: balance and no conflicting lore > arbitrary reasoning

Alright, so.  I did a little reading into the 1st Edition *Warcraft The Roleplaying Game* campaign setting guide.  According to that, it's implied that goblins, as a whole, prefer traditional combat or mechanical augments.  They use things like maces, crossbows, firearms, blunderbusses, et cetera, but also use tools if attacked in their warrens.  They can also make awesome machines of unrivaled power, if they feel like it, and can use them for both productive and aggressive means.

They are "tricksters" and "con artists" "to the extreme".  They like machines and they like money.  They also have a +2 racial bonus to Appraise, Craft, Diplomacy, and Listen checks, and a +4 on Mechanical Craft checks.  This doesn't exactly lend itself to being magical in any capacity…
Additionally, they tend to savage the land for all available resources which is like **severely** against Shamanistic teachings.  They could probably fake it with technology, but that would require some really stupid world building lore crap, and why would they bother to begin with?  There's no money in it.

In short, there's as much reason for Goblin magic-users to exist as there for them **not** to exist.  They prefer to worship the Almighty Dollar over some "spooky voice" like The Light, and there's nothing some doofus in a dress can do that their machinery can't do better, faster, and more efficiently – especially in terms of destruction and devastation.  They're better off being Warriors, Rogues, and Hunters, since there's no "Goblin Tinkerer" class.

I suppose it's worth mentioning, however, that this same manual also specifically mentioned that only Alliance members can be the "Priest" class, but that's kind of out the window with even Vanilla *World of Warcraft*.  It also says only "evil" alignment characters can be Warlocks.  So, there's that.

Lore and mechanic wise, most race class combos should exist. We have priests that include humans, trolls, night elves, dwarves, and undead, all of which should have quite different religions. It's a mechanic that all are the same class for balance in game yet being quite different in terms of lore. Put otherwise, one shouldn't equate mechanics with lore.
One simple example is that, by all rights, night elf paladins should exist. Lore wise, they're warriors employing the light of Elune. If anything, they'd be the closest thing to what I imagine priestesses of the moon being.
Tauren mages? They're are tauren mages (grimtotem) in game, but it's easily enough imagined as being another type of elementalism. Same as how troll mages are extremely different than Dalaran mages according to lore.
The examples can go on and on.

For the goblins, magic users do make sense. Magic makes money. For priests, the televangelizing goblin asking for your money is something I want to see.

There are a few exceptions to opening class/race restriction I'd want maintained:
 1. Shaman and paladin should be kept faction exclusive to maintain vanilla faction distinction.
 2. Forsaken not being allowed to become shamans; it makes no sense by any stretch.
 3. Druids maintained as night elf/tauren exclusive to preserve vanilla distinction and lore - none of the other races have been allowed into this part of the Cenarion Circle. As druids must go to the Moonglade often, changing this racial lock would be a complete break of lore.
