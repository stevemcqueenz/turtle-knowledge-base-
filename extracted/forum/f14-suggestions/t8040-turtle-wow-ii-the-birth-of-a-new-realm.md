You know what old meme?  "If X is so good, then why isn't there 'X 2'?"  Well, that's what *I'm* saying.

"If *Turtle WoW* is so good, then why isn't there a '*Turtle WoW II*'?"

According to the "[Frequently Posted Suggestions](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?t=6841)" thread, there are no plans for a "*Turtle WoW* II" – or a second realm – because they want to maintain a "warm and welcoming home for people to play at their own pace".  Well, that's all fine and dandy… except that the current realm really isn't any of that, anymore.  It's more of a "hostile and closed off place for people to play at an arbitrarily controlled pace".

I know.  That's a rather strong statement.  Well, I have rationale to back that up.
—

Overpopulation
This has been a problem for a while, now, but especially after some other popular, emulated servers went ker-blewie.  The latest newsletter boasted a whopping **7,100+** players online at one time.  Retail *World of Warcraft* was probably never intended for that many players on one realm – which is likely why the US / Oceanic servers have 246 of them.  Even at its recent peak [back in January](https://activeplayer.io/world-of-warcraft/), there were only 853,250 players online across 246 realms.  That's an average of 3,468.5 players to any given server.  Yes, I know that's a gross generalization.  Some realms are perpetually **dead** ones.  Even so, I can't imagine the live player count for even the most populated server being much higher than 4,000 during events or peak hours.  And while raw numbers aren't really much of a problem – and, in fact, seems to be something to be *proud* of – this *does* lead to other, more *tangible* issues…

Available Resources
If you log in and make a new character, chances are that you're going to be sharing mobs and resources with about 1,000 other players at any given time.  If you manage to get to Level 20, say, the same thing may happen depending on where you wander.  From what I've been reading from the last couple of months, this is actually a problem all the way up to Level 60, where the gameplay shifts from World PvE to Instanced PvE.  You're always going to be knocking heads with other players over that mining node, or that bit of Peacebloom.  And yes, Skinners are also affected due to the fact that a lot of mobs are being hunted for quests.

Balance and Unbalance
So, you're new to Elwynn Forest.  You decide to investigate the Fargodeep Mine.  What do you see?  Oh, about a hundred players all camping out around Goldtooth's corpse.  But, as you're sitting there, waiting to get Auntie Bernie's necklace back… suddenly, a small pack of kobolds jumps you from the shadows!  Where did *they* come from?  As it turns out, they **respawned** out of nowhere.  And then, as soon as you kill them, here comes another respawn.  And then another.  And another.  You get the idea.
Now, let's go the other way.  You're a big, strong Level 10 ready to fight the scourge of the forest, Hogger.  Once again, you head off to the general quest location and… there's a queue.  You sit there for thirty minutes, watching Hogger get messed up by a swarm of ravenous players every seven minutes or so.  And the players just keep on coming…

This ties into the previous point a bit, but yes.  Due to the overwhelming amount of players, spawn timers for mobs, quest objectives, and other things are severely screwed up.  Too many spawns in *this* area, not enough spawns in *that* area, objective camping, and so on and so forth.  You spend most of your time either waiting around, or you wind up getting ripped to shreds by some mobs that just appeared out of nowhere.  I realize that the philosophy of *Turtle WoW* is "slow and steady" or doing things at one's own pace… but this is neither.  This is an artificial hurdle created by, say it with me, **overpopulation**.  And you know what?  Sometimes, it's not as easy as simply joining a party and "making friends".  Do you know why?

Culture Clash: The Art of Playing the Game
There are players of all kinds on the server.  We have the people who just want to quest their way to Level 60.  We have people who live in Battlegrounds and people who thrive on world player-vs-player combat.  We have roleplayers who want to weave magnificent tales of glory.  And then, if that wasn't enough… we also have people in War Mode who just want that extra XP boost, and people in Hardcore Mode who may as well be on their own server since they can't interact with anyone in any meaningful way.  Between roleplayers and War Mode players whining about being ganked, Hardcore players begging to be revived on a daily basis, and fresh players who simply can't play the game due to the previous two points, it doesn't seem like anyone's able to be fully satisfied.  And that leads me to my final point…

A General Unfriendliness
Sorry to say, but it's simply not possible to have this many different players from different backgrounds with differing ideas all in the same place and expect them to cooperate 100% of the time.  Bored Level 60s are always going to go to low-level areas and gank the crap out of low-level characters.  Roleplayers are always going to be interrupted by non-roleplayers if they roleplay out in public.  People just trying to do quests aren't going to be able to accomplish much of anything because there are so many other people doing the same.  The "melting pot" isn't just nasty tar, at this point.  It's boiling over and making a mess on the floor.

Your "warm and welcoming" server is neither, anymore, despite the best efforts of the staff.  This "melting pot" of players started out with good intentions, but now, it's bubbling over and making a mess on the floor and you're just mopping it up as you go.  How long do you intend to keep this up?  With all that said **and** a new, major content patch on its way… I genuinely think that it's time to open the doors onto a brand new realm – if only to cut down on the overpopulation problems.
—

Now personally, I'd love to see a realm that's specifically tailored for certain groups of players.  Yes, yes, I know, I'm skirting around that awful, *awful* word that Turtle Team seems to hate.  "What word"?

 ***"[color="#a7c408]Ｓｅｐｅｒａｔｉｏｎ．[/color]"***

Oh, *God*!  It *burns* the ear holes and *chills* the blood!!  But, you know what?  Sometimes, separation is a **good** thing.  And ideally, I think it would be a clever idea to separate PvP players from the PvE ones.  I'm not saying should **disable** PvP interactions on one realm or the other.  I just think having a dedicated PvP server would maybe take some of the load off the currently singular server that you have – especially if the current realm maintained its "RP-PvE" status.  I mean, let's be honest…  Roleplay went out the window as soon as the exiles from that one emulated server rolled in.  If you *truly* wanted to be "warm and welcoming", you'd try a little harder to accommodate the PvP players.  Just sayin'.  And yes, you should definitely keep the two realms in the same "Battlegroup".  There's no reason to separate the realms in *that* regard at all.

Anyway, that's all I have for now.  Hopefully, you'll seriously consider my words and do some serious thinking on the matter.  If not?  Well… it's your house.  "We just live in it…"[

Goodness, I should have posted this yesterday when I was so sleepy I thought it wasn't a good idea and I should sleep over it before posting. Now I come back to this. It would appear I am one of the few to recognize the effort put into this elaborate write up and meet it with at least some effort of my own. So lets get right into it ...

First off, I feel that Jolikmc presented a solid concept and ideas as well as a number of really well thought out and presented reasons in favor of them. And he has (before deleting it) done so in a manner that is easy to read and, goodness, even actually enjoyable, would you look at that. Its 2023 and someone went through the effort of writing an enjoyable, elaborate forum post (please, consider putting it back? It was really good). That alone kinda deserves mention. Anyways ...

Generally, I feel there is a lot of merit to what Jolikmc brings up here. I will try to address it in order ...

Overpopulation
Unfortunately, thats a no brainer. At the moment it does actually feel a little crowded and I am saying this as someone who played here when the peak time population was about three hundred.

Available resources
Interestingly, I have not run into too many issues on this and I am saying this running a small crafting group so, as much as I think it would make sense that there is a general scarcity and lack of available resources, I just can't seem to confirm it as I do seem to find enough available resources. Sure, sometimes you get a bit of a dry spell with whatever you are looking for but its not too bad honestly. Though, granted Im not actually even level forty yet so maybe I haven't yet reached the level range at which this issue would become so rampant that I would notice it as well.

Balance and Unbalance
I can't say that it isn't true (because it is true) what Jolikmc says but I can say that I have seen a fair amount of restraint shown by people and at times even something that remotely resembled *cooperation*. Admittedly, those were the highlights because what I have also seen is just blatant and inconsiderate egoism to the point of people interrupting in-progress runs of scripted quests that must not be interrupted (the theramore triage quest for example).
And while overpopulation is certainly a factor in this that I feel amplifies this problem, I honestly feel that it isn't the root cause.
So then, what is the root cause?

Culture Clash: The Art of Playing the Game
While I agree and feel that Jolikmc makes an excellent point here about the "melting pot" and the problems it causes, I do feel there is one factor that is left unmentioned here that oh so contributes to it all. Courtesy. Its something that no matter what culture or background you are from any civilization seems to be able to agree on regardless of language by the way. The other thing is something that I have touched on in a different forum post just recently. Responsibility.

So, courtesy and responsibility. Would that solve all those problems? Goodness, I wish. Unfortunately, the problems Jolikmc mentions do persist but if there was a minimum of courtesy and responsibility among the general populace at large it might not be as bad as it is at the moment. Which leads me to address the last point of Jolikmc's post.

A General Unfriendliness
You know, as much as I want to disagree here, I find myself unable to do so. There does appear to be a general unfriendliness and, again, I say this as someone who has actually seen different back in the day so I do have a first hand experience for comparison.
Where I do have a different view is the solution of creating a second realm or even a third one.

But why? Why wouldn't it help solve the aforementioned problems?

Don't get me wrong, I feel it would help solve some of these problems. Like for example ...
- Overpopulation
- Available resources
- Culture Clash (specifically in regards to RP and PvP)
However, I don't feel it would do anything at all for the other issues ...
- Balance and Unbalance
- General unfriendliness
Even if there were more realms to play on and a separation into PvP, RP-PvE and Hard-Core courtesy and responsibility still remain a factor. I mean, certainly, maybe this new environment would breed more courtesy and maybe even invite taking more responsibility as a person. Might happen for all I know.

However, I do feel obligated to point out one fact here ...

As was mentioned an influx of people from a certain server that was shut down, caused a dramatic increase in "General Unfriendliness" as Jolikmc put it, So then, one server shutting down and a mass migration happening ... I would say that classifies as a "change in environment". And given the previous server was a PvP server that even classifies as a change in community, given Turtle WoW is not a PvP server.

But did it help foster courtesy and responsibility? Nope. No, I did not.

Instead, what happened is exactly what Jolikmc tried to point out in his very well written post just above (which he unfortunately deleted   crying_turtle   ). Which leads me to bringing up what points I would like to contribute to this.

- What would stop people from say the PvP realm to log onto the roleplay realm and just exploit the general ineptitude born of the reluctance of most roleplayers to PvP? Or just grief the hard-core realm because everyone on that realm is guaranteed to be a HC?
- What would stop progression guilds on the PvP or PvE realm to then, once they "won" the game to flood into the HC realm or the roleplay realm and pollute the "magnificent tales of glory" with their understandably very non-roleplay dungeon and raid progression?
- Would it do anything at all to cause people to be more courteous and take more responsibility for their actions and choices?

The way I see it, the singular structure of the server is not the main problem, though I do agree it is actually a problem in some ways. However, the other arguably bigger problem in my opinion is the extreme lack of courtesy, consideration and capacity to take responsibility among many players.

Afterall, even in an over-populated, over-crowded place there can be good company if there is courtesy, responsibility and consideration. An overcrowded environment just requires more of it. Unfortunately, people either bring these things and be good company or they don't ... can't change people.

And the way I understand the Turtle WoW Developers with not wanting separation is that if you can't change people and people are going to be people anyways, regardless how many realms there are to play on, you might as well keep them all in one and the same realm and let the proverbial swarm of locusts burn itself out and move on and thus the problem solve itself. Sure, the server and community will take some damage and hurt for a while but if enough of it remains it'll heal. And if there isn't enough left? I suppose we all got another lesson of why you don't advertise the lost server to invite the swarm ...

Thats just my take on this, though.
