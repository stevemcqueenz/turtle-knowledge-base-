# Quest NPCs and buffed guards

## Post 22177 by Saltypete — 2022-08-13T03:08:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=22177#p22177 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

We have all, at one point, dealt with a quest NPC being dead. This is of course massively annoying, but even more annoying is when the killer is a level 60 with a few hours to waste who just wants to camp the NPC to make everyone mad. While I would not personally define this as griefing — and neither does the official World of Warcraft strategy guide written in 2004 by BradyGames, which defines griefing the opposing factions strictly as utilizing exploits and bugs, while everything else is fair game — the fact of the matter is that this behavior is also not something we want to encourage. Killing a quest NPC once or twice and starting a WPVP event with several 60s who come to stop you is interesting and in-line with the game's title. You know, *War*craft. Killing it literally all day and forcing people to either waste their time stopping you or go to another leveling zone is tedious and obnoxious.

So how do we discourage this behavior? First, let's look at the current method of discouragement: buffed guards and buffed quest givers. Put simply, this system is bad. Put elaborately, this system is really bad. It is bad for two main reasons; and I will give the lesser of the two first.

 1. It removes player choice by cutting off access to a mechanic of the game.
 2. It doesn't even mainly affect the people it's meant to stop.

You heard me right. It does not primarly affect the quest NPC campers. Go to Thousand Needles and Hillsbrad and take a look at all the HC tombstones that are *suspiciously close to the opposing faction's zone* if you don't believe me. Ironically, the superbuffed mega-aggro turboganker guards are more of a threat to *the people they are meant to protect*, i.e., normal players, than the questcampers. This also makes certain aspects of PVP, such as city raids (which often are composed with a strict no-civilian-casualties rule due to wanting to avoid DKs, and thus do not generally affect normal people questing) effectively impossible. The mechanic punishes normal players while doing basically nothing to stop questcamping, because the questcamper doesn't care if they die after killing the NPC. They killed the NPC, you're mad about it, they win. Their death is mostly irrelevant.

Thus I return to the question posed earlier: how do we discourage this behavior? Honor was very important throughout most of human history. A dishonorable person could quickly find themselves ostracized by their community, friends, and even their family. Well, as it so happens, we already have both of the systems necessary to lean into this in a way that feels very in-line with the RP origins of the server. We just need to utilize them. The first is the glyph system; which I will get into more later. The second is the Dishonorable Ranking system, which needs only some tweaking to become viable for what I propose.

Firstly, the Dishonorable Ranking system needs to be easier to descend. All quest givers need to become civilians — yes, *all* of them, even quest givers who are clearly active military or dragons or some such. As of now, I only see people with Dishonored. I have never seen someone with the rank of Exiled, Outlaw, or Pariah, even people who very, very clearly should either be at that rank or very close. If you frequent the tents at Crossroads or Goldshire, you know what I'm talking about. This suggests the titles are hard to get, which should only be the case for Pariah. Secondly, the Dishonorable Ranking needs to utilize the glyph system, which I will outline in detail below.
 1. Dishonored Rank
This rank is not hard to get into nor get out of, which I feel is appropriate, as some players might not even know what a dishonorable kill is, and could achieve this rank by accident. Thusly, it should not have crippling penalties, which would be as follows:
A version of the Warmode glyph is activated on your character, allowing the enemy faction to attack you at will. This glyph is different from normal Warmode in three ways: firstly, it can only be deactivated by clearing your name and leaving the Dishonored rank. You *cannot* turn it off at the glyph vendor. This applies to all glyphs which are gained through dishonorable ranks. Secondly, players receive 1.1x the honor for killing you. Thirdly, all items in all NPC shops cost 10% more.

 2. Exiled Rank
This rank shouldn't necessarily be hard to get, but it shouldn't be something you can stumble into either. It should be tough enough that you're making a choice if you get this rank, because, true to its name, this rank would give you a version of the Warmode glyph which sets all of your faction reputations to neutral. The rest of it is similar to Dishonored Rank's Warmode, except now players receive 1.2x the honor for killing you, and all items cost 20% more. Additionally, using the auction house incurs a 20% fee of whatever you're buying or selling.

 3. Outlaw Rank
This is where things start to get truly dangerous for the dishonorable player. At this rank, you receive a version of the Warmode glyph which doesn't just flag you — it flags you **for both factions**, utilizing the Gurubashi arena free-for-all flag, and sets your faction reputations to Disliked. The Outlaw player could still utilize goblin faction vendors, so the game wouldn't be unplayable, but, it would be very nearly. Players receive 1.5x the honor for killing you, and every item in every shop (including goblin shops, because the goblins need heavy incentive to make deals with such an infamous war-criminal) costs 50% more. The goblin auction house incurs a 50% fee.

 4. Pariah Rank
This rank is where the game becomes red karma from Black Desert Online levels of unplayable. All factions are set to hostile, even your own, players receive twice the honor for killing you, and all items in all shops cost three times as much. The goblin auction house incurs a 70% fee. But that's not all. Players receive honor for killing you regardless of circumstance. If they're ten levels higher than you, they still receive honor. If you have res sickness, they still receive honor. If you just hearthed, they still receive honor. If they killed you fifty times that day, they still receive full honor. It could even be added into the rules that corpse-camping a Pariah player for hours is not against the rules — after all, they were happy to camp gray level players and quest NPCs for many hours to reach their rank.

Even if someone were to miraculously climb from Pariah to Scout/Private, their faction reputations would still be at neutral, where they'd be left upon climbing back to Exiled. So, even if you clean your name up, you're still not done working. I think this system would dissuade people from obnoxious behavior while still leaving the choice for raids, running into Stormwind to get to Stockades (which takes, no joke, about an hour if you don't have a healer to keep ressing you to the entrance because the mega-aggro insta-spawning guards kill you the moment you res), walking through Thousand Needles without getting a homing-Tauren sprinting at you from 5000 feet away and smashing your skull in with a hammer, etc.

Another less-RP focused aspect of dissuasion could be a faster respawn time for the quest giver — 60 seconds or so — and perhaps a grace period, where for about 60 seconds after spawning they have Divine Intervention or something similar to prevent them being damaged, allowing players to get their quests done. This is mostly for convenience because having to wait 5-ish minutes for a quest giver to respawn really sucks.
