## Post 47628 by Bob022 (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2023-05-27T10:50:00Z

On the one side, druid and paladin, arguably shaman too depending on intent for the class, could stand to use some tweaks on the tank side of things.  This is an ongoing process on T-wow and a lot of positive changes have already been implemented.

On the other side, there are indeed tank players out there who play those classes and who have no interest in raiding.  I'm one such.  Tank job is all my paladin does.  However...I'm not doing it for pick-up groups.  Why should I want to?  With my wife and kid we can trio many instances (did a nice karazhan crypt trip the other day), and the areas we can't, we can invite a friend or two along.  The average player skill level is so bad on T-wow that a straight majority of random players I've grouped with are active hindrances who make the content harder on us than doing the same content trio.  I'm not going to ruin my own fun carrying someone who can't be bothered to even try....let those types languish in LFT.

Beyond that, as a role, the tank job will probably always be somewhat under-represented in this game.  Compared to some other MMOG's, Warcraft seems to go out of its way to make the tank job less rewarding and at times more irritating than it needs to be.  Tank types are bad in most PvP and stack poorly in groups.  Raids need proportionally fewer of them than groups do.  The majority of combat mechanics affect the tank worse than others in the group.  Add the community expectation for the tank to also pull and lead the group, it's only natural and expected that fewer people will want to do that job.  These issues don't necessarily crop up to the same degree, or at all, in other games of this type so in some sense Warcraft can be at least partly blamed for the issue.
