## Post 153503 by Caaarlosyt — 2025-09-28T00:44:00Z

For me it's a huge downgrade, first just on the unit nameplate health bar drawing distance, on 3.3.5 that distance is much further than the 1.12 client and 2.4.3 client.

Turtle however is not a regular 1.12 client, it does require a bit of effort, and a lot of moding which is mostly done by the launcher (wow tweaks etc) but also a huge plethora of addons that just bring basic 1.12 functions to the 3.3.5 standard.

Very basic stuff like loot at the mouse instead of at the top right corner.
Some of the stuff from the original 1.12 client is downright corny and quirky to the point it feels like a 30 year old game for PS1. TBC client already shows huge improvements and requires much less addons and it is knowledge of everybody that many TBC content was in the works already during vanilla.
For me the original team vision was always to arrive at something either like 2.4.3 or 3.3.5a client wise, that was the full realization and potential unlock of the original ideas regarding gameplay.

Stock 3.3.5 is still a bit "barebones" compared to something like MoP (5.4.8) which is already miles ahead and provides tons of things that are impossible to achieve on the 3.3.5.

I think most people want to play vanilla in the 3.3.5 because of onyxia which was the first time anyone tried it and opened up that can of worms and possibility.
The thing is, there is so many dif pservers on that client, so many addons, so many backports of famous and very high quality, optimized performance easy to use, config, save and share addons for 3.3.5.

I can have 3 diff custom pservers running on 3 separate modded 3.3.5 and both addons and WTF configs are a basic drag and drop, maybe load 2 or 3 profiles in less than 2 minutes every single client looks/plays/feels exactly the same. Same weakauras etc.

But if you really want to have a "Vanilla like" experience, just as a "TBC like" experience, you might want to stick to the original addons that worked on the original client, and that is huge effort of research, relearn new ways to do old things, config etc.

But at the same time you can think, everyone else playing here had to adjust the same, everyone is on the same boat, therefore people can always be helpful trying to mimic what we want.

After some reading I've found replacements for weakauras, dominoes, platebuffs, or plater or threatplates. All the basic stuff. Even if you went to play retail, without the 3.3.5 client, you would probably end up having to search and config a bunch of new addons anyway, just by changing the client.

It's more about leaving the comfort zone, regardless of game version you play, by changing you always need to put in the work.

The UE5, I have no idea how much the game will change on it but for me the biggest most impactful improvement as soon as I saw it was the lighting and how fluid the animations looked.
At the same time the models didn't seem to have a huge polygon count, the shadow complexity doesn't seem to be "overdone" and if everything is done carefully UE5 might not be as taxing as some people would expect it to be.

My biggest UE5 disappointment was ACC, where even though the game is compatible with VR it's nearly unplayable with it and still doesn't look "that sharp" and it's hard to have consistent 144fps.
