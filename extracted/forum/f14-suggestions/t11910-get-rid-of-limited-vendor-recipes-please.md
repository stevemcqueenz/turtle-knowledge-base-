# Get rid of limited vendor recipes, please.

## Post 78775 by Cancanned — 2023-11-26T09:34:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=78775#p78775 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

This is a quality of life issue. I don't find it fun to run into a wall with professions because a bot is grabbing the single copy of a vendor pattern then posting it on the AH at extortion prices. There should be no patterns at all that are a single copy. All patterns from specific vendors should be unlimited in stock. There is no upside to the game whatsoever when there is only one copy that gets purchased instantly by a bot.

Please get rid of single copy patterns.

Thank you.

## Post 78791 by Mativh (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2023-11-26T14:37:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=78791#p78791 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Limited items make the vendors less of a lifeless vending machine and more an immersive part of the world that reminds you that there are others there with you whose actions leave impact.

I think that limited amount of vendor items should be much more prevalent and extended on other merchandize as well, albeit with much faster refresh rates, which would vary depending on the rarity of the item. Too long refresh rates indeed lead to bots (from a certain region) camping the vendor and just putting it all in the AH (then trying to sell the gold for real money). There should still be a possibility of getting lucky with the vendor having something limited and special, and making some money on the side reselling it, but it shouldn't be abused.

So the limited items should remain but perhaps refresh faster, as a response to the growth of the server and practices of fellow turtles from a certain region.
And maybe there could be more items that are limited, with a fast refresh rate.

There could be more often conditions of sale too, like the reputation requirement. I liked in TBC that such a low level town as Tranquillien in Ghostlands had a quartermaster with items for that level.

Also vendors should have limited amount of gold to spend on buying from you, which increases when you buy from them. And it'd reset every x minutes.
What they are willing to pay you and what their items cost should depend on your reputation with the faction they are in.

And there could be exceptions where certain vendors prices vary depending simply on your class or race, if it'd make sense lore-wise.

Stats could have additional purposes, in this case Strength could slightly decrease the cost of purchased items (haggle through intimidation), and Intellect could slightly increase the cost of sold items (haggle through persuasion).

I agree that there are some recipes that are annoyingly hard to get for the intended vendor price.
Formula: Enchant Bracer - Lesser Strength is notorious because it is part of the optimal path to level enchanting so the Alliance vendor gets insanely camped.
Recipe: Undermine Clam Chowder is perhaps the classic scalping recipe that goes for absurd prices on the AH because there is 1 single vendor who has it and he gets camped very hard. But this is more a "vanity" recipe, as it offers very little utility.

But I'm leaning more towards what Cancanned is suggesting: To look at those recipes that are both very desired and also very limited, and perhaps increase availability.
More in the vein of what happened with Recipe: Frost Oil. In Vanilla this is only sold by a neutral Goblin in Alterac. I've visited him multiple times - the recipe is always sold out. But on TWoW there is a second NPC in Arathi that sells this recipe and whenever I visited them, the recipe was in stock.
Now *this* is how you reward exploration and research properly.

I don't think making limited recipes soulbound is remotely a good idea.
a) I want to be able to pick up recipes and send them to my alt. There are already some rare exceptions in the game where vendor-sold recipes become soulbound (like Plans: Bloodstone Warblade) and they are generally very annoying and lead to unintentional waste of money.
b) People paying for convenience is part of a free and healthy market. There is a benefit for both the seller and the buyer when recipes that come from remote locations or that are limited in stock can just be picked up at the AH. Now I agree that shouldn't mean it *only* goes on the AH because the vendor is just camped by scalpers. But the fix in my opinion is more vendors (in equally remote locations), not killing the entire market wholesale.
