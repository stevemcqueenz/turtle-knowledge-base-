## Post 141888 by Azcron (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2025-07-28T14:42:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=141888#p141888 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

You're right to bring up the Holdout Medics in Gnomeregan—it's a clever and often overlooked detail. And you're not the first to notice that they clearly cast Holy spells, just like a Priest would. It does raise eyebrows when people argue that gnomes categorically cannot access the Light or use Holy magic, because clearly some are doing exactly that.

But here’s the thing: lore and gameplay don’t always align 1:1—especially in Classic WoW, which was built with strong but not fully developed lore foundations. Blizzard has often used existing spell schools and NPC templates to fill out zones without committing to broader lore implications. Holdout Medics using Holy spells may not necessarily mean they’re trained priests of the Light—it could just be a mechanical way to show a healing NPC, especially back when spell diversity for NPCs was more limited.

The Lore Angle
In the Warcraft universe, using the Light generally requires more than just training—it usually involves belief, devotion, and a spiritual connection. That’s why most races who have Priest classes are tied to some form of religion, tradition, or philosophy aligned with the Light:

Humans and Dwarves follow the Church of the Holy Light.

Night Elves have Elune and the priesthood.

Undead priests draw on a tortured connection to the Light, often to self-harming degrees.

Trolls have voodoo Loa priests.

Gnomes, on the other hand, are famously secular. Their culture revolves around logic, invention, curiosity, and technology. They don’t have temples, Light-focused traditions, or spiritual doctrines. This isn’t to say no gnome could ever believe in the Light—but culturally, they’re not a race that has built institutions or teachings around it, which is what most playable priest races in Classic share.

Why Not Just Add It Then?
Well, it’s not just about lore. Back in Classic, race/class combos were also used to shape faction identity and balance—not just power balance, but thematic flavor. Gnomes are the only Alliance race without a healing class; Goblins, the same for the Horde. This may have been intentional, to make them feel like a quirky "outsider" pick. Adding healing to them could blur the uniqueness of races like Dwarves or Trolls, who have deeply rooted healing traditions.

Plus, expanding race/class combos can create ripple effects in game balance and PvP/Raiding metas, especially in a Classic environment where class identity is very rigid.

So in the end, while you are right that Gnomes technically use Holy spells, and that there are examples like Holdout Medics that show it’s possible, there's a difference between what’s possible in the world and what’s appropriate for player classes. Until gnomes develop an actual faith-based tradition or spiritual movement in the lore, Priesthood—at least in Classic—is something they just aren't a part of.

But hey, maybe a rogue sect of Light-wielding gnomes is forming deep in the irradiated caverns of Gnomeregan. Who’s to say what could happen next?
