# Set Tier Tokens instead of items

## Post 24719 by Novola — 2022-09-30T07:05:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=24719#p24719 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Reading this forum, i see that a lot of people are participating in healthy discussions about upgrading this and that class, adding or removing abilities, increasing damage, threat, etc etc.. and its all fine. We all have our opinions what needs to be done, in our own way.

But since we are trying to alter our classes in small, true to vanilla manner, not making them overpowered or underwhelming, this way we will **NEVER** reach true equilibrium between hybrid classes and pure damage dealing ones.

I know what you will say. But they are hybrid classes, they aren't supposed to do same damage, to heal the same way, to tank equally well. And again, you are right. They shouldn't, and they never will! But that also doesn't mean that they should be useless. Even if we don't want to admit it, we are still playing the SPECIALIZATION, rather than CLASS. We play enhancement shaman, or feral druid, or protection paladin, and we want to be successful in it, but i think that without proper raid sets dedicated to all specializations, rather than 1 spec per class, it is simply not possible to "complete" our characters. Usually it ends up on us hunting some off pieces of gear, making us look like idiots, wearing leather instead of mail, cloth instead of mail, leather, etc etc. (paladins in skirts come to mind, of course)

 ***So give us raid tier tokens instead of actual items.***

You grab the token, run to your trainer, he gives you choice of three quests, or two, or even one, depending on which specialization you choose. You are required to gather some minor (or major) items, depending on tier of the token you are trying to complete. I am talking few gems, crystals, pearls, orbs, some rarer loot from mobs, or plain bunch of regular loot, cloth, leather, ores.

And there you have it, hybrids getting their sets, stats tailored to all specs, proper itemization, and something to aspire to. Careful stat distribution, to make it viable to use, not just viable, but satisfactory. Getting tier sets is for many greatest achievement you can get in early wow, and it should feel equally epic and rewarding once you actually get one. Or several.

And with that you have solved one of two major issues with vanilla classes: **poor itemization for some specs**
on one hand, **poor spec design** on the other.

What do you think?

I don't think this suggestion should be dismissed out of hand. Regardless the various different "reasons" people could make for why someone might suggest this, in a vacuum, I think that it's objectively better game design to have exchangeable tokens drop rather than loot when you're asking 20-40 people to dedicate hours of their time every week to something which may not even yield loot for them. It's only made worse when RNG decides that specific loot for specific people drops.

tl;dr something that literally every single raid member can benefit from in some way every single time should be what drops, and at that point it's only rolls/DKP/whatever distribution that stands between any raid member and a reward, not serverside RNG.

## Post 26678 by Redmagejoe (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2022-10-03T23:59:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=26678#p26678 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

The above assumption relies on the idea that there is a select group of raiders who are relied on to carry raids, and if they aren't raiding, even if there are full raids worth of population wanting to raid, a raid will not take place. This sounds like a logical fallacy to me. Counterpoint: If people are aware that getting gear is less of an unreliable and arduous chore, more may be encouraged to actually raid.

There is a nonzero population of even vanilla WoW players who have never once tried to go to a raid and settle for pre-raid BiS. The reasons for this are varied and no one thing can be pointed to as the reason for their decisions, but I feel it would be foolish to assume that at least part of it isn't wanting to dedicate hours of their time every week to something which may, after months, never pay off for them.

There's no guarantee that players are even going to get the tokens, either. I am not proposing that everyone who attends a raid is automatically given a token. Rolling rules would still apply. But what do you do if a tier piece drops for a class that isn't even in your raid? That entire encounter/roll was a waste of everyone's time. I'm suggesting you make it physically impossible for anyone not to be able to benefit from what a boss drops.
