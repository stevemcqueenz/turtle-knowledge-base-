<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=30015#p30015 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

As someone who did 11 runs of Scholo without once seeing his set item drop; and hearing many, many, stories about even worse experiences...

I think it would be great if there was a token, similar to the transmog one, with which players could buy their Dungeon Set 1.

Cost could be of 3 tokens per item with 100% drop rate at dungeon end boss, in dungeons where said boss drops dungeon set loot, and 35-45% on any other boss that drops a BoP set item (this would also help UBRS groups be more incentivised to help priests get their shoulders).

Having every set buyable by every class can also mean perhaps less embaressing situations like class X rolling on class Y dungeon set with class Y in the group.

Dungeon Set is not that strong, and dungeons can sometimes be more difficult than some of the early raids, making a lot of people skip this set altogether. So this change would see a higher number of players sporting DS 1, and even DS 2.

## Post 31257 by Gladeshadow (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2022-12-21T00:32:00Z

I understand the point of view about tokens being a slippery slope or shortening the length of time needed for some. So please take the time to understand these points.

 1. RNG is generally a terrible thing in gaming. It’s gambling, which means some players are lucky and others are unlucky. Talking about a 2% drop rate, for example, does not equate to something like 50 runs to guarantee getting the item, which is the statistical statement someone above was using. That’s not how probability works and I’ve seen it in games like this and others that the unlucky players get a raw deal. It’s why a general good rule of gaming is to use pseudo-rng rather than actual rng. This is more or less what tokens would accomplish.
 2. We are adults. This means we generally have more limited time than we did as children. We play because it’s fun, not to have a second job. This adulting and having real life concerns comes up in the honor decay conversation as well
 3. Lowering time to get gear means more actual playing and less being on a treadmill. Someone above said that tokens would decrease play time and mean more nothing to do. This statement presumes grinding with no foreseeable end equates to meaningful play time and that this grinding is the thing to do - after that, there is nothing. This is simply untrue for most of us. Personally, I consider indeterminate grinding to be an artficial increase of gameplay time, like we see in other games that boast 100s of hours of gameplay… because the gameplay is grinding. For me, meaningful gameplay comes from things like RP, exploration of the world, and PvP. I never run out of stuff to do that isn’t sweaty and tired grinding. Many players likely share this sentiment and find grinding dull.

I’m saying this so you understand where we in favor of tokens are coming from. I don’t want to tell you your view is invalid. However, I do want you to understand that other views have validity as well, both in terms of in game gameplay values and in terms of real world time limitations for something we’ve already spent time grinding for almost 20 years ago.
