# DK's, DH's, and A Solution for Expansions Without Increasing Level Cap

## Post 99512 by Khalthul — 2024-07-15T13:42:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=99512#p99512 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Everyone's tired of hearing how they want Death Knight and Demon Hunters in this game, and I agree with them.

But I feel like the reason we disagree with them is not because we don't want those classes, but the way they were implemented.

So, allow me to introduce to you some no-nonsense ways to create expansions in the games without all the headache and worldbreaking that comes with raising the level cap.

~

The solution is very simple. If your campaign took you to Northrend or Outland, the way forward isn't through "leveling up," but through Acclimation.

Picture it like this, you take the boat from Stormwind Port all the way to Northrend to find yourself a stranger to the crippling cold and unholy grip of the desolate continent of Northrend. No matter how strong you are, this climate weighs down on you like a debuff that doesn't go away, like at all. The only way forward is to get used to this area until it becomes natural. When you "level up" here, you certainly do appear stronger, but only because that crippling debuff is lifted from you slightly, while still carrying the same gear you may have farmed from raids prior. (You could even implement the Survival profession here as a way to not freeze to death until fully acclimated)

This concept can apply to Outland just as easily as Northrend.

As far as the Hero Classes go, I think it should be similar to how Shamans were for the Horde, and Paladins were for the Alliance. The Forsaken would have gone to incredible lengths in necromancy to create their very own Death Knights, and the Alliance may have figured out how to do the same through the Illidari (Night Elves) to form Demon Hunters. Each faction gets their claim at being an edgelord, but not too much for one major city to handle.

With all the said, I do want to return to a set of ideas I mentioned here in the OP. I'm not begging for these things to happen, I just want to know if they could somehow fit with the spirit of the game. Namely, the Death Knight and Demon Hunter.

This might be a repeat of what I wrote earlier, but I'm just trying to flesh it out to see whether or not it can work with both the lore and the feel of the game.

There is a number of ways to approach it, but I'll start with one that makes sense to me, which is by relying on faction identity in the same way that the Shaman is in the Horde, and the Paladin is in the Alliance.

I think there is room in the lore for the Horde's warlocks and the Forsaken's necromancers to find a way to create Death Knights to bolster the Dark Lady's cause. What keeps this class from being accessible from the Alliance are the humans and high elves aversion to them because they have lost too much to consider such an abomination as being welcomed into their fold.

Similarly, the Demon Hunter is a class that only the Night Elf have roots in. There could be a case for High Elves, but as the lore currently has them, they refuse to be tempted and corrupted by the fel and other forbidden magics. This feat could be achieved by a sect of night elves who were taken aback by the destruction of the World Tree to consider learning about the fel and following in the footsteps of Illidan Stormrage to get even with the Burning Legion, even if not directly through him. The Horde, for lack of Blood Elves (thank you Turtle WoW), cannot claim this class.

Now that we covered the lore (hopefully), how would it effect the game?

Instead of introducing two more melee classes for each faction to contend over, it's just one class, per faction. That's less of a shock to the system than a free-for-all of Death Knights that could come out of any race, which was something I heavily disagreed with when looking at Wrath of the Lich King.

Personally, I never played a Death Knight, and neither a Demon Hunter, so I don't know how they work, or what their differences are. I only really can say that I enjoy their class fantasy. There currently does not exist a melee class with a strong dark fantasy to it, so these two options provide for it in a way that can fill a niche for each faction.

What level do they start if they are a hero class?

This consideration is difficult to balance. I think starting at level 40 would be the most fair, and gives access to the flavor of a Death Knight summoning their undead mount and the Demon Hunter the ability to run speedily like a ninja (or whatever Demon Hunters do). However, I think even at that level, it poses an unfair advantage to those with crafting professions, because you can essentially create free lvl 40s to bolster your characters. I think it would make more sense if they had a class specific primary profession that they specialized in solely, unable to specialize in anything else except for, perhaps, Secondary Professions. For the Death Knight, this would be Runeforging. For the Demon Hunter, this could be called Enblazonry. This "profession" would only empower the blades they wield, adding to the "hero" class fantasy without taking anything away from the other classes.

Personally, I believe that the problem of verticality in blizzards expansion design cannot be resolved by adding a new "artifical" scaling mechanic like the debuff introduced. World building wise, I don't understand what makes Northrend different to other "hostile" environments on azeroth already released?

Regardless of debuff or not, with adding WotLKs Northrend (or Outlands) you reduce the player density simply by spreading out the content. That's one of the main issue by expaning the world. With the introduction of Shattrat City in TBC every other HUB became pretty much meaningless (and all except ORG/STW already are, kinda). That can be avoided by simply not adding that one. TWoWs team isn't big enough (I believe) to lift a full scale expansion and introdcing the new continents step-by-step has to be done by adding invisible walls? Then you have to do all the ports, assets, reshaping (both continents are designed for flying mounts) etc. etc.
New classes
That idea isn't that fleshed out in the OP.
Adding new classes is a tricky business and even Blizz has been rather hesitant to do so. I'm not sure how this can be realised in such a small team based on vanilla (+) without destroying the entire balance of the game. Theorycrafting has always been a lot of fun, but finding a suitable niche is very difficult! Blizz made a complete (/working) design in their first iteration of WoW. Adding more classes is more a "fan service" / selling point than a necessity.
