# Forays - randomized 1-3 player content

## Post 147761 by Allarielle — 2025-08-22T23:23:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=147761#p147761 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

So I enjoyed doing Delves in The War Within. I enjoyed them immensely... for about 3 weeks. Even while enjoying them, I could see the... issues... glaring at me through my rose-tinted glasses. I feel like there's a core in there that's worth exploring, though. Here's my idea.
---
So, Forays!

 **Basic Premise**
Randomized mini-dungeons that you enter from portals that randomly spawn anywhere in the open world, tuned for 1-3 players, and with multiple difficulty tiers.

 **Portals**
There are Foray Nodes/Portals spread all across the map in every zone, potentially even including within cities. These nodes work like gathering nodes, in that only so many of them can be active at any given time, and after consuming one, it despawns until further notice. Each Foray Portal has a level requirement and a difficulty, both of which are visible in the tooltip. Once a player enters a Foray, the Foray Portal is locked (and ideally made invisible) to all players except those who were in the original player's party at the time they entered. Once all players leave the Foray, the Foray Portal despawns. The goal of this system is that you need to *explore* to find them, and if you happen to stumble onto one, it feels like a reward you want to capitalize upon. Sort of like running into a treasure chest in the wild, but with a bigger risk and reward.

Once inside the Foray, it'll be a semi-randomized instance that is based very roughly on the location where the Foray Portal appeared. If the portal appeared inside the Gold Coast Quarry, then the Foray takes place inside the Gold Coast Quarry. If the portal appeared inside a random hole in the wall, then the Foray might include a hidden cave that doesn't exist in the overworld. If the portal appeared on an open patch of grass? Well, it'll probably involve some exploration.

 **Mechanics**
As for the actual mechanics, it'd depend on the specific Foray and could vary dramatically. It might be a hide and seek encounter. It might be a race against an NPC. It might be a timed gathering challenge with enemies that'll try to throw dynamite at you to distract you. It might be a king of the hill style defence mission. Or it might be a mini dungeon. Regardless, it'll be tuned to take 5-10 minutes tops, unless you're way underprepared.

Unlike Delves, though, there's a much looser correlation between the specific Foray location and its mechanics. When you enter a Foray, you really don't know what you're getting. You don't know what the boss will be, or even if there'll be a boss at all. For that matter, you don't even know if there'll be fighting. Any given Foray location might have 1-3 different layouts it could choose from, and each layout might have a dozen or more different mechanics it could choose from. Rather than thinking of it in terms of individual Foray locations having a set of versions it can randomize between, it'd be more like there's a list of different Foray mechanics (dungeon, king of the hill, race, etc), and you might run into any one of them at any location.

Likewise, while the mob types are highly correlated with the location, even they can be randomized somewhat. A Foray in Redridge might involve gnolls, Blackrock orcs, spiders, murlocks, demons, Defias bandits, or, heck, maybe a secret sect of ogre mages hiding away from the world behind an illusion spell... It wouldn't be hand-crafted for each location, but rather chosen from a table of possibilities that are designed to be able to fit in multiple scenarios or locations, with a few hand-crafted hooks for more complex mechanics to plug into.

 **Tuning**
The content is tuned for 2 players at 1-3 levels above the Foray's level requirement, but it scales a little bit in either direction so 1 player with a bit of gear or 3 players who are new to the game don't feel out of place. At 4 or 5 players, the difficulty scales up dramatically so it's still technically possible, but a *lot* more difficult in relative terms. At 1-3 players you can go in with a Hunter Pet tank and a Enhancement Shaman healer. At 4-5 players you need dedicated roles just because the monsters are hitting too hard. In addition, if the average level of the party is higher than the level requirement of the Foray, the Foray will adjust its level up a bit up to about 5 levels higher. IE, it'll try to make sure the mobs are always a level or 2 above you, even if it's a Foray of a slightly lower level than you are.

As for the difficulty, the Forays come in 3 difficulty levels.
- **Difficulty 1 Forays** are approximately the same as the open world.
- **Difficulty 2 Forays** add additional mechanics to all of the mobs and bosses, but is only at the level of stuff you need to interrupt or dodge on the floor.
- **Difficulty 3 Forays** add the kind of mechanics you'd expect to find in end-game dungeons or raids - things you need to carefully time, practice for, and use all the tools in your toolbelt to overcome. You aren't necessarily expected to be able to clear a difficulty 3 Foray alone, simply on account of not having enough tools, but some classes might have an easier time than others.
 **Rewards**
Rewards scale based on difficulty, but you can generally expect to get 1-2 pieces of gear or an equivalent value in consumables, as well as a 50-100% boost in gold-per-hour while inside, and a healthy collection of gathering nodes for which you don't need to compete.
- **Difficulty 1 Forays** reward whites and greens. Similar to what you'd find grinding mobs of the same level but with a bit higher of a grind-to-reward ratio.
- **Difficulty 2 Forays** reward greens and, potentially, a blue. Better gear than you'd find in the open world, but only as good as dungeon gear if you're *really* lucky.
- **Difficulty 3 Forays** reward anything from greens to purples, but the rewards are blues about 95% of the time, and greens most of the rest. You can get lucky, though.
Regardless of difficulty, all Foray gear is either completely randomized, pulled from the loot tables of mobs in that general level range, or pulled from a general loot pool shared by all Forays. Potentially there'd be some zone-based loot mechanics where loot from Forays in Swamp of Sorrows has a swampy theme, but it'd never be designed on a per Foray basis. There's never any expectation that the gear that drops will match the classes or specs of anyone inside, any more so than you'd expect open world loot to match. It'll almost always be BoP, though, but there might be exceptions now and then.

 **Design Goals**
The goal of this system is multifold.
- **First**, to add a new game mode for solo-enjoyers to enjoy *without* disrupting the regular progresion of dungeons and raids. If you're out in the open world, you get some spice and some progression, but if you want *reliable* loot-ups, you still need to party-up.
- **Second**, to add solo progression. You might not be able to take difficulty 3 Forays right upon hitting 60, but after you've geared up a bit you can come back to try again. Every Foray is different, but for every time you attempt one you get a little bit more prepared for whatever they might throw at you.
- **Third**, to add value and fun to exploration in the open world. You never know if there might be a Foray in that cave or around that next bend, and the reward for finding one is enough to make the chance worth taking. If you're out questing or grinding for your next raid night, Forays break up the routine in a fun and rewarding way.
- **Fourth**, to give another reason for players to form random pick up groups while out and about. You can take them on solo, sure, but you'll be artifically increasing the difficulty, denying yourself potentially valuable mechanic-countering tools, and denying the playerbase the chance to loot BoP gear that might otherwise go to the vendor.
 **Potential Expansions**
Though this would be the core of the system, it could easily be expanded in a number of intuitive ways. For example:
- Additional difficulty levels, particularly at level 60. Unlike with Delves, these would continue the trend of having each increase in reward quality be tied to a *qualitative* increase in mechanic complexity. Not just tighter tuning, but more and more complex mechanics over all.
- Additional locations, scenarios, and mob types.
- Storylines and/or quests that involve Forays or that span across multiple Forays.
- Ways to get other party members to the Foray location rapidly, such as Warlock-free summons or some kind of ferry, flight, or portal system.
- Ways to track currently extant Foray Portals.
- Ways to force Foray Portals to spawn in, potentially allowing for high level Forays in low level zones.
- Ways to influence Foray Portals before you enter them, such as increasing or decreasing the level or difficulty, forcing specific mob types or scenarios, or adding Path of Exile-style affixes to increase the challenge and reward.
 **Main Design Challenges**
Besides the amount of content that would need to be added, the main difficulty in designing this system would be in making it fit in with the Classic design aesthetic. The potential pitfalls are many, and the worst case scenario would be if it felt like it trivialized the gearing process or discouraged grouping. The idea is to give player options and expand the number of ways the world can be enjoyed, *not* to create an endless seasonal grind for meaningless loot that trivializes other forms of content.
