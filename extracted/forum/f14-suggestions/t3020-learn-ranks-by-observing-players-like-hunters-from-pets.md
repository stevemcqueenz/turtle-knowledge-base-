# Learn ranks by observing players like hunters from pets

## Post 18636 by Mativh (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2022-05-15T15:28:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=18636#p18636 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

**Players could have a chance to learn higher ranks from being in a party with someone of their class and observe them use those ranks/abilities.**

Learning not only from trainers, but by observing other players and personal experience.

 *The hunter can tame an animal, observe it doing something, learn that ability, then teach it to other pets. What a cool concept, it could've been you clicking on a trainer, paying him gold and your pet learning it. But no, you experience first hand what is assumed that the NPC did in order to teach you, Vanilla WoW refuses to not be an amazing immersive game! :)*

This feature could be expanded to player interactions, enriching the social aspect of the game.

Just a small chance so the interaction wouldn't feel forced, merely to save gold, but big enough so there is a possibility that this can happen from being in a party for other reasons, and when it happens, being a pleasant surprise that adds depth to the game.

**A related idea is that abilities could have a very small chance to trigger learning a higher rank when used.**

Small enough so it isn't the primary source of higher ranks so visiting trainers is still incentivised, but at least being a possibility to learn from experience as well. It could could happen only once per X time, as to not incentivise spamming an ability in order to learn it. Of course level restrictions would still apply.

Once it happens it could trigger a dialogue bubble from a trainer during the next visit, the reaction could vary by race or class, a forsaken wishing others did the same instead of annoying him, a minor praise by a warrior, a goblin being angry that you didn't pay him instead etc.

I disagree, you learning all abilities and ranks that are available to you when you level automatically is a step away from roleplaying, most roleplaying features are basically simulating real life things, or at least follow somecsort of internal logic of a fantasy world, what Blizzard did went against it. That is pure automatisation. What I propose is simulating learning an ability in a way it makes sense, not only from being taught from one source (trainer), but an alternative one (player, experience). Imagine you are knowing how to do something and you watch someone else with more skill doing it but better, and you learn to be better as well. The small chance to learn ranks by using the ability might be considered more automatic than learning every time from a trainer but it does make sense, the same way getting better with a type of weapon from using it makes sense. I'm not saying that learning from a trainer shouldn't be the main source, but learning from players that have higher ranks and from experience being a possibility isn't the same as mindless automatic learning from leveling up like in retail WoW.
