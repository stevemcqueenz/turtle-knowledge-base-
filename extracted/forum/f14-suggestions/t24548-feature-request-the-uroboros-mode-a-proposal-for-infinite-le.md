# Feature Request: The "Uroboros" Mode - A Proposal for Infinite Leveling

## Post 172777 by Lc — 2026-03-27T18:37:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=172777#p172777 | page 1 | era: post-1.18.1 -->

Subject: Feature Request: **The "Uroboros" Mode** - A Proposal for Infinite Leveling

To the Turtle WoW GM Team,

Since I am from a non-English speaking region, please forgive any minor translation nuances.

As a dedicated Turtle WoW player who has leveled over a dozen characters, I am writing to propose a brand new leveling mode—one that is distinct from anything currently available.

This is an idea I have been nurturing since last year.
We need a mode that is "eternal," "infinite," "cyclical," and "non-entropic."
I call it: **The Uroboros Mode**.

With this mode, players like me, who love the process of leveling alts, won't have to struggle with character slot limits anymore.
We won't need to delete a beloved character just to make space for a new one, feeling "crowded" and "complicated" by our own progress.

Here is my initial model for your consideration:

 **Uroboros Mode**: (Can be stacked with any other leveling mode)
While playing in this mode, you can group and trade with other players as usual.
However, once you reach level 60, you will be presented with a choice to enter "Reincarnation."

New NPC in the Capital City:
 **Option 1: Enter Reincarnation**
Your character will reset to Level 1 and return to the starting zone.
All equipped gear will be destroyed, but Gold and Bag contents are retained.
Crucially: You can re-select your Class, while your Race and Appearance remain unchanged.
Additionally: Toys and Mounts are preserved.
(GMs could also configure this to optionally not retain gold/bags).
Simultaneously, your Level 1 character earns the title "**Uroboros I**".

(By analogy, completing the cycle a second time makes you **"Uroboros II"**, and you can choose to reincarnate indefinitely.)

 **Option 2: Do not enter Reincarnation yet**
Continue with end-game activities in your current state.

In the future, we will frequently see a group of "Reincarnators"—the Uroboros.
That will be a very cool sight.
They transcend the result (max level) and purely enjoy the cycle of Uroboros.
This is something only Turtle WoW can do.

## Post 174162 by Kefke (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2026-04-06T10:10:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=174162#p174162 | page 1 | era: post-1.18.1 -->

Stumbled across this while looking to see if anyone had posted about something else, and it's actually very similar to an idea I've had for a long time. I didn't really think it was a good fit for Turtle in the past, after reading the OP, I remembered that the Caverns of Time are a thing. So my pitch would be to tie it to that. Maybe give the player a small reward for prestiging, like a bonus to skill levels (not applied during PVP and raids) or better drop rates, IDK.

I would say that, rather than losing your stuff, it should be added to a "time vault". Some kind of special storage that you can take things out of, but not put them back into. Or...maybe something that works like a store. Any item you've previously had is added to the Time Vault, like adding appearances to your transmog collection. At the Vault, you can "retrieve" unlocked items using a resource gained by doing repeatable quests for the Bronze Dragonflight (from questgivers you can only find after being "sent back in time"/reincarnated). Possibly also located, if not in the Caverns, at least somewhere where the player has to work to get back to it. So if you really liked having a certain boss drop, and don't want to farm to get it again, you could buy it back.

I just think that the ability to reset a character without losing them is useful for MMOs in general. A lot of games suffer "Jenga tower design" where they're constantly adding more endgame at the expense of the other parts of the game. Plus not everybody is super into raiding and doing the same couple dungeons over and over for months on end. Players going back through the game to explore more encourages having a "wide" design with lots to do at low levels as well as high ones, which is something Turtle already seems to be going for. So it seems like a good fit to me.
