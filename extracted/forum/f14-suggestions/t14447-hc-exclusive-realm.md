# HC Exclusive Realm

## Post 98381 by Gnarbrok — 2024-07-02T17:04:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=98381#p98381 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Turtle wow should open an HC-exclusive realm and run the hardcore challenge on that realm rather than on Nordanaar. I don't want to claim to speak for hardcore players as a body, but this seems to be a common desire expressed in /h and amongst many hardcore players I know (although certainly some dissent).

An HC realm would provide a gameplay experience that is better for everyone. As it is now, hardcore and softcore players must compete for the same mob tags, quest objectives, resource nodes, and so on, with only a minimal possibility of interacting or cooperating or working together. HC/SC are completely separated from trading and grouping, and so players from each game mode effectively act as drains on the resources of the other mode, often leading to hostility. So a HC realm would benefit even softcore players by ensuring their herbs are not picked by characters they cannot even trade with and that they can always group with players on the same quest as them.

An HC realm would also provide an experience that is fairer and more comprehensive for hardcore players. While it is annoying to have your mob stolen by a HC player you can't group with, the situation is even worse for hardcore players, who can both lose mob tags to SC players and be actively griefed by SC players who cannot die. In addition, hardcore players are at a disadvantage in open world gathering, since softcore players can beat hardcore characters to the punch by engaging in behavior that would be reckless from a hardcore perspective. This disadvantage is epitomized by the fact that hardcore characters will likely never get a single world boss kill on Nordanaar (aside from Concavius I suppose) because they would simply be griefed out of the kill by softcore players who can freely die in an attempt to wipe the hardcore raid.

In addition, an HC-exclusive realm would help HC players in a number of unexpected ways. For example, finding groups for dungeons on HC can be difficult because looking for group members in /h is just about your only option. But /h chat is bugged and can only be turned off/on for ALL chat windows - you cannot have a dedicated /h chat window by default. As a result, many players simply turn /h off for good and never get hooked into running dungeons.

It was strongly suggested to me on discord that there are a number of questions I need to answer as a bare minimum for this suggestion to be taken seriously. Frankly it seems to me highly unreasonable that I need to answer every worry and concern and question about a HC only realm before turtle wow staff decide it could possibly be worth pursuing. (If any turtle wow staff would like to know why this is such an unreasonable demand I would be happy to explain it to them, although I must admit I think they might not enjoy hearing the explanation.) Furthermore, meeting such a demand is likely to produce solutions to things that end up not being problems and not solve problems that only reveal themselves in the course of development, making the whole exercise a bit futile. Nonetheless, I will oblige, quoting from discord the concerns raised.

"do you really think there should be a transfer (more free work for the devs? keklol) at all?" My personal view is that, yes, hardcore characters should be transferred to the new hardcore realm. That seems to be fairest to all those who have put in countless hours on their hardcore characters. (I'm also not really sure what the alternative would be - deleting my Nordanaar hardcore characters rather than transferring them or simply turning those characters SC seems out of the question.) Also, the quote (from discord) suggests that by suggesting that something would be good for turtle wow I am just some guy trying to get the devs to do "free work". I have played on this server for over four years so I strongly resent the implicit claim that my suggestions, intended to benefit the server, are simply attempts at getting the devs to waste their time.

"Shouldn't there be HC only gameplay with no immortal shenanigans?" I assume the question is: should there be immortal characters on an HC-exclusive realm? The answer to that is obviously, "no". I think the easiest solution is simply to give hardcore players on the hardcore realm an option once they hit level 60: stay hardcore and on the hardcore realm, or transfer your character to the softcore realm.

" No AH, no mail, no pvp?" My personal opinion is that AH/mail/pvp should be treated exactly the same on the hardcore realm as they are on Nordanaar HC currently. Perhaps others have a different view. Notably, the answer you give to these questions has VERY little bearing on the question "should there be a HC-exclusive realm?"

"Which quests tag you for PvP? Do you have a list?" Similarly, my view is that quests that flag you for PVP should not flag you for PVP on the HC realm to the extent that that is possible. (E.g., I shouldn't get flagged doing my warrior class quest at level 10.) I don't think I am obligated to provide a more detailed answer than this.

Thank you for reading. I would love to hear other perspectives on the topic and suggestions about how such a change might be implemented.

I appreciate the passion and thought you’ve put into advocating for a Hardcore-only realm on Turtle WoW. That kind of commitment to a game and community is admirable, and the fact that you’ve been around for four years gives your opinion weight. However, I think your proposal, while well-intentioned, carries with it a number of assumptions and unintended consequences that would ultimately make it a bad move for the server and the community as a whole.

Let me try to go point by point and explain why.

First, the idea that a HC-only realm would provide a “better experience for everyone” is built on the assumption that the current problems hardcore players face are best solved through complete segregation. That’s not necessarily true. Most of the difficulties you describe—like competition for mobs or nodes—exist in *any* multiplayer environment, not just HC vs SC. The real issue here isn’t that the players are on different modes, it’s that WoW’s open world content is inherently limited and was designed before layered servers or scalable instancing existed. Creating a whole new realm won’t fix the scarcity—it just shifts it somewhere else. You’ll still be fighting other hardcore players for tags and gathering nodes, and you’ll have *fewer players overall* to interact with. So it may feel less crowded for a while, but that effect disappears as the realm matures and fills.

Second, your point about HC and SC being “completely separated” from trading and grouping is true—but it’s *by design*. That’s the hardcore experience. You’re not meant to rely on other players in the same way you do in softcore. If the devs wanted integrated grouping and trading, they’d have built it in. The separation is part of the challenge. Suggesting that the solution to this separation is to move all HC players to their own sandbox feels like it misses the point of HC—it’s not supposed to be convenient, and you’re not supposed to have access to every amenity that softcore players do. That friction is what makes the experience memorable.

On the topic of griefing: this is one of the stronger points you raise, but it’s also one that’s hard to solve by making a new realm. Griefing happens in any MMO, in any mode, on any server. If a SC player can grief you on Nordanaar, what's to stop a HC player from doing the same on a dedicated HC realm? You mention players dying to griefers in boss fights, but the reality is that coordinating boss kills is already a niche activity among HC players and not a widespread expectation. A new realm doesn't magically fix bad behavior—it just moves it. And it divides the moderation and development attention the team can give. You're asking a small team to police yet *another* server with its own flavor of issues.

As for the bugged /h chat and trouble finding dungeon groups—those are solvable problems that don’t require realm-splitting solutions. Fixing the chat is a technical issue, not a structural one. Adding a dedicated LFG channel for hardcore players, or improving the UI to make HC grouping easier, is far more efficient and more realistic than launching and maintaining a second realm. You don’t demolish your house because the living room light doesn’t work.

Now, let’s talk about transfers. You say HC characters should be transferred to the new realm. But here's the thing: character transfers, especially from mixed-mode realms to mode-specific ones, are *not* trivial. There's tracking involved. Audits. Testing. Potential abuse to monitor. There are real costs in dev and QA time. Dismissing these as “free work” or suggesting that they owe you that because you’ve played for years is unfair. Your long-time presence should be valued, but it doesn’t entitle you to dictate how dev time is spent. Every player thinks *their* idea would benefit the server. That’s not unique to HC players.

You also imply that the devs’ hesitation is unreasonable—that they want you to answer “every worry and concern.” But that’s how game development works. Changes ripple. You don’t just flip a switch and create a realm. You have to ask: will this split the playerbase? Will it shrink the existing realm? Will it hurt leveling populations? What happens when players get bored at 60 with fewer others to play with? These aren’t “stalling tactics,” they’re due diligence. And not every problem is obvious until you’re knee-deep in implementation, as you said yourself.

You then suggest that players should be able to transfer from HC to SC once they hit 60. That’s actually not a terrible idea in a vacuum—but again, it requires more dev effort, and for what? A system to support a tiny percentage of the HC population who actually reach 60 and want to “retire” their character? You’re building a complex migration system for a problem that affects a very small group.

When you respond to the AH/mail/PvP questions with “just do it the way it is now,” that kind of weakens your argument. If the goal is to create a *different*, more fair, more “pure” hardcore experience, then rethinking systems like these should be on the table. Instead, it feels like you're saying “make a new realm but don’t change much,” which begs the question—what is this really solving?

Same goes for the PvP flagging issue. You brush off the need to provide specifics about which quests flag you as a PvP target. But again, that *matters*. If you want devs to take your suggestion seriously, saying “I don’t think I need to do more than say it’s a problem” is not persuasive. They're not asking you to code it. They’re asking for a list. That’s not unreasonable. And if you truly believe this issue affects fairness and gameplay, the burden is on you to show that it's more than a handful of corner cases.

Last point: You say you’d be happy to explain to devs why you think their standards are unreasonable, but also imply they wouldn’t like the answer. That comes across as confrontational. If you truly want to be heard, assuming bad faith from the people you’re trying to convince is probably not the best move.

In the end, the core issue is this: Turtle WoW is a small, custom server with limited staff, limited dev time, and a playerbase that already exists in a niche corner of WoW fandom. Splitting that base further into multiple realms increases maintenance costs, weakens the community, and creates more problems than it solves. The HC challenge *already exists*. It’s imperfect, but it’s there. If the solution is more communication tools, better grouping options, or modest system tweaks—that’s what the devs should focus on.

A whole new realm is a massive ask. And there’s just not enough gain to justify the cost.
