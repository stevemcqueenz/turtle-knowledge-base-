# More areas of Azeroth filled with zones

## Post 156587 by Allwynd01 (Bug Report Enthusiast) — 2025-10-22T09:21:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=156587#p156587 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

I notice that there are still plenty of areas on the map that aren't filled with zones and content where players can go and do things.

Just expanding existing zones or creating more new ones will open up Azeroth a lot more. Some inspiration can be taken from Retail, like how Stonetalon Mountains or Blasted Lands were originally "landlocked" zones, but after Cataclym they were "opened up" to the beach.

And there are a lot of zones like this - Badlands, Redridge Mountains, Blasted Lands and even ones that already have an opening to the water on some side, on other sides they are walled off for no good reason.

My dream for Vanilla (after finding out how much unused areas they were) was to see it one day fully developed and back then, before TBC even existed, I thought that this was going to be the path that Blizzard will take, little did I know they would release trash like TBC and everything onwards.

Turtle WoW has been sort of "my dream coming true" in that regard, but while they've done some things in this direction, there are still a lot more areas that are neglected and I wish for them to be given more attention.

Northwind is a good example of that - while it connected the empty space between Elwynn Forest and Dun Morogh, it also left some empty space and the coast is largely unused with one tiny port area on the very north.

And the concept from Vanilla that path between zone A and B should necessarily be a choke point is not very good. For example, zones like The Barrens, Durotar, Elwynn Forest, Westfall, Duskwood have a large open border created by a river and this should be more common, not just some small pathway between the mountains. This seems more like a technological limitation and in my opinion that philosophy needs to be eradicated going forward and more zones to have a wider transition between one another, because it creates more open space that can be traversible and just accessible for players to reach.

Hopefully with UE5, cities like Stormwind and Orgrimmar can be better incorporated into the map so you don't get clipping issues and disappearing assets if you use the Hunter's Eagle Eye ability on some peak to look around and just see a lot of undeveloped areas that are only intended to be viewed from a certain angle on the ground.

That's my main hope for UE5 - to allow Azeorth to look more "geographically" realistic and feel like one giant landmass, rather than small pockets of zones surrounded by mountains on all sides in the most laziest and convenient way to wall off places where you aren't supposed to go because either the developers (Blizzard at the time) didn't have time to make every place accessible, or didn't want to at all or the technology and time constraints prevented them from doing it. I don't like expansion content, but just because Outland and Northrend (and after Cataclym Azeroth too) were developed to look good for flying mounts, the world looks more coherent. And Turtle WoW, without flying mounts should have this too.

Because focusing on creating new islands for content like Balor, Tel'Abim and Gilijim is cool and I like them, I think the main focus should've always been to "populate" the already existing landmass with as much content as possible and not leave out any area or space uninhabited or inaccessible.

I never viewed Hawk's Vigil as out of place for its terrain. If we take into account all the biomes and everything in every zone, it feels a bit chaotic to be honest. Badlands being a desert next to Dun Morogh that appears to be covered under snow (all year round? ... maybe UE5 will have seasons and we can have summer Dun Morogh, although it will very much like Loch Modan).

Changing some assets is not that hard, but removing mountains and replacing them with terrain you can walk on is in my opinion more important. This can be the biggest draw to Turtle WoW a year or two from now, along with UE5. Azeroth reimagined - all the original content is there and 90% of the mountains are replaced with more content and expanded zones.

And in the few places where you really want to have mountains separating zones, you can leave them too.

But, for example, LOTRO has A LOT of mountainous zones, like Trollshaws, Evendim to name a few, and I remember very clearly in those two there were very steep mountains, in WoW you could barely climb those without "wall running", but in LOTRO they have trees, you can walk there and they even have mobs for quests and even NPCs and questgivers. Basically the terrain in LOTRO looks more like the real world. Mountains are not perceived so much as some wall, but a place you can walk upon.

I think Turtle WoW should embrace that, because frankly, they can add new zones in the places where we have currently empty space, they can add new islands or entirely new continents, but I think giving more attention to the original content and improving it even more is more important, because that's what people experience the most, not Gilijim or Gilneas, but Elwynn Forest, The Barrens and such. I think that's what people will appreciate the most. If you walk north along the eastern coast of Stranglethorn Vale, you can enter Blasted lands, Swamp of Sorrows, Redridge Mountains, that empty space between Redridge and Badlands, then Loch Modan, Grim Reaches, all seamlessly.
