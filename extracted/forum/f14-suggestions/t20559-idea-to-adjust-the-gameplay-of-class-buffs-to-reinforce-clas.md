# Idea to adjust the gameplay of class buffs to reinforce class fantasy and make their gameplay more active

## Post 142125 by Allarielle — 2025-07-29T20:52:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=142125#p142125 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

So my thinking is that a lot of buffs are just fire and forget. They aren't particularly fun, and they don't occupy a whole lot of space in your mind. More importantly, while they're thematic in terms of their names and flavour text, the gameplay usually doesn't do much to reflect the class fantasy. Thus, I went through all the different buffs I could think of and adjusted them with consideration to how their gameplay fits their class.

I didn't pay a whole lot of attention to specific numbers or balancing, mostly because I figure those things would need to be tweaked and tweaked again constantly for a few months before they felt good and were balanced once again. Once the core idea of the change is in place, getting the numbers right can come after.

---
 **Druid**
- Mark of the Wild: Now requires melee range to cast. Cannot be cast on self. 1.5 sec cast time. 30 sec cooldown. Lasts 10 min.
- Gift of the Wild: Removed.
- Improved Mark of the Wild buffed by roughly 50% to compensate.
- New talent added at tier 1 in Restoration, requiring 5/5 Improved Mark of the Wild, called Gift of the Wild. Allows the Druid to cast Mark of the Wild on themself with further increased effect.
- Thorns: Duration reduced to 1 min. Damage increased by roughly 100%. Range reduced to melee, but is increased back up to 30 yards with 2/2 Nature's Reach. Thick Hide increases the duration when cast on yourself by 3/6/9 min.
- Omen of Clarity: Back to being a buff with a 10 min duration, 3 sec cast time. Nature's Reach gives it a 50/100% chance to avoid being dispelled.
Instead of being a buffbot, Druids are fierce custodians of the Wild. Making Mark of the Wild require melee range reinforces that fantasy, with the idea of you personally drawing the mark on the other character. For Thorns and Omen of Clarity, reducing the duration makes them a more conscious part of your toolkit - instead of firing them off and forgetting about them, they are now skills you need to pay attention to and make choices about, even if that choice is to spec into making them more forgettable.
---
 **Hunter**
- No changes.

---
 **Mage**
- Arcane Intellect: Removed.
- Arcane Brilliance: Removed.
- New skill added: Brilliance Aura: Increases intellect of all party members within 30 yards, decreasing based on distance from the Mage. Drains mana every 3 while active. Lasts until cancelled.
The fantasy of a Mage isn't as someone who bestows intelligence on others, but as someone who, by being in their presence and listening to their words, naturally causes others to grow smarter. Thus Arcane Intellect simply makes more sense to be an aura. By giving the aura a mana drain component and making it only affect your party, this also adds some gameplay decisions about how and when it should be used, making it a more active part of your gameplay even when there's multiple Mages in the raid.
---
 **Paladin**
- All blessings now last 5 min once again.
- New skill added: Renew Blessings. 1.5 sec cast. Refreshes the duration of all your blessings on party members within 30 yards. (Does not affect raid members)
- To compensate, all blessings are buffed by roughly 25%.
- All auras now drain mana every 3 sec while active, and have decreasing effect based on distance from the Paladin.
The Paladin fantasy is ALL ABOUT being the centre of the party, the Holy Lynchpin, the one who blesses others by their mere presence. That Paladin blessings are simply fire and forget undermines this fantasy. There's a good reason they were made that way, though, and that's because it simply isn't fun to spend half your play time simply recasting them. Thus: Renew Blessings. Once again your Blessings are an active part of your gameplay that you need to pay at least a little attention to throughout a fight, but they don't consume a large amount of your game time. As for Auras, by making them decrease in power with range, you're now tasked with being slightly more aware of their positioning in the group, and by giving them a mana drain effect, you have to consider when and if the auras are needed instead of simply forgetting about them.
---
 **Priest**
- Power Word: Fortitude: Now lasts 2 min, but is refreshed any time you heal the target. This would include healing from Vampiric Embrace and Lightwell.
- Improved Power Word: Fortitude moved several rows down in the Discipline tree, but buffed by roughly 50%.
- Prayer of Fortitude: Removed.
- Divine Spirit: Now a 5 sec cast, much higher mana cost, costs a Sacred Candle or lower tier equivalent. Now affects the target's entire party.
- Prayed of Spirit: Removed.
The main idea for Priests is to differentiate the two buffs from one another. Instead of simply being two buttons you need to press as you cycle down the party list, they're now two different thought processes you need to pay attention to. Divine Spirit being a party buff gives the fantasy of the Priest calling upon the divine to come and pay attention. Power Word: Fortitude being a short duration buff aligns it more closely with Power Word: Shield as part of the Discipline spec fantasy. Making it refreshable by heals is a quality of life boon so that it's simultaneously something to which you need to pay active attention, and not something to which you need to dedicate a whole lot of time.
---
 **Rogue**
- No changes.

---
 **Shaman**
- All totems now have a 1.5 sec cast time baseline.
- All totems are now completely free.
- Casting another totem of the same element as a totem already out (or recasting the same one) now recalls the totem of that element instead. If you're within melee range of the totem in question, this is instant. If you're not, it takes ~3 sec to cast and costs very considerable mana. You can now also recall a totem of a particular element by right clicking the totem of that element in your inventory - but this is no different than simply trying to cast another totem of the same type.
Shaman totems are physical objects that exist in your inventory, not spells like any other. Making them free allows you to throw them down as a matter of course regardless of level, the first part of any battle without needing to consider to cost... and a part of your identity. By making it so you need to physically run up to totems in order to pick them up before you can throw them down in a new location or with a new effect, it also reinforces the fantasy that these are physical objects of which you only have one of each type, and it forces you to be more conscious about where and when you throw them down as well as how you position yourself relative to them after doing so.
---
 **Warlock**
- Demon Armor is now a toggle with a 1.5 sec cast time to activate, and now drains a small amount of mana every 3 sec while active, but the health regeneration is increased by maybe 3 times.
Making Demon Armour a toggle is just a little quality of life. Making it a trade of mana for life makes it a choice you need to consider. If you're just going to be afk or running around, you might want to toggle it off so you don't need to keep topping yourself off with lifetap. If you stack enough spirit you can balance it to the point where there's no drain. If you ignore spirit, though, you'll need to at least be aware of how it's affecting your pools.
---
 **Warrior**
- Battle Shout now has 3 stages at 0-30 sec, 30-60 sec, and 60-120 sec. At the first stage it's roughly 50% stronger than currently. At the third stage it's roughly 50% weaker than currently.
By making Battle Shout diminish over time, it gives you a meaningful choice to make about it. Do you refresh it early to get the most benefit, or refresh it later to save on Rage? It isn't a big difference, but it makes the gameplay slightly more active.
