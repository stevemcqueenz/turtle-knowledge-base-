# Woodworking Profession

## Post 148777 by kalevala206 (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2025-08-27T22:59:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=148777#p148777 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I made some suggestions a while ago for quite a few Profession adjustments that I think fits the WoW Classic+ vibe. I wanted to dive in a little deeper into what a new profession (Woodworking) could look like in a little more depth. actual material costs are not thought out especially or meant to be balanced, but exist as a vibe check for the kinds of materials you could ask for and have it makes sense.

 **Woodworking: Recipes & Materials (1–300)**

Softwood → Ironbark → Darkwood → Elderwood progression for common recipes.
Specialty hardwoods (Runewood, Fireshard, Glacial Heartwood) for rare/raid recipes.

 **Apprentice (1–75) – Softwood Timber**
 *Base Material:* **Softwood Timber** (Elwynn, Durotar, Tirisfal, Mulgore).
 *Supporting Mats:* Minor Gems, Light Leather, Linen Cloth.
 *Player Level:* 1–10

• **Rough Walking Stick** (Staff, +2 Spirit)
— Materials: 4 Softwood Timber

• **Practice Bow** (Bow, 2–4 damage, +1 Agi)
— Materials: 5 Softwood Timber, 1 Light Leather

• **Carved Javelin** (Polearm, 3–5 damage, +1 Strength)
— Materials: 6 Softwood Timber, 1 Copper Bar

• **Ashwood Wand** (Wand, 8–10 Arcane damage)
— Materials: 4 Softwood Timber, 1 Linen Cloth, 1 Moonstone

• **Light Hunting Spear** (Polearm, +2 Agi, 2.6 speed)
— Materials: 6 Softwood Timber, 2 Light Leather

 **Journeyman (75–150) – Ironbark Lumber**
 *Base Material:* **Ironbark Lumber** (Redridge, Barrens, Ashenvale).
 *Supporting Mats:* Bronze Bars, Medium Leather, Wool Cloth.
 *Player Level:* 10–25

• **Oak Longbow** (Bow, 9–18 damage, +3 Agi)
— Materials: 8 Ironbark Lumber, 2 Spider Silk

• **Tribal Totem Staff** (Staff, +4 Spirit, +3 Int)
— Materials: 10 Ironbark Lumber, 2 Light Feathers

• **Bronzewood Pike** (Polearm, +4 Str, +2 Sta)
— Materials: 12 Ironbark Lumber, 2 Bronze Bars

• **Maple Wand** (Wand, 21–25 Shadow damage)
— Materials: 4 Ironbark Lumber, 1 Pearl, 1 Bronze Bar

• **Sturdy War Staff** (Staff, +6 Str, +6 Sta)
— Materials: 12 Ironbark Lumber, 3 Medium Leather

 **Expert (150–225) – Darkwood Planks**
 *Base Material:* **Darkwood Planks** (Stranglethorn, Hinterlands, Stonetalon).
 *Supporting Mats:* Heavy Leather, Steel Bars, Mageweave Cloth, Elemental Essences.
 *Player Level:* 25–40

• **Emberwood Bow** (Bow, +6 Agi, +3 Sta, Fire proc)
— Materials: 14 Darkwood Planks, 3 Spider Silk, 1 Elemental Fire

• **Runed Spiritstaff** (Staff, +12 Healing, +6 Int, +6 Spirit)
— Materials: 16 Darkwood Planks, 2 Mageweave Cloth, 1 Elemental Water

• **Heavy Hunting Pike** (Polearm, +12 Str, +10 Sta)
— Materials: 18 Darkwood Planks, 3 Steel Bars

• **Elemental Wands** (Fire/Frost/Shadow variants, 45–55 dmg)
— Materials: 4 Darkwood Planks, 2 Elemental Fire/Frost/Shadow, 1 Mageweave Cloth, Enchanting Mats

• **Totem-etched Warstaff** (Staff, +10 Agi, +10 Sta)
— Materials: 18 Darkwood Planks, 4 Heavy Leather, Enchanting Mats

 **Artisan (225–300) – Elderwood Logs**
 *Base Material:* **Elderwood Logs** (Feralas, Un’Goro, Winterspring, Azshara).
 *Supporting Mats:* Rugged Leather, Thorium Bars, Dream Dust, Essences.
 *Player Level:* 40–60

• **Thorium Recurve Bow** (Bow, +15 Agi, +7 Sta, +1% Hit)
— Materials: 20 Elderwood Logs, 4 Rugged Leather, 2 Thorium Bars

• **Livingwood Staff** (Staff, +55 Healing, +15 Spirit, +10 Int)
— Materials: 22 Elderwood Logs, 3 Dream Dust, 2 Essence of Water

• **Warbringer Pike** (Polearm, +20 Str, +20 Sta)
— Materials: 24 Elderwood Logs, 4 Thorium Bars, 3 Rugged Leather

• **Crystal Focus Wand** (Wand, +22 Arcane damage)
— Materials: 4 Elderwood Logs, 8 Dream Dust, 4 Essence of Air

• **Dreamwalker Staff** (Staff, +20 Int, +30 Healing, +12 Spirit)
— Materials: 10 Elderwood Logs, 3 Rugged Leather, 12 Essence of Life

• **Obsidian Warstaff** (Staff, +30 Fire/Shadow spell damage)
— Materials: 10 Elderwood Logs, 4 Essence of Fire, 4 Essence of Undeath

• **Titanic Pike** (Epic Polearm, +40 Str, +25 Sta, +1% Crit)
— Materials: 12 Elderwood Logs, 6 Thorium Bars, 3 Essence of Earth

• **Dragonbone Longbow** (Epic Bow, +28 Agi, +15 Sta, +2% Crit)
— Materials: 12 Elderwood Logs, 3 Rugged Leather, 2 Black Dragon Scales

 **Specialty Hardwoods (rare mats)**
• **Runewood** – Elderwood + Dream Dust, created via Enchanting. Used for caster epics.
• **Fireshard Timber** – Dropped from Molten Core, used for fire bows/wands.
• **Glacial Heartwood** – Winterspring raid drop (see my other posts), used for frost resist/damage items.

Great suggestion, though it might need some more brainstorming.

I think the biggest selling point for such a profession is adding crafting options to items that are otherwise not yet obtainable that way.

From what you listed that's **bows** & **polearms**. I would add **Crossbows** and then **arrows** with an exclamation point to that list, since hunters have a very clear demand for an alternative to engineered bullets. Wands & Staffs are already craftable by Enchanting and Jewelcrafting, which is why I'd leave them out. It seems very central to classics design that you specialize and only certain groups will have access to specific things, giving them some form of identity.

 **Shields** could work - since I think there is only a handful craftable by engineers? It would break the aforementioned rule, but since those shields are afaik just endgame, having a profession that can build shields while leveling seems distinct enough.

Maces would fit well, but are already covered by blacksmithing. Maybe an exception could be made to craft additional 2-handed **totempoles** since that would fit perfectly with a woodworking theme.

Then there is **relics**. Totems, idols and librams. The first two very likely to be made of wood, the last indirectly, since paper is made of wood. I think there is a case for woodworking to make craftable relics, maybe even generic ones that can be equipped by any relic class. You spend a lot of time leveling with no relevant or just any relic as a druid/shaman/paladin if you don't go out of your way to specificly seek one out. Having a cheap craftable one that gives you just +4 on a stat at level 15 or something seems fine.

Also **fishing poles**! You could pad out the steps between +stat increases, adding additional ones or just add some color options.

Then the last slot I can think of is **off-hands**, as in nice figurines / staffs / foci for casters. These aren't craftable yet I think and I don't know if woodworking would be the first profession I would assume could craft these, but they are "open".

That's all equipables. Aside from that you could brainstorm about whether the profession adds anything else. Like if it makes librams out of paper, maybe it could also provide *scrolls*? Maybe it could have some other gimmick. The first thing that came to my mind was engineering style consumable totems, that you put on the ground for a little aura. But that's probably too close to shamans identity. There is probably a lot of other wood-themed things you could do.

Nevertheless, love the suggestion. I think it can definitely have its niche in turtle.

> Milling
>  You take wood from branches and turn it into paper. This does not give level-ups. The paper is used to make Vellum and Books.

> Enchanting
>  Make Vellum
>  Made from some combination of Paper, Leather, Cloth, and Shards/Motes. Vellum is used to receive enchantments, which can then be sold on the AH, and applied to weapons/armor by the user. This would be an absolute game changer for Classic WoW. It doesn’t fundamentally shift balance, it just makes the process of getting and giving enchants 1000% easier, and is a huge QoL improvement. Enchanting is already a horrible profession from a user experience point of view, and this would make it a lot more viable without tweaking any balance.
>
>  Can Disenchant Magical Constructs
>  Mana Wyrms, arcane elementals, and so forth, can now have their remains disenchanted after being defeated, if the enchanter is of the appropriate level and skill. This gives a skill up and appropriate mats based on level.
>
>  Cloth Upleveling
>  Let enchanters turn Silk into Mageweave, Mageweave into Runecloth, and Runecloth into Felweave, by expending enchanting mats as well as the lower level cloth. They literally add the mage to the weave, and the runes to the cloth.

I'd let Jewelcrafting have Mage and Priest staves, then let Woodworking have the Feral Druid and Hunter staves. Shields I think are an excellent idea and would be a cool way to have Wood+Metal Bar recipes and make things useful for tanks.

I love the idea of Totem/Idols/Librams being craftable.
- For Librams I might say it's gear made by Enchanters out of paper they turn into Vellum and maybe wood and enchanting mats?

- Totems and Idols would be ready to have better itemization though and including a Woodworking that covered those item slots would definitely make the "only wear what you crafted" challenge make more sense for anyone not a plate wearer.
Fishing Poles is a funny one that I hadn't considered. That's really interesting. Maybe you could craft rods that allow you to catch rare fish more often? Or ones that allow you to catch loot in addition to fish sometimes? Maybe ones that are regional and give you bonuses in certain zones?
