# Mages feel bad in PvP

## Post 173975 by Jelulish — 2026-04-04T05:07:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=173975#p173975 | page 1 | era: post-1.18.1 -->

I know this might come off as another “my class sucks” post, but I genuinely think mages feel terrible in PvP right now.

With the recent glow-up to hunters and buffs to most melee (whether through gear scaling or direct changes), mages are in a really rough spot. In almost every team fight, I get focused immediately—and with good reason. Hunters, shamans, and competent warriors can practically global me if I don’t Ice Block their initial engage.

The problem is, Ice Block is on a 5-minute cooldown, and Cold Snap is on a 10-minute cooldown. That means in most BG fights, Ice Block simply isn’t available. Without it, I’m extremely vulnerable to getting deleted or chunked down in seconds.

So the gameplay loop becomes: kite or die.
But if I kite, I’m not contributing meaningfully. And unlike other classes, mages don’t have strong DoT pressure to stay relevant while disengaging. That leaves us mostly ineffective either way—stand and die, or survive and do nothing.

I’m fine with losing fights. What feels bad is that against competent players, most engagements are decided instantly because I get focused and have very little counterplay outside of one long-cooldown button.

Shamans can global a huge portion of players
Hunters can do the same at range
Paladins can delete cloth wearers while effectively having multiple health bars
Warriors can take half our health on their charge if I don't block.

So what exactly is the mage supposed to do in that environment?

I’m not asking for mages to dominate, I just think we need something to better handle the level of burst and utility other classes currently bring, so we can actually compete in PvP.

Among pure DPS classes, I’d argue mages are currently among the weakest in PvP. I know people will point to the occasional mage topping killing blows—but that only happens when they’re allowed to free cast, which realistically doesn’t happen against competent opponents.
