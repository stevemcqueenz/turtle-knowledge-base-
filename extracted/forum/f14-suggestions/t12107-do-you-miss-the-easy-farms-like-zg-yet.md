Thinking about this "non iflating goldfarm" I come to following considerations.
When tryhard raiders spend gold for consumes, who will actually get it? I believe it is all gets accumulated at herbalists and miners. Exactly those people who camp nodes. Other guess is - auction house players.
How much gold one individual playing "gathering" game actually need in this game? I believe that if you are not obsessed with consumables, then you need around 2k gold for mount and spells. If you camp single node, then you don't even need 100% mount and don't really need all your spells.
If you are pure AH player, you don't even need high level character, so investment gets close to 100g/character.
So if you can invest a lot of time and play AH and economy instead of raiding, you will get enough gold to cover your basics even before you hit 60.
What will you do next? When you finally get your basic gold. There is great temptation to sell that gold to other players. I know it is bannable and traceable, but anyway.
You can hide your traces by selling cheap lots and buying out them from other account. Then you can resell this lot with high buyout price. There are enough stupid lots with low bid price and without buyout. They are getting cancelled and outbidded pretty fast. Especially when they are bought by "wrong" character.
How to transfer this gold for RMT buyer? Via overpriced AH lots. There are enough of them on AH. Like Thorium Bar for 20g per stack.

Imagine that there are organised group of players ready to invest 12*7 hours per week to get profit out of game. They will be able to manage high amounts of gold this way.

So in the end, I think that tryhard raiders are doing dirty work for farming gold while herb campers are getting spoils in real money for reselling gold from raiders to players willing to buy. Each potion you drink in the raid, is the money to pocket of RMT traders.

What can be done against this RMT strategy?
From players perspective - move to PvP realm. At least there you can kill 50% of node campers. If other faction is playing too, then node campers from your faction will be getting killed too. You can control supply of ore/herbs/whatever-you-need-to-farm in open world.

If you don't mind me butting in, I had a thought on this issue.

The core of this post and its predecessor seem to be the inability to produce the needed amount of gold to purchase consumables/materials to create them or to find a way to counter the ever-increasing cost of such. I would like to explain my idea starting from the statement made about creating a vendor that sold consumables for X amount being a solution to the issue and the counter point that it would make alchemists semi-obsolete. The post in question makes a fair point about the effect such a vendor would have on the farmers and the follow-on post highlights the unintended secondary effect of the alchemists rather well. I believe that a potential answer can found by going further down the supply tree.

I think that it would be possible for the developers to create an NPC vendor running some form of auction house monitoring system. This vendor could then offer a player character specific inventory based off of character level that allowed them to purchase production materials at a set price. This price would be based off of the standard accepted prices that are considered fair or even a calculation based off the total number of the item present in all character's possession across the server. The number of items available per day to the player characters would be based off of a calculation comparing the price of items on the auction house to the 'fair' set price or calculated price. the larger the difference the more of that item would be available.

As an example of the above calculation-based solutions consider the following.
There are 10,000 of a type of item in the possession of the player characters across the server. Due to its relative high numbers this item would end up with a price barely above the vendor price. If there were only 200 of that type of item on the server then perhaps it would end up with a cost many times that of the vendor price. This calculation would be usable to set an effective price for items while punishing hoarders that fail to post and sell their goods in hopes of driving prices to unfair levels.

With a price established based on the total numbers we now check to see how many of that item a player character may purchase. Let's assume that the difference between the vendor price and the AH is minimal perhaps a vendor price of 15s and an auction house price of 20s. This would result in very few if any of that item type being available to purchase as the auction house appears to be functioning in good faith between the players. Now, assuming that the price on the auction house is many times the price set by the vendor, such as a 2g item being 20g. This would result in an increase in the inventory of the vendor allowing player characters to purchase from an alternate source naturally decreasing the cost.

This system is not perfect and could only really be used for production materials due to their for-use nature. However, using the held inventory of player characters as a base for the price would work against farmers that are most guilty of driving prices up and increase the total amount materials available on server without crippling the use of gathering skills. Additionally, this method could be introduced in tiers as a black-market quest chain. As the player characters level up, they would get a quest to upgrade their black-market access by going out into the world and performing certain actions. This would provide good player immersion and prevent someone from creating a million low level alts to purchase infinite cheap materials.

If this seems like a good idea or you have something to add please leave some feedback or perhaps quote the idea to garner some attention.
