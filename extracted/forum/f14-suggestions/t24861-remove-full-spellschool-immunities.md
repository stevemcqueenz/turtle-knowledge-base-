# Remove full spellschool immunities

## Post 175315 by Wolfmanpaulx — 2026-04-15T11:48:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=175315#p175315 | page 1 | era: post-1.18.1 -->

Remove full spell school immunities and replace them with resistance caps: up to 350 for fire elemental-based raid bosses like Ragnaros, 300 for fire red/black dragon raid bosses, and 250 for other fire-based raid bosses.

Additionally, change mob and trash fire immunities into resistance caps: 200 for elementals, 150 for dragons, and 100 for other creatures.

PS: "Numbers can be adjusted, based on tests and effective spell-pene capabilities"
PS2: "Should be taken alongside with other full immunities for other spellschools for example nature immunities of Emerald WB dragons"

This would give spell penetration a new important role and make fire spell school gear and fire-based raid builds viable. At the moment, there are spellhance fire-based builds, Immolate-Conflagrate Warlocks, Fire Mages, etc.

This should be applied consistently across every spell school in the game, effectively removing hard spell immunities by replacing them with high resistance values—capped at 350 for raid bosses, and minimally at 250. For trash and mobs, caps should range from 200 down to 100 depending on creature type.

This change would significantly improve the PvE caster experience by preventing situations where players are heavily punished by immunity mechanics, leaving them unable to contribute damage and forced to abandon their chosen builds and respec into specs they did not originally prefer.

This would also mean that Curse of Elements and other spell resistance debuffs/tools would gain niche value. While currently players are able to stack at least ~180 spell penetration passively from gear (with even higher values possible in later gearing), under this system the hardest resistances would likely still result in around a 70% DPS reduction even at high optimization.

The argument isn’t that frost should deal extra damage because it’s a “counter.” The point is that there should always be some possibility to deal damage, regardless of the element.

Using real-world physics or chemistry as an argument against change doesn’t really hold up here. If we follow that logic consistently, it quickly falls apart. What matters isn’t just the element or material itself—it’s the force, energy, and interaction behind it.

By that reasoning, even something made of fire could still be affected by sufficiently strong or disruptive forces. So saying “fire vs fire = no effect” is an oversimplification, not true realism.

The real issue is not realism—it’s design philosophy.

The goal should be to make the game and raids accessible to all specs. That doesn’t mean everything must be equally strong, but it should always be playable. High resistances can act as a natural limiter, but they should be something players can work against—through gearing, spell penetration, and optimization—rather than a hard “you cannot participate” wall.

Spell penetration could fill that role very well, similar to how armor penetration scales for physical damage. Instead of removing mechanics, this approach would deepen them—turning resistance into something you interact with, rather than something that simply shuts you down.
