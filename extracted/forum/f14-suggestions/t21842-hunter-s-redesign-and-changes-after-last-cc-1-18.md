# Hunter's redesign and changes after last CC 1.18

## Post 151287 by Skurridizo (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2025-09-11T14:57:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=151287#p151287 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hello, I’m Skurridizo. I’ve been playing Turtle WoW for more than a year and a half, and I play Hunter exclusively. At the moment I have 4 level 60 hunters across different servers, and I’ve completed all the content in the game, including Karazhan 40.

With my most known character, Skurridizo, I’m raiding in a fresh progression guild, without anyone overgeared coming in to help us. Right now we’re 12/15 in Naxxramas.

I have a very broad vision of my class. I also run a YouTube channel in Spanish where I mainly make guides about Hunter, including pets. I’ve probably dedicated more time than anyone else across EU and NA servers to researching and testing different Beast Mastery builds and pet mechanics. In fact, many players who come with the old WoW Classic mindset are surprised—or even tell me I’m doing it wrong—because in Turtle WoW things work radically differently compared to Classic.

With this introduction, I hope this makes my background and experience clear. On top of that, I also have a past as a professional League of Legends analyst, having been part of teams that competed in the European Masters. I believe this helps explain my way of understanding games and the balance that needs to be developed within them.

The purpose of this post is to lay out the strengths and weaknesses of the Hunter class in Turtle WoW. Since it hasn’t been in the best spot for a while—especially since patch 1.18—I set out to draft a realistic proposal of changes that could be introduced to this class. I’ve asked the most veteran players, people topping PvE logs, rank 14 PvP players, both hunters and non-hunters, for their opinions. What I bring here is a summary of what the community is asking for, and a proposal of changes.

I hope you’ll take these considerations into account for future patches. Let’s get started with the changes:

PVP Talents are kind of different and they usually combine CC abilities and defensive skills from Survival, so I won’t suggest any combination because everything has to fit the player’s playstyle.

Now I’ll go over the main issues of each tree and the ways I’ve found to address them—sometimes through talents, other times through minor changes to pet abilities.

 **Proposed pet ability changes:**
 - **Claw** hits 3 targets (like the Druid’s Swipe).
 - **Claw** attack power ratio increased from 1 damage per 42 Attack Power to 1 damage per 35 Attack Power.
 - Gorilla’s **Thunderstomp** cooldown reduced from 40 seconds to 10 seconds.
 - Gorilla’s **Thunderstomp** Spell Power scaling increased from 0.21 to 0.3.
 - Bear’s **Roar of Fortitude** cooldown reduced from 120 seconds to 90 seconds.
 - **Screech** cooldown reduced from 5 seconds to 3 seconds.

 **Changes explained in Beast Mastery**

 *Extra ability:*
 **Duality**
 **New passive** effect from Survival taught by the trainer at level 60:
The player adds half of the melee attack power to the ranged attack power

Note: It could be done adding (Melee Attack Power - Base Attack Power)/2 to the Ranged Attack Power calculation.

---
 **Weaknesses and shortcomings**

General

 - Bad synergy with **consumes** as a ranged. Winterfall Firewater, Sharpening stones, Potion of Quickness and many others. There are a lot of consumes that are useless or inefficient like the Potion of Quickness compared to other Attack Power users. Spell Power consumes are strong but the Spell Power abilities the hunter has are very poor and they have very high mana costs for a very low damage.

 - No strong **pet** for clearing raid trash. Gorilla deals very low damage for its 40s cooldown.

 - Low talent **variety**, since you’re forced to combine with Marksmanship and pick the same talents in BM. Many talents are useless.

Beast Mastery

 - Very **limited pet** variety for raids: Scorpion for long fights, Wind Serpent for PvP, and Cobra for everything else. Anything else means losing damage.

 - No strong pet for clearing raid **trash**. Gorilla deals very low damage for its 40s cooldown.

 - Low **talent variety**, since you’re forced to combine with Marksmanship and pick the same talents in BM. Many talents are useless.

 - The two new **abilities are unreliable**. The best BM build still skips the final talent and plays 29/22/0.

 - Very steep **learning curve**. The spec is considered extremely hard to play (which is true). Pets die easily, not all healers heal pets, and there’s no reliable way to heal them yourself. This includes melee mechanics with your pet.

 - **Claw** is an extremely **weak ability** that drains pet energy. On a training dummy, with full Naxx gear and BM talents, a Scorpid hits for ~42 damage per Claw (double on crit, ~90 with Kill Command). Raptors deal slightly more, but even a crit with Kill Command barely hits 120. After raid buffs and armor reduction, the damage doesn’t go much higher—maybe x2 at best, which is still poor. Without BM talents, damage is even worse, and with 300 weapon skill and 0% hit, pets miss very often.

 - **Thunderstomp** deals very **low damage** (around 180 AoE damage in the same scenario). The cooldown feels excessive; it could even be 0 CD and still balanced given the high energy cost. Screech has the same issue.

 - BM is the highest single-target DPS hunter spec in Turtle, but lacks burst. Many see it as a “support” tree, but in reality it’s high damage and hard to master, which should be its main **identity**.

Marksmanship

 - Lacks a clear **identity**. It’s considered a support spec because of Trueshot Aura, but has poor synergy with melee groups (no benefit from Windfury Totem or melee AP buffs like Blessing of Might, Strength Totem, or Warrior shouts). I’ve seen creative solutions to this, but I chose not to touch it, as I think it’s up to the devs. One idea was to change Piercing Shots so crits benefit from melee AP and allow Windfury to affect ranged attacks. You have a Trueshot Aura that buffs melee groups, but a Battle shout from the warriors and blessing of Might doesn’t affect the ranged hunter.

 - No burst, no consistent AoE, and overall the **weakest hunter tree**. It’s the easiest to start with, though.

 - Too many **useless abilities**—either outdated or not scaling well.

 - **No interesting pet** to add utility to the tree. Wolves don’t work well with ranged attacks, bears and tallstriders are weak, so you just end up using a DPS pet like BM.

 - **No synergy** with BM or SV, meaning almost all points go into MM, with no alternative builds for utility.

 - **Spell Power** Hunter (from Vanilla/TBC) isn’t viable here. Even though it’s niche and shouldn’t dominate, having it as a possible playstyle adds variety.

Survival

 - **Lacks synergy** with the other two trees, so you spend almost all points here, with no alternative builds.

 - **Traps** are the main source of damage, but in fights like C’thun or Thaddius where you’re forced to stay max melee range, they become useless.

 - **No mobility**.

 - No **tier sets** with synergy for this tree. It’s also much harder to reach 16% off-hand hit compared to warriors and rogues.

---
 **Strengths**
Despite the fact that everyone thinks hunters are in a very bad state, it’s true that in its **Survival** version the class is still one of the **strongest for AoE** damage.

 **Beast Mastery**, in progression guilds where fights last a **long time**, is a spec that **rewards** you very well if you play your class flawlessly. However, it has a very high skill ceiling and is probably the hardest spec to fully master alongside Feral Cat Druid.

 **Marksmanship** is the **favorite** of many players who want something simple and with utility. It appeals to a lot of people, especially newcomers.

---
 **Other suggestions**

Right now it takes a lot of time to **train a pet** just to test it and then discard it. With the patch changes, I’ve spent around **34 hours** of playtime just training and testing all the pets I needed.

The fact that you can’t switch pets while in combat makes it even harder. Before, you could swap pets while farming in places like Zul’Gurub or Dire Maul, and they would still gain all the experience. I understand that this mechanic with the Bear could break balance, but could it be changed so that you’re allowed to swap pets from the portable stable as long as they’re not in combat? Or at least a consistent way to train them easily.

Another alternative is to **increase the number of stable slots**. Personally, I paid for the 5th slot back in the day, and it still feels like too few, especially considering all the changes that have been made. I wouldn’t mind if each extra slot cost 1,000 gold, as long as we could have up to 10 slots.

For any other class, testing different talents or damage just costs 5g and a quick gear adjustment before hitting the Training Dummy. But if I want to test a pet, it can easily take me several hours—for example, hyenas took me over 10 hours in total—only to end up discarding them all when I realized they weren’t efficient.

Many older **pets are obsolete**, and I’ve suggested that some could become “walking buffs,” inspired by the Strider Presence ability, but granting different group benefits.
The pets that should receive bigger changes are:

Crab, Hyena, Fox, Turtle, Boar, and Gorilla (if not changed as suggested).

---
 **How I approached the problems and talents explanation**

Beast Mastery:

 - Pet with **low survivability**: Buffed and combined Improved Mend Pet + Improved Eyes of the Beast + Improved Revive Pet. Also, there is a new talent providing 2% Vampirism to the pet.

 - You have to play Marksman as a second tree and rely on the **Steady Shot** rotation: Now it’s possible to play BM melee due to some changes in the early Survival talent tree.

 - Not much **versatility**, as there are only 3 useful options for pets: scorpids, wind serpents, or cobras. This isn’t included in the talent tree, but I will detail them later.

Marksmanship:

 - Low AoE damage: Spell power buffs for Volley and an extra effect for Scorpid Sting that allows AoE damage.

 - **Arcane Shot** and **Aimed Shot** are **useless**: I added a nice combination effect so you can use one of them each time.

 - Not many possibilities to **combine your talent** tree: going deep MM, splashing into BM, or even SV if you want to focus on PvP.

 - Not much **burst** and lacking a real sniper feeling: The new ability Sniper Focus makes your timing and decision-making crucial to when you want to burst.

 - Not top **DPS** nor a **support**ive spec: Added a new support ability to make it more useful, along with higher-damage talents.

Survival:

 - **No mobility**: Added an extra 5% speed to Lightning Reflexes.

 - **Bad** tree to splash talents into as a **secondary tree**: Now it’s possible to get +100% extra melee attack power from agility earlier, and another talent reduces minimum range to 0 yards. Feel free to play full melee BM or a hybrid melee-ranged hunter.

 - **Traps** are very **clunky** in certain situations. In many boss fights, you have to hit at max melee range and lose a large percentage of your total damage: Added a radius expansion for traps if they are not triggered instantly, to increase damage, and also a new mechanic if you want to min/max your traps.

General:

The idea was to provide a bit **more versatility** by reducing the number of points needed to max out some talents, allowing you to choose between different talents that offer utility based on your playstyle.

I think this final version won't make Hunters as good as Mages, Warriors, or Rogues, but it could provide a significant **improvement**, especially to the **quality of life** of certain talents or skills that are experiencing bugs.

---
 **Conclusion**

The way I’ve tried to address these issues is with small tweaks to certain talents and adding new ones. I’ve reused existing effects from other parts of the game where possible, or simplified things that don’t always work properly, to keep implementation straightforward.

Overall, these changes would clearly improve hunters, especially in ranged builds, but likely not enough to put them on par with Mages or Warriors in terms of DPS — which I believe is fine. I think some changes could get increased a bit more to find the balance, but overall, I prefer to give a little boost rather than a huge one and see all the other people complaining.
