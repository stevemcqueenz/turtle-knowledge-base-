I think that ogres in the Horde make sense for much the same reasons as High Elves in the Alliance. They're both classic faction races that were left to the wayside, despite still having an in-game presence.

As far as giving the Alliance a counterpart to keep the numbers balanced...I actually think it should be Goblins. In-game and in lore, Goblins care more about coin than creed. They have neutral cities. They have merchants in both Alliance and Horde territory. They also have beef with members of the Horde, as seen in the Tauren zones. Having them loyally serve the Horde honestly just clashes with their whole identity. Even if DLU has a deal with the Horde, there's no reason why a rival faction of Goblins wouldn't cut a deal with the Alliance to move into an untapped market. If any faction is to be neutral/split, it would be them.

What you mean is clear that goblins have separate factions. But, this game is a RPG of fantasy genre, where races and classes are intentionally designed to fit in the styles and vibes of factions. Warcraft I is based on such value, making orcs ugly and savage and human with a civilised and Medieval European styled flavour. Goblins in Warcraft is a non-human race with minimal human element. Trolls, orcs, taurens and goblins are on fantasy basis suitable to form a united horde because they don't look like humankind.

Meanwhile, on the side of Alliance, the three races that should ally are human, dwarves, gnomes and high elves, who have historical and early endorsement for that in pre-WCIII lore. (Night elves in Kalimdor are more like human in the game, rather than trolls. The Forsaken and night elves should be separate factions.) Goblins, on the other hand, are depicted as a being of greed. This flavour outweighs love, loyalty and honourthose positive aspects of goblin races, just like the savage and exotic elements dominate the design of all Horde races. To maintain the vibe that first Warcraft game presents is what should be done in Turtle universe in my opinion.
