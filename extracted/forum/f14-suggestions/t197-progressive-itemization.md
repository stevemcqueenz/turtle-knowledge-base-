# Progressive Itemization

## Post 1034 by Potatoknight — 2018-10-31T13:46:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=1034#p1034 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

[embed: https://www.youtube.com/embed/gdy_uSbFZEY]

I mentioned this in the Discord a few times. I understand this server at the moment has all gear at 1.12.1 levels and that this was a conscious decision but I am concerned if it was a decision made with all the facts known.

A lack of progressive itemization encourages people to race to 60 (as people who are already 60 are likely to increase in power and boom far faster than they otherwise would, thus making everyone else weaker by comparison, which is not a nice feeling), a lack of progressive itemization effectively strips out catch-up mechanics built into the game designed to help new 60s catch up to their friends (If everyone has utility of the same catch-up mechanics, no one does).

I would ask the development team to kindly watch the video I've linked as it goes into greater detail than I ever possibly could and the decision one way or the other will have significant long term effects on the lifespan of the server.

It is not too late to implement this as there are very few level 60s - the level where this is most significant.

Please consider this and make an official definitive decision based on these facts. Thank you. :)

## Post 1057 by Maka — 2018-10-31T16:45:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=1057#p1057 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I understand your concern Potatoknight, but not sure I can fully agree when it comes to Turtle WoW.

In your post you mention how "progressive itemization strips out catch-up mechanics" but would argue quite the opposite is true.
I'd say that similar to Zul'Gurub and Dire Maul progressive itemization is a catch-up mechanic that could trivialize previous content, mainly some regular (end game) dungeons. But on a server that is currently starting to balance out in population and any kind of 40 man raids are still quite far away, I feel this might only be a good thing. Without the prospect of Zul'Gurub I would be concerned that some people that are hoping to eventually enjoy raid content might get discouraged in their efforts.

Apart from that I feel Turtle WoW has always been more about the journey then the destination, so if you are looking to 'race' your character to lvl 60 why not play on a server that maintains regular (or even increased) XP rates.

Personally I like to experience the content for the content and not because of any possible loot or upgrades. But if these catch-up mechanics allow me and my friends to continue our journey where otherwise it would be put on hold Im really not opposed to them.
Possibly there are some offenders that may have to be looked at but in general I feel it would only benefit the server.

Lastly we are applying a video voicing concerns about Classic itemization and progression to Turtle WoW, which is inherently unfair in my opinion since it would be a safe assumption that our little server will never be able to put the 'M' in Mmo like Classic will. Turtle WoW won't be suited for everyone (even more so then Classic) and this is exactly the reason I'm personally, and hopefully many others, enjoying the Turtle WoW experience and community so much.

## Post 1063 by Josefina — 2018-10-31T17:07:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=1063#p1063 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I tend to agree with the above post. I agree that progressive itemization is important for larger servers where focus is on end-game and raiding. But Turtle WoW isn’t that. I’d rather it not become a min/max place, where if you can’t make a raid roster (imagine we get to end game and there are 70 players ready to raid... 30 simply cannot!) you lose out on improving your character. Personally, I’ll be going into MC, BWL and all regardless of whether or not I have better gear than what is available.

Also, I think it could help balance the fact that people want to play here without being pressured into min/maxing. If I want to play a Beast Mastery Dwarf Hunter that uses guns and a bear because that is who my character is, then having access to better items will level the playing field when going into the first Molten Core raid. Same with the Tauren enhancement Shaman, the Feral or Moonkin Druid and the Night Elf Shadow Priest.

EDIT: That said, I would be fine with things being rolled out slowly as well. I just think that making more gear obtainable through smaller raids and dungeons will lead to more players getting a chance to get upgrades.
