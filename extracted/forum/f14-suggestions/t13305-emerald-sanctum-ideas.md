# Emerald Sanctum ideas

## Post 89264 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2024-03-03T09:02:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=89264#p89264 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!

I have seen that most people dislike, maybe outright skip this raid. Partially due to the high attune cost, partially because of the raid experience. The raid itself is not that hard (however, hard mode is a very decent mana management and positioning challenge) but the raid somehow feels bland, mostly at the trash clearing phase. Trying to give my take on why this is the case and what I'd change here.

The map was designed for much more trash. Half of them were removed later for better player experience. It does, however create a very big distance between mob packs and in my opinion suffers from the same thing Black Morass (space desert part) suffers: while the individual power-level of the mob packs is high, you can be sure you won't pull anything else (except for the roaming boss). It means that you learn how to react to the packs (tremor, de-poison, beatdown, prioritizing the right dragons) and then rinse and repeat. After learning the basics of these packs, the party does the same thing over and over again with no real chance of pulling anything else than the roaming boss.

How I'd change this:

 1. People usually prioritize whelps as the last targets. Would give whelps an interruptible ability which, if successfully casted, summons a bigger dragon. This would require the party members able to interrupt to keep an eye on the least priority targets at all times and interrupt if needed. Would allow only one whelp to cast this at a given time to avoid snowball.

 2. The distance between the mob packs is quite big. Making the distance smaller usually works by adding packs or by decreasing map size. Another option though is to make each mob packs patrol a bit. Not much, you could find all around the usual places but enabling them to be pulled if a fight with another group is positioned very badly. It would require the raid to keep an eye on the surroundings and move if a patroling pack comes close (just like in the case of the patrolling boss).

 3. I would add a magic-based, castable fear-like effect for some mobs. Normal fear wouldn't really be effective since every party having a shaman spams tremor anyway against the sleeps and having half of the party run around in fear while another half of them being totally chill is not a good design in my opinion. Magic-based fear-like effect forces some classes like priests to pay extra attention and de-magic if needed. Specially since with the extra patrolling, running around in panic can easily pull a new pack of mobs.

 4. I'd add a poison cloud (like Hakkar's poison cloud) to certain parts of the map which reapply instantly if cleansed and would deal a decent amount of damage). It would require further attention at positioning and if the poison clouds are placed with those intentions, you can define most mob patrols outside of the clouds, meaning a smaller actual map size even if the map size did not change. Would make bumping into patrols more frequent and feel like the map is more filled with enemies than it actually is. Also, dying from fearing into a cloud at low HP would become a thing.

+1 I like one more idea but this one might be overdoing it. During the last boss fight there is a very interesting mechanic: the game punishing you if you cleanse specific debuffs by applying a strong, non-dispellable debuff on the target. Would add pairs of disease and curse to some mobs which can be casted. One has to be cleansed to prevent a strong debuff, the other one has to be ignored to avoid a very strong, non-dispellable debuff. It would make the work of druids, mages, priests, paladins and shamans much more interesting. No more open decursive and click on everything type of gameplay.
