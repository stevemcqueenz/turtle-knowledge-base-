# Scarlet Shore — Replace the Alah’Thalas → Tirisfal AFK swim with a real coastal zone

## Post 164402 by Wilsonsds (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2026-01-04T14:56:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=164402#p164402 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

**TL;DR**
A lot of players already travel **Auberdine → boat → Alah’Thalas** and then cross toward **Tirisfal Glades** as a practical way to approach the Scarlet region. The problem is that the final leg often becomes a long, straight **autorun/AFK swim** where nothing happens — it’s not meaningful travel, it’s downtime. My suggestion is to add a thin, walkable coastal strip called **Scarlet Shore**, turning that dead corridor into a compact zone with real gameplay: coastal threats (strong **Murloc/Naga** presence), a believable **four-force tension** (High Elves, Undead, Scarlets, sea creatures), and a small **Scarlet** foothold/port that uses cannon flavor to explain how they hold the shoreline. This is **content, not convenience**, and the iconic Southshore pilgrimage stays intact for anyone who wants the classic journey.

![Image](https://i.postimg.cc/RVqpRqQy/Captura-de-tela-2026-01-04-124154.png)

 **Why this matters in Turtle WoW (no summoning stones, so travel must be worth playing)**
Turtle WoW has made its position pretty clear: the world should be traveled, not bypassed. Summoning stones are not coming back, and the philosophy is that getting somewhere should feel like part of the game, not a UI button. That’s totally fair — but it comes with a responsibility: if players are expected to move through the world, then the travel itself needs to be **interesting, readable, and interactive**, not a reward for alt-tabbing. Right now, the Alah’Thalas → Tirisfal crossing is one of those rare spots where travel turns into pure dead time. It’s not “dangerous exploration,” it’s not “a memorable route,” it’s just a long swim that encourages autorun, AFK, or disengagement. If Turtle’s identity is “walk the world,” then stretches like this are exactly where adding content creates the biggest payoff, because you’re improving the experience without changing the philosophy.

 **What’s happening right now (the corridor already exists in player behavior)**
Because **Alah’Thalas** exists and is conveniently reached via **Auberdine → boat**, a practical corridor toward **Tirisfal** naturally formed. This is not about inventing a new meta route; it’s about acknowledging the one players already use. The geography made it inevitable: if there’s a reachable coastline that points toward Tirisfal, people will cross it, especially when their goal is the broader Scarlet region. So the question isn’t “should there be an alternative path?” because there already is an alternative path in practice. The real question is whether that path remains an empty, repetitive AFK segment, or whether it becomes a small piece of world content that feels intentional, immersive, and consistent with Turtle’s approach to the game.

 **The current problem (the AFK swim is dead gameplay, not world travel)**
The last stretch often boils down to swimming in a straight line where there’s no meaningful decision-making. No landmarks, no pressure, no route choices, no risk management — just time. Vanilla travel is slow, but it’s slow in a way that creates stories: you remember patrols, roads, crossroads, escort risks, hostile pockets, and the feeling of entering a new region. That’s what makes “walking the world” feel like gameplay instead of a chore. This corridor currently lacks that texture, even though it’s now part of Turtle’s real player flow. When a route is both common and boring, it becomes a perfect candidate for a small zone that doesn’t need to be massive to be valuable. Even modest content would turn that travel time into something you actually play through and remember.

 **The proposal: Scarlet Shore (content, not convenience)**
Add a thin, walkable coastal strip between the Alah’Thalas side and the Tirisfal coast, and name it **Scarlet Shore**. The core idea is straightforward: replace the dead-water corridor with real terrain, encounters, and atmosphere — so crossing is interactive rather than AFK. This is not intended to delete or replace the iconic Southshore pilgrimage route toward Scarlet Monastery. The classic route stays untouched for anyone who wants the old-school experience on purpose. Scarlet Shore is about improving the modern corridor that already emerged from Turtle’s geography, and doing it in the most Vanilla-friendly way possible: a real place you move through, with danger and decisions, instead of an empty shortcut.

 **Zone identity: a four-force pressure frontier (not a “campaign,” just a believable contested coast)**
Scarlet Shore becomes truly unique when it isn’t framed as “High Elves vs Scarlets” only. This coastline makes far more sense as a pressured frontier where four forces collide for different reasons, creating constant instability that naturally generates content. **High Elves** have a capital in Alah’Thalas, but that doesn’t mean their borders are peaceful — a hostile coastline under sea-creature pressure would make their frontier feel permanently defensive. **Undead of Tirisfal** are already in direct conflict with the **Scarlet Crusade**, so any Scarlet foothold near their coastline becomes a natural flashpoint. **Murlocs and Naga** dominate the shallows as the persistent “environmental enemy,” which is important because it prevents the zone from feeling like a designed highway — it feels like a dangerous coastline that keeps biting everyone equally. And then the **Scarlets** themselves become more believable and more thematic when they’re not dominant, but stretched thin: defending a precarious position while being harassed by the sea and threatened by nearby enemies. This isn’t “fanfic drama”; it’s simply giving in-game representation to pressures that already fit the geography and the factions involved.

 **Signature feature: a small Scarlet port + cannon defense flavor (grounded, thematic, and easy to understand)**
A strong visual and mechanical anchor for Scarlet Shore would be a **small Scarlet port/foothold** on the coast. Not a huge fortress and not a new capital — just a fortified point that clearly communicates “the Crusade is trying to hold this shoreline and it’s not going well.” This single feature explains a lot: why there are patrols, why there are supply problems, why there are skirmishes, why there’s sabotage, and why the coastline feels like a living conflict zone. It also creates space for a unique, believable detail: the Crusade using **cannons** as part of coastal defense. Armory already has cannons and cannonballs, and Scarlet cannon flavor exists elsewhere too, so this isn’t a new concept pulled from nowhere. The point is not to create a lethal artillery corridor or a scripted “tower defense” gimmick — it’s to represent, in a controlled and flavorful way, how a small Scarlet foothold could defend itself against constant Naga/Murloc pressure from the sea. That kind of grounded worldbuilding makes the zone feel real and gives it a signature identity that players will remember.

 **Quest/content direction: small-scope, Vanilla-style, readable factions**
This doesn’t need to be a massive storyline campaign to work. Vanilla zones are built on simple quests that build atmosphere: kill/collect, patrol/ambush, supply recovery, scouting, and short chains that reveal the reality of the region. Scarlet Shore could do the same, using the four-force pressure to keep it varied without being complicated. Alliance-facing content can lean into coastal defense, rescue, and reconnaissance tied to the Alah’Thalas frontier. Undead/Horde-facing content can lean into disrupting Scarlet presence, harassing patrol routes, and exploiting chaos. Neutral or shared content can focus on the sea threat itself: Naga sabotage, Murloc raids, wreck recovery, shoreline clearing. The lore doesn’t have to be delivered as a speech — it can be fragmented through quest text, notes, camp placement, patrol behavior, and small environmental touches. That’s exactly how Vanilla makes a region feel alive without feeling “modern.”

 **Level range and route safety (interactive travel, not a hard gate)**
A natural target range would be around **level 14–20**. The coastline should have a passable edge route so travelers aren’t hard-blocked, while the center can be more dangerous and more rewarding so it feels like a real contested zone. If optional spice is desired, small pockets can host stronger threats — a tiny island or two, or a limited underwater area — and that would still fit Turtle since Alah’Thalas already explores underwater segments in its quest chains. But those are optional. The core win is simpler: the crossing becomes interactive, with meaningful threats and decisions, instead of a mandatory choke point or an empty AFK swim.

 **Preemptive answers (the predictable forum objections)**
 **“This is QoL disguised as lore.”** The route already exists in practice because Turtle’s geography exists, and Alah’Thalas isn’t moving. The proposal is not convenience; it’s **content** where players already travel, so the game stops rewarding AFK swimming.
 **“You’re killing the iconic pilgrimage.”** Nothing is removed. The Southshore route remains fully intact for anyone who wants the classic journey. Scarlet Shore just makes the modern corridor less empty.
 **“Four forces is messy / retail vibes.”** This is intentionally small-scope and Vanilla-style: simple quests, patrols, camps, and environmental storytelling. Murlocs already exist on both coasts, Scarlets have always been in the region, and Undead conflict is foundational — this is representation, not a new “campaign.”

 **Closing**
If summoning stones are not coming back, then travel needs to be something players actually play through, not alt-tab through. Right now, the Alah’Thalas → Tirisfal crossing is a common corridor that feels like downtime. **Scarlet Shore** would convert that dead segment into a compact coastal zone with a clear identity: strong **Naga/Murloc** pressure, a believable **four-force frontier tension**, and a **small Scarlet port** whose cannon defense flavor explains how they can even hold the shoreline. It fits Turtle’s worldbuilding, matches Vanilla tone, improves the experience of world travel, and keeps the iconic Southshore pilgrimage intact for anyone who still wants the original journey.

And there is a lot of unused space to be remade in something cool

Hello. Yes — I used AI to polish the English, because I’m not fluent and I want the community to understand the idea clearly. The idea itself is mine. If we’re discussing suggestions, I’d rather keep the focus on the proposal, not on the tool used to write it.

And also, Yes — and I think you already know the answer from the lore, which is exactly why you asked it that way.

Scarlet Crusade naval movement is not a stretch here. The Crusade has operated by sea in multiple points of the lore (including the whole Northrend expedition reference you brought up), and the connection between key Crusade holdings being maintained through maritime routes is one of the few “logistics” elements that is actually explicit in Scarlet material. So a small naval foothold / port on that coast isn’t a lore-breaking convenience — it’s the most natural way to represent how they would even attempt to project power along the shoreline.

That said, the existence of a naval link does NOT automatically imply a clean, safe corridor where they can simply ship elite troops and “solve” Tirisfal. In fact, the whole point of my Scarlet Shore concept is that this coastline is a pressure cooker: constant Naga/Murloc raids, contested waters, and the Forsaken presence nearby. So the port works best as a desperate, constantly-bleeding outpost — something they keep trying to hold because it matters strategically, but it never becomes a stable highway.

On your “something more interesting than murlocs and naga” point: I agree the “dead sailors / Flying Dutchman” flavor never gets old. I’d happily layer that on top of the coastal conflict. In fact, it fits perfectly because it gives the sea itself an identity beyond “generic coastline mobs,” and it makes shipwrecks and underwater pockets meaningful.

If we want to take it one step further, I’d even propose a dungeon concept: a large stranded or haunted Scarlet ship (or a fortified naval installation built around wrecks), aimed at roughly level 47–53. It could be framed as a Crusade vessel lost during the Northrend-era push, now claimed by undead sailors and sea creatures, with Scarlets trying to recover supplies / relics while everything around them turns into a nightmare. That would make the naval angle feel “real” in gameplay, not just in dialogue.

So yes: a naval base there is lore-consistent, but it should be portrayed as contested and costly — not as a free redeployment corridor. And the undead-sailor theme is a great addition, potentially even a full dungeon hook if the team ever wanted to expand the concept beyond a small coastal quest zone.
