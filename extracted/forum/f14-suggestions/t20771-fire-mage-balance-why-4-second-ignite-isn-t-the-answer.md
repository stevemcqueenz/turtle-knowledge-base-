# Fire Mage Balance: Why 4-Second Ignite Isn’t the Answer

## Post 144075 by Bolthardrok — 2025-08-09T23:07:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=144075#p144075 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Fire is fine right now, but the change to a 4-second Ignite will severely harm it. This isn’t hyperbole... Fire already requires careful execution to compete, and cutting the Ignite window to 4 seconds means you would realistically need four or more Fire Mages just to reliably maintain a roll. That’s simply not feasible when Arcane and Frost are both strong, enjoyable, and have dedicated players who aren’t looking to swap.

The goal should be to make Fire viable in both solo and teamplay. There are far better approaches than this quick 4-second Ignite “fix,” which feels like a step backward to an outdated model that doesn’t work in today’s environment, where Arcane and Frost are both competitive. Fire’s past dominance existed only because it was the only viable spec at the time... that is not the case now.

Here is what could actually work:

 1. Make Ignite rolls personal. If it is not possible on the current engine, implement a temporary rework until the new engine allows it.

If, indeed, it is the case that it cannot be changed, then one or more of the following changes should be implemented instead:

 2. Reduce Ignite’s damage and make it supplementary.

Right now, Ignite is the majority of Fire’s damage, which makes having more than one Fire Mage feel low-value. Shift the balance so more damage comes from core spells, and Ignite becomes a bonus rather than the entire identity.

 3. Buff core Fire spells.

Increase Fireball and other spell damage directly, possibly with a crit damage percentage bonus, to compensate for the Ignite damage reduction and make it feel like Mages are contributing meaningful value outside of pushing the Ignite roll.

 4. Fix the ramp-up problem.

Losing Ignite as a solo Fire Mage is brutal because it takes forever to rebuild. Allow Scorch to stack to three and Ignite to stack to three. Keep Scorch at 15% (5% per stack) and keep Ignite’s reduced damage values, but make it easier to resmolder as a solo player and as a team. This would massively improve ramp-up time and prevent the spec from feeling dead in the water after a drop.

 5. Make Hot Streak actually appealing.

Allow Scorch to proc Hot Streak when you are the only Fire Mage in the raid so you are not locked into an endless Scorch rotation just to push Ignite. This would make solo Ignite pushing feel better than spamming Scorch and Fireblast.

 6. Make Combustion passive.

Turn Combustion into a passive effect with reduced potency instead of an active cooldown. This change would help solo Fire players maintain competitive DPS while still contributing to Ignite rolls without relying on teammates. Additionally, it would allow for more frequent procs of Hot Streak, resulting in a more varied and engaging rotation rather than repetitive spamming of one or two spells. Making Combustion passive would also ease the difficulty of resmoldering a new Ignite after it drops, since the ability would no longer be locked behind a cooldown.

A massive reduction in Ignite dependency paired with buffs to Fire’s active spell damage, faster ramp-up, and better solo viability would let Fire thrive in both solo and group contexts without pigeonholing people into one role. It would erase the “low value without Ignite” stigma, give players more agency in their playstyle, and make the spec fun to play again.

Right now, the proposed 4-second Ignite change risks doing the opposite. Instead of supporting Fire’s potential for solo and teamplay, it would make the spec dependent on stacking several Fire Mages in a raid to function at all. That limits diversity, reduces flexibility, and moves the spec further away from being a healthy, fun option in the current meta.

Modern raids going forward, starting with Karazhan 40 and beyond, will be much more mechanic-heavy, with frequent target switching to handle adds and constant engagement with fight mechanics. This environment naturally causes more Ignite drops, which makes Fire feel punishing to play.

The adjustments I’ve suggested would better align with this style of encounter design by shifting more of Fire’s damage into active spells rather than an Ignite roll. This way, target switching and dealing with mechanics wouldn’t feel nearly as punishing, and Fire Mages could still deliver strong performance without being overly dependent on maintaining a single Ignite.

I understand the nostalgia for old Fire and the appeal of rewarding team coordination, but the “old Ignite” model simply doesn’t fit the current game environment. Back then, Fire was the only truly viable raid spec for Mages, so stacking the roster with Fire players was easy. In today’s meta, Arcane and Frost are both competitive, fun, and already have established players so you can’t just “require” everyone to swap to Fire. At least with the current 6-second Ignite, I can solo Fire or duo Fire and still perform. The proposed 4-second change would remove even that flexibility.

Modern raid design (starting with Karazhan 40 and beyond) leans heavily into target switching, add waves, and frequent mechanic engagement... all of which disrupt Ignite rolling. The second a roll drops because you have to DPS down adds, dispel curses, or handle fight mechanics, you’re back to square one. Even if you could get multiple Fire Mages to cooperate perfectly, that environment alone would make old Ignite rolls far less consistent. And for smaller guilds with 4 or fewer Mages, sustaining an Ignite roll under those conditions would be nearly impossible.

It doesn’t have to stay the way it is now, and it certainly doesn’t need to revert to an even worse state. The changes I’ve suggested offer a way to keep Fire fun, viable, and rewarding for both solo and teamplay without pigeonholing people into a single role or comp. Turtle WoW has shown it’s not afraid to make sweeping changes, so we should take that opportunity to improve Fire instead of falling back on a lazy reversion that doesn’t work in the current meta or with the raid content coming out.

Calling old Ignite “an actual playstyle” ignores the reality that it doesn’t fit the modern state of the game without enough Fire Mages... which most groups simply won’t have or the increasingly mechanic-heavy raids being released. We can preserve Fire’s identity without shackling it to a system that breaks the moment conditions aren’t perfect.

I get the nostalgia for the high-risk, high-reward Ignite of old... it was undeniably flashy when it worked. But the reality is that it only worked under a very narrow set of circumstances: a roster stacked with Fire Mages, extremely coordinated play, and fights where you could sit still and tunnel a single target for the full duration of the roll. That’s not the environment we’re in anymore, and it’s definitely not where raid design is heading.

The “higher throughput” argument ignores how modern encounters punish that style. Yes, you could drop Ignite on purpose for threat back in the day, but now you’re dropping it because you have to... adds spawn, priority targets appear, mechanics force you to move, or you need to dispel. Those drops aren’t strategic, they’re forced by encounter design. In those situations, the old Ignite doesn’t give you a “burst advantage,” it just makes your damage wildly inconsistent and overly reliant on having the perfect comp.

And while the suggestion of “just bring more Fire Mages,” that’s unrealistic for most rosters. The very issues that's acknowledged... respec friction, people already committed to Arcane/Frost, and the risk of stacking a comp for Ignite playstyle under mechanic heavy conditions are exactly why reverting to the old Ignite would further limit Fire’s viability instead of expanding it.

As for Fire’s toolkit being “inferior,” that’s exactly why we should make its baseline spells stronger and Ignite less dominant. That creates a playstyle that works whether you’re solo Fire, duo Fire, or in a full Fire group. It also raises the skill ceiling in a healthier way... through decision-making in your rotation, Hot Streak procs, and adapting to mechanics... rather than through an inconsistent Ignite roll that collapses the second conditions aren’t perfect.

Nostalgia shouldn’t blind us to the fact that the old Ignite wasn’t a flexible or universally viable interaction. It was fun for a small subset of players in ideal conditions, but that’s not the same as a good design for the game today. Fire can have an “upside” without requiring the stars to align... and that’s what the changes I’ve suggested aim to deliver.
