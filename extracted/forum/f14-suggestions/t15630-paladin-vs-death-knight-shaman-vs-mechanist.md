Will share my 'sketch version' of the mechanist.
For selectable races, I exclude humans since they have the most option in the game already (tied with trolls) and night elves, because I can't imagine them being 'techy'.
I think gnomes are a perfect match, dwarves can work (I imagine a dwarf crafting a turret like Torbjörn from Overwatch) and helves since they are extremely adept at arcana and a mechanist should basically be a mixture of an engineer and an archanist.

So.

Selectable races: gnome, dwarf, helf.

Also, it seems a bit counter-intuitive but no engineering required. Making a primary profession obligatory for a class selection is bad design.

A mechanist should be the alliance version of shaman using arcane but should not be a carbon copy of it.
It's not like you take windfury totem, rename it to windfury turret and call it a day. They need a unique gameplay and identity which resembles shaman but is not shaman.
They will be mana users.
Also, a change in the base design: WOW defines lightnings as nature spells and a class (partially) attacking with electricity will need nature damage in its disposal. This version of mechanist will use arcane and some nature compared to the shaman's nature and some fire (+minimal frost).

Shamans divide their totems into four elements and can only have 1 active from each element at a time.
Mechanists will have 3+1 turret types which follow the same logic as the shamans' totems.

 1. Arcane turrets - a more support-based element; can provide mp5 buff to the party, spell haste to the party and arcane resistance (among others, just doodling rn).
 2. Nature turrets - lightning-based theme, more offensive turrets spewing out electroshocks, an electric shield providing spell damage reduction and of course a nature rez turret.
 3. Purely mechanical turrets - diverse utility which doesn't use magic, for example an anti-fall turret negating fall damage in an area, or an annoy-o-turret decresing enemy hit against your party members.
+1. Mines. Mines are specific turrets which can be placed remotely (the same way you aim a blizzard). They either stay in an area, hidden from the enemy and deal damage when an enemy gets too close to it or start moving around inside an area until they are destroyed or they hit an enemy target. My closest comparison are hunter traps.

While shamans have a wide-range of utility but mostly benefit melee, I'd imagine mechanists as a utility class which mostly focuses on buffing casters. Swap windfury totem to a spellhaste turret and strength totem to for example a mana refund-when-casting turret and here we are.
They can provide defensive turrets to grant arcane res and nature rez (compared to the shaman's nat, fire and frost resistance totems).

So basic gameplay would look like a guy summoning 2-3 turrets and throwing out a mine which may or may not be very similar to a hunter trap. After that they usually start their rotation.
Rotation highly depends on the talent tree they have invested in the most.
 1. Healer
Grants additional support turrets and grants access to nature healing (currently only holy and nature healing exists, I don't want be so much of a pioneer to introduce arcane healing).
Their healing is based on four fundamentals:
 * Simple HoT
 * Turrets granting a spell absorb (let's say 5-10% of the incoming spell damage), absorbed damage turns to HoT.
 * Specific turrets duplicating the HoT you cast on a target to all party members but at a much lower efficiency.
 * Healing tree capstone is a medium CD spell which summons an antique healbot which runs around healing adjacent allies until it shuts down automatically (or gets blown up sooner by the enemy).

 2. Ranged DPS
Extra damaging turrets and a high emphasis of their equivalent of shock spells. Nature-based shock and arcane shock.
Getting their equivalent to chain lightning.
Getting an amplifier turret which targets enemies (one at a time) but does it randomly, within range. Targeted enemy gets extra damage from any arcane or nature source.

 3. Puppeteer DPS (pet class)
Imagine it as BM hunter but instead of a pet you get many abilities which let you build specific robots on the spot which you can send against the opponent. While building these robots, you are casting, making you unable to fight or move during this time.
There would be a max amount of robots you can have at the same time, each having a different utility (blowing up, shielding a targeted ally etc). Tree focuses highly on 'cast time reduction' when you build robots and the increased efficiency of these robots.
Damage outside of turrets, mines and robots would exist but would be negligeable.
