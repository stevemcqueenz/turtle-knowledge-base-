## Post 5908 by Gifted (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2020-05-10T13:06:00Z

1) I don't PvP, so no opinion to offer.
 2) Now I have an opinion to offer...

I've observed a consistent failing of many private WoW servers of the last 2-3 years is a lack of player investment. You look at servers that offered instant 60s or x2, x5, x10 XP rates and there tends to be a lack of player investment in their characters. This results in unusually high levels of trolling and the edgy/offensive personalities dominating world chat. I personally feel that when players are invested in their toons (or the server) they tend to be better virtual citizens, engage the community in more positive ways, and actively work to maintain a civil atmosphere on the server in general (world chat, pvp scene, raiding scene, RP community, etc..).

Turtle WoW started with the 0.5x XP rates and I think that + the release of Classic WoW 2-3 months after the Turtle WoW launch has been a saving grace. We saw the Reddit horde descend on the server when it launched, but most of that group was looking for the quick 60 route, lost interest in Turtle WoW, and moved on to wait for easier leveling with Classic WoW. The players that stayed with Turtle WoW leveled through the 0.5x XP rates and are **VERY** invested in their characters.

I understand the GMs desire to continue to grow the server population. And I agree that reduced XP rates are probably a nice 'hook' to attract new players. But I would encourage you to consider the above. How to balance reduced XP rates with getting players to feel/stay invested in their characters? Maybe a level 1-20 (or even 1-30) reduced XP rate (as an optional glyph) to encourage new players to try out Turtle WoW? It gives them time to get a feel for the community while powering through the first half of leveling. Then the remaining levels to 60 go back to regular XP rates. It would be nice to attract like-minded players who want to invest in their characters for the long-term.

 **~Gifted**

Vanilly has a very well implemented double XP system called Rest that was designed just address this kind of problems. Adding ways to gather rested XP like attending BGs, gardening, and doing dungeons could be made into powerful incentives. Just look at how many players are attending tent parties.

Similarly for XP events, the actual rested bonus could be raised from 200% to 300% for example. Rested cap prevents people from hoarding this bonus and somewhat forces them to gather it back up every now and then if they want to continue grinding with increased rates.
