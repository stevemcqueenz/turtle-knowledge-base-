# On Behalf of the Blue Flight, to TWoW Devs

## Post 168943 by Drakarathas — 2026-02-19T20:24:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=168943#p168943 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

As of today having seen the blue dragons showcased in the new trailer for Moonwhisper Coast. As a blue dragon roleplayer, my immediate reaction was excitement. That excitement soon faded when the following thought crossed my mind; "They're enemies, aren't they."

It has been well-established that the Blue Dragonflight is a Lawful Neutral faction, similarly indifferent towards mortals as the Bronze Dragonflight are (that is, more concerned with performing their duties than interacting with the lesser races.) This does not mean the Blue Flight does not hold any respect for mortals, however, as the RPG books go into detail about how they often slip into mortal societies undetected to learn about cultures and traditions. Societally, they merely value knowledge above all else. They are territorial due to most of their numbers being wiped out by Deathwing and his brood, but they are by no means evil, nor are they enemies to mortalkind.

Blizzard failed the Blue Dragonflight by twisting them into villains come Wrath solely because they needed to create new raid content. Malygos' prior insanity was confirmed to have subsided after Deathwing's defeat at Grim Batol during the Second War, but that was retconned so that players had a new loot piñata to beat. I see the first inklings of TurtleWoW doing the same, with the added content in Azshara involving slaughtering uncorrupted members of the Blue Flight, as well as those within Karazhan. If anything, this should be something shifted to player choice as with most other neutral factions, as the Blue Flight has a history of accepting assistance from mortals (Even in TurtleWoW.) In addition, the Blue Dragonflight are ancient allies of the night elves, having fought together in the War of the Ancients and the War of the Shifting Sands (as well as Malygos' assistance in the creation of the world tree, Nordrassil.) That presents a clear narrative issue, as the kal'dorei should actually be in full agreement with the "Great Dampening" that TurtleWoW's devs have presented, due to their 10,000 year old ban on use of arcane magic in their society.

So if these new blue dragons in the Moonwhisper Coast are enemies, this is my message to the dev team: I implore you to consider an alternate route, do not twist the Blue Flight into villains as Blizzard did for Wrath of the Lich King, it's a cheaply-made narrative and I expect better. Like I said, if anything one's opinion of the Blue Flight should be a decision made on behalf of the player, not the game's writers, as they are a neutral faction. To tack on one last comment for my reasoning, I do not wish to have to miss more of this lovely newly-curated content because it involves betraying my character's people. I already had to completely discount the concept of running Karazhan 40 because it would force me to murder my uncorrupted brethren in cold blood.

 - Sincerely, the Blue Dragonflight Fact Checker, **Lyragosa** - Nordanaar

Based on the few original classic quest texts concerning the blue flight, the presentation of the dragonflight as we know and Blizzard's use of them (questionable as their depictions may be in WotLK they do seem to recycle original ideas they once had), it seems likely that the original idea for the blue flight is to actually hoard the world's magic.

The given logic in WotLK is reasonable and fits their actions in Classic, that mortals cannot be trusted with it after devastating incidents involving their misuse (Well of Eternity & the Sundering, Opening of the Dark Portal and subsequent invasions by Scourge and Legion as examples). As the appointed Guardian of Magic, it was well within Malygos' right to decree a ban on magic.

Where there is an issue is the depiction of such reasoning as a result of "insanity". Rather it should be based on more grounded reasoning such as follows:
"For nearly 10,000 years the Blue Flight found themselves unable to perform their duties to their fullest extent due to the decimation of their flight and the weakening of their Aspect, both in power and mental clarity. It was only after the events of Grim Batol when the Black Flight seemed defeated and in hiding did the Blue Flight believe now is time to reassert their ancient authority to a greater degree to ensure the mistakes of the past that resulted in their near extinction doesn't happen again."

Writing motivations off as "*He was nuts!*" only cheapens the plot as Blizzard seemed to have a tendency to do then.

However, Drakarathas does raise an interesting point. The Blue Flight isn't malicious towards mortal races by nature, only greatly distrustful and overbearingly harsh at most. Depicting them as a threat as though they are like the Legion or Old Gods is a disservice to their faction's characterization. To that end I suggest the following:

- The creation of a reputational faction of the Blue Flight known as 'Brood of Malygos' similar to the 'Brood of Nozdormu', starting at the lowest tier but earning trust over time. Quests and interactions are to explain the Blue Flight's reasoning and motivations

- Reputational quests will largely involve you covertly sabotaging your faction's reckless magical experimentations which showcases the Blue's justification for harsh measures (e.g. Forsaken attempts to create the magical New Plague). Other duties involve investigation of ancient sites, acquisition of dangerous artefacts and elimination of dangerous magic users and other threats across Azeroth.

- There can also be an epic questchain within the Brood once exalted that rewards players with the forging of a powerful weapon enchanted by the magic of the land for the purpose of striking down Deathwing who they believe to be hiding in Grim Batol, revealing the second purpose of needing to gather so much magic. In the RPG it was stated that amongst the many threats they have the Black Flight is still their largest concern and since Grim Batol is to be released eventually, we can link the two stories together by having the Brood of Malygos play a role involving their most hated enemy.

If we're theorizing about Deathwing, there is a reason why I only alluded to Deathwing being alive instead of stating he is. In the RPG, Blizzard's original [idea](https://wowpedia.fandom.com/wiki/Deathwing#Dragonblight) for him was that he was dying and had returned to Dragonblight seemingly with the intent to perish there as most dragons would do.

In Classic after the events of Grim Batol, the Wetlands has an underlying corruption spreading out that was alluded to be [recent](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UNiHxOaLfjE). This means that even though there is an Old God presence beneath the Wetlands for millennia and Modgud did her magic centuries ago, only some new development kickstarted its decay. Since there is strong evidence they had intended Deathwing to be in Grim Batol, the question then becomes what did he do?

This is where I strongly believe that what Deathwing was originally doing beneath the mountain was instead given away to develop a new character, which is something that Blizzard likes to do at times. I believe that Galakrond is that part of Deathwing that was meant to be in Vanilla. Even his backstory (showed in DF's book) and legacy is a near mirror to the RPG's Deathwing: A great dragon that was once benevolent but then turned evil due to the Old Gods, died in Dragonblight and his remains are sought out by groups like the Scourge and Twilight's Hammer in WotLK and Cata.

Hell, I would even say that Galakrond's color scheme is the same as the Warcraft 2 icon for Deathwing.

Based on this knowledge, the RPG's description of Grim Batol, what we know of Deathwing ourselves and Blizzard's own subsequent themes and protrayals of him the following below is my guess to Blizzard's original intention for him:
"Retreating deep into the abyss beneath Grim Batol, Deathwing is effectively a prisoner in the lower reaches of the shadowed city. Even though the Red Flight are unwilling to risk delving further below the last floors of their former prison, they felt confident enough to watch over the few entrances to prevent his escape, hoping to simply starve him to death within."

Wandering the cavernous deeps Deathwing had no choice but to feast on whatever creatures he could find, including the corrupt Skardyn. With each consumption of their black flesh however, the dragon felt the presence of a power enamating deep below. It wasn't long before he discover the truth of what Modgud had summoned into being long ago and soon he begins to formulate his return and the rise of a new, *undying* dragonflight. A dynasty whose rise heralds the end of the era yet the sun will never set upon their rule, surpassing even his now-near decimated Black Flight.

Performing self-experimentation with this newfound power and even managing to raise a brood out of the few Red eggs that were forgotten and missing within the depths, Deathwing has raised a nascent flight of cannibalistic dragons, imbued with dark magic and growing stronger with each being devoured, all the while throwing up their remains that eventually rose as undead thralls. The Skardyn, now worshippers of this great black beast whose breath reeks of Death, offer themselves by the dozens as sacrifices upon the whispered urgings from unlit corners.

As these unholy acts takes place deep below the mountain city, the Red Flight in their complacency, can't help but feel a strange malaise across the land they now watch...
