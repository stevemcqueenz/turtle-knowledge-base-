# Draenei, but for the horde

## Post 108195 by Brikoth — 2024-11-16T21:45:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=108195#p108195 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I'm typing this up fully aware there are no intentions for them to add new races/classes and such, and this is most likely shouting into the void. Still, I wanna get my thoughts out and hear what others think.

High elves added to the alliance is a very clever play, it's an incredibly popular race, but you avoid adding paladins to the horde by giving them to the alliance instead. There are no contradictions to this, and they got to flex some world building at the same time.

It would follow then, that you could do the same for draenai and the horde to keep shaman horde-side (and just pretend like they can't be paladins). Another incredibly popular race given a whole new twist to recontextualize it's presence! But uh... how do you do that exactly? Don't orcs and goats have a really nasty history together? Also why should we have any respect for the retcon that was the draenai/eredar story? ALSO spaceships and weird crystal tech is such a weird fit for the otherwise high fantasy of WoW, do we really want to get that integrated?

These are all valid concerns, but I believe we can work around them.

First, yes, the orcs and draenai had a history of conflict. So did the humans, though they could still put aside their differences in times of great peril. And especially the forsaken, with nowhere else to turn, the horde became their sanctuary in a world that turned against them. The question from there is "can you really do that with draenai?"

Let's consider what draenai are, but *only* from WC3 and early WoW. They weren't eredar, they were a peaceful smattering of clans across draenor that got curbstomped by the orcs in their fel revolution. The broken ones we see in WC3 are warped and hanging on for dear life before Illidan gives them a victory against Magtheridon. There are also handfuls of broken clans in azeroth that migrated from draenor (perhaps forcefully) that rarely get along with anyone.

So what we might want is something like this: Shamanistic in nature, and able to re-obtain their old forms thanks to the natural world of azeroth being in balance in comparison to outland. My thinking is this, the elemental chaos of outlands is why the only draenai we ever met before TBC was broken, they cannot avoid degrading if they live there. Coming to azeroth is very appealing to them, healing, even if it wont restore them. Over the years, clans have given birth to new draenai, and thanks to the balanced elements of their new home, they retained their original forms. These children would come together to form a faction of their own to find a place in azeroth, remaining reclusive for their own safety, until they hear about Thrall's horde.

Despite the gruesome past, and likely much naysaying among their own, these younger draenai never lived through the orcish-led genocide. Orcs have been little more than a boogey-man to them, but appraising the shamanism of the orcs, as well as the tauren, so incredibly in touch with nature, they find a home with the horde, seeking to learn of their mastery of the elements and perhaps one day pull their elders from their deformation. And much like the tauren, they share a deep sympathy for the forsaken, wishing to use whatever knowledge they have to remove the affliction cursing these people the world has condemned as monsters. Also trolls... they'd probably like trolls, I think.

The alliance seemingly wouldn't have any beef with them before they joined the horde, and given the dark portal's proximity to alliance territory, how exactly do they end up with the horde instead? The simple answer is that they look alien and don't speak common. Their reclusive nature makes them seem more akin to quick-hooved demons in the eyes of nearby humans, and they've engendered no sympathy from them. Draenai speak orcish on the other hand, and the orcs at least know what draenai are. The shaman of the horde visiting stonard might even be able to sense them, hidden away in the mountains or wherever. This leads to them creating the first proper contact, and appealing to them as fellow shaman. You aren't the draenai of the past, and nor are we those shameful, bloodthisty orcs of ours.

And with that, we have playable draenai without any of the weird retcons or spaceships. They're less light based and more naturalistic, so the lack of paladins is a given. No more connection to the naa'ru, the elements are their new best friends now. With a bit of touch-up to his story, Nobundo would make a good candidate for their racial leader, leaving Velen out of the picture, and Akama in outland.
