## Post 148586 by Allarielle — 2025-08-27T07:50:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=148586#p148586 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

To be honest? It's actually not nearly as cool as it seems on paper.

Imagine this scenario: You're in the Ban'ethil Barrow Den in Teldrassil, fighting Gnarlpine Shamans. You've got your 16 slot starter bag and another 3 6 slotters, mostly filled with quest and and a few profession items. The Shamans are equipped with a staff. You kill your first Shaman, and oh wow, it dropped its staff! It's a sort of side-grade, but still super neat! You then kill another Shaman, and it drops another staff. The second staff is identical to the first. By the time you're done questing in the Ban'ethil Barrow Den, you've killed some fifth-odd Shamans and got close to a dozen of those damnable useless staves, and the only reason you haven't started discarding the blasted things on sight is because they vendor for just *slightly* more than the greys you've also been getting. Your bags are extremely overstuffed, though, and the whole time you just can't get past the idea that every single one of these Furbolgs is equipped with literally *the same* gear. They're clones of each other, you realise, and your immersion is irrevocably shattered.

Now imagine that you're questing in Northwatch Hold in the Barrens, culling Kul Tiran invaders like the pestelent little pinkskins they are. One of them drops a piece of their gear. Oh cool, neat transmog! Now I just need to collect the whole set to make it a thing! And then you're grinding for the next four hours just to get the last piece of the set because who cares if you've got humans coming out of your eyeballs and a now-permanent aversion to anyone wielding the holy light as long as you've got the complete RP transmog set you'll never use.

No, it sounds like a neat idea, but from a design standpoint you either make the drops so rare they're hardly distinguishable from the regular loot table, or you make them so common that they break immersion and make the content annoying and dull, or you strike that perfect balance where you get *both* no immediately noticable effect *and* break immersion.

Bronzebeard's *other* RP items - the worldforged items you find when exploring the open world - are amazing. The immersive drops, though, are a total bust. At least IMO.
