Isn’t it as simple as reworking items that surpass level 60 item levels and bringing them closer together, to actually deliver on what they should realize as a core difference to the original and potential leading philosophy—more horizontal progression instead of vertical?

So let’s say all legendaries meet at item level 80, while all epics cap at item level 75, and new content doesn’t push that ceiling higher. Because, believe it or not, we don’t actually need to keep getting stronger.

There’s a lot of room to make gear interesting without constantly increasing raw power—please recognize that. The overall experience would be better if the gap between high-end gear and lower-tier gear weren’t so large. We could mix and match much more, combine sets for their bonuses, and generally have far more freedom in choosing what gear to use.

So yeah, I agree—the legendaries should never be surpassed. They’re hard enough to obtain, and they should feel like an incredible reward. In my opinion, they should also have more unique and epic quest chains to match their status.

## Post 167898 by Morkahja (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2026-02-10T18:06:00Z

The only issue is that everyone seems brainwashed by the idea that new content must be more difficult by requiring higher power levels—an idea that ruined WoW expansions from the very beginning. Vertical gear progression delivers exactly two things: a split community and imbalance.

Turtle WoW has already added a lot of interesting bonuses to items, and the overall loot pool would not become any less interesting if items were instead leveled horizontally.

There are many ways to give new raids unique challenges that are met by the gear itself, as has always been the case. Molten Core becomes easier through fire resistance, Naxxramas through frost resistance, Ahn’Qiraj through nature resistance, and so on. Beyond that, there is still unexplored space: epic trinkets with real utility—swiftness, blink, immunity, disengage, and similar effects.

New content can be hard in a different way instead of just harder. It does not need to escalate endlessly. Is this game really about raw challe or is it always the case that we meta-optimize to mitigate and minimize that challenge anyway?

I take that a muddle-through method for addressing power creep issues. Adding something like "attack power bonus for fighting demons/undead creatures" or "decreasing less favorable attributes like spirit or resistance for space for powering up more useful aspects" is not technically appreciable. It's short-sighted with overly conspicuous intention of avoiding overpowering new gears.

Why do we think powercreep should be avoided? The answer is we instinctively would like to see our game's life span elongated in a scientifically sustainable way. The game should stick with its plot and mechanism, not the stronger gears that dwarf older ones. In TBC, non-heroic lv. 70 dungeon gears are at around 100 of item level. Raid gears from 10-player Karazhan of TBC was at about item level 115. When TWoW updates with its own Tier 4 gears, I wouldn't feel surprised to see a two-hand weapon with over 110 DPS. To make comparison, Terestian's Stranglestaff and Despair in TBC's K10, melee two-handers, have 114 DPS each. In TWoW, [Shar'tateth, the Shattered Edge](https://database.turtlecraft.gg/?item=55127)'s DPS is already 100 in version 1.18. Even if TWoW uses "specified attack power and reduction of attributes", it's still detrimental to the game's sustainability. That only replicates what Blizzard/Activision did in around 2005, in a less aggressive approach.
