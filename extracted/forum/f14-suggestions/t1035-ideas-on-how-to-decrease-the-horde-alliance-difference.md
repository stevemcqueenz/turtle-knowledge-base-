# Ideas on how to decrease the Horde/Alliance difference

## Post 5834 by Volitare — 2020-05-06T13:56:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=5834#p5834 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Just by the aesthetics, Alliance will always get more players. I understand that, but on small servers such as TW, it's very damaging - without the diplomacy system, I doubt that Horde players could run many dungeons. But we're all aware of the inequality between the faction's populations, so I just want to throw out some ideas that would make established players roll more Horde alts, and newcomers be more attracted to the Horde side.

First, let me just state that I have no experience with custom, or any, server development and I do not know how possible any of these ideas truly are.

 **Forsaken Paladins**
 - The lore is there, the depth is there, the roleplaying possibilities are amazing.
 - Now, you might say, Paladins and Shamans are the dividing line between the Horde and the Alliance. Yes, as much as I think that line ought to be Paladins and Warlocks, it's what Warcraft established. BUT with the release of TBC, they went back on this decision. Besides, the Forsaken aren't really "Horde", are they?

 **Troll Warlocks**
 - Trolls are power hungry, and to me it makes more sense than Trolls being mages, fundamental arcanists.
 - Trolls already have 6 classes and adding another one might prove problematic, so - Mages are gone. *insert crab meme*

 **Orcs should be pushed via custom quests to travel to the Stormwind region.**
 - As of level 20, they can go to Hillsbrad and later on Arathi Highlands, where they can learn of the events from the Second War. But what about the First War?
 - Orcish custom quests might benefit from a voyage to Elwyn, Redridge and Westfall. These quests could range from simple kill and collect, to gathering relics, to establishing peaceful relations.

 **Lastly, Tauren**
 - Tauren are pretty straight forward. They don't like the centaur, but the centaur were defeated in W3. Their history with Cenarius only extends to druids.
 - The Tauren leveling experience would benefit from more quests focused on their never-ending conflict with the Centaur. There are centaur scouts present in Mulgore, but as far as I am aware of, they aren't objective of any quests. Instead of the gnolls in Mulgore, Tauren should fight against Centaur. This would not interfere with the Venture Co. quests in Mulgore.
 - In Barrens, the campaign against Centaur is a good quest line, but it's all Orc quest givers. There should be more custom quests to further the Tauren/Centaur hatered, no Orc or Troll NPCs involved. Other Horde players could still do these quests, of course. Ideally, we would ally with some hate-driven Night Elves in order to fully erase the Centaur threat, showcasing the Tauren and Night Elf bond. It would also allow Night Elves to be more savage, as they were originally intended to be.

 -------------------------------
I don't think that these two changes would be damaging to the player experience what-so-ever, and most people can probably get behind it.

Now, time to get a bit more radical-ish. As I said above, I don't believe Warlocks have any reason to exist within the Alliance. It makes sense for all races to be attracted to the demonic powers, but it makes no sense to allow them within the faction itself. Humans have no reason for being tolerant towards the demonic, gnomes even less so. Just like for the Night Elves, who would - by the way - never ally with anyone who allows Warlocks, Human and Gnomish Warlocks should be killable NPCs only.
 **If there would be no playable Warlocks on the Alliance side, Warlock players would naturally trasition to the Horde.**
If needed, I would replace these losses with Human Hunters and Gnome priests. Lorewise it makes sense, and really, what more do you need?

Just want to add, if warlocks were to be removed from a faction for lore reasons, that faction would be the horde. While not as pronounced as in later expansions, the horde is still a primarily orcish organization, and no one hates practitioners of fel magic more than the orcs at this point. At this point in the lore, the orcs lost their honor, lives, and world by being tricked by Gul'dan into demonic servitude. Thrall even says in the questline for Ragefire Chasm that the only reason he allows the warlocks in the city to live is so the horde can kill them all at once instead of letting a few remain in hiding.

Naturally, the Forsaken would have no moral quandary to practice fel magic, but their very existence is on thin ice with the horde. The only reason they were allowed entry is Cairne basically called Thrall a hypocrite for wanting to turn his back on people unfairly turned into monsters. Fel magic would easily be grounds to say they're a lost cause.

While the alliance isn't a fan of fel magic by any stretch, they've no intense hatred of it, either (aside from the night elves who hate magic entirely, of course). It's a tool to be used to protect themselves, no different from any other magic, or even a sword. Each can be dangerous in the wrong hands, but wielded competently, it could be a great boon. Fel magic could lead to disaster, but it could also protect the world should the Legion invade again. So there's reason to allow it under supervision.
