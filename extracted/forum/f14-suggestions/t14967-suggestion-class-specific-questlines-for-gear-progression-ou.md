# [Suggestion] Class-Specific Questlines for Gear Progression Outside of Raiding

## Post 101815 by Demray — 2024-09-24T12:03:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=101815#p101815 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hello, Turtle WoW community!

I'd like to suggest a new system to expand gear progression options for players who prefer to explore the world, engage in roleplay, or who simply don’t have the time or desire to participate in the endgame raiding scene.
My idea is to implement class-specific quest lines that reward gear on par with Tier 2 and 2.5.

 1. Why is this important?

Turtle WoW's focus on worldbuilding and roleplay
Turtle WoW has always stood out by expanding the established Warcraft universe and offering deeper roleplay and worldbuilding opportunities. Adding class-specific questlines that offer meaningful rewards would further enhance the player's immersion in the world. These quests could be rich in lore, unique to each class, and provide players with a sense of meaningful progression that doesn’t rely on raiding.

Bridging the power gap for non-raiders
As it stands, there is a growing power disparity between the early raid tiers, PvP, and craftable gear compared to later raids like AQ40, Naxxramas, and the newly added Upper Karazhan. This creates a large imbalance for players who do not raid but still want to advance their characters in terms of power. By offering a quest-based progression path, Turtle WoW can provide an alternative that respects players' time and preferred playstyles without forcing them into content they don’t enjoy.

Strengthening class identity and lore
Class-specific questlines would also offer an opportunity to dive deeper into each class's lore, reinforcing their identity and giving players a stronger connection to their character. Imagine a Paladin venturing to reclaim lost relics in Hearthglen, or a Warlock making pacts with the Shadow Council—each class could have a journey that reflects its essence and history in the game.

Revitalizing old content
Many iconic zones and dungeons have fallen out of regular use. These class questlines could reintegrate areas like Dire Maul, Stratholme, Tyr's Hand, Hearthglen, and Darkwhisper Gorge into the game, giving them relevance once more. Expanding on these areas with new quests would breathe fresh life into old content.

Reusing and expanding existing factions
The game already has many established factions that could be reused and expanded upon in these questlines. Factions like the Argent Dawn, Shen’dralar, Earthen Ring, and Cenarion Circle could serve as quest givers. The quests could be locked behind certain reputation ranks, requiring players to build up from Friendly to Exalted to access these rewards, creating a sense of progression and involvement with these factions.

 2. How could this work?

To revitalize the old areas, I propose adding lengthy, immersive, and challenging class-specific questlines that take players on a journey through these forgotten zones, forging alliances with factions in exchange for gear and glory.

Key elements:

The questlines could consist of around 10-15 quests, much like the Tier 0.5 questlines, but tailored specifically to each class.
These quests would send players to classic areas, dungeons, and zones that have significant lore or relevance to the class or faction they are working with. I suggest that all the quest lines should have an exploration aspect, a group-focused aspect such as dungeons or world mini-bosses and a crafting/gathering aspect.

Upon completing these quests, players would be rewarded with epic-quality gear comparable to Tier 2.5 (AQ40) in power level. I envision this gear to be a 4-piece set + 1 ring, tailored to each class and offering a unique set bonus.
The gear would be designed to complement Turtle WoW’s custom playstyles, such as Shaman tanking, Retribution Paladins, and Arcane Mages, making the gear both class-relevant and lore-appropriate. For example, Paladins might receive a Silver Hand-themed set, while Warlocks might earn a Shadow Council-themed set.

The gear sets could consist of the following pieces:
Shoulders
Wrist
Hands
Feet
Ring

I’ve been experimenting with the Turtle WoW item creator and will share some of my ideas soon as I refine them. :)

 3. Disclaimer

This suggestion is not meant to remove the need for raiding, nor is it intended to hand out free gear to non-raiders. On the contrary, these quest lines should be lengthy, time-consuming, immersive, and fun.
The high-tier raids like AQ40, Naxxramas, and the upcoming Upper Karazhan will still hold the best gear in the game.

This is merely a way to offer additional progression options for players who prefer not to raid or those who wish to engage more deeply with the world and its lore. It’s about offering choices that enhance the overall experience without diminishing the value of raiding.

Lastly, I’m no lore expert, so I’m sure there are ways to further enhance this idea to suit each class better. I welcome any feedback or suggestions the community might have to improve this concept!

Thank you for your thoughtful question! The key to ensuring these questlines seamlessly integrate into the existing game world—and not feel like a disconnected or isolated experience—lies in making them feel like natural extensions of the lore, zones, and factions that already exist. Here's how I envision this integration happening:

Tied to Lore and Factions
Each questline would be deeply tied to established factions (e.g., Argent Dawn, Shen’dralar, Cenarion Circle) and would expand on their ongoing stories. This way, players won’t feel like they’re engaging with something completely new and separate, but rather building on relationships and stories that have always been a part of the game’s world. These factions already have roots in the zones they inhabit, so the questlines would logically involve traveling to key locations in the world where they have a presence.

Revitalizing Zones with Purpose
The old-world zones we’re revitalizing are already rich with lore and purpose (like Hearthglen for Paladins or Dire Maul for Mages), and the quests would use these locations as natural environments for class-specific tasks. The intention is to make players revisit these areas for meaningful reasons tied to their class identity and progression, rather than just for farming or grinding. This reinforces the idea that they’ve always had a role in the world, even if they had been underutilized.

Blending with Existing Content
Rather than creating entirely new instances or areas, these quests would repurpose existing dungeons and zones while possibly adding some new NPCs, quest hubs, or mini-events that naturally blend into the environment. For example, a Paladin quest in Stratholme might have you complete a unique task in a part of the instance that feels connected to the Scourge’s ongoing story. The goal is to ensure that when players are traveling through these zones, it feels like they’re continuing a story that’s already in progress rather than embarking on something disconnected from the broader game.

Building on Reputation Systems
Since the quests would be tied to factions, they would work through the reputation systems that are already in place, offering players additional reasons to engage with these factions on a deeper level. Progression through these quests would feel like a natural extension of the work players are already doing with these factions, whether that’s in Stratholme with the Argent Dawn or elsewhere with other faction allies.

In essence, the focus is on enhancing the world that’s already there, deepening the player’s engagement with it, and making older zones and factions feel as though they’ve always been waiting for this next chapter in their storylines. I hope this addresses your concern, and I’d love to hear more thoughts on this!

Thank you for your thoughtful feedback! I completely understand your concern about players gravitating toward the path of least resistance, especially in an MMORPG like Turtle WoW. However, I'd like to emphasize that the enjoyment of raiding should be rooted in the experience itself: the teamwork, the coordination, and the thrill of overcoming challenging bosses with a group of friends or guildmates. If the only focus is on obtaining gear, then the actual method of acquiring that gear becomes less important. In that sense, a class quest system offering gear progression could easily coexist with raiding without undermining the experience.

In fact, I think it's important to remember that raiding is just one aspect of World of Warcraft, and while it serves those who enjoy large-scale PvE encounters, it might not resonate with everyone. Turtle WoW is an MMORPG, and a significant part of the 'RPG' component can be lost if players only focus on raid content. Class quests are a way to reintroduce that RPG element for players who enjoy storytelling, immersion, and character progression without needing to raid.

To clarify, the gear rewarded from these class quests wouldn’t replace raiding gear. It would be on par with some raid tier sets, but not exceed them. This way, players who prefer not to raid or are unable to participate in end-game raids can still feel that their character is progressing and remaining competitive. It's about providing an alternative path for those who enjoy other aspects of the game while still maintaining a balanced progression system.

Ultimately, raiding will always be the premier way to earn the best gear and have those epic, large-group experiences, but not everyone plays the game for those reasons. Class quest lines would offer another avenue for players who enjoy different aspects of the game to feel rewarded and connected to their characters.
