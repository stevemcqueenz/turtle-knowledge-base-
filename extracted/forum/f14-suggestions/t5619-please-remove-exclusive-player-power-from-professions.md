# Please remove exclusive Player Power from Professions

## Post 34815 by Ugoboom (Bug Report Enthusiast) — 2023-02-15T14:16:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=34815#p34815 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I've seen a worrying shift in game design philosophy from the turtle devs recently. Truefaith vestements now require 300 tailoring to wear, you can no longer craft them and drop the profession. With the Alchemy Stone getting attention from the devs, the requires 300 alchemy bit has not been removed, and if implemented, would make Alchemy mandatory for all healers and many mana dps specs.

This sucks. Vanilla was the one time where professions all kinda really just sucked, which was great because you ended up being free to pick whatever proffesion you liked, or that you enjoyed the RP of.

Engineering was the odd one out, and when you look at blizzard's designs, its clear that OP engi was a mistake. An awful lot of engi's tools were designed to be quirky weird gadgets of questionable value for investment, that would often screw you over as much as they would benefit you. But of all the weird and useless aspects to engi, there accidentally ended up being a few tools like grenades, sappers, reflectors, MC/Rocket caps, that ended up noticeably increasing pve player power, and massively increased PVP player power. Players quickly realized this when revisiting the 1.12 game on private servers, and because of social forces and PVP competiton... well it didn't matter what spec you played, what your charater's RP was... you HAVE to go engineering, or you will forever be vastly subpar in pvp and do less damage in PVE. I'll never forget the awful sad moment when I dropped 300 herbalism on my flowerpilled resto druid, because I was just such a worse player only for the reason I didn't have engi.

Anyway, this problem is super easy to fix. As vanilla's profs were never really meant to be player power, just interesting moneymakers, let's fulfil 2004 blizzard's original design and return all professions to moneymaking, and player power derived from professions would never be strictly tied to having the profession. This can be done by removing Requires: where it doesn't make sense, and where a restriction does make RP sense, alternative sources of the same player power would be added available for everyone else.

We actually have a number of posts on the forum already, asking for exactly this in various cases:

Sappers: [viewtopic.php?f=14&t=4779](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?f=14&t=4779)
Grenades and bombs: [viewtopic.php?p=26952](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=26952)
Reflectors
Battle Chicken
Goblin jumper cables: Fixed by smelling salts, yay!
Engi net: Hateforge's net trinket solved this issue, yay!
Smoking heart of the mountain: Fixed by additional itemization. yay!
Epic MCP: [viewtopic.php?p=28950](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=28950)
Bloodvine caster 3set: Fixed by bloodvine revamp making it no longer bis, yay!

For those that understand vanilla is sooooooo close to pure freedom of professions, the discussion is unanimous: remove the last odd remaining player power boons from professions. Professions then become free for people to pick for RP, and metagamers will simply pick whichever professions the guild needs, and which ever ones make the most money, and this is fine!

But yeah, the change to Truefaith, and the proposed addition of Alchemist stone... this is going the WRONG way! By adding player power to professions, you end up making the same mistake TBC and especially WOTLK made, and guess what... even blizzard never got it right, they still ended up with dumb metas with impossible to balance values, and players simply pick whatever the meta is, despite if it flies in the face of their character's RP.

This sucks! Why are we going *towards* those awful tbc/wotlk changes? Come on man, we were so close to perfection. Go back, remove the prof requirements where that makes sense, and where it doesn't instead there should be alternative itemization provided for those that chose other professions.

I mostly agree with the overall sentiment, but I disagree with the idea of completely decoupling player power from professions. The same argument could be made about racials. I agree that racials and professions should primarily serve role-play purposes, but I also think it’s fine—and even good—for them to have a small impact on player power. Embrace the differences.

If you prioritize RP and another player focuses entirely on min-maxing, why should that be a problem? The power gap between RP-focused choices and optimal min-maxing should be minor, but it doesn’t need to be zero.

Personally, I actually prefer that these systems affect player power. People need to accept that it’s impossible to perfectly balance a game like WoW—and trying to achieve total balance isn’t even desirable.

Metas will always shift and evolve; it’s essentially a rock-paper-scissors dynamic.

I like that racials, classes, and ideally professions all offer unique advantages. If someone wants absolute balance, there are games like League of Legends that are built around that philosophy.

That said, I’m not against balance adjustments. Small, gradual tweaks to balance things within a reasonable range are still important and should continue to happen.
