# Classes reimagined

## Post 95711 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2024-05-26T15:29:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=95711#p95711 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!

This will be a lengthy article. **My goal here is not to propose class changes or to advise anything in general. It's a creative game** - let's say it's 2004, WOW just getting started. This is how I'd personally imagine the builds if we started everything from scratch. Will cheat a bit because I'll use some ideas from later expansions, though.
My goal is mostly entertainment - to create 'what if-builds', the way I'd have imagined them. Will keep the builds and the base structure of WOW but will change the flavor itself.

 **Hunter**

I'd keep hunters as a pure DPS class. Marksmanship would stay ranged, Survival and BM would be melee.

 **Marsmanship**

I'd make the class identity revolve around a talent which gives agility and ranged crit chance as long as your pet is not summoned. Marksmanship hunter would be Legolas from LoTR. Generally not using pets, relying on great ranged DPS and some unique utility. Would be the option for those who wanna play as archers or any other non-magical ranged-damage dealers.

Their unique utilities would include:
 * A medium CD shot which reduces the target's hit chance for a time period (utility against damage spikes).
 * A medium CD aoe which has an added crit chance.
 * A very big-range shot with low damage but with slow effect (n CD).
 * A party utility giving ranged (including caster) hit chance.

 **BM**

Rexxar. A DPS class specialized in fighting with multiple beasts at once. The talents would include increasing pet avoidance and a pet damage increase which scales with your agility. However, to keep them balanced, using the 2nd pet comes with serious setbacks: summoning the second pet disables the hunter's own attacking abilities as long as both pets are fighting and the hunter is also receiving a part of the damage dealt to the beasts. At PVP they can double-pet-attack a target but this is risky for the BM hunter: some crits on the pets might kill him. The gameplay would be mostly about managing the pets' movements and abilities, not shooting. The build is ideal for people who wanna enjoy collecting and using beasts.

Their unique utilities would include:
 * A beast-bloodlust which only affects their pets.
 * A party buff (stackable 3 times) which increases pet haste and damage (including warlock pets).
 * A high-CD defensive ability for the pets to mitigate damage.
 * A talent increasing the effect of the pets' special abilities: for example better howl for wolves.

 **Survival**

Kraven from Spiderman. Purely a melee DPS. Can use a pet but has a talent which increases melee crit chance greatly if no pet is being summoned. Their utility includes laying down traps mid-fight and tauning/kiting mobs into those traps. Otherwise they attack the targets with decent damage. Good choice for players who wanna imagine themselves being a 'brute'/'primitive' kind of fighter, just like Kraven is.

Their unique utilities would include:
 * A talent which gives them strength based on a fraction of their agility.
 * A medium CD talent which gives them a big agility boost.
 * Being in touch with the wildlife, having a shorter pull range against beasts.
 * In combat-traps, also new kind of traps: DoT trap, movement impairment trap, freezing trap, aoe damage trap, increases damage received trap, confusion trap which resets agro (let's say a tank needs to regain threat while being far behind).

 **Rogue**

I'd keep rogues as a melee DPS class but would give the builds different roles. Combat being pure DPS would have the highest damage numbers in a vacuum while assassination would have poison-based utility and subtlety would have threat-based utility.

 **Assassination**

Imagine a current iteration of rogue without some end talent at the combat tree but with better and more useful poisons. Rogue representation includes pirates, thieves, missfits, assasins. Assassination rogues would mostly remind me of Geralt from Witcher 3 if he went full alchemy. Preparation, initiating a fight with an optimal setup and having the advantage with the help of cooked-up substances. Their weakness would be the inability to switch poison on the fly, by giving the poisons a shared CD.

Their unique utilities would include:
 * 'Curse of nature' poison.
 * DoT poison.
 * Damage reduction poison.
 * Tranquilizing poison.
 * Mana burn-poison.
 * Many highly-specific poisons, like:
 * Anti-shapeshift (making shapeshifting impossible as long as the poison is in effect)
 * Poison making totems rot away in 5 sec after being cast.
 * Poison reducing the damage of specific schools of magic.
 * Poison reducing armor.

 **Combat**

Combat rogue would be the no utility, all DPS version. You want to be Kill Bill, John Wick etc, you name it. Combo points not resetting with target switch, adrenaline rush, AoE via fan of knives. Imagine them as the current PVE rogues with AoE and with somewhat higher DPS ceiling but without poisons and with weaker sap and stealth. Not much subtlety, just pure havoc. Also, losing the option of clearing their threat.

Their unique utilities would include:
 * Aoe via fan of knives.
 * Bleed effects and damage boost on bleeding targets.
 * Combo points not affected by target switch.
 * Some kind of execute look-alike.
 * No group utility.

 **Subtlety**

You wanna play as a ninja? There is subtlety rogue. I'd imagine them as a melee DPS build focused around threat manipulation and abusing this effect to raise its own DPS. Having a strong stealth, good sap and two added abilities: smokebomb and lightbomb. Smokebomb would fill up an area with smoke and would reduce the threat of the players who attack the opponent from within the smoke. Lightbomb would do the opposite, enabling tanks to keep threat more easily. In addition, the build would have some bonus effects within the smoke like added dodge and melee crit chance. They would also have an AoE threat reset on a big CD which nullifies the threat of anyone within an area. Optimal for people with ninja roleplay-fantasies.

Their unique utilities would include:
 * AoE threat reduction.
 * AoE threat increase.
 * Increased dodge and melee crit chance from the smoke.
 * AoE threat nullifier on a big CD.

 **Warlock**

I'd change warlocks a lot, creating two DPS classes and 1 hybrid DPS/tank class, similar to the current feral druid. Warlocks would be the ideal fantasy for those, who want to play a shady, dark character. But besides that... Do you want a hooded, evil spellcaster mumbling curses like Quirinus Quirrell in the fist Harry Potter book when he tried to yeet Harry from his broom? Affliction warlock. Rarely casting, mostly debuffing. Do you want to be a magical equivalent of Joker, blowing up everything around you, yourself included? Destruction. Do you want to roam Azeroth with a strong demon friend, giving away some utility and being as hard to kill as a plate user? Demonology.

 **Affliction**

The way I'd imagine affliction warlock is a build which doesn't rely on castable spells. Either uses instant ones like curses or channeled spells. Would have no talent to make shadowbolt faster or stronger, nor a realistic way to get it from another tree. In exchange they'd have a load of debuffs and HoTs to deal damage and to support the group, including a debuff which makes it impossible for the target to remove your other debuffs first and which has a 70% chance to resist dispell. The main gameplay would be setting up curses like warlocks do nowadays. After this using some additional debuffs or DoTs and channeling spells which deal more damage if a specific debuff is on the target. No shadowbolt for them, DPS would rely on the DoTs and the channeling spells. Enjoy your evil curse-mumbler!

Their unique utilities would include:
 * A DoT which deals AoE shadow damage every time it ticks (on CD) - their way to deal AoE damage.
 * Current warlock curses.
 * A passive allowing them to put up two curses onto the target.
 * A debuff 'protecting the other shadow school debuffs and having 70% resistance to being cleansed.
 * A debuff increasing the damage of channeled spells on the target, including non-warlock channeled spells.
 * Drain life having a damage multiplier on targets with that specific debuff.
 * Drain soul having a strong damage multiplier on targets with 20% or less life having that specific debuff.

 **Destruction**

The manifestation of high risk-high reward playstyle. I'd give them a lot of high-threat spells, and abilities which greatly reduce their healing taken in exchange of big damage windows. No threat-reduction talent. At PVP those guys would be the glass cannon-types you have to kill ASAP. In raiding you'd need a bunch of support for them not to pull aggro but if it's successful, they could output a monstruous amount of damage. Also, keeping them alive can be a pain. What I think represents destruction warlocks the best is hellfire. You take risks, do a bunch of damage and might also die. Would move demonic sacrifice deep into this tree. I think a demonologist would want the demon alive while sacrificing the demon for further gains resonates with a destruction warlock in my opinion. Would give them a limited amount of raid utility but would mostly handle them as a 'pure DPS' build. Enjoy destroying stuff? Ka-boom!

Their unique utilities would include:
 * Demonic Sacrifice.
 * A medium CD ability increasing spell damage and threat but reducing healing taken.
 * A passive talent granting them added spell damage below 30% HP.
 * A medium CD talent which channels 10% of their health into 10% of their mana every second for 5 seconds. Warlock cannot be healed in this time period.
 * A passive talent cursing the mob/person who kills the destruction warlock with a big DoT.
 * Reducing shadow bolt cast time, increasing its crit chance but increasing its threat.
 * A self-desctruct ability, causing AoE damage based on the warlock's remaining health.
 * Limited access to curses and debuffs.
 * Giving a party-wide buff which provides mana leech to the party members (mana returned based on spell damage dealt). Unlike shadow priests, here the party members receive the leech buff and they have to damage the target to get mana back.

 **Demonology**

I'd imagine them as a mixture of feral druid and BM hunter. You buff up your pet, get a bunch of armor via demonic aegis and talents, then you either become a utility DPS (okayish damage made by you and your pet but having good party utility buffs) or you tank. As a utility DPS you'd focus on putting up some curses on the target then spamming (non-improved) shadowbolts while maintaining the pet's attack movements. In the meantime the party benefits from the master demonologist party-wide buff (which are weaker than the current iterations but are party-wide).
As a tank you have some challenges. You are a cloth user, you can't bear a shield either.
To handle the problems:
 * Demonic embrace moves deep into the tree and gives even more stamina.
 * Improved voidwalker's taunt becomes a fully-worth taunt.
 * Fel stamina gives more stamina buff for the pet.
 * A new talent next to unholy power which gives added threat modifier to demon attacks, not nonly damage.
 * Soul link giving a 10% damage reduction first and after that distributing 40% of the damage + gives threat instead of damage. However, soul link also decreases the damage you and your pet does for balance reasons.
 * Improved health funnel would become a non-channeled HoT for the demon on CD which can be used to keep it alive while tanking.
 * Adding a talent increasing the threat modifier of your spells.

These changes would create a very bulky character with high threat generation and a defensive CD in form of the voidie's sacrifice.
To keep things balanced, demonologist only has access to base shadowbolt and limited debuff options. Most of the debuffs and curses are reserved to affliction warlocks.
Created for people who want to emerge into the fantasy of being a proud owner of a demonic monster. Also works for those who'd try a 'cooperative' tanking style where both the character and the pet have to be managed symultaneously for a good outcome.

Their unique utilities would include:
 * Party-wide master demonologist buff.
 * Increased pet HP, avoidance and damage, also getting benefits from BM hunter's pet buff.
 * A raid-wide buff increasing pet haste, also for hunter pets.
