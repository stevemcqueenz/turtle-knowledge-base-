# A custom class - Meet the enhancer

## Post 124790 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2025-03-31T22:53:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=124790#p124790 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!

Wanted to theorycraft a custom class: the enhancer.

 **What is that?**
An enhancer is a cloth-wearing character who uses robotics enhancements, turrets and bombs to fight. It has a slight overlap with engineering.

![Image](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcTu2G4xQzVTK0Y8ys1Tn25txX66XNGz41ag-Q&s)

 **What races can be enhancers?**
Only the most mechanically gifted ones: goblins and gnomes. It also helps them a bit since they are underrepresented on the server.

 **What is the enhancer gameplay like?**
I'd say imagine a druid with some hunter, rogue and shaman characteristics. Just like druids, they have forms - in the sense of mechs they use. Each talent tree is specialised on one of these forms.
Except for travel form (at level 30, just like the druids) and (possibly) water form (imagine a scuba suit).

So enhancers jump between forms, just like druids. Unlike druids, they barely use magic, though. Resource type depends on form. The base form and the ranged form relies on mana but their affinity to spells is closest to hunters. In later expansions I would have given those forms focus instead.

There is the basic form. The random guy. As the character levels up, the following forms become available:
 * Shredder form - tank, using rage
 * Brawler form - melee DPS
 * Blaster form - a floating vehicle, granting additional ranged attacks
 * Travel form
 * Maybe water form

Unlike druids, enhancers cannot heal and their ranged damage is mostly physical. No enhancer really requires spell hit. Just like hunters, caster enhancers love hunter's mark.

 **How would the different builds look like?**

 **Tank build - Shredder form**

Similar to a bear with some key differences. They cannot rush but they have a ranged physical attack on CD with a threat modifier.
Unlike bears, the form gives a fixed amount of armor based on the character's level and ignores item armors, allowing the enhancer to focus fully on the stats.
They have a lower HP total but they are a bit better at defensive CDs.
They are similarly good at cleave damage.
They can't wear shields and are bad at dodging but have a decent parry.
They have some turrets specific to this form. Turrets are basically medium CD totems on a shared CD.
One of them works as a defensive CD, another one is for AoE threat. They can immensely help the shredder but only one can be used in every CD.

 **Melee build - Brawler form**

Just like the tank, their armor level is level-dependent only, they just have a different calculation for it.
Unlike cats, they are a strength-based energy-using build. They have no stealth.
In exchange they also have turrets on a shared CD. One of them grants attack power, another one reduces threat and a third one deals close-ranged damage to enemies.
Just like cats and rogues, they build up combo points, but unlike them, they have no access to bleeding (or poison). They can interrupt just as well as rogues and stun somewhat worse than them.
Many turrets' effectiveness depends on the number of combo points used up when creating them - it is only true for the Brawler's turrets since only this build uses energy and combo points.
Because of this, they often use up combo points for better utility instead of using them up for a finisher.
Just like the ranged DPS, they have an underlying bomb theme - sometimes they throw one at the enemy.

 **Blaster form - Ranged DPS**
[Just like the tank, their armor level is level-dependent only, they just have a different calculation for it.]
They are most similar to hunters, heavily relying on ranged physical attacks. Most of those come from the blaster which is a floating vehicle but some is being performed via their bow/gun just like in the case of MM hunters.
They also have a set of specific turrets which have the same rules - shared CD, so only one can be in use at a time.
They excel at build-up damage (also leaving DoTs behind most of the time) making them an excellent choice for progression raids. However, they are not very good if the targets die too fast.

 **Some important stuffs they can do:**
 * Just like paladins, they have some auras - they are just called differently. Some examples:
 - Brawler aura increases energy regeneration of each party member by a little.
 - Steel aura prevents bleeding and halves poison and disease duration for each party member.
 - Gunslinger aura gives haste to non-magical ranged attacks.

 **Some notable turrets are:**
 - Threat reduction turret for 1 min which reduces party members threat by 6% for each combo point.
 - Damage reduction turret reducing damage taken by party members by 20% for 5 seconds.
 - Second engine turret giving the party members mobility boost in its area for 30 seconds.

Just like the druids they get an upgraded version of their forms (for each form here!) at level 40.
The upgraded form always have more armour and usually automatically gives an upgrade to the turrets.
At level 40 the enhancers can start to equip leather items (but still, they only care about the stats, not about the armor).
Enhancers have a bow/gun slot at the class-specific item slot.

Enhancers cannot use shields, polearms and 2H weapons.
Enhancers can dual-wield but mostly use their off-hand weapon as a stat-stick.
Unlike the druids, the forms can learn their respective weapon skill (up to 5%) via talents.

An enhancer without using any forms mostly works as a petless hunter. He has some basic ranged attacks, including a slow, a melee hit and a high CD stun to get out of problem situations.

Hey!

Hmm... I agree that balance can be off, this is an initial design concept. But not really because of those examples.
Had some sudden ideas which would probably translate badly into the game:
 1. Making them cloth-wearers until lvl 40 would mean we either introduce a bunch of +str, +stam and +agi cloth items or they should somehow gear up with +int and +spirit. That was an oversight from my end.
 2. Giving the forms a fixed armor based on the level makes raid-scaling impossible.
 3. By retrospect I would have made the melee DPS form a rage user, imitating a fury warrior, since we have the rogue-imitator via the cat form druids already.

These are things I would probably change during a playtesting.
For the things you mentioned:
It is true that I gave them auras which is a strong thing. But besides auras paladins provide blessings which are usually super broken.
Enhancers would provide turrets. The thing with a shared CD is that you can only have 1 or 0 turret down. Compared to shaman totems, this is a way weaker setup. F.ex. let's take this:
'- Damage reduction turret reducing damage taken by party members by 20% for 5 seconds.'
If the shared CD is 1 minute, it means that for extremely short-living turrets, you get like 5 sec turret support and 55 sec nothing.
Shapeshifting is indeed a strong thing but the forms' main strength would come via the talents.
I think a ranged DPS enhancer could tank dungeons up to the lategame but for lategame dungeons and raids, the effective usage of a form requires a bunch of talent point investments.

___

In retrospect, tinkerer would have been a much cooler name.
In retrospect, giving them a mini robot which resembles a warlock's imp would have been interesting.

______

Thx all for the kind words!
The current design is by no means a final form, anyone with a good idea is free to build upon it.
