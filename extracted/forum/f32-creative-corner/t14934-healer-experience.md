# Healer experience

## Post 101606 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2024-09-21T17:02:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=101606#p101606 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!

I want to write this article to those who consider rolling a healer but have limited experience with them.
Will focus on things like what they are unique at, what their weaknesses are etc.
Might slightly mention raidingand PVP but the main focus will be on dungeon runs during leveling.
Did not include DPS specs doing a dungeon as a healer, focusing only on healing specs.

Please note that I am not an expert, **this is only an opinion**. I played all 4 healer classes at TWOW but two of them are still leveling. This is supposed to be a general guideline for interested players, not an expert analysis.

 **Priest**
Priest was my smoothest leveling healer experience. Mana management is really good, healing toolkit is huge (compared to the others). I went for inner focus and meditation early and this allowed fast dungeon runs. Priest is a unique healer in a way that it can use wands - you can dump out some damage during dungeons from caster range when healing is not needed without using mana.
Fade can make sure that you're not being attacked - giving the aggro back to the tank is huge.
Shadow word pain, I mostly used it on bosses, but totally mana-dependent how you approach it.
Holy Nova is tempting but it's rarely a wise decision.

Positives:
 * Extremely large healing toolkit
 * Being able to range DPS without using mana
 * Having a 'curse' to put up
 * With a proper toolkit and spell downgrading, absolutely mana-efficient
 * Fade saves you usually

Negatives:
 * Buffs. They cost a bunch of mana and you're limited to stamina and spirit. You're not a 'universally good buffer' like a pala who can give everyone something useful.
 * Shields are very situational if tank is using rage. Needs a learning curve when to shield a rage-user.
 * HoT can give you high threat (but you have fade)
 * You can only use cloth, you have to avoid many cool items
 * Keeping up inner fire if you somehow get repeated hits can be annoying
 * Race selection really matters here (looking at you, fear ward)

All in all, was my favourite healer leveling experience. Always timed inner focus to stamina buffs or max rank prayer of healings to manage mana.
I suggest priest to those who want to have the healing experience to the fullest and who generally have a healing > utility mentality.
Also, MC-ing certain mobs is hilarious.

 **Shaman**
Shaman was a unique experience but leveling it wasn't really smooth. When you level a shaman, your character is unplayable until you get a bunch of drinks somewhere. Unlike with priest, it is very hard to stay mana-efficient. Even if you downgrade properly, two things stop you: 1. you have no real talent to get mana back, 2. totems have to be more or less spammed and they are expensive. Totems also have to be set at the beginning of the combat for full effect, you usually have to break your full spirit mana regeneration earlier than the other healers.
It's not all negative though. Shaman is the class who has a totem for every bullshít you can imagine. Unlike at priests, I suggest shamans for the utility > healing mentality players.
Your melee utility is also huge, a mostly melee dungeon party becomes a monster with you. I also suggest picking the shaman if you level together with some friends and they are all melee.
Another interesting aspect of the class is interruption. Put rank 1 earth shock to an easy-to-access key and you become the most consequent interruptor at the run. Can be extremely valuable.
All in all, leveling a shaman healer is good if you're okay with more frequent drinking breaks and more mana pot usage in exchange of making melee partys monsters and having a very unique wide-heal which kinda does the healing for you.
You can also pick any healing items up to mail.
Personally, I enjoyed priest more for leveling. Shaman healing toolkit is basically a slow heal, a fast heal, a wide heal and a totem which is so bad at its job that we don't count it.

Positives:
 * A tool for almost any situation
 * Very good interrupting
 * A unique healing
 * Skyrocketing melee DPS
 * Ankh can save you guys from so many corpse runs

Negatives:
 * Very mana-hungry - goes OOM eariler than usual
 * Healing toolkit is kinda simplistic
 * People can have weird arguments about which totems you should use
 * Totem positioning is super important to keep the effect on the allies
 * You can DPS from caster range but only using expensive spells
 * Talent tree is kinda boring
 * Almost useless when you have to run a lot. No instant heals or shields, all you have is your NS to chease one heal if really needed
 * Later can be a really consumption-heavy healer to make sure it has all the mana to go on
 * No Atiesh-crew

 **Paladin**

I think my mostly negative opinion is my own problem here. I imagine paladins being an extremely fun PVP healer build with freedom, but I almost never PVP. I also have that inner conflict that I want them to be a melee ranged-healer (feels unique) and what in actuality they are is obviously different.
They are arguably the best buffers of the bunch.
Honestly, all 4 healer classes have a different philosophy of buffing:
 * Priests have two great buffs but they can be nearly useless on some targets.
 * Druids have a 'jack of all trades buff' which helps everyone but not as much as a specialized buff
 * Shamans have area-of-effect buffs which are mostly melee focused
 * Paladins have a way to personalize their buff. They choose which blessing to use on which character.

Honestly, I think that this latter is the most efficient way. Holy paladins, in my opinion are the manifestation of efficiency in any situation. Most effective philosophy of buffing, most mana-efficient healing. But efficiency comes with simplicity. The gameplay itself is really repetitive. You have 3+1 ways to heal, counting in lay on hands: fast heal, slow heal, a healing shock on CD. In practice you probably go full +healing and spam the extremely mana efficient flash of light.
That's what I can really tell here. They are extremely good at what they are doing but at the expense of a narrow gameplay. No way to wide-heal. Helping in with the DPS or adding some offensive utility is hard since your tools would provide all those utility from melee range.
Just like at the shaman, I suggest this build for players who are more like utility > healing. Both your auras and blessings are super-valuable. But at the healing end you'll most probably die of boredom eventually.
One more thing: BoP and BoF are extremely interesting. But BoF is only relevant at some dungeons, better suited for PVP. BoP can save some casters though. Using these at the right time gives some fun to the gameplay but during dungeons they don't really needed too often.
I'd argue that a warrior's best friend at PVE is shaman, at PVP is a pala. BoF is so cool, I just wish PVE would abuse the mechanic a bit more.

Positives:
 * Imo the best buffs (personalized buffing)
 * Powerful auras
 * Extremely mana-efficient single target healing
 * Can use healing items of every item type
 * Have some interesting things to do besides healing (but all requires melee range / judgement range)
 * Best friend of meme tanks because of salvation
 * Can help the tank a lot (retribution aura and if needed, salvation on DPS)

Negatives:
 * Boring gameplay
 * Imo the most interesting blessings are way too situational at dungeons
 * Buffing is a mess (at least it is cheap)
 * HPS can be good at raid-level but when entering raids, holy pala HPS is usually really low
 * No wide-healing
 * No Atiesh-crew

 **Druids**

Dru healers are weird. It feels like a build which has the wackiest of unique utilities (like innervate or battle rez) at the expense of some really basic things which they don't have (normal rez) or if they have the normal utility they meme it into oblivion (tranquility as a wide heal - I don't wanna compare it to prayer of healing or chain heal).
I first and foremost propose dru healer for those who like the flavor. Turning into animals, having an own druid crew teleport to Moonglade, all very unique.
Gameplaywise I felt them a bit lacking.
Will talk about the non-tree healer dru, I mostly used that for dungeons.
They have a single-target heal with speed dpeendent on the rank, a HoT, a heal combined with a HoT, a healing spell healing up based on HoT size and tranquility. They also have a meditation talent just like priest for better mana management. For leveling, I definitely found them more 'manageable' than resto shamans.
However, I had problem with threat. HoT generates quite a threat since every time it ticks, you get some threat besides the things you are otherwise doing. And druid toolkit is really HoT-based, having two HoTs and a heal which requires a HoT on the target. Unlike a priest, you don't have a fade-like ability either.
Having brez but no normal rez is a double-edged sword. If things don't go too bad, it's a powerful tool to save the party from a wipe. In less ideal comps though it just ensures that you corpse run a lot and people will hate you for bringing a dru.
Mana-problems for dungeons are basically solved the moment you learn innervate at level 40. You also have many tools to help the team's DPS from caster range. You can fairy fire, insect swarm and moonfire, just to mention the instant ones. You are the only healer also which can just turn into a tank in an emergency and ask a hybrid to spam-heal you to save the day.
I also like the concept of swiftmend. It really requires forward-thinking. To use it on full potential, you have to time it well.
I'd avoid trees on dungeons. I'm convinced that HoT spamming makes threat keeping more difficult and tree turns off your non-HoT-relevant heals. However at raids, trees are excellent 1-ofs at any group for their healing aura.

Positives:
 * Second to priest in healing toolkit size
 * Many debuffs or instant spells to help team DPS
 * Can offtank if needed
 * Some extremely unique utility
 * Can thorn the tank for more threat
 * Can wear leather and cloth, more item variety than the one the priests have

Negatives:
 * Half of the healing toolkit is skewed towards HoT
 * No normal rez
 * Feels like healing output is limited
 * At raids people rarely want more than 1-2 trees
 * No real tool if you pull threat from all the HoTs
 * Buffs are good for everyone but not excellent for anyone

How much fun I had with them when leveling: priest > druid/shaman > pala
How good I think they are for leveling: priest/pala > shaman > druid
