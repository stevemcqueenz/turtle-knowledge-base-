# Meme-tanking dungeons

## Post 100795 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2024-09-06T13:38:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=100795#p100795 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!

I wanna write an article about possible (but not optimal) dungeon tanking builds.
Target audience contains of raiding people who sometimes wants to have unusual fun in dungeons.
Will mention some consumables and engineering items here and there to help builds in their lacking aspect of tanking but only to a limited degree. I believe that a Naxx-geared mage with flask of the titans, some greater stoneshield potions and some target dummies and sappers could tank Scholo. But this only proves that an irrealistic spending at consumes and engineering items itself makes everyone capable of dungeon-tanking. The article is not about this - I will propose some consumables or engineering items here and there but spending hundreds of golds to maintank Scholo is not fun for anyone.

Will exclude warriors, druids and paladins because they are perfectly valid tanks.
Will also exclude mages and priests because I think they would need to rely too much on consumes and engineering items, making this less of a fun and more of a challenge.

Before starting, some core ideas:
 1. Dungeons were designed for leveling gear. Let's say you're a shaman and you only wear mail. Since raids offer much higher quality items, you can easily reach or exceed the 'designed defensive stats' just by SRing some items from raids.
 2. Taunts and defensive CDs are useful but are not strictly necessary for dungeon tanking. As a tank you have to reach enough health and armor plus you gotta make sure that your single-target and AoE threat is not too low. This article is for situations when you do this with friends who accept this challenge - they can check the threatmeter and DPS carefully if needed. Many things I write here won't work with zugzug mentality people.

Let's see the classes!

 **Hunter pet tank**
Kinda self-explanatory. You go deep into BM and turn the pet into a half-decent tank. It is advised to relearn pet talents to mirror the resistances of the spells you will face in the dungeon. High armor/health (defensive) pets or pets knowing screech are a good option. Since your pet is not a fully functional tank, it is advised to CC a mob via freezing trap and ask for additional CCs if necessary. Also, scorpid sting on the main target reduces the damage your pet takes. Keep on healing the beast whenever needed.
If you have a bat, carrion bird, owl or wind serpent, it is recommended to use dive for cases when you have to get to a target really quickly (f.ex. saving a healer).
Bats, carrion birds and owls can learn screech. This helps a lot regarding damage reduction in case of elite mobs.

Giantstalker (T1) set helps a lot here, specially until 5 set bonus.
Dragonstalker gives more pet bonus but Gianstalker also gives a mend pet bonus.
Cryptstalker 4 set bonus also helps (for plus attack power)
Breastplate of Beast Mastery is huge for this build.

In theory there are certain buffs you can put to your pet:
[https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t ... ets/655791](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/what-buffs-can-you-give-pets/655791)

 **Hunter tank (survival tree)**
You have a lot of agi, a bunch of dodge, too. One of the best defensive CDs via deterrence and 2 melee abilities which get procced if you dodge or parry.
The main concept behind this build is pack size reduction. You can set up a freezing trap and pull via Wyvern sting. You can also send out your pet to OT 1 mob. All you have to care about is the rest of the mobs. Wyvern sting fizzles out soon, though. You can really reduce the size of the fighting pack but you have to act quick to kill the targets before a CC ends.
If the healer overaggroes, you can send your pet to him ang growl.
Go with a slow mainhand for bigger raptor strike and a very quick offhand to keep triggering the haste bonus from improved predator aspects. Use aspect of the wolf by default and switch to monkey 'as a defensive CD'.
Your threat won't be that high but raptor strike crits and melee haste will help.
For bigger packs, you have the alternative option of aoe fire trapping the mobs which you pull via multishot and managing your pet to keep aggro on 1-2 mobs which are otherwise not being attacked by the DPS.
If you feel that your armor is too low, get the Hakkar trinket (the armor+defense one) and use it almost on CD.
Gear more towards stamina than usual.
Have a glove enchanted for 2% threat and go for the Kara dodge enchants for head and leg. Unassuming trinkets like the Kara10 blue one or even the Hateforge blue one can help a lot at surviving.
If you feel that you can't reliably 'reduce the size of the fighting pack' with CC, keep some oils of immolation or sapper in your bag (if you're an eng) but only as emergency. No need to spam them.
If you lose aggro on a mob with kinda low health, just wingclip it. It will slowly crawl towards the casters and just die before getting there.
One more thing: DoTs can help keeping a decent aggro on a mob. You can do something like this:
Aspect of the Hawk -> Wyvern Sting -> mobs come -> Serpent Sting on secondary target -> Aspect of the Wolf
You can also use Scorpid Sting on bosses for damage reduction.

 **Warlock tank**

There are two talents which enable dungeon-tanking as a warlock. First one is intensity in the destruction tree which gives you a 70% spell interruption resist for fire spells. A paladin in the group is a must-have for the warlock tank but it results in a full interruption resist, making searing pain spammable.
The other talent is soul link at the bottom of the demonology tree. It makes the warlock surprisingly bulky but it also requires the demon to stay alive for it to work.
Play this build with a voidie (large health pool) and keep it in passive around the healer. Voidie is chilling, it only has two roles. First, it gives you the soul link buff. Second, use it to taunt off a mob if the healer takes aggro. Otherwise don't use it, you don't want the voidie to die.
Key talents include demonic embrace, improved voidie, fel stamina (these two to effectively save the healer), demonic aegis, master demonologist (giving 10% physical damage reduction), intensity. Any point you can put to the searing pain crit chance also helps but it will be hard to do so.
Gameplan is to keep voidie passive around the healer and pull. When mobs come, quickly immolate the secondary target (you don't have insta corruption here). In case of 3+ mobs, hellfire. Yes, it further damages you and makes the healer miserable but in dungeons big packs die soon and you can focus on the smaller pack rotation. It consists of searing pain spam with tabbing, casting shadowburn as a finisher, death coiing as a quick CC and threat generator. In case of total emergency, sacrifice voidie for shield and consider aoe fearing if the room is clean enough.
Itemization is huge here because you need some armor. Clear Naxx trash. Get Necro-Knight's Garb. 422 armor on a cloth item is a treasure for you.
Consider Cryptstone Circlet from Kara Crypts, too.
Basically get as many stam, armor and spellpower as you can. Also consider the Hakkar trinket for armor gain. And be reasonable, don't approach dungeons with high fire rez inside.
Some tanking rings and necklaces also help. Just have enough defense before going hellfire on 5 elite mobs.
And make sure there's a pala in the group.
Also make the healer's life easier, keep up shadow absorption in places like Scholo.

 **Rogue tank**

Rogues have the single-target threat which they can turn into a cleave with blade flurry when really needed. From level 60 they also receive a poison which generates extra threat (agitating poison).
Another TWOW ability, flourish gives them a parry increaser-finisher.
A rogue tank's main strength is their big single-target (sometimes cleave) threat which is combined with very high dodge and parry chances and the evasion ability, while also being able to reliable incapacitate mobs.
Ghostly strike also helps - your defenses against melee are always very high, you are an evasion tank.
However, a rogue tank wears leather, has no shield and caster mobs can grief him really hard.
But these things are mostly manageable. Basically you rarely get hit but if you do, your HP drops really fast -> higher prio on stamina items + the hakkar trinket can give you the necessary push to survive melee.
For casters, make sure that you have a party member which can give you resist against the dungeon's main school of magic. Is it shadow? Make sure there's a priest or pala in the team. Nature? Get a shaman. Asking you to have different resist tank sets for dungeons would be too tryhard and is not the scope of this article. But getting a buddy from the correct class to give you 60 spell rez is doable and helps a lot.
An interesting item I could mention besides the Kara blue trinket and the Hateforge bluie trinket is Thick Silithid Chestguard from Kara20. Gives you everything you need plus adds some overall resistence to you. If you have some items like that then those 60 resistences you get from your friend at the dungeon, quickly become 80 resistences.
Another thing which helps (and you're an engineer and you have some spare coins) is getting the reflector trinkets. Resistence and also an emergency button to reflect spells if you're about to die from casters. You go to tank BRD? Switch in the flame deflector. Scholo? Shadow. Etc. Same can only help the others in the list of course.

So. You put up the agitationg poison and basically act as if you were a DPS. Bladeflurry to keep threat on several mobs. You might wanna just stealth to the middle of the new pack, sap one target, gouge another one and start the beatdown one by one.Try to keep up your haste and your added parry both if you can. If you're unsure which one to sap in the beginning, go for the caster. You hate casters. Keep that agitating poison always up. Have fun.

Almost forgot vanish. If you're about to die, you can vanish -> restealth -> health pot -> sap a target and take care of a smaller pack temporarily.

 **Shaman tank**

It's the least meme-y. But honestly, all you really need is some aoe threat and maybe some raid items to compensate for the lack of plate. Get a force reactive disk (if you're eng - but really, I don't wanna advise you to spam 100x oils of immolation at every Scholo instead) and get some + block chance items. Get some raid mail items for added stats and armor. You are basically ready. Earth shock is your taunt, it's mostly reliable at dungeons. In case of high nat rez, frost shock is your fairly inferior taunt. Rockbiter on, chain lightning pull, you know the drill.

 **Some 'ideal friends' to the meme party**

 **Top buddies**
 **Paladin**, specially holy. Salvation is a gamechanger for these builds and retri might do too much threat. Also, almost every aura is a gamechanger for you.
 **Priest**, specially smite priest or healer. They can fade, the give stamina buff, many heals and a shield. Very good pals. Avoid the shadow ones though, vampiric embrace is a clusterf*ck of threat.
 **Cat druid**. Can reduce own threat like rogues but won't ask for WF unlike rogues (for each of the above 'tanks', agi is better). Can switch to bear to OT anytime. Can innervate you if you use mana. Can brez. Usually lower DPS -> threat than a rogue.

 **Good buddies**
 **Shaman** - mostly for agi totem and resistences.
 **Freeze mage**. Can help a lot with sheep and freezing targets.
 **Hunters**. Traps are kinda useful but their main caveat is threat management. They can kinda make sure that the mob runs back to you.

Some consumes to consider:
 * Juju for haste and dodge -> as a threat button and as a survival button (kinda). Just for emergency.
 * Juju int if there is no mage around and you want more int.
 * Ungoro thorn effect if there is no druid around.
 * I usually advise a greater agility pot. Because mongoose exists, it's pretty cheap to get.
 * Can use * resistence potions but at dungeons I feel it's too tryhard.
 * If you're eng, 1-2 target dummies in the ready, just in case.
 * If you tank anything in Blackrock and have a priest friend, get that fire rez aura from the MC-d mob, it will last for 1 hour.
 * Zanza for a bunch of stamina

Good write up.

As a shaman you don't even need raid gear to tank dungeons as long as you take the survivability talents in the enhancement tree. It's quite viable on Turtle WoW. I've tanked anything at 60 just with dungeon blues. Some dungeons --like scholomance with all its casters -- are even easier than on my warrior.

Warlock really benefits from having a priest in the group: extra health, pre shield and renew on every pull are great tools. If you want to go full hellfire spam it helps to have additional off healers too. So for example a group consisting of the warlock, a druid, a priest, a shaman and a paladin will be able to keep the warlock alive easily. Works better in some places than in others of course; Stratholme undead side comes to mind.
