# Classes as tanks

## Post 113784 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2024-12-22T01:03:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=113784#p113784 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!

This is not a theorycrafting, there is no should in this article. I wanna imagine how each class would tank in the game if they had a tree for it, highlighting their potential strengths, weaknesses and unique aspects.
I don't seriously propose a mage tank. But wanna see how this goes.
Excluding warriors, paladins, druids and shamans since they have a tanking equivalent in the game. Shaman tanking is so-so but me presenting the same thing they have now and saying 'this + better water/earth shield, a def CD and better itemization' would just be boring.

 **Who remained?**
Rogues, hunters, warlocks, priests and mages.

 **The key ideas based on which I want to build up the tanking builds:**
Rogue: avoidance, high CC-specially on casters, passive threat from avoidance, high single target threat combined with some cleaves on CD.
Hunter: avoidance, character+pet tanking, soul link-effect, reducing the number of mobs they have to handle at a time.
Warlocks: character+pet tanking, soul link-effect, high aoe and single target threat, fear as CC, reliance on stones.
Priests: high aoe and single target threat, fear as CC, self-HoT and shield.
Mages: kite-tanks with tools to reduce or reflect incoming spell damage.

To make things fair and boring, each of these classes receive some form of taunt just like shaman and pala did.

 **Rogue**
Rogues are in a weird spot. Flavor-wise a guy sneaking in the shadows is the anti-thesis of a tank whose job is to make sure every enemy is focusing on him.
But still, it's a very interesting concept. A ninja jumping out of the thin air, stunning enemies and forcing the others to fight him to save his allies just sounds cool.

What stops rogues from tanking atm?
 * They die too easily.
 * Problem keeping up threat on several targets.
 * Threat poison comes at level 60.

First thing I'd do is to move up Blade Flurry talent to 2nd row and blade rush to 3rd row to make them more easily reachable. All rogues have the right to have some kind of cleave after all (no fan of knives though!).
After that I'd make the threat poison attainable instantly when you complete the rogue poison questline.
This new tanking tree, let's call it the Sentinel tree would have to give some survivability really quick to the rogue. But giving everyone a bunch of stamina and armor is the boring way.
I'd bring flourish earlier, so you can learn it by level 15-ish. After that would add an early talent for this 'Sentinel tree' which gives the rogue a stackable damage reduction buff after parries and a stackable healing received buff after dodges.
Let's say it increases the healing received by 6% after dodges and the damage reduction after parries by 6% for 15 sec, stackable 5 times.
This would give the rogue tanks a base survivability but they'd have to work for it by parrying and dodging. HoTs would be extra beneficial for these tanks.
Would make them possible to either range pull or to have a very unique form of pulling: walking in the middle of the enemy pack stealthed, starting with a stun opener on a caster, gouging someone else, and going all out on the others.
Later would give them an ability which doesn't reset their threath upon vanish. They could use vanish as a last resort, get some heals, and de-stealth, pulling the mobs back with the earlier threat amount they had.
Later would give them some extra damage reduction against spells and would gie them a talent which generates threat on nearby enemies after a parry or a dodge happened. Like when you wanna smack a fly but you just can't hit it and now you're overfocused on punching it down. This would give them a passive aoe-threat without them being able to actively use any tools to keep up aoe-threat. It would be hard for them to get the attention of many mobs but the more mobs are focused on them and miss them, the bigger this passive threat gets.
Would give them a talent which increases their stamina based on a fraction of their agility and would increase the armor they receive after each agility point (which is by default 2).

The end result is a tank with a very high avoidance against melee opponents which can generate unmatched threat as long as enough opponents focus on him. Can face problems though if many mobs are casters and if they cannot establish a threat lead on enough mobs on a large pack to make the passive threat generation keep going.
Their uniqueness lies in their special pulls, where they start the fight inside the enemy lines with some well-placed stuns and in their ability to vanish, patch up and reappear with the threat they once had.

_____________

 **Warlock**

Warlocks are also in an interesting spot, because they can more or less tank with their current setup.
Citing CC2:
'Wrathstone (Level 42): Increases the threat generated by your Fire spells by 40% and reduces self inflicted Fire damage from Hellfire spell by 30%.'

This means that they already have their own tanking 'stance'.
They lack a normal taunt (sorry voidie), but I promised a taunt for each of the classes for this article.
They also lack survivability. Do they though?
 * Demonic Embrace
 * Master Demonologist with voidie
 * Demonic Aegis
 * Soul Link

Warlocks can have a disgusting amount of stamina but they lack the armor. They have no shield and wear cloth.
But what is armor anyway?
Armor gives you a percentual physical damage reduction, usually calculated for mobs at your level. Depends on a dungeon but 40% reduction is okay-ish, 50% starts to be good and from 60% it is really good.
Not mentioning raids, those can be a bit different. But as we saw fury prots ruled the tanking world pre-patch only using shields at clutch moments. I'm convinced that maintained, high armor is not as necessary as people think, if you have a decent 'oh shít button'.

Why I'm bringing this up? Master Demonologist with voidie already gives a 10% reduction which is equivalent to the damage reduction pala, warrior and shaman tanks get if talented properly.
Next to that, you get a 25% damage transfer from yourself to your pet, which is a damage reduction as long as you can make sure that voidie stays alive. And these are not only physical damage reductions!
What I want to say is that damage reduction is already pretty good. The article will buff it of course since we imagine a tanking tree for a warlock, but all I'm saying is that it doesn't need as much tweaking as people might think.
Besides these, warlock tanks will need 3 more things:
 * Getting 0 spell pushback from talents alone.
 * Getting a way to tank places with high fire resistance (optional).
 * Better defensive CD than the voidie's sacrifice.

So - in my imagination, this build is using voidie all the time.
Voidie itself has three roles:
 1. Move around/tank a smaller target, maybe annoy a caster while you're tanking the main targets.
 2. Staying alive. The moment you lose him, you lose 25% 'damage reduction'. Like losing your shield.
 3. Carrying the buffed version of sacrifice, which works as your defensive CD.

 *Before I forget, warlocks should be able to learn parry just like shamans can in TWOW. I won't base talents on parry but it's 5% extra mitigation during damage rolls.*

Let's call warlock tanks Netherguards and the tree shall therefore be the Netherguard tree.
First I'd make Wrathstone available from mid level 10s so Netherguard warlocks can tank any dungeon within the proposed level range as they level up.
Would also snatch Demonic Embrace and Demonic Aegis from the Demonology tree and would give them an early talent which seriously buffs voidie's taunt (and base threat as long as you have a Wrathstone) while lowering the taunt's CD.
The goal is to create a waiting game. I don't want to spam the higher end of the tree with things like a strong defensive CD or extreme damage reduction to prevent other builds snapping some talents and become super toxic.

The concept of the Netherguard warlock is to have a shift in gameplay. To initially play his role heavily relying on the voidie, mostly pet tanking and only stepping in personally in case of an emergency, mostly being a secondary healer of your pet with health funnel. And as the character levels up, gearing options open up and more OP talents can be added to the talent tree, the warlock's tanking style would slowly switch from pet-tanking to proper tanking with a backup pet as an OT.

A bit later would snatch the Improved Stones talent, at least the Wrathstone part.
Lower in the table a full spell pushback prevention (as long as you use a Wrathstone) can be applied.
Then, somewhere during master demonologist and soul link we could add two interesting things:
 1. A new curse. Curse of vampirism, which heals your pet equal to a fraction of the damage dealt to you by the target. Good at pulls and making sure that your voidie stays alive. Voidie is a useful asset.
 2. Changing how Sacrifice works. Instead of absorbing an amount of damage it would blow up your voidie for big AoE damage and would double the soul link damage reduction for a time. Voidie dies. Sad.

There's also an option to create a talent which prevents resisting your spells if you have a Wrathstone but I think accepting that warlocks wouldn't be able to tank some instances properly is just better than introducing something like this.

As a caster tank they would have the option to sneak in auto-attacks between casts. Making the warlock's melee damage relevant would be hell (and gearing for hit would also be a pain), so what I'd propose is a talent which reduces the casting time of your next searing pain after a melee crit. Gearing for melee or crit in particular would still be bad but this would add complexity to the gameplay and if people use this complexity and time auto-attacks correctly, they can be rewarded with some instant searing pains every now and then.

Also, somewhere below the 21TP line I'd add them some extra mitigation. Probably a follow up talent of Daemonic Aegis which rewards the warlocks pretty decently with armor and also has some defense associated with it.

So you have a Netherguard Warlock, with a voidie at his side. He has many ways to range pull. Setting up a curse, aoe pull, shadowbolt, soul fire, you name it. They can also play prot pala and run to places and use their red consecration which hurts them a bit though.
During a multitarget fight (3+) they would usually use hellfire to keep threat on everyone. On smaller packs, tab, searing pain (timed auto-attack) etc would probably be enough.
To annoy casters, they can use death coil, curse of tongues or voidie to keep hitting them.
In case of a smaller emergency, death coil is the key to press. In case of bigger emergencies, voidie's Sacrifice or an aoe fear should be done.
Voidie has to be micromanaged all the time so it stays useful in a fight but it never has the chance to die. If he dies, chances are that you follow him very soon.
You also (might) have shadowburn for snap threat.

 *Also:*
'Spellstone (Level 52): Reduces threat generated by your spells by 10%.'

This could be a consumable which you can give to any caster, from earlier levels. No, you don't have a salvation, but you'd have some control over that caster guy who entered the dungeon totally overgeared and wants to zug.
Maybe even a cookie table for it at level 60.

Unless salvation + tranquil air totem + spellstone trivializes things too much.

This kind of tank would be really cool since they would be one of the two classes who'd tank micromanaging two characters.
Not only that, if you include spellweaving, this build could be micromanagement hell - both pets and the tank has to keep positioning while maintaining a tanking rotation while poking in melee hits.
Just like shaman tanks they can resurrect themselves if needed in the middle of a fight.
Also, they would be the tanks with the greatest access to different fears. Could lead to a very interesting gameplay.
Just remember that the only cool demon pet is the voidie.

 **Hunters**

Honestly, I made a detailed proposal for it earlier. A half-proposal. I'd like it in the game while understanding why people would consider it stupid specially in a Vanilla+ server:
[viewtopic.php?t=16550](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?t=16550)

My only addition is that I'd use the same leveling shift as with the warlock. Avoiding giving the tank tree called 'Warden' too much mitigation early so other builds can't just quickly buy into the tree and be OP in PVP.
Early hunter tanking would work mostly as pet tanking, having the hunter as a second healer with mend pet. For that playstyle talents like Imrpoved Growl could be useful at the top of the Warden tree.
This playstyle would slowly change into proper hunter tanking with the proposals mentioned in the earlier posts made available a bit later.

 **Priests**

 *Note: I will use exact numbers with high confidence here. It's not because I know that the numbers I propose are correct. It's to paint a generic picture of something's power level. Numbers can be tweaked in a way that the result shall stay fair.*

Well, priests now have aoe threat, a spell with a threat modifier and they have their extra survivability with inner fire, bubble and self-hot.
Aoe fear can also be regarded as a very risky form of a defensive CD.

For priests I don't want to add a passive threat modifier. No wrath stone, no extra threat from aspect of the monkey.
Instead of that I would give them an extra percentual threat based on the percentage of the missing HP they had when casting a spell or when they were hitting the enemy.
To get to the extra 30% threat, they need to cast things at 70% health. With full health they probably won't keep threat and with minimal health noone can take the threat away from them.
Would also give them parry, just like to warlocks.
So we have a cloth user with no shield item and we want to incentivize tanking with missing HP. How to make this safe enough?

 * At the top of the three, next to the threat talent, there would be another one which reduces your healing output on others by 40% but makes each heal landing on you 40% stronger, including yours).
 * They'd later get an improved inner fire with armour, SP, and def stats. Armor would scale really nicely from stam, making the priest armor' comparabe to a mail user with a weak shield if inner fire is active (initially), scaling up respectively with gear.
 * Another talent would make the shields a bit stronger and they would blow up if they get destroyed by damage, causing extra threat on each enemy within the explosion's radius.
 * For later game they'd receive shadow form's tanking alternative, 'monk form'. In my eyes, the tank priest is not a shadow priest. If anything, it is a disc one.

Since TWOW will probably never incorporate monks in the game, I think we can use the expression here.
Monk form disables your access to spells with shadow damage (while still enabling aoe fear or mc) and also restricts your healing only to yourself. You can't heal other anymore in this form. In exchange you get a dodge chance 5% of your stamina and each missed hit will put a HoT onto you which stacks up to 3 times.
To avoid just jumping in and out of the form, entering monk form has a CD.

So how would priest tanking look like?
You are super fragile by default. Always keeping inner fire up when possible, just like a HoT.
You'd probably ask the healer to spam fast and cheap heals because of your healing modifier and low armor.
You'd use power word: shield at key moments - not to die or to solidify yourself around an HP level where your threat is good but you're not in danger.
To help this out, you'd get a talent which casts a twice as strong power word: shield on yourself when reaching 50%, 25% or 10% HP. Ignores 'cannot be shielded' debuff on you and doesn't cause such a debuff itself but could only trigger 1x every 10 sec.
Late game makes you somewhat of an avoidance tank which spams self HoTs passively, just by dodging.
Aoe threat is maintained by holy nova and power word-shield procs.
Single-target rotation focuses mostly on smite (having 100% pushback prevention from a talent if your HP is below 75%) and the timing on smite. With as low HP as possible while staying alive.
For defensive CD you kind of have the aoe fear but I've also prepared something else - a big CD ability which gives 50% haste to anyone casting a healing spell directly targeting you.
Instead of you getting reduced damage like it usually goes with a defensive CD, you make sure that all of those buffed heals arrive on time.

Flavor-wise it is very unique. Most aspects of the tanking are easy compared to the others: no pet positioning, no 'stealth opener making sure half of the pack is stunned'. Easy aoe tanking, easy single target tanking.
And this is needed since you'd need a bunch of extra focus on your HP bar and the damage you are expected to take.
The beauty and uniqueness of the priest tank lies in the way how they have to manage their own health pool while being fragile. Do you need more threat? Does it worth it? Can you catch up to almost max health with the healer? Priest tanking would be all about setting up a bungee jumping rope so that you almost crush to the ground but you still stay alive.

As a last touch, I'd add the old ardent defender talent to them with a twist: damage reduction starts at 50% HP at 10% and goes up to 30% the lower your health gets.

I like the idea as far as fun consideration goes.
I think one big thing with this kind of idea is that it goes the direction of Guild Wars 2, where each class can do everything, but generally some classes are better suited to performing roles in particular situations. Unfortunately, this kind of thing is also awful to balance in a fun way; it leads to the homogenization of classes where the meta still eventually leads to one class tanking best despite all being able to do so and the same for dps and support roles. However, I think it is still a fun idea for exploration.

My thoughts:
The warlock *can* function as a tank via the common drain tank build. It's also a distinct way of tanking from the standard damage reduction route via armor path. As such, it's pretty cool. As with other classes, I think this should be a common theme, the tanking needs to be distinct from standard armored tanking to offer anything of particular interest.
Rogues: Dodge tanking. It's pretty cool in theory, but in practice leads to a lot of frustration since it's RNG based and leads to spiky damage, which is a pain to heal and is difficult to mitigate. However, I think it's nice when this tanking route is at least an option *even if rarely the ideal option*.
Shamans: The development has gone the already done direction of sword and board, but they do offer some interest as caster tanks. Unfortunately, this is in many ways the same idea as the already existing paladin. I think the team was going the right direction with the shared life mechanic, which means *everyone* is tanking. It's a cool mechanic that could be explored more.
Paladin: While they are already tanks, they offer the best option of being a healing based tank. That is, they draw agro through healing themselves *and* tanking at the same time, essentially performing double duty as a tank and healer. This is made possible since paladins are uniquely able to elimate spell pushback altogether between their talents and concentration aura.
Other classes: Gets a bit more difficult. Minion tanking via a hunter's pet or perhaps mages summoning temporary illusionary tanks (living armor?) to fill the role might work? Not sure of how priests would do it, but they were at one point planned to have a monk build via the discipline tree. Edi: Mages and hunters both kind of do kite tanking, which is a mechanic that is fairly unique to them and could be explored more. For example, there could be melee opponents that do massive damage, more damage than a melee tank could withstand, that could be kited between hunters and mages exchanging agro at range, thus keeping people safe. This is already a lot of what what happens with the slimes in Maraudon that do massive damage in melee range but are fine when fought at range.

I put this together kind of quickly but this is how I would consider class tanking in Vanilla+.

![Image](https://i.imgur.com/YZSvrRF.png)

 **S tier:** Cream of the crop, reliable to final tier raiding (T5?).
 -Prot warrior with a shield excels at all aspects of tanking.
 -Bear druid is a close second. Has the highest health pool of all tanks. Slightly less utility and CDs than prot warr (which is appropriate for hybrid class).
 **A tier:** Great tanks, reliable through majority of raid fights.
 -Prot paladin has excellent AoE and the best self heals.
 -Fury warriors are not far behind. They have superior threat to prot paladins and fulfil the fantasy of a berserker juggernaut tank.
 ****B tier:**** Good tanks. Can tank many raid fights with the right gearing, but not likely to MT end-game raids.
 -Enh shaman has the potential to be a solid tank, and is the best magic tank. (Speaking of, can we get more raid bosses with magic focused mechanics that increases demand for sham tanks?)
 **C tier:** Poor tanks. Not advised or intended for raid tanking at all. Can perform decent dungeon tanking.
 -Arms warr has the best utility within C tier
 -Ret pal has the best self-heals
 -Survival hunter has pets and traps, and Carve (but this ability is cringe and should be removed, replaced with a standard 2 target cleave at the most, so it is not outshining warriors)
 -Combat rogue is strong single target tank but lacks AoE, besides blade fury
 **D tier:** Meme tanks. Possible to tank dungeons but not at all optimal.
 -BM hunter basically just tanks with pet and can uses traps and some melee as needed
 -Demo lock is most viable due to strong pet and soul link ability. Aff and destro are the least viable but still have pet, and occasional unique usability such as Twins in AQ40

And technically any other classes could be meme tanks as well in certain circumstances...

In conclusion, I like the idea of flexibility while respecting core vanilla design and class fantasies.
