# How a shaman tank could look like

## Post 98538 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2024-07-04T20:33:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=98538#p98538 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!

As always, this is not a proposal. I mean, it's kind of a borderline case. I will create a shaman tank concept below which will take up a whole talent tree. If TWOW ever considers making a proper shaman tank and ever considers putting a 4th talent tree for classes, this becomes a suggestion. Until then, it's not.

About the shaman tanks. They obviously lack some core aspects of being a tank while they're also having some tools, which on paper sound very good but need a little push to work. For example shaman tank mana regen. Keeping up water shield while being hit all the time actually solves the problem by itself, the tool is there -> as long as shaman tanks can generate enough threat to give up on lightning shield. The idea is there, the execution needs a push.
Also for armor. A shaman talent currently gives +30% armor to shields. TWOW's intention is obvious: they do not want to give plate to shamans but they also want to give them a way to get comparable armor in mail. It's understandable, plate shaman is a flavor fail but a high enough armor is a tanking requirement. This talent is also a good 'tool' which can be used for further sketches.

 **What do shaman tanks have now?**
 * Extra threat from each melee hit
 * A 5 sec CD shock with a high threat modifier
 * Talents giving +30% armor on shield, +10% extra armor on items, 5% dodge + shield specialization
 * Stormstrike for threat spike
 * Bloodlust for a 20 sec spike when more threat is needed
 * The most underwhelming threat tool of all time (stoneclaw, best used with fire nova for minimal aoe threat)
 * An elemental shield which either gives survivability or threat or mana when being hit
 * Multi-target range pull to establish threat

 **What shaman tanks lack now?**
 * They need both melee hit and spell hit to work properly but that talent is in a very awkward place, kinda deep in resto tree
 * Earth shock is a nature spell, it can both miss and be resisted
 * Dreadful aoe threat
 * No defensive CD
 * Despite the defensive talents, they are still fragile
 * The problem of 1 elemental shield: they need all 3 effect but can only have 1 on them at a time
 * No actual taunt

 **What are the main things I'd add to them?**
 * Hit talent, indirectly -> Would move it to enhancement tree first row
 * Next level of earth shock which deals less damage but taunts (got from an AQ20 book)
 * Linking fire totem damage to the shaman and increasing their threat
 * Slightly buffing survivability
 * One defensive CD
 * Making stoneclaw useful
 * Since shield has a specific meaning for shaman tanks (it gives them the armor to tank despite not having plate), will give them a shield-based tanking utility for flavor (not mana recharge on blocks, that's unique for paladins)

 **How it would look like talent-wise?**

 1st row
 5tp - Moving shield specialization here from enh tree (+5% block chance, +25% blocked amount)
 5tp - Increasing defense by 10 (usual staple in a tank tree)

 2nd row
 5tp - Reduces the damage taken by 10% if you have rockbiter weapon active
 1tp - Links the threat of damaging totems to you and increases their threat by 20% (would also move ele's call of flame to first row so it becomes easy to get)
 1tp - Upgraded tranquil air totem would only give the threat reduction to other party members, not to you
 5tp - Increases your agility by 10%

 3nd row
 5tp - Moving ancestral guardian here (+10% armor +5% dodge)
 1tp - Upgraded stoneclaw totem. The totem now has a 1 min CD and gives a high amount of aoe threat for 2 seconds, after which it gets destroyed automatically.
 2tp - Decreases the chance that your target can resist your earth shock by 10% and increases the threat generated by earth shock by n%.
 2tp - Increasing stoneskin totem effectiveness by n% and grace of air by n% (stoneksin bonus scaling with your stamina, grace of air bonus with your agility )

 4th row
 3tp - Moving spirit shield here (+30% armor on shields)
 1tp - If you ankh within 3 seconds from dying, you keep your original threat on the targets
 5tp - Increasing the threat modifier of rockbiter weapon by n% of your agility

 5th row
 5tp - A successful block reduces the CD of your earth shock by 1 sec. Can only happen once every 2 sec
 2tp - Reduces the cost of your elemental shields by 20%.

 6th row
 1tp - If your health is below 20%, your next healing wave spell becomes instant. Can only activate once in every 5 minutes.

 7th row
 1tp - A 5 min CD talent which is active for 10 sec and targets you. Recharges 5% of your mana every sec and gives you +20% haste. Their version of bloodlust. Goal is to create threat spikes and help with mana problems in really long fights.
 1tp - A 1 min CD ability which gives +25% block chance and +100% amount blocked for 10 sec. The defensive CD.

This shaman tank would lose some weapon damage, stormstrike and 10 sec of bloodlust.
In exchange it has a way to generate aoe threat via fire totems, have a high aoe threat on CD, better survivability and overall having a better tanking toolkit.

I think it would still be the most fragile tank, but the emergency healing wave would help them a lot, as well as being able to resurrect while keeping their threat, which is unique.
They would look like a bit like old rogues with high dodge (while not approaching any caps). Their tanking style would be based on dodges, blocks and emergency heals.

Your post is overall well thought out and nuanced, good job.   turtle_in_love
I pretty much support all of what you wrote.

I'll simply add my own experience from tanking most dungeons  from 1-60 as a shaman. Initially I did it because of the ubiquitous tank shortage but over time I actually found shaman tanking fun and viable. It certainly plays different from a warrior/paladin/druid and that's part of the appeal.

Some players were sceptical at first and in rare cases people even and up and left right away when they saw a shaman tank.    mad_turtle  . But overall others were supportive and loved the idea of a shaman tank. Many simple didn't know about the changes in Turtle WoW to shaman tanking and had to see it in action first.

As a dungeon tank shaman is certainly viable and in some places it's actually better than the others. For instance in Scholomance there's a plethora of spell casters and earth shock (which interrupts spell casting) being part of your core rotation every 5-6 seconds wrecks them hard.

In the following I'll outline the problems I've encountered as a dungeon tank:

 **enemies with spell immunity**
Enemies that are immune or highly resistant to your nature damage threat kit (lightning bolt, chain lightning, earth shock) pose a substantial problem. Shamans tanks also suffer heavily from being silenced (as do paladins). The dinosaur packs in Maraudon for instance are a real pain. In either situation you certainly need to advise the group and devise an alternate plan for these depending on the group composition.

 **mana dependency**
The overall most frustrating issue with shaman tanking (before 60) was the mana dependency. There's the obvious problem of running out of mana mid fight but also the long term sustain. I've had so many runs were the entire group was fully ready but waiting for me to drink and replenish mana before the next group. Remember that in addition to your tank rotation you also need to plant down totems before (and often during) fights.

You see warriors and bear druids have the principal advantage that getting hit (aka tanking) rewards them with their primary resource (rage). Paladins get a similar boon via the the shield specialization talent which restores a portion of their mana on blocking. With shamans it's kind of the opposite: you start with a full resource bar and have little ways to recover your resource over the cause of a fight. At 60 that finally changes with water shield but the journey there is long and arduous. There's also a must-get totem from the Black Morass dungeon (Totem of the Calming River) which further increases the efficiency of water shield. If there's a single quality of life change that I could advocate for then it is to make water shield available much, much earlier, perhaps from level 20 upwards.

 **gearing**
A third issue is that there isn't a lot of gear for shaman tanks, not compared to what the other classes get anyways. Turtle has introduced some new items aimed at shaman tanking which is much appreciated and should be be continued. For starters all the shaman sets and quest rewards largely focus on either healing or damage dealing. I have the full dungeon set 2 on my shaman (The Five Thunders). It's an amazing set for elementalists and also healers but part of me wishes the quest line also gave you an additional set focussed on tanking. Ironically my Five Thunders set also has higher armor values than most of the dungeon blues I use for tanking (disregarding any armour gained from agility).

The big problem with gearing a shaman tank is that you need a wide gamut of stats and attributes. In addition to the classical tanking stats (stamina, armor, defense, block) you also want spell casting stats: intelligence for a bigger mana pool, spell hit chance for your pseudo taunt Earth shock to not miss but also spell damage and mana regeneration. The classical enhancer stats hit chance, strength and crit/agility provide more threat via auto-attacks and Stormstrike. Finally agilty // crit chance also provides mana efficiency via the Elemental Focus talent. Uff, now that's a lot of stats that you want all at once. The stat budget allocation method that WoW items typically uses doesn't mesh well with this requirement, a problem from which enhancement shamans also suffer. In essence in the current iteration good shaman tank items need to be deliberately designed to go over the budget. With a clever combination such items need not necessarily be overpowered in PvP though. Note for instance how the +5 defense on the Time Looped Headguard from Black Morass (<https://database.turtlecraft.gg/?item=61053>) isn't of much use against other players.

 **lack of taunt**
It's simple: shaman tanks lack a hard taunt. There exist encounters designed around the idea that enemies drop all threat and chase and chase a different target. The boss fight Doctor Theolen Krastinov (the Butcher) from Scholomance is an example. Earth shock and Rockbiter Weapon alone won't reliably to get back aggro in such a scenario. In dungeons you can generally warn your group of this ahead of time and come up with a contingency plan but in raids that just seems like a no-go. In general my strategy for trash mobs is to open up with a strong ranged pull and than never let go off the aggro. Of course occasionally things go wrong: someone pulls an extra group, your spells get resisted while the damage dealers field heavy crits, etc. In such situations a hard taunt would also be much, much appreciated.

 **defensive limits**
While generally viable as a dungeon tank, it's notable that you simply aren't as sturdy as a bear or a protadin. Shaman tanks don't have emergency defenses like Shield Wall or Berserk. At best Earth Shield provides a couple of hundred hit points extra. This became very notable for me in Black Morass where even basic enemies hit hard. I simply took too much unmitigated damage. Without the healer/group carrying me it didn't feel possible. I suspect Lower Karazhan, Zul Gurub, AQ20, etc will be a similar story if not worse. Which is why I've abstained from tanking raids so far, in addition to the whole lack of a hard taunt issue.

 **talents**
The changes Turtle WoW makes to the shaman talents are much appreciated and lead the way into shamans being viable tanks, but the situation could be even better. As a 60 dungeon tank I ultimately settled on these talents:
![Image](https://i.imgur.com/vcNt36C.png)

Each tree provides some benefit here:
Restoration tree grants access to 3% spell and physical hit via Nature's Guidance (you really don't want your earth shock to get resisted). Unfortunately we have to waste 5 points in the first row before we get to it. Totemic Focus reduces totem mana cost and so does Totemic Mastery indirectly because the increased range means you'll need to replant them less often in between groups. Besides that having 30 yard range on totems is obviously great quality of life.

Elemental Focus and Convection from the Elemental tree provide some direly needed mana sustain. Turtle's change to the mana reduction of Elemental Focus also being triggered from melee crits is a god sent as ideally you'll play with a very fast one handed dagger as a shaman tank (1.3-1.5 attacks per second). More melee crits = less mana expenditure on earth shock.

In the enhancement tree Shield Specialization, Ancestral Guardian and Spirit Shield provide survivability while Thundering Strikes, Flurry and Elemental Weapons give increased threat. Again note the vital synergy of physical crits lowering the cost of Earth Shock via Elemental Focus. Because survivability stats on gear take precedence this will be one of the main avenues to address the lack of a bigger mana pool. Stormstrike is nice to have but since you want the fastest weapon you can get your hands on it will do little damage by itself. If I can hold threat well enough I often skip it in my rotation to conserve mana. You could theoretically hot swap to a big 2-hander each time before using it and I sometimes do it but it's a big hassle and being caught taking strikes without a shield equipped notably hurts. Plus you need to constantly keep up Rockbiter on 2 weapons now.

The capstone talent Bloodlust unfortunately is a let down. 10% attack and casting speed simply isn't that good for such a long cooldown. What's more is that the shaman isn't even typically the best beneficiary. As a tank you don't hard cast spells beyond the initial pull and 10% more attack speed doesn't even equate to 10% more threat. And it doesn't even increase movement speed which made it so powerful in Warcraft 3. Even if you were to pick this you would be better off casting it on a fury warrior or a mage. At minimum Bloodlust should affect both the shaman and the chosen target.

 **lack of multi target threat**
This is another area where shamans tanks simply lag behind. You can establish good initial threat when pulling a group with lightning bolt + chain lightning + earth shock but after that you're down to rotating earth shocks and auto attacks between the different targets. For swarms of enemies you simply have no tools at all. A talent that redirects the threat of fire totems to the shaman, as the original poster suggested, is very sensible. This could also finally give Flametongue Totem a place in the shaman's arsenal which hitherto has always been overshadowed by Windfury Totem. In addition I propose a new dedicated tool, for example a *Magma Shield* (akin to to Earth/Water/Lightning Shield). Each charge of Magma Shield degrades the elemental resistances (frost, fire, nature) and causes additional threat to nearby enemies.  When the 3rd change is expended the magma shield erupts into a fiery explosion causing additional fire damage and threat to all nearby enemies.

 **quality of life**
Finally as a quality-of-life request: Please make the shaman weapon imbues last 15-30 minutes instead of a mere 5. It's really annoying to constantly having to rebuff and just one more thing that eats into your mana pool. If you ever forget to reapply Rockbiter you'll be punished harshly as you don't have a hard taunt to regain threat. I don't see the point of weapon imbues lasting so short.
