# Builds after class change

## Post 100183 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2024-07-23T11:14:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=100183#p100183 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!

These are my takes for the builds after class changes. Nothing official, writing about what I think would be optimal.
My main perspectives regarding class balance:
 * No class should be either S or D tier neither at PVE nor at PVP.
 * Each build should be able to show up something unique because of which you'd totally take one to Naxx, even if it is a 1 of.
 * Each build should have some awesome stuffs going for it while should also have some weaknesses.
 * The end result shouldn't be too strong (weak builds can be lifted up but noone should feel like a lvl 65 character).

 **Warriors**
I'd give arms a raid utility debuff, probably after critting on a target. Would also give them a bit of melee haste.
I'd mostly keep fury as it is.
For prot, would put shield wall into the prot talent tree (in place of improved shield wall). Meaning that for added survivability, you have to go deep into prot.
Would give prot a talent which increases the rage they receive (in non-raid scenarios rage generation is a problem for them). Would also increase the threat modifier of improved revenge.
As a general change, would give stun abilities a secondary effect. If the target cannot be buffed, it could give a stackable haste penalty (let's say stacking up until 3-5%). That would make stunner classes more useful at raiding and would also make concussion blow somewhat useful.

Arms: slight overall damage buff, good utility (1), becoming a possible 1 of at raids.
Fury: just like now (they are slightly nerfed with shield wall nerf and by the fact that most other builds are being buffed).
Fury prot: similar to now but survivability would take a hit; would still be a good tank but not the undoubted n1.
Deep prot: better rage generation, extreme survivability, struggles with big packs, decent threat (better than now), added utility.

 **Druids**
This is controversial but I'd rework leader of the pack, giving 20% stamina for the party if you are a bear and 20% agility if you are a cat. I like the current talent but I think that making the buff build-specific is good, would make cats more unique. Besides that would consider a moderate threat update on swipe and would decrease maul damage but increase the threat it does to keep mit leveled. I think it keeps the bear at a similar power-level. Losing the crit chance bonus is harsh but that stamina bonus can really help in the tank group.
At the same time cat's would 'get access to a grace of air totem'. 20% seems much but you need 400 agility for it to become an 80 agi buff. As a comparison, talented grace of air gives 88 by default.
Would add one more thing to cats. Imagine a 5 min CD enrage ability which visually makes them glow in red and become 2x bigger for 20 seconds, giving a damage spike.
Moonkins would receive an improved mana regen somewhere at balance talent tree. They wouléd also receive an improved insect swarm which reduced crit% done of the target. They'd get some (talented) DPS buff to hurricane. They would still stay a utility build but with more mana, more AoE damage and better utility. 1-2 of per raid.
Trees. Trees could learn a talent which enables out of combat rez. Also, trees would be allowed to stack a 2nd HoT on a target with the 2nd one being 30% as effective as it should be. As a flavor-oriented nerf, druids in tree form would receive more damage from fire spells (watch out at Magmadar).

Bear: even harder to kill but less damage done, slightly better with bigger packs.
Cat: a good offensive CD and a better than leader of the pack DPS buff once geared enough.
Moonkin: better with mana, some added utility, somewhat better at AoE.
Tree: better at healing, can rez, hates fire.

 **Paladins**
As a default step I made holy work as the other schools of magic, introducing holy resistence for characters and mobs. This however is a big nerf for paladins, balancing should make sure they end up as totally valid choices.
I'd add a talent deeper to prot which drastically reduces the target's chance to resist holy damage (they need it to keep aggro). Prot would also receive a damage reduction talent and a way to range pull. All paladins would receive added buff durations for the normal blessings (greater blessings got really controversial, I won't take a side there).
Might increase the mana they get back from blocks to make sure they don't struggle at really long fights (let's say 3% from 2%).
For retri, I'd add them a talent which amplifies the blessings on them. Salvation gives more threat reduction, kings give more stats, might gives more attack power etc. I think that this buff can offtake the reduced damage from holy attacks and could also help them with threat management. If retri stays underpowered after the holy damage changes, I'd consider giving them an offensive CD. For example something like this: 3 min CD, for 10 sec your opponent cannot resist holy damage and increases yout holy damage by n%.
For holy I'd add a talent which makes holy light's cast time smaller (and holy shock deal more healing) after you crit with flash of light to incentivize some kind of rotation.

Prot: less effective with holy-based threat, better at surviving, the ability to range-pull, more mana.
Retri: less holy damage unless using CD, more stats to compensate for DPS.
Holy: better rotation, indirect buff to non-flash of light healing spells.

 **Shamans**
Would nerf base WF and remove chain heal from the baseline. Good WF would come as an enh talent, chain heal would be the resto tree capstone. Would also remove purge rank2, only keeping rank1. In exchange shamans would receive a passive mana regen weapon enhance and elemental shields would be available from level 20.
Elemental would receive a spell penetration talent and elemental mastery would be replaced by a spell-damage totem as a capstone talent. They would be placed in caster groups, enabling them to use tranquil air.
Enhancement would get a weapon haste talent, WF totem and a reworked, 10 sec CD SS, making their next shock spell free. Bloodlust would become 3 min CD but would only give melee haste and would only be self-castable.
Elemental devastation would give spell crit% after a melee crit and vice versa. I think this should be enough for a valid enh tree.
If we wanna enable shaman tanking (if), they need more shield armor bonus from the talent, a way to link fire totem damage to the shaman and improved weapon enhancement talent increasing rockbiter TPS. Should be enough for dungeon tanking.
For restos I wouldn't change anything. Mana regen weapon enhancement and water/earth shield when leveling should be enough aside the chain heal they can get from level 40.

 **Hunters**
I'd mostly keep MM as it is. For BM I'd enable a playstyle where you can rely mostly on your pet for DPS even at raids. There is the puppeteer playstyle and pet-users should have a tree to play that way (many people love that playstyle). However, this can break PVP so I'd increase pet DPS with a capstone talent which at the same time either reduces your own damage greatly to balance things out.
Would also add a talent for certain defensive pets which generate aoe threat. A BM hunter could become a valid dungeon tank and a valid raid DPS (not a raid tank!) relying mostly on the beast for damage. Beast DPS would scale on the hunter's agility. Would also give a party buff which increases pet haste (including warlock demons).
I'd make survival a melee tree. Would change mongoose bite into an overpower-like ability (you won't be hit too often directly as a DPS in instances). Would also increase hunter strength AP from 1 to 2. Sounds like an overkill but honestly melee hunters rarely rely on ranged playstyle and ranged hunters rarely go to melee range, I think it's fine. They'd also receive a talent which raises pet damage after a raptor strike crit and receiving a damage buff after the pet crits. Would enable a cooperative melee playstyle. Might get in-combat traps if they need additional push to be justified for raids.
To explain this. If you've watched Naruto: Sasori would be the BM hunter, mostly relying on the 'puppets' to deal damage. Kiba would be the melee hunter, dealing damage by cooperating with his pet.

BM: Becoming a valid dungeon tank and pet-reliant raid DPS.
MM: Mostly as now. Maybe increasing trueshot aura numbers if they fall behind.
Survival: Becoming a valid melee class with high reliance on the pet.

 **Priests**
Holy would mostly stay as it is, except for better champion buffs.
Discipline would get deep-tree buffs for shield (also scaling based on spirit). Holy would be the reactive healer, disc would more likely be a preventive healer.
Shadow would receive an on CD spellcleave, similar to chain lightning. If mana problems arise too often, would add some mana regen to shadowform.
Smite priest is in a bad spot, specially with this holy damage change. Would give them a talent which reduces the enemies holy rez after being hit by smite, holy fire or holy nova (also helping paladins) and would make smite spellcleave if it crits. Could grant them a raid spot if the raid has many paladins.

Holy: Better champion buffs.
Discipline: Better shields.
Shadow: Spellcleave plus optionally a better mana regen.
Smite: Holy resistence utility, spellcleave.

 **Warlocks**
They are very hard to handle since all trees work like 'half-trees' and at raiding the question is 'which 2 half-trees you pick'. Would make the end of the trees more meaningful to give variety for warlocks.
Deep affliction would be able to put up up to 2 curses on a target and would receive a damage buff if targeting someone with a curse on them. They'd also receive a unique utility curse only available for deep affliction.
Deep demonology would be the BM hunter of warlocks. Would leave DS as it is so you can still get your default warlock builds but would considerably buff demon damage output at the bottom of the three (same as with BM hunters, in the expense of nerfing your DPS or health). At the bottom of the tree they'd also receive a party buff which gives added pet attack power for party members. BM hunters give the haste, demonology warlocks give the AP (quite a lot).
Destruction... There is currently a design fail with warlocks - they can't deal maintained non-shadow, magical damage. It is fine now, but if TWOW ever creates a 'shadow BWL', the whole class would feel more or less useless. I'd make destruction tree a tree focused on destruction spells (clever, I know). They'd have maintained shadow and fire damage (without the added threat). Would give them a deep-end talent which grants an on CD damage spike ability which reduces spell threat and increases destruction spell CD for 10 seconds (for the whole party). So DS-Ruin would arguably be better with sustained damage while destro would be better at damage spikes, also providing help for other casters in the party.

SMDSSMruinDSRuin - Unchanged
Affli: Better at curses, bigger damage against cursed targets.
Demonology: Puppeteer build with pet utility.
Destro: Fire+shadow damage build having a CD to reduce spell threat and to give destruction spell crit.

I'm mentally tired, don't wanna write ideas of low quality. Skipping mage and rogue for now.
