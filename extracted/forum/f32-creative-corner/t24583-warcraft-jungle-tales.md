# Warcraft: Jungle Tales

## Post 172956 by Acepox (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2026-03-29T01:29:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=172956#p172956 | page 1 | era: post-1.18.1 -->

Everyone has fond and not so fond memories of Stranglethorn Vale.

Or STVietnam, as some call it.

Whether it was the corpse run to Booty Bay, getting ganked by freshly-mounted 40s as you were hanging out with Nesingwary in your lowly 30s, or duking it out with pirates on the sandy beaches, this zone is truly unforgettable.

Yet, it is also somewhat constrained. There's one central path running down the zone, restrictive hills on both sides as soon as you're past Lake Nazferiti, itself the culmination of a river basin that should be as daunting as the Amazon yet remains rather humble, an underdeveloped exploration of Troll culture, and an entire mountain range to the East that remains unexplored, not to mention that Retail WoW doesn't even look at Lapidis and Gillijim!

Thus, inspired by reading and hearing about Diablo IV and Crimson Desert, I realized a whole game could be dedicated solely to this zone. Stylistically, a return to the nerdgasm-oriented vision that built Warcraft - as opposed to the "democratized" mess that modern WoW offers, with the grittiness, raw violence, and depth of the aforementioned RPGs, in particular the darkness of Diablo and the gameplay of Crimson Desert. Yet, maintaining breathing room for the light-hearted adventures and subtle comedy of America's third golden age (91-08) in which WoW was born. No DEI, this is peak white boy era.

 ---

The story begins with you as a young Forest Troll, in a tribe of fishermen and jungle stalkers. You learn the moves through sports, hunting, fishing, gathering, interacting with your kin and kith. A few scuffles with another troll tribe, but no all-out war.

But everything changed when the Fire nation attacked. And by that, I mean Stormwind's Kurzen expedition.

The moment is chaotic, violent. The jungle is set ablaze by the cannons of the Kurzen ships blasting away at your village. The human invaders are depicted as violent savages, in the style of the human in the MoP cinematic, but even more ruthless and bloodthirsty. The sky is darkened by ash and blackpowder clouds, only the flames brightening this or that scene of horror under the thick canopy. The Nazferiti tributary your village was built on turns into a river of blood. You do what you can to save your family. In the end, exhausted, scorched, and injured, but filled with anger, you attempt to take the jump on a passing squad of Kurzen troops, but you're held back by your best friend - and first love interest - who pulls you into the ferns, chastising you for this suicidal attempt.

You both escape, finding shelter under a rock, and passing out for a time.

 -

Until you're waken up by a roving band of noisy war-bound ogres. One of them picks you both up by the collar, and interrogates you. Pleased with his finding, he takes you to his leader. At their camp, it is revealed the Humans are after something in the Ogres' possession. They are thrilled to witness your hatred for the humans, and enlist your aid, you swift and agile young Trolls. Through guerilla warfare and coordination with the Ogres, you manage to whittle down the enemy's rations and escape routes, scuttling their ships and sabotaging their morale in various ways.

At some point, when the Humans have grown paranoid and desperate enough, the Ogre leader, Mai'Zoth, exploits their spiritual weakness by revealing what they were after - the Mind's Eye - and using it against them to drive them crazy. Their situation turns to havoc as they split, fight one another, and eventually retreat, defeated. You've won. But your friend has perished. Your tribe's lineage has ended.

The Ogres are satisfied. And have nothing else to do with poor, little, depressed you. So they put you in chains and sell you off to a nearby Venture Co operation, which sends you down to work the Silvershard mines. Rebellious, you keep getting into fights... and winning them. Sniffing out the opportunity, the Venture Co leader decides to throw you in a cage and send you off with the next shipment of cargo.

 -

You arrive in the first major city you've ever seen. Booty Bay. This lively but wretched hive of scum and villainy. All sorts of misfits move about, drinking, gambling, haggling, fighting, fishing, slave-trading. At the auction, you're sold off to pirates, always eager to find a lad or lass worth their rations' share in a fight.

A hazardous life of swashbucklin' is what you're forced to find meaning in, slowly garnering the respect of your shipmates, and a growing pouch of gold, until you're eventually rich enough to buy your freedom. At this point, the Jungle is your oyster. Whether you carry on with this life of piracy, seek fortunes in Booty Bay, attempt to reunite with your brethren by reaching out to other tribes, taking up contracts for the Venture Co, or develop trade routes for the Horde outpost at Grom'Gol... you're a free Troll.

Choices you've made have dictated how you've grown, starting from your gender, how you chose to avenge your family against the Kurzen, how you behaved during your days of slavery and piracy, what you've fed yourself and what hobbies - physical or intellectual - you've engaged in, shaping your appearance and your talents. Further specialization and soul-searching will determine how good of a headhunter, witch doctor, jungle-stalker, or swashbuckler you become.

 -

Subsequent plotlines would include a grand biannual troll festival at the Gurubashi arena, the Atal'ai plot to invoke a blood god, the emerging tensions between Venture Co exploitation and naturalist troll tribes, the Zanzil occult practices, the captaincy of your own pirate ship (à la AC:Blackflag), the suppression of the slave trade by the Horde, a renewed, hardened, and more jungle-adapted Alliance invasion of the region (as opposed to the square and unwieldy practices of the Kurzen expedition), the conflict between the Blackwater raiders and the Bloodsail buccaneers culminating in the battle of Booty Bay, the Orcish incursions from the East, etc.

 ---

In parallel, a human storyline would begin with the Kurzen expedition, starting at sea, when you, as main character, call out Land Ho! as the squadron approaches its final destination. You come out of the aforementioned battles bruised and beaten, steadying up a rebel camp in the hills, between a rock and a hard place. The rock: the unfamiliar and dangerous wilds of the jungle. The hard place: the undead horde squeezing out of Duskwood, forcing you to cut the bridge and litter the border gulch with forbidding traps.

You grow by adapting to the jungle along with your remaining fellow men of war, scrounging about in your new environment, your new home. Meeting up with Nesingwary's band, and developing the Alliance's understanding of the region. Over time, specializing into a ranger - diving deep into the Jungle's ways of life -, marine - honing his skills with the pirates and the Kul Tirans -, sorcerer - driven by curiosity, chasing forgotten jungle legends, taking part in troll rituals, listening to fortune tellers in the Bay - or chaplain - providing guidance to your fellow survivors, keeping their faith strong and leading them through these hardships.

Your goal: to free your old friends from the corruption that has taken hold of their minds, lay the groundwork for the Alliance's future domination of the Nazferiti, and learn to give up on a life you've left behind back in dreary ole Westfall.

 ---

Different environments would include the northern border, the western beaches, the Lapidis and Gillijim islands, the Silvershard Mines, the South Seas, Zul Gurub temple city, Booty Bay's alleys, docks and shanty town, the undersea, the eastern mountain range. Gameplay would be a 3rd person combat rpg akin to Crimson Desert, along with naval battles, bar fights, gladiator combat, magic and necromancy, flintlock and cannon action, and so on. Multiplayer integration would be similar to Dark Souls, except in cities where you'd see many folks. The style would be much more gritty, violent, and close-in than WoW, with greater environmental effects, such as during blazing battles or torrential downpours.
