I am mostly observing lore as I play the game, I dont read books about what presumably is happening somewhere without any players involved. I have a basic grasp of the story that lead up to "where we are rn" in the game.

So my question to the lore community.

How does faith work in warcraft?

I see the holy light cathedral in SW and northshire abbey a quick trip away, but nowhere else do I see a solemn church or chapel of the holy light in human lands maintained.

Duskwood has a chapel tucked away in a corner of the zone occupied by undead, thats about the only one in SW aligned lands.

in the North around Lordearon you see many chapels, repurposed into general places for gathering by the forsaken, like Tarrens Mill. Southshore, which is occupied by remnants of Lordearon doesnt.

Also the Dwarves and High Elves presumably also "believe" in the Holy Light yet I dont see any places of worship whatsoever in their lands. Why?

I gathered from ingame books that Lordearon was the original seat of the Holy Light church but the clergy moved to SW after the events of Warcraft 3.

And ofc theres the Scarlet Crusade and Argent Dawn, the first a human only group of zealots that presume every non human and suspect every human that isnt part of their group to be agents of the scourge. The latter an anti scourge group that seems to not care who they enlist as long as they are willing to fight the scourge of undead.

Is the faith just in a massive decline? Did the fall of Lordearon convince people that their belief was misplaced? If they dont belief in the Holy Light anymore why arent they searching for a different faith?

I am eager to learn or discuss about this. I think faith can be an amazing storytelling vehicle and makes a fantasy game much more immersive.

Some individuals could lost their faith after the Third War, but mostly humans are as faithful as they were before. Especially in Stormwind that harbored many refugees from Lordaeron, and that has its own branch of the Silver Hand.

You can see that every zone of the kingdom has at least one church (except for Redridge), but here's another thing: game scale doesn't reflect real scale of Azeroth. WoW paints only the most basic details with broad strokes, so no wonder that Goldshire or any other minor settlement in this game is represented by two houses and the inn. Game-scaling like this simply can't add a church or another detail to every settlement. It's sad but it's true for a 20-years old game.

Regarding dwarves and high elves, they adopted human faith, but they are not even close to humans in terms of religion. Besides, sometimes they adopted it in their own ways - for example, from lore books in Gilneas we know that dwarves of Gelnor don't build churches: instead they do their religious rituals in caves. And even high elves, with their agnostical society, have enough Light worshipers to build a chapel in Alah'Thalas - perhaps now, more than ever, their people need the hope that the Light brings.

Faith in the Holy Light is not just a religion. It is a real and tangible force that responds to belief. It is based on three core virtues: respect, tenacity, and compassion. When someone truly lives by those values, the Light answers. Paladins and priests do not just pray. They act. Their faith fuels miracles, shields, and healing because they walk the path of those virtues with conviction.

You are absolutely right about the geography. Lordaeron was once the true heart of the Church of the Holy Light. It had cathedrals, abbeys, and a deeply faithful population. But after the events of the Third War and the rise of the Scourge, much of that infrastructure was destroyed or lost. Many of the faithful were slain, scattered, or turned into undead. The remaining clergy fled south, and the center of faith shifted to Stormwind. That is why you see the great Cathedral there now, while the north lies in ruins or in Forsaken hands.

Duskwood’s chapel is a great example of this contrast. A lonely place tucked away in a cursed land. It shows the remnants of what was once a larger network of worship and spiritual guidance. In the south, only Stormwind and Northshire remain as active and well-kept places of worship.

As for the dwarves and high elves, their relationship with the Light is a bit different. Dwarves tend to revere their ancestors and the Titans more than they follow formal Light worship. Some of them do become paladins, but they do not build cathedrals. Their faith is more practical, less ritualistic. High elves and blood elves learned to wield the Light through study and discipline rather than devotion, especially after their magical society evolved. For them, it is less a church and more a school of power.

You also mentioned the Scarlet Crusade and the Argent Dawn. The Scarlet Crusade was born from the trauma of Lordaeron’s fall. They clung to the Light with fanatical desperation, convinced that only purity and extreme action could save humanity. The Argent Dawn, on the other hand, took a broader view. They welcomed anyone with the will to fight the undead, no matter their race or background. That contrast says a lot about how faith splintered after the war.

So is faith in decline? In some ways, yes. The fall of Lordaeron shattered many people’s trust in the Light. Others lost faith entirely or turned to vengeance, like the Scarlet Crusade. But many still believe. Faith did not die. It simply changed. It became more personal, more battle-tested, less tied to buildings and more tied to action.

And you are absolutely right. Faith is one of the best storytelling tools in Warcraft, also a great resource for lots of good Roleplay. (IE Us Scarlets). It shows us who people are when everything else falls away. It shapes heroes, drives villains, and adds emotional weight to every victory and loss. Whether you are a devout priest or a bitter ex paladin, your character’s relationship with the Light can be a powerful part of your roleplay. Hope my answer helps you out.

Game mechanics =/= lore. Sometimes you need to mentally fill the blanks. Remember, it's an MMO design and not a novel. You rarely see outhouses, but people still have bodily functions. Frequently, supplies and logistics make no sense whatsoever, but in reality those thing would have to be taken care of. Canonically, Azeroth is as big as the Earth, but developers, whether they are the official ones or the fan devs making this fan project called TurtleWoW, simply cannot actually bring that scope into the game.

So, there is a lot that happens "off screen" and personally I believe that you essentially have to apply game scale to everything.

And then after that, there is the simple fact that the real canon game is a business primarily so they aren't writing it first and foremost for artistic passion or for realism, but for a completely unhinged and unmoored profit motive.
