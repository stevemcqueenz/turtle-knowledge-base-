The base story implemented in classic lvl 60 version is actually a relatively more "rogue-like" progressive design. No matter when we create a new character in game, the story begins from "Defias conspiracy", "New World Tree's corruption", "Hidden enemy in Orgrimmar" and so forth. Adding some roles in later story in the same version of game to some permanent scenes could be a choice, just like the Greymane prince in Ravenshire hall, who has been rescued from a mine and acts as a permanent NPC. On the other hand, even if we have finished the quest line of unmasking Onyxia in Stormwind City, the disguised Onyxia still stands there (possibly due to technical difficulty in early game designing).

If scene's set becomes so progressive involving phases, there will be a variety of content requiring modification. For example, where should Bolvar go and initialise the quest line of eleminating Nathanos after Varian returns as the king? Is it possible for Stoutmantle's militia force to secure more of Westfall after we finish quests culling and taking back mines in Westfall? You should know that Varian's return in lore is simultaneous with the story line of fighting Defias on time basis. Does Abercrombie, who sets up Stitches, stay at his hut when Stitches is destroyed by local residents?

Is it worthy risking going too far?

## Post 116435 by Allwynd01 (Bug Report Enthusiast) — 2025-01-12T16:43:00Z

From the poster above me, I drew parallels between older MMORPGs like WoW and newer ones like ESO where things change based on when you are on a quest. I remember in ESO there was an NPC questgiver who in one zone is just a questgiver, but later, in another zone he gets assassinated or something like that, I don't remember the details. (which is an issue in current ESO when they made the game scale to your level so you can do all zones in whatever order you want, but it messes up the story for the reasons above, but that's another topic)

And I think as it is, Vanilla WoW, and Turtle WoW in particular uses the old formula where due to the technical limitations, you have to use your imagination more. I think when the game migrates to UE5, there will be more freedom with just about everything. They even showed screenshots of Elwynn Forest covered in snow, which appears to be in real time as it covers even mobs and player characters, so they can use this to take the game to a whole new level, not just graphic-wise or performance-wise.

But I don't think they will go as far as to make quests and use the phasing technology so NPCs appear in different places or in different way (or not at all) based on where you are in the story. I think the immersiveness it brings is far outweighed by how redundant it feels to me, because instead of focusing on creating a living, breathing world, it tries to emulate a single player story-focused RPG rather than what a REAL MMORPG is all about. And it actually breaks the immersion when you see a player "talking" to the air, because you know there was an NPC there, but now it's gone for you, because you completed the quest.

So in that regard, I think the approach of MMORPGs like Vanilla WoW is better, because the world remains the same for everybody regardless of where you are in the story. So I think they should keep that philosophy and not try to change it. Rather, focus on how they can use UE5 to make Turtle WoW even more fun and enjoyable.

I think, from a technical standpoint, the UE5 migration will lift any technical limitations of the current engine so that the developers can probably add entirely new continents or merge Kalimdor and The Eastern Kingdoms into one big landmass and also add things like The Maelstrom and everything else in between... in due time.

Personally, I really long to see all the unused areas on both continents completely populated with zones so there is no terrain left that lacks texture and the player is obstructed with mountains so as to not even see it on the minimap. I wish for a more easier to traverse Azeroth, where there are more entrances between different zones, which is the only thing I dislike about Vanilla WoW - all the zones are separated by mountains and there is one or two choke points at best where you move from one zone to the next, because it makes it look less realistic, more like some "world of zones" rather than a REAL WORLD.

That's also one thing that LOTRO did better - there are large open areas where one zone ends and another one begins, as well as some mountainous zones actually look like mountains - they have hills so steep, that in WoW you would think they are almost inaccessible and just part of the mountains used as "natural borders", but are in fact traversable, have flora, fauna, even quest objectives or NPCs, in comparison, the "so-called" Stonetalon Mountains in WoW barely even qualify as a mountain - more like a ravine within a mountain.
