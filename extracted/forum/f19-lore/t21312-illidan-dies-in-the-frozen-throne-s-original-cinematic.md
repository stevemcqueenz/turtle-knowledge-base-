# Illidan dies in The Frozen Throne's original cinematic.

## Post 147788 by Sleeplust (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2025-08-23T05:57:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=147788#p147788 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Illidan had been shaped into a stereotypical vengeful character with dual personality who seeks only power as an exile. As long as Turtle WoW develops with inclination to the lore in old Warcraft games, we should agree that the elements and sets in Warcraft III outweigh those from later games, such as retcons in Vanilla WoW and later expansions.

To be exact on the topic, Illidan is the one who does not gain the upperhand in the duel between him and Arthas. His troops (naga and blood elf coalition) are defeated by undead forces mainly led by Arthas and Anub'arak (under the player's control) and his lines exhibit unwillingness and despair due to failure. During the combat, unlike the description in a later novel Blizzard published where Illidan fights with elegance and confidence, Arthas, in the game's cinematic, nearly overpowers Illidan from the beginning while Illidan only fights back before he is forced to leap back, evading a heavy horizontal slash from Arthas, with defence pried open, hence being mortally hacked bottom-up into his torso by Frostmourn.

Does Illidan die at this point? My deduction is yes. Illidan succumbs to the wound with a big pool of purple blood splashed out and stops all his body movement lying in the snow. Arthas walks peacefully away, showing no urgency of approaching the Throne. As what I have posted, it can't be more explicit that Illidan dies as a charismatic villain as he was designed in the original game he appears in. It can be taken as a kind of respect of not depicting ghouls devouring Illidan's corpse.

To assume Illidan survived that duel in Icecrown Glacier is more risky than making Kael a traitor to high elves. Honouring the original game, let's allow Illidan to pass away. Even if someday we do want him back, just come up with some other method as we should never retcon what's explicitly shown in Warcraft III.

It's already canon to WoW's original run. Illidan is alive in Outland and driven even further insane by his defeat, per Malfurion in the quest "[Waking Legends](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Waking_Legends)" (originating from the Nightmare Engulfed Object that drops from the Dragons of Nightmare world bosses Emeriss, Lethon, Taerar and Ysondre).

It has been this way ever since Patch 1.8.0 in October 2005, and Turtle WoW has not touched or modified any of Blizzard's original quests in such a way in the past. Thus it is highly unlikely and highly improbable that Illidan's survival as stated by Malfurion *ever* be overwritten by Turtle WoW's team, as their priority is working on content going forward in the narrative (currently as per the roadmap: Kalimdor, Quel'Thalas and Grim Batol).

Edit: The chances of Illidan’s survival being undone are even slimmer than I originally thought when writing, as I just remembered that the primary blood elven forces in the Thalassian Highlands are explicitly identified as being of the Illidari and that Kael’thas is explicitly back in Outland when he’s contacted by Rommath, while still searching for the cure to his people’s hunger. Thus the narrative team has evidently already moved forward with the idea that Illidan still lives and that the blood elves are still a part of the Illidari.

Since there is some leeway given about the chance of his survival (even if somewhat minor looking), it is in my opinion that rather than asking "Did Illidan die in his duel with Arthas?", I think the question to ask is "Should Illidan die in his duel with Arthas?".

Illidan I feel as a rather villainous antihero willing to commit any action for more power in service to his goals makes him too interesting as a character to be removed from the story just yet.

In regards as to just letting him be dead first then resurrecting him whenever we feel like it, that comes with the risk of cheapening the stakes of death in the story. I remember reading some discussions about a manga series that one of the biggest issues with it is that no one "actually dies". Because the author kept having his characters return to life for whatever reason, acts of sacrifice and tragedy eventually meant nothing to the readers as they all know that some issues down the line, they will come back.

To avoid this kind of issue, death must be treated with some permanence and the question of whether Illidan lives or dies largely depends if he is still needed alive for any story to progress on well or if his death would set up for better future stories.

If Illidan needs to live, then rather than a retcon we can just simply add 'context' to the outcome. Here's a potential example:

"Arthas dealt a grievous blow to Illidan, a deep upwards slash across his torso. The injury sustained by Illidan is too much for him to continue fighting; he falls to ground rapidly losing consciousness as blood flows from his wound."

"Assessing the state of his oppenent, Arthas knows that regardless if Illidan lives or dies he is in no shape to continue opposing his ascent to the Frozen Throne. Satisfied with leaving the now-unthreatening demon-hunter for dead, whether be to the cold or blood loss while stewing in his failure to stop him, Arthas turns away from Illidan as he heads beyond the gates."

"Although time slowly pass Illidan has yet to die. While the blow dealt to him would have killed a mortal man by now, Illidan is far from mortal having since consuming the Skull of Gul'dan to become something more. Of course without aid, even he would eventually succumb with each small breath taken becoming fainter and fainter."

"As luck would have it, the surviving forces led by Vashj and Kael'thas eventually came to his aid in their search for their master. After stabilizing his wounds the Illidari forces make their hasty retreat back to Outlands, eager to escape the grasp of the newly-crowned Lich King and to prepare for the eventually wrath of Kil'Jaeden for having failed their mission."
