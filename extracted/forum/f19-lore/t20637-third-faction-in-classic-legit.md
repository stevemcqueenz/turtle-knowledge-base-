I always thought less rigid faction restrictions along a four-faction system would've been interesting. Each with an exclusive faction class.

Kalimdor = Tauren-Worgen-Furbolg-Nelf = Druid
Alliance = Human-Dwarf-Gnome-Helf = Paladin
Horde = Orc-Troll-Ogre-Goblin = Shaman
Forsaken = Undead-Sanlayn-Broken-Belf = Death Knight

Zones and Battlegrounds would typically focus on one or two factions where the other two queue/quest as mercenaries for either agonist factions. Never acting, of course, against one's own faction.

Eventually expanding to include Monk races, respectively:
Jinyu
Pandaren
Hozen
Mogu

As well as subraces like Highborne, Dark Irons, Mag'har, Withered.

Would allow for much more interesting diplomacy and inter-faction dynamics.

Needs to remain flexible to avoid dividing the playerbase too much.

> The Two Faction System works well for our purpose of Alliance vs Horde, I don't know why people keeps asking to changet this, I mean, it's a lot of work, new zones, new quests, change things in the actual game for adapt it to it...

 4 factions to 2 factions, casued the initial plot logic problem from WAR3 to WOW. Blizzard never tried to solve the problem, so the plot became more and more unreasonable in subsequent versions.

For example, the background story of the Forsaken is that they are villains, at least that's how vanilla wow defines them. They just use Horde to protect themselves, but players can't be villains. I guess Blizzard tried to design a plot before CTM where the Forsaken escaped the control of the Banshee Queen and became a true member of the Horde, but it didn't happen. Until now, modern wow still hasn't solved this problem.

In short, the 2-fation system is not a problem now, nor in the near future, but it will slowly become a problem as time goes on. However, the writers of Turtle WOW can also reasonably design the future plot direction with this issue in mind.
