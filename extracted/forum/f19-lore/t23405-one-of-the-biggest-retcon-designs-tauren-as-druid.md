# One of the biggest retcon designs - tauren as druid

## Post 163825 by Sleeplust (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2025-12-27T03:21:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=163825#p163825 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

This topic is not on which race(s) should be deemed the canonical druidism practitioners in the game's lore, but only on retcon changes of diversifying druid class compared with druids in Warcraft III.

Druid is a kind of units in Warcraft III that can be played with specific racial-gender inclination. All original units in that game with the title/name "druid" are night elf males, whereas all archers or sentinel units are females. Females also play roles in night elf society such as warden forces (disregarding Cenarius' offsprings and other creatures). In short, night elf culture is divided by gender, which Warcraft III explicitly shows. We can even assume that all mainstream night elf males (except infidel members like Illidan) hibernate while all females stay vigilant, guarding the burrows (hence a low reproduction rate). This is the original set.

Players can still find some clues in World of Warcraft, including the fact that all night elven druid class trainers are males and Malfurion as the cult spreader to other races.

I admit it is hopeless to shift back to the fundamentalist lore in our game with the huge player base of tauren, and even troll and worgen druids due to the prevalence of Blizzard's retcon games. The point is that Turtle WoW developers should have the lore more respected and prioritised, by promoting the original and historical lore.

Only Orcs can be Warlocks.
There are no female Orcs or Tauren or Trolls at all.
Only High Elves can be Priests.

A lot gets a bit weird if you take WC2/WC3 unit models as gospel. Some abstraction from the old RTS games and the pre-established lore is necessary to give people choices and options in an MMO. An RTS just works fundamentally differently, where you want stereotypical units that are recognizable at a glance.
The older stuff is world-building and now you get to play in a world where priests and warlocks exist, and one heals with the power of the Light and the other damages with demonic powers. And for a variety of reasons (balance, variety, diversity, customer appeal) the chose to open up those classes to other races as well and to allow players to freely pick the gender of their character as well.

It's a balancing act. You have to maintain the spirit of the lore while ensuring it plays well in an MMO setting. In general that is a thing that players and Turtle devs often get wrong. They want to tell grand and epic stories firmly rooted in the rich lore of the universe. But this is still a game. Classes, spells, instances have to work as a game, because if they're not fun and balanced, nobody will play them. Lore is important, but if push comes to shove, a sensible balance change should be able to override a (minor) lore concern.
