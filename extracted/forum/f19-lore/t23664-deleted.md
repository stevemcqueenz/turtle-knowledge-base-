A good reminder that these stories and worlds are evolving with every product in the series.

As for my pick: Night Elves existing is arguably a retcon.
Warcraft 2 only knew generic "Elves" but they were dwelling in woods and employed druidic magic. In the WC2 manual elves from Quel’thalas are described as archers, "sylvan woodsmen", "intimately bound to the wildlands of Lordaeron", even as "rugged". And the iconic Runestone at Caer Darrow is described as "an ancient monolith erected by the Elven Druids".

In WC3 and WoW lore all of these traits belong no Night Elves on an entirely different contient, while the elves from Quel’thalas are now scholarly noble "High Elves" addicted to arcane magic which were cast out of Night Elf society.

I'm not sure what counts as a retcon nowadays but if it just means deviation from both mission and manual stories in Warcraft 1 to 3 or simply not stated in them, I would say the retcons I like are the events portrayed in the early Warcraft and WoW novels such as "Tides of Darkness" by Aaron Rosenberg. One example is that in Warcraft 2 Auduin Lothar simply dies in an ambush but in the novel his death in a duel with Doomhammer during the assault of Blackrock Mountain is made into a relevant moment of character development for Turalyon.

'Lord of the Clans' by Christie Golden is also a great story that had retcons, introducing new characters such as Taretha that are so integral to Thrall's characterization now that I cannot see his story without them.

Another I do prefer is the events of the 'War of the Ancients' series written by Richard A. Knaak as it's what really sold me on the characters featured in the novels such as Illidan. In the Warcraft 3 manual, it was stated that Illidan was mad with arcane addiction when he betrayed the Kaldorei and that the creation of the new Well was out of greed and vanity. Matter of fact the novel's Illidan felt a lot more in line with his depiction in the actual game itself than the manual for some reason, blending nicely his power-hungry, vain and treacherous nature with his actions that still intend to bring forth a net positive such as the death of Tichondrius.

As for additions in WoW, while I understand that some dislike the factions being only two instead of four like Warcraft 3, I actually like the idea of groups like the Night Elves and Forsaken being forced into alliances of convenience due to their situations after the Third War. It was made even more believable for me because even when they became formal members of their respective coalitions, you can still see self-serving interests of those two. For the Night Elves the enemy of my enemy is a 'friend'; they only wanted support from Theramore just to maintain their territorial borders against Horde encroachment by keeping them in check. The Forsaken have been scheming with the Grimtotems to develop a plague to wipe out all their enemies - Alliance, Scourge and eventually Horde since the Grimtotems see Kalimdor as Tauren-only lands.

Lastly, Azeroth does sound better as the world's name rather than just the name of Stormwind's kingdom.
