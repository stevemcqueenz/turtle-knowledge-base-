## Post 150651 by Makaciolas — 2025-09-07T12:18:00Z

Dwarves
The Dwarf Shaman lore has roots that go all the way back to Warcraft II, and was officially introduced in Cataclysm.
The Problem: The core Dwarf race of Ironforge is known for its technological prowess, mining, and use of the Light. Shamanism was not a part of their culture.
The "Solution": The introduction of Dwarf Shamans was made possible by the integration of the Wildhammer Clan into the Alliance. The Wildhammers, who live in the Hinterlands and are known for their gryphon riders, have a strong and ancient tradition of shamanism.
The Lore: The Wildhammer Dwarves have a deep reverence for the elements, particularly the air and earth. They are known for communing with the spirits of the mountains and the sky, which is a perfect fit for a shaman. Their gryphon riders, who used to throw lightning-imbued hammers in older games, were in essence, a form of shaman. With the formation of the Council of Three Hammers, the Wildhammer and their shamanistic traditions were brought to the forefront of Alliance culture.

Tauren (The Sunwalkers)
The introduction of Tauren Paladins in Cataclysm was a bigger surprise for many players. Their lore, while different from the Blood Knights, is also deeply rooted in their culture.
The Problem: The Tauren have long worshipped the Earth Mother, and their culture is a blend of shamanism and druidism. However, their Druids, guided by the Night Elves of the Cenarion Circle, primarily focused on worshipping the moon, Mu'sha (Elune).
The "Solution": A Tauren warrior named Aponi Brightmane, recovering from her wounds after fighting the Scourge, had a conversation with a Druid, Tahu Sagewind. They realized that the Tauren had neglected to honor the Sun, An'she, which is the other eye of the Earth Mother. They theorized that to truly honor the Earth Mother and maintain balance, they needed to balance their reverence for both the sun and the moon.
The Lore: By focusing on the power of An'she, they were granted the "Light" in a way that was uniquely Tauren. The Sunwalkers were not knights in shining armor. They were martial warriors who drew on the power of the sun to fight for their people and bring balance to the land. Their powers are often described as being more "druidic" in nature, and their holy spells have a distinctly golden or orange hue to reflect their solar source.
Current Status: The Sunwalkers are an established order within Tauren society. They are honorable, wise, and devoted to protecting their people and the balance of the world. They represent a more "noble" and traditional Paladin archetype, but with a cultural twist.
