Yeah I figured as much, I'm guessing any official literature from 2000-2006 (that doesn't include the Eredar retcon) would be considered canon.

Warcraft from Reign of Chaos to Vanilla WoW was where the lore was at its most coherent as Warcraft III was a soft reboot of Warcraft II that was also a soft reboot of Warcraft I. There was a semi-unspoken agreement between Metzen and the fans that Warcraft III, the novels, and the tabletop RPG would be respected as canon with the RPG acting as an unofficial lore bible, this was confirmed, funny enough, in his apology for the Eredar error and the RPG books were even advertised by the old WoW website as a source for lore for those who were interested in it, the ads are still there if you want to check them out on the Internet Archive.

The transition from Warcraft III to WoW still had its problems, like the Night Elf Sentinels losing their semi-matriarchal amazonian warrior ethos and joining the Alliance despite their society being overwhelmingly anti arcane magic and suspicious towards foreign technologically advanced societies that would require nature to be consumed in mass to produce, the Druids being an exclusively male and Night Elf order allowing female Night Elves and the Tauren to join, the Undead Forsaken joining the Shamanistic Orcish Horde, both Thrall’s Horde and the Alliance tolerating  warlocks and the study of demonology outside the confines of the Kirin Tor or any other organization that would limit its application to research purposes or having a small number of sanctioned warlocks to help combat the demonic threat, and so on and so forth. Blizzard would either give very little information explaining the reasons for these changes like the Forsaken joining the Horde, or not at all like with the massive cultural shift with the Night Elves in Vanilla, making it the fan’s job to fill in the dots.

Blizzard being a "Gameplay First!" company made Warcraft great but also stunted its longevity as a franchise, after the Eredar retcon Metzen did something Blizzard would rarely ever do again and fully admit its error. There seems to be a habit where Metzen would be great at world building, but terrible at being a custodian of the world he built, being completely shackled to the rule of cool. The gameplay might be great, but the constant retcons make the world incoherent and ignoring all the lore problems eventually leads to the death of immersion finally culminating into the calamity that was shadowlands. Metzen is one guy who was apparently given the daunting task of overseeing the narratives of multiple active franchises (Warcraft, Diablo, Starcraft, and project Titan), but what, Blizzard couldn’t hire more lore masters or at least recruit volunteers to proofread and make sure the stories and the world weren’t a retcon of the week adventure. Apparently, they hired some lore people back in the cataclysm days, but it seems they were just ignored.

The past is past and I’m honestly glad TurtleWoW is diverging from the Eredar retcon timeline and trying to stay true to WC3 and the RPGs. It’s so rare to see any developers that are passionate about the lore and actually take the time to understand it while expanding on it in a consistent manner.

I think in retrospect, the two-faction inclusion into WoW was the right decision, at that point Blizzard was a smaller company with limited time and resources. I think if you had good enough writers, you could sell some of the Lore changes going from WC3 into WoW, maybe include more context in quests and lore books to explain why things went down the way that they did in Vanilla. It’s just the changes were so sudden and a lot of them were unexplained. If you were lucky, you would get a couple of sentences explaining why things changed in the WoW manual or maybe a quest referencing it, but for the most part nothing. I get that later on in the development of WoW they really needed to finish the game and so a lot of lore had to be subverted or changed for gameplay purposes in order to ship a functioning game. My main contention is that they didn’t go back and try to make it make sense, no short stories published on the website or quests later added in patches or books in game or the player to read explain why things are the way that they are in after the events of Frozen Throne, even Destron’s travelogue, a fanfiction blog, spent more time and effort explaining the changes than Blizzard who just ignored it even after they gained the success and resources to patch up the holes they left behind. What I think Blizzard failed to realize was that the lore of a game becomes a part of its gameplay especially for RPGs where the lore is essential to the immersion that makes a coherent world that keeps players captivated and interested in that world, its not the only reason, but it is one of the pillars of an RPG, it provides context and meaning to a players actions. I think Tolkien said it best with regards to the laws that govern fictional worlds:

“What really happens is that the story-maker proves a successful ' sub-creator'. He makes a secondary world which your mind can enter. Inside it, what he relates is 'true': it accords with the laws of that world. you therefore believe it, while you are, as it were, inside. The moment disbelief arises, the spell is broken; the magic, or rather art, has failed. You are then out in the primary world again, looking at the little abortive secondary world from outside.”

Where old and perhaps even new GW had an advantage over pre-Activision Blizzard was that lore was essential to games like Warhammer Fantasy and 40K for the reasons I mentioned above. GW made a system in which any retcons were actually clarifications of old lore that were previously fabricated by malign forces or it simply being a legend shrouded in falsehood. Both Warhammer Fantasy and 40K had mountains of lore as both spanned countless eons of history and contained within them numerous factions hellbent on conquering each other and all of them spreading misinformation about each other making it plausible that the lore we get about the setting isn’t always accurate. GW knew keeping track of both franchises was going to be a herculean labor, so they made a bug into a feature (which is its own can of worms). Funny enough Danuser tried to do this in shadowlands when he decided to soft retcon the lore of the WoW chronicles which was Metzen’s attempt at creating a lore bible for Warcraft after the RPG was retconned by a random intern back in the cataclysm days, but the concept of lore books or novels being unreliable was not a precedent set by Metzen or any members of the WoW writing team before and the chronicles were originally marketed as a word of god final retelling of the lore. I can’t speak to the lore of current day Warhammer, but I don’t see the Warcraft franchise recovering anytime soon until some serious introspection is done within the company or at least the writing team, which seems highly unlikely based on the history of the franchise and the company post Activision merger. I’m just grateful that the TurtleWoW team gets what made Warcraft Warcraft and is advancing the lore while relying on WC3 and the RPGs as a foundation from which to go forward.
