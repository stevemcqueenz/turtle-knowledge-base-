# 2 Tank 2 Shaman

## Post 107678 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2024-11-12T14:02:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=107678#p107678 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!

This is an almost-guide. I wanna share my experience with yall with the hope that
 1. It helps warden tanks.
 2. It motivates someone to try out shaman tanking.

I don't wanna act like a know-it-all. Changes happened not a long time ago, we don't have established guides and in-depth knowledge about these topics.
At any point, I might be wrong.

So, about 2 Tank 2 Shaman

![Image](https://i.postimg.cc/nrL0m3Zs/tank.png)

 **The scope:**
Shaman tanks are designed to be 100% viable tanks during leveling. They can be used at raids, too but they are less optimal there. The lack of a defensive CD becomes a pain point.

 **Strengths:**
 * Very good initial threat.
 * Very good AoE threat during leveling (lack of good totem scaling makes it worse later).
 * High dodge, many times you won't even get hit.
 * High utility via totems.

 **Weaknesses:**
 * Mana user, can get oom mid-fight.
 * Lack of defensive CD.
 * Squishier than the other tanks.
 * Aoe threat? Yes. Aoe taunt? No.
 * Mobility. No inherent speed buff, no charge options.

 **Other interesting points:**
 * Usually relies on totems which make WF totem and strength totem harder to use. Melee DPS might hate shaman tanks.
 * They are the closest thing to be an 'against spell damage-tech tank' with some gimmicks (elemental warding talent, T2,5 set bonus). But all in all they are not anti-spell damage-specced, they just have some limited tools for it.

 **Their toolkit**

 **For pulls**

Lightning bolt and chain lightning are great for pulls. Chain lightning can worth it even for 1 target. Don't forget that r1 lightning bolt can be cast faster than the max rank one. If you have the time, you can cast some r1 bolts to try proccing clearcasting until the mob gets to you.
Also, if you have relics, make some good relic swap macros. Totem of crackling thunder loves lightning bolts while totem of the storm loves both bolts and chain lightnings.
If you wanna min-max, keep in mind that weapons and shield can be swapped while being in combat. You can macro a pull with a SP weapon set and immediately swap back to the normal set if you wanna do that.

This will get you a great initial threat and hopefully a clearcasting buff, maybe also a haste proc from lightning bolt.
Your next step is to maximize threat on the mobs who run to you:
 1. Time a fire nova totem well! Make it explode the second the mobs arrive. Huge threat spike. Follow it up with a magma totem for maintained threat.
 2. If you go full on threat, support this with a stoneclaw totem. Your logic should be something like this:
 -> Do I expect fear? -> Tremor
Nah. -> Do I need more threat? -> Stoneclaw.
Nah. -> Am I not confident in my defenses? -> Stoneskin
All g. -> Strength (Helps you and all of the melee.)

In practice it usually means strength totem unless fears happen or DPS does really high numbers.

Don't forget: as a warden shaman you can range-pull really well but you're 'charging' is awkward. You just randomly jog up to the monsters.

 **During fight**

Use **shocks** on CD but only if elemental devastation is procced (it usually should be). You can still use shocks without the buff but if the mob is a higher level one, I'd wait for the proc. You need that spell hit buff.
Use earth shock (max level) for threat, around rank 5 for Tesco budget threat and r1 if you wanna only interrupt and mana is really low.
In practice after these changes I suggest using max rank almost always.

 **When not to use shocks?**: If your threat-lead is enough, **preserve mana**. Parties hate having a 2 min break after each pull. The art of shaman and pala tanking is finding the necessary minimum of abilities to keep threat while saving the rest of the mana for the future. It's a long process. When practicing, just spam shocks on CD initially and when you learn to have reliable threat always assess if you can allow yourself to save mana at any given moment. That is the moment when downgrading or skipping shocks can start to become inportant.
Frost shock is the weird replacement for earth shocks for high nat rez mobs.
Flame shock gives a 'long term threat' on a target. Personally I don't find this so important since we have AoE threat now.
Don't forget, shocks like totem of rage, macro them to a good old relic swap.

Earth shocks also benefit from...

 **...the new, 8 sec CD stormstrike.**
As I said, shocks like elemental devastation buff. They are specially happy if they get the stormstrike buff, too. Use stormstrike on CD unless you can afford to save mana and use it strategically keeping the option of a bigger earth shock in mind.

 **Lightning strike** has a 10 sec CD. It's super good. And elemental shield-dependent.
I usually go for water shield to be better with mana. But for shield selection you should ask these questions:
Are my defensives high enough? -> If no, use earth shield and LS with it. After all, worst tank is a dead tank.
Is threat okay? -> If no, go for lightning shield.
Am I confident in survival and threat? -> Go for water shield. Help with mana means less downtime in a dungeon run.

 **Taunt** is of course there as an emergency button.
I've seen an error people do sometimes. When you pull and the first mob comes, you don't need to taunt it as a first action, unless it is targeting another party member. Just greet them folks with a stormstrike for initial threat (aided by your totem threat).

 **Bloodlust:**
You can use it as a threat spike and as a utility button. You really struggle keeping threat on a boss? Raise your haste. You want a target to die asap? Bloodlust and your crits grant haste to party members.

The big pain point here is the lack of defensives. I can't advise you to go to AQ40 f.ex. as a warden shaman. You def can but it's really hard both for you and for the healers.

PS: I personally like to make a startattack macro after the strikes, just in case.

 **The totems**

There are 4 elements. Each one can help you a lot. My priorities are as follows.

 **Air**
Is there any instant spell I need to avoid (think of Hakkar or the Kara Crypt mobs)? -> Grounding
Things go normally? -> Grace of air.
Is melee DPS throwing a tantrum? -> WF totem (but only then).

WF totem is actively hurting you. While it gives nothing to you since you use **rockbiter** (never question this, USE ROCKBITER), it raises melee DPS threat with the extra attacks. So you give up much needed crit and dodge so that DPS can take over threat easier. I only use it if melee is REALLY nagging.

 **Earth**
As written above.
In a sequence I suggest using stoneclaw for initial threat then swapping to stoneskin or to strength.

 **Fire**
You don't benefit from flametongue. Same problem as WF totem but people don't have the reflex to expect it fortunately.
So.
When pulling back -> Fire nova, followed by magma.
Normally -> Magma.
For single-target, if there are no nearby sheeps etc -> Searing.

 **Water**
Healing stream totem is still bad. If you really have a hobby of dying, try it out but water totem logic for me goes like this:
Any poison/disease? -> Related cleansing totem.
If not -> Do I want fire rez? -> Fire rez totem.
If not -> Mana totem.

So
 **You pull back:**
Fire nova is ticking, stoneclaw totem is helping to get threat. Mobs arrive, fire nova explodes, you switch to magma and put down grace of air and (usually) mana totem. After some stoneclaw ticks you can switch to strentgh unless health is a problem in which case it is stoneskin.

 **You run in:**
Stormstrike for inital threat and magma+stoneclaw (+grace of air+mana). After some seconds switch to stoneskin or to strength.

 **You fight one big baddie:**
Grace of air, stoneskin/strength, mana and searing totem.

 **The main logic of shaman tanking is this:**
You have several tools to get more threat, get defenses or to get mana. But at many times you can only choose one. You have to observe yourself (how is your health bar moving, how much mana you have, what does threat addon say) to decide which utility you need at the moment. This is the main logic which should determine what shield you use or f.ex. you use stoneclaw, stoneskin or strength totem.

 **PS:** Since I shaman tanked before the changes, I have some old reflexes, f.ex. having some oils of immolation at me at all times. I suggest the same, specially during early practice. It helps you out if you did something wrong. Think of items like oils and target dummies as get out of jail cards while you're practicing the build.

 **For races:**
All are viable but if you're a minmaxer, not all are equal.
Orcs have some threat spike with blood fury and have some weapon skill advantage but they are probably the worst option. At least their shaman racial is cool for tanking, too.

I think trolls are the mid options. Health regen is better now but still not a serious racial. Their main selling point is berserking which got nerfed but it's still awesome. Hex is mostly useless for a tank.

Taurens give +life for a mail-wearing tank. New racial also seems good (but never seen it in practice). I think they are the strongest choice if someone really cares about these minimal differences.

PS: If I can send a message to devs, we are 1 step away from making warden a valid tank at high raids. **Please give them a defensive CD.** Please.

___________

I almost forgot: an old trick you can do as a shaman tank, if casters have really high threat is leaving a tranquil air totem behind. You don't have improved totem radius so if you position well and you're okay with giving up an air totem slot, you can give 20% threat reduction to the casters. It's a very niche thing though.

If there's melees in the dungeon group i would almost always prioritize windfury totem (interject spellbind totem where necessary), it's just that good and you don't gain that much from the bonus agility.

Another great weapon at 58+ is the epic dagger Alcors Sunrazor. Picked up mine for 70ish gold from the auction house which is a very fair price.

Before the talent changes I would actually go into the resto tree so grab the extended totem range. More range = better uptime and less replants in between groups. Mana management is indeed key. But also a very fun skill to master with a high skill ceiling. While your inexperienced you'll often be drinking while the group is waiting on you.

Shaman tanks humiliates caster packs btw. Scholomance is a joy to visit when your main rotation interrupts spells every 5-6 seconds. Downranking earth shock is worth it too. You should have Rank 1 earth keybound separately just for cheap interrupts.

Oh and remember to have any priests preshield you whenever as you don't suffer the rage gain penalty.

What's not nice for tank shamans are nature immune (or resistant) mobs which hamper use of earth shock. Best strat is to simply warn your group ahead of time, come up with an adhoc tactic for difficult packs (depending on group composition).
