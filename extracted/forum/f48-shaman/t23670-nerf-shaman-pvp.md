# Nerf Shaman PVP

## Post 165721 by Sylbrosa — 2026-01-20T19:08:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=165721#p165721 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

I’m a min-maxer — always have been. I enjoy analyzing data and doing the math to squeeze out every bit of effectiveness in online games. That’s my hobby.

Lately I’ve been digging into Shaman PvP balance, and I think I’ve found the core of the problem: the Ancestral Guardian talent.

Originally, Shaman was designed as a glass cannon: very high burst damage, but a bit more on the fragile than other meele classes. If a Shaman got focused, they were supposed to go down quickly. That was the trade-off for having such explosive damage and strong offensive cooldowns.

But that trade-off no longer really exists.

With current tuning, Shamans still have the damage of a glass cannon — they can delete people in a couple of globals — and with only three talent points, they can massively increase their survivability.

Ancestral Guardian is a third-row talent with only three ranks and reads:

> “Increases your armor value from items by [5/10/15]% and your chance to dodge by an additional [2/4/6]%.”

This talent was clearly meant to make tank-style Shaman builds more viable, which is fine in theory. The problem is how much power is packed into such a small investment.

For comparison, Warriors and Rogues usually need to spend around five talent points to gain 5% dodge or parry. Meanwhile, a Shaman spends only three points to gain 6% dodge plus 15% increased armor from items.

In raw defensive value, that’s roughly equivalent to 8–10 talent points worth of stats, condensed into just three points.

So now we have a class that keeps its glass-cannon level damage, but for the cost of three talent points gains near tank-level resilience. That breaks the original class identity and removes the risk that was supposed to balance their burst.

The problem isn’t that Shamans have defensive options — it’s that Ancestral Guardian is massively overtuned for its tier and cost, especially in PvP where dodge and armor scale extremely well with gear.

In short: the design goal makes sense, but the numbers don’t.
