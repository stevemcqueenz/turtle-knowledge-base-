 1. **What's good in a shaman that is tanking?**

If you plan to raidtank in the long run, then mostly for challenge and to provide a lot of utility in the role of an OT.
Warden shaman has some niche against enemies which rely on mostly fire, nature or frost spells but the toolkit is not consistent enough for them to be called the optimal choice for a specific enemy type.

During dungeons however, shaman tank has many things going for it. Unlike raids, where the raid comp has access to each possible buffs, a bunch of kicks, different decurses etc, a dungeon run usually comes with a limited scope of utility.

A warden shaman can bring a lot of it himself by bringing the usual shaman toolkit.

That's fair, but you might ask what's the point to have a shaman tank for the toolkit instead of a resto or even an enhancement DPS?

 * From one hand, queue times. People usually wait a lot for a dungeon party to pop up because tanks are scarce. A
warden shaman makes LFT faster while also bringing the shaman toolkit to the group.
 * Another cool thing about this build is the aoe threat, no matter how many targets we are talking about. It is not the
new prot pala, but it is decent enough to keep even several packs together without a chaos which would likely happen to a warrior for example if aoe taunt is on CD.
 * Range pulls are a big plus. I'll talk about shaman pulling later on but in short you have many way to range pull. Just
mark an enemy real quick, having big threat on one target, establishing threat on several targets, etc.
 * If you have many casters in a party, you can range pull, mark skull, put down an earthbind totem and watch as you
are killing the main target before it even arrives.
 * Kiting. If your consumes are on CD and you are close to dying, you have an exit strategy against melee. Not joking. Putting down earthbind totem and running for your life is a valid strategy if things turn out to be really bad and it
usually ends without anyone dying.
 * Just like enhancement DPS, you can give bloodlust at later dungeons, which is cool.
 * Ankh can be a wipe-saver if you get a heal fast enough so that you can start taunting things to you again.

But everything has two sides, tanking with a shaman has of course a negative side, too:

 * Initially your armor feels like butter and you need a top level healer (or a bunch of consumes) if you don't wanna
outlevel a dungeon, just to properly do it.
 * To the last point: until you gear up decently and have enough experience with the ins and outs, you REALLY prefer an attentive healer, not the average half-AFK dungeon healer. Until the mid 30s I regularly got the feedback (was actively asking for them) that it is very noticeable that I was tanking as a shaman based on how fast my HP started to drop during fights. This however gets solved mid 30s (talents and custom items help, more on that later).
 * You will never have a defensive CD. Nearest thing you can get is swapping in the Hakkar trinket.
 * You use mana so sometimes the team will wait because the tank is thirsty. Initially it can be very bad until it is
learnt how to spare mana during gameplay.
 * Warden shamans are less willing to use utility totems by default since their mana management also defines the pace
of the run. Specially a melee DPS might feel like that they got LFT-d together with a utility tank who refuses to do
utility.
 * Gearing can be hard. The game was not designed with level 40+ mail tanking items in mind.
 * It is player-dependent but warden shamans, as hybrids, might have a tendency to roll for whatever with the excuse
of 'I might play xx someday'. I am not like this but this is a typical hybrid behaviour I'm aware of.

 2. **My early experiences**

In my opinion, the earliest level shaman could properly tank is level 16.
During early leveling you need 5 talents in shield specialization and if you immediately follow it up with totemic alignment, you are good to go to RFC.
I know, people of the old days did the tanking without any aoe threat but in my opinion, with level 10's' toolkit it would turn half of RFC into a kiting fest because of healer aggro at larger packs, even if symbols were used properly.

So, in my opinion, the minimum talent set for tanking:
 5tp in shield specialization
 2tp in totemic alignment

But honestly, I skipped RFC and started tanking with WC, leveling was just too fast during this event.
But in case you wanna do RFC:
At this stage, your threat consists of lightning bolt range pull/rushing in and earth shocking a target, tabbing with rockbiter on and stoneclaw totem/fire nova totem. Earth shocks to keep up threat and taunt if needed.

 3. **What I learnt:**

Always use marks. I keybinded them to f1...f8 (?) keys. F1 is skull. I was trying to give more raid-like instructions first with skull+x but in my experience it usually goes bad and half of the team starts hitting x unless you discuss this during every dungeon. Skull and moon are universally understood.
So what I suggest is marking skull dynamically - marking skull at pull, once dead, immediately skulling another target etc until all die. You should have a basic understanding of mob priority but if you have no idea (like me sometimes), just be confident and put the skull up wherever once the main target died. Wasting time thinking without any skull diversifies threat and can result in mobs running around. So just bullscheisse something if you're not sure and that's the next main target.
I can't stress how important this is. Keeping up 1 skull always with an easy-to-access key makes tanking 1000% easier.

 **Tip:** Sometimes there will be a DPS 5 levels above you with way better gear and the tranquil air totem trick (later) doesn't help. You won't overaggro the guy. Just mark something specifically for him, he'll have no issue mauling that mob down and you don't have to waste a bunch of mana trying to keep threat in a losing battle. Works best for melees but can do it with ranged, too, they have slows or pets after all.

Regarding marks I used 1 other trick, too. Drinking time is no downtime. While you're drinking (with mana spring totem summoned before drinking started!) check the mobs in front of you. Identify walking ones. Half of the wipes in dungeons happen because patrols are ignored and one pull pulls three packs.
While drinking, I was usually marking all the pats I saw with 'neutral' markings, like arrow, star or coin. If you clearly see all pats at all times, pulls become very predictable.

So you're in the dungeon, setting up marks and pulling packs. Before continuing with my 'story' I want to share two further key concepts for tanking. Once we are over those, we can talk about more shaman-specific stuff. But these things are important, they gotta be mentioned.

 1. Overthreat exists since you are a mana user. Let's say that you tanked many times in Vanilla but did so always with a warrior or a bear. Mana-tanks work differently. Here, you have a starting resource at time time of the pull and it will mostly just goes down during fight. You have to be as economical as possible. Make sure you have enough resource to respond if something unexpected happens or just save up enough that you can avoid a drink.
Download a threat addon. During fight always check your threat-lead. Remember that taunt just brings you back to the top for no mana.
And even if your spell is not on cooldown, if you're not forced to do anything, don't do anything. Bonk with rockbiter. If you need to get a bit of extra threat but not much, use a downgraded earth shock. Yes, have several earth shocks on your action bar. If you can get away with a cheaper shock, you saved mana again.
If you make this 'economical thinking' natural for you, you'll notice that you barely drink anymore (unless DPS forces you to go all out) and you confidently pull a pack with 20% mana.

 2. There are different kind of pulls and they are better in different situations. It's important since shamans have a variety of ways to range pull.
In short:
 * Cast a ranged spell and run behind a cover if you want to LOS pull. I usually do this if the pack has at least one caster and I want to force him to run to me. Caster at melee range is very susceptible of interrupts and we didn't need to rush where they are, risking an arsepull.
 - Use lightning bolt on skull plus chain lightning if you want to establish as much threat as possible.
 - Use max rank lightning bolt (twice if you can) if you want threat on the main target but there's also a poly or something you shouldn't be hitting.
 - Use rank 1 lightning bolt as a warrior's arrow. Fast to cast, super cheap, has basically 0 initial threat though.

If you want to give yourself time to cast these spells safely and run to cover in time, earthbind totem can slow the enemy down.

This kind of pull has some disadvantages, though. It is slower to pull back and run to cover every time. If you use it by default, runs can feel long and slow. Also, mobs with silence (razorfen bats or some ghosts) can really grief mana users unless they move FURTHER back just to be out of range.

 * Run to the mobs. Shock one when you're close, stormstrike another and lightning strike a third one, put down aoe threat totem if needed and focus on skull.

Much faster but since you 'move into the enemy's territory', it's also much riskier. Other enemy packs are nearby. Moving wrongly or letting a low HP mob run away can mean an extra pack.
One more thing to note: if the pack you're targeting has casters and melees, note that melees will run towards you while casters will be standing still, casting. This can break up the pack into two and you find yourself tanking half of it while the casters are attacking you or a DPS/healer from a safe distance. To prevent this, don't stop when meeting the melee. Mark the caster with skull and start running. Earth shock a melee, stormstrike another one, maybe lightning strike a third one and keep on running to the caster, melee will follow you. This way pack stayed together and you can control whom the caster is targeting. You can also grief caster mobs as a shaman tank with earth shock and grounding totem.

 * Like last time but with several packs. You ask the DPS not to do anything until you reach the destination pack. You run in, maybe give a quick spell or two to the first pack, they start to chase you and you 'settle in' at a later pack, tanking all together with the help of your aoe totems. Works better against melee, casters from the first pack might stand still, casting. Again. Do it only if you are really geared.

 * There is a specific kind of pull. It is a bit complex, trying to explain it. You are currently in fight, tanking mobs. While performing the tanking, you look around with your mouse to see the other packs' position. While tanking, you start moving towards the new pack, and establish threat on them, so you now tank both packs. You can also send out a shock, to make them rush to you, but unless you can break LOS while doing that, casters from that group might become active without rushing to you. This kind of pull is basically skipping the 'drinking and moving phase' between pulls, forcing a new one immediately. Its main purpose is to save time. Only do it if healer mana is good and you are confident in your defenses.

There is a very similar things a lot of experienced tanks do, but it is not a form of pulling (by itself). Mentioning it here since it's a very similar thing.
You're tanking a pack, but the mobs are almost dead. 1-2 are alive now, they die soonish. As a tank, you can look around and start moving (and as a shaman, you can make this strat even more ridiculous with earthbind totem). Since you have established enough threat lead on the mobs, they will follow you, while being damaged by the DPS. It has two purposes:
 1. Makes things faster. You move to the right direction while fighting, skipping the moving part after fight.
 2. Damage reduction. As a general rule, casters are killed first. Those mobs you are tanking now are melee. As you walk away, instead of just attacking, they swap between walking and attacking, meaning you receive less damage. This with some movement impairment, like frost shock and earthbind totem, and those mobs will be walking to their death following you while DPS kills them and when it happens, everyone is at the right place for the next pull.
If you want so, you can finish this process with a pull also.

 4. **These out of the way, let's go back to the shaman tanking story!**

I said that you need 5 in shield spec and 2 in totemic alignment for the minimal tanking setup.
But the optimal start in my opinion comes 4 levels later.
As a next step, take stable shields (3x) and lightning strike. Stable shields is a great passive threat generator early on, you have to waste much less mana to keep up lightning shield. Lightning strike at the same time gives you a new spell to your rotation. Instant one, decent threat, very useful.
You're level 20 now, ready to tank WC. Kind of. You are super fragile, wearing leather, being way worse-off than any other tank. If the party is good and someone can take offtank duties at the Mutanus mobs, you are ready to go. But you'll need to get survivability fast.
Because of this, the next three talents are ancestral guardian. This, just like the later spirit shield gets way better with better items but still, 6% dodge and some armor makes you way less likely to die.
Follow it up with thundering strikes (1/5) for 1% crit chance so you have 15 talent points spent in this tree.
Only 1 is enough, at this level your crit chance is not reliable and you still lack the talents to combo off from this crit chance. The name of the game is still gaining survivability.
As a next step, go 2/2 into spirit shields. By this time you are level 26, maybe got some cool shields, too. Let's say that the shield you got has 1000 armor, this talent alone gives 300 armor for you + ancestral guardian talent also has a bonus for it. Things start to feel better, you start to compensate for wearing an inferior item type with extra survivability from talents.
But still, a bit more is needed. My char was kinda fragile until the mid 30s despite focusing on the defensive talents.
As a next step, get thundering strikes into 4/5 for 4% crit chance. It means 20 talent points used up in this tree, you are level 29 now.
Next four talents in any order: 3 points into elemental weapons for the 10% damage reduction with rockbiter and stormstrike. You are level 33 now and coincidentally, level 33 is when you can buy yourself 2 obsidian brooches at AH. It is a new, non-unique custom trinket, giving 11 stamina each.
At this point you have finished the most important part of the talent tree and with the 22 stamina you just got (when I bought them, 2 costed like 10-12 golds) you are decent in survivability. The biggets problem, holding you back until now got resolved. Before reaching level 33 and having these trinkets, don't visit SM, you'd be slaughtered (or carried by an overleveled team). You can do RFK if you wanna gain exp with an easier dungeon though.
Level 33 opens up the monastery (but seriously, you have no defensive CDs and wear leather; get those brooches!).
From this point, talent-wise it's way less strict, do whatever you feel like.
I honestly think that the first 24 talent points have to be spent in a very strict manner if you want to tank with your shaman properly but after that you have many options. Rush bloodlust?  Get tidal focus to save mana? Rush elemental focus?
Personally I rushed elemental focus because it also enabled elemental devastation (+ added hit chance against higher level mobs I'm tanking is also good) and thundering strikes talents finally made sense.
Also, after level 33, when getting that 22 stamina (I swear I'm not a jewelcrafter and this is not an advertisement), you have the option to reset your spec and relearn the same with 1 minor change:
 -> Dump shield specialization
 - > Learn ancestral knowledge

You are bulky enough now that shield spec is not mandatory (and capping as a shaman tank even at the very lategame is almost impossible).
Also, your gear should have a bunch of extra stats at this point and ancestral knowledge, despite its description, gives the 10% after the stat bonuses are added. This talent respec makes you feel much stronger.
Don't do it before level 33 though, until then survival is the name of the game.

Two talent points in tidal focus is something I'd never use for a raid but since I spam many totems during dungeons, it just helps so much. You can even go further if you want to, up to 5/5, depending on what you are willing to give up for cheaper totems.

Reverberation is something I have to talk about more.
I have a theory but it needs testing which honestly, I won't do. As far as I know, shaman GCD is 1,5 sec.
Currently stormstrike has an 8 sec CD.
Usual shaman tank rotation looks like this:
SS -> ES -> LS -> SS -> ES -> LS -> SS
However, we mostly use mana shield and mana shielded LS is not optimal for threat.
But without reverberation, you can't fill in two shocks between two stormstrikes if you want to cast stormstrike on CD:
SS -> 1,5 sec ES -> 7,5 sec ES -> 9 sec SS (it has a 8 sec CD).
With reverberation, I can make it so that every 2nd 'stormstrike rotation' has a double earth shock which adds way more threat
SS -> 1,5 sec ES -> 6,5 sec ES -> 8 sec SS -> 9,5 sec LS -> 11,5 sec ES -> 16 sec SS

Other noteworthy things:
Concussion > Convection
You want the shock damage not the shock mana reduction. If you struggle with mana, please make sure that you have the 'economical thinking' I described earlier and make sure you don't spam totems unnecessarily.
Yes, totems.

 5. **The next topic.**

Totems cost a bunch of mana and your job as a tank is to dictate the flow of the dungeons. If you overspend for totems, you drink more and the run becomes slower. Usually a shaman's way of thinking is 'what combination of totems gives the most useful buff for this pull?'. The warden shaman's way of thinking is 'what totems do I have to really invest here to make it smooth?'. For many trash fights I use zero totems to make sure drink doesn't have to happen and the run becomes smooth and fast. Will go through each totems I use and the circumstances under which they worth it.

 **Air totems**

Grace of air totem
Gives agi which gives dodge, crit and some armor. Would have been cool before level 33 (would have used almost always) but comes around level 42. Think about it as a defensive tool! If you are in any form of danger, you can invest its price to be hit less often. Also use it if the comp is heavy on ranged hunters and maybe there's a rogue next to them for fights where bigger DPS is needed (rogues get 1 AP for each agi).

Windfury totem
The controversial one. Even though it is our most useful totem, it is working against us. You use rockbiter so no WF for you. Melee expects you to spend the mana for it. Melee gets WF so their threat gets bigger, forcing you to waste more mana to keep threat, ultimately forcing drinkings.
But it has its uses. If the party comp is melee-heavy, windfury totem is a huge DPS upgrade. I use it as a defensive with melee-heavy comps when I don't care who pulls aggro, we are in a bad shape, mobs gotta die fast.
This is its optimal use from our pov but there are two exceptions I tend to make.
If I overgear the melee I tend to drop a windfury for them since they won't force me to waste further mana to keep threat and they are happy.
If the melee really insists. But depends on my mood. From the team's perspective, WF is still an advantage but if they overaggro and die and complain, I will cancel my WF donations for that day.
I just hate it that you are expected to waste mana on something which forces you to waste even more mana to keep threat just so DPS gets happy on a pull where we would have easily managed without the whole thing. Seems like the totem is speeding up things but me being forced to drink much more means it even wastes time and makes the run slower.
I also never use windfury totem if I expect to use grounding totem.

Grounding totem
If you know that a certain mob has very nasty insta casts (like those hexer trolls), or just want total control over a caster enemy (even more than what the 5-6 sec CD earth shock could provide), I keep on grounding totem. It is specially true if troll casters (specially shadowhunters) are nearby. I don't wanna be a frog and watch the team wipe.

Tranquil air totem
Surprisingly, it has its uses. If the caster DPS is over the charts, leave a tranquil air totem behind and make sure that you are not within its range. Poor man's blessing of salvation. Can only work against pumper casters though.

Nature resistance totem
Very situational. Think about it as a defensive in a nature damage-heavy encounter. Some parts of Maraudon for example. But that's all.

Windwall totem is useless, and that selfie totem is for PVP only.

 **Earth totems**

Stoneclaw totem
Your best aoe threat, but has a 30 sec CD. First practice your multitarget rotation though, people tend to burn mana on totems where the pack is small enough and you could easily handle it with clever tabbing and marking.
Casters shouldn't really aoe unless there are 3+ mobs. In case of three mobs only, marking skull and using your shocks, SS and LS on tabbed targets should do the trick, maybe unless casters do aoe.
In case of a bigger trash (let's say 3+ mobs), stoneclaw totem is your friend.

Stoneskin totem
A defensive tool. Shamans have no defensive CD but can set up a 'defensive toolset' if needed. It usually means grace of air totem, stoneskin totem, healing strem totem, earth shield and LS using earth shield. This totem is there if you really need to survive.

Strength of earth totem
It gives strength -> it gives you and every melee added DPS. I use it as an offensive tool, if I want to makie sure that mobs die quickly. In a melee-heavy group, this, flametongue totem and windfury totem means that I don't care who pulls aggro anymore, we gotta kill these mobs asap. At least strength of earth totem benefits you, too.

Earthgrab totem
My secret MVP. Has 2 uses, both are kiting related. If I'm about to die to a non-caster (!) mob, I place it and start to run. DPS can kill the mob and if my threat lead dwindles, I just earth shock one from afar, making sure I'm never in range. A lifesaver.
Second use is at LOS pulling. If you put it down, the casters and you can unleash magical hell unto the approaching mobs so they get much weaker by the time they arrive. Just make sure that you regain threat on them asap. Best is if they focus fire 1 guy so even if he survives getting to you, you can just taunt it away.

Tremor totem
Utility when you expect fear, sleep or charm. Not much else to say.

 **Water totems**

Mana spring totem
Has two uses. Use it every time when someone is drinking, and use it if you plan to stay in your immediate surrounding for more than 20 seconds (costs you 100 mana, gives the party 10 mana every 2 seconds). If you get good at expecting when you stay longer at one place and use it regularly, it helps a lot with mana management. Best case scenario is +200 mana for you, +300 mana for the party members after each full mana spring totem.

Healing stream totem
Very weak but a member of your 'defensive toolkit'. If you need to do everything to survive, use it with grace of air totem, stoneskin totem and earth shield.

Poison/disease cleansing totem
Use it when reoccuring, aoe poisons or diseases happen, f.ex. the belchers at Strat UD.
For 1 poison, manual removal via Decursive addon is better.

Fire resistance totem
Same logic as all resistance totems. Specilly good in Black Rock Mountain.

 **Fire totems**

Fire nova totem
If you made a big LOS pul, it is good to start with a fire nova totem. It is counting down, then explodes when the mobs arrive if you're doing it right and gives you a ton of initial threat. It can be followed up by magma totem.
So its use is to maximize aoe threat output, usually for LOS pulls when maximizing threat is important.

Magma totem
Your expensive aoe threat option. Has no CD, it's spammable but it's super expensive. Use it when you'd use stoneclaw totem if a) stoneclaw totem is on CD, b) stoneclaw totem is not enough to keep threat. Use it sparingly, it is the biggest mana burner.
You can also downgrade it to save some mana but even lower versions are expensive.
Can also be used against a single target (usually boss) to maximize threat but only do it if you absolutely have to. Eats your mana like nothing else.

Searing totem
A cheaper solution to raise threat against 1-2 targets. I'd only consider using it against really overeager DPS since you should be able to handle 1-2 targets without totems usually.
Never used it if a target is sheeped, shackled or if there is a nearby pack or patrol.

Flametongue totem
The windfury situation again but at least melee is less aware of this and it is expected much more rarely. If asked, just say no. Less beneficial for melee than WF, you get nothing, you spend mana and you get locked out from useful fire totems which you might need.
Only use it for the melee emergency scenario when you give your melees each toolkit just to kill mobs asap.

Frost resistance totem
Same as the other resistance totems.

_____________

So, as a warden shaman, unlike any other kind of shaman, your thinking is not 'what totems are the best here'. Instead you go by 'what totems are absolutely needed'?

You are under emergency and are about to die?
grace of air, stoneskin, healing stream

You are under emergency but melee can save the day going Rambo?
WF, strength of earth, flametongue.

You are almost dead and started kiting the melee boss?
Earthbind, optinally searing totem and healing stream totem.

Etc. The situation determines the need of the totems and you usually go for the least amount necessary.
WF is the exception if melee really wants it.

 6. **Elemental shields**

Your default elemental shield is mana shield from level 26, when you learn it.
It helps restoring mana, enabling the 'smooth and fast' gameplay idea.

The other shields have uses, too, though.
Lightning shield is to maximize threat, if you really need to race for it (or to maximize damage).
Earth shield is to maximize survivability (alongside its best friends, grace of air totem, stoneskin totem and healing stream totem).

Usually you wear water shield but your threatmeter (lightning shield) and your health bar (earth shield) can convince you to swap if needed.

___________

 7. **Shocks**

Earth shock has an innate 1,5x threat modifier (further affected by rockbiter weapon) and it's the only nature damage shock we have thus only this shock gets buffed by stormstrike. It is our default one.
I usually have max rank, rank 4-5 and rank 1 on my action bars. Rank 4-5 to save mana while still being effective, rank 1 if you only want to interrupt a casting and threat is good.

Frost shock usually comes into the picture either when the mobs have high nature resistance (some parts of Maraudon for example) or if you switched to kiting. Otherwise doesn't worth it. If you use frost shock for kiting, using a rank 1 is perfect, it saves mana (unless damage is important, too).

Flame shock does the most damage if the dot can fully expire on the target but has no threat modifiers. Can be used on your target to maximize damage if threat is in control otherwise, or you can use it on a non-skull enemy to 'give some passive threat' to you. It can be good if not everyone is following skull and you want some extra insurance against overaggro on that target. Flame shock is also excellent against targets which occassionally reset aggro, ensuring that even if it happens, you quickly (and automatically) reestablish some threat on the target. The same can be applied to each of our periodic damage-dealers, like magma totem or searing totem, too (as long as searing stays on target).

___________

 8. **Relics**

If you wanna min-max, once you have several relics, macro them to spells. Shock tied to totem of rage, lightning bolt tied to totem of crackling thunder etc. Can cheat you some extra threat.
Heard about problems about relic swapping and GCD but never experienced it.

 9. **Weapons**

If you played a warden shaman before the class changes, you went for the fastest weapons.
The old mantra was:
 1. While rockbiter's TPS scales with weapon speed, missing a hit with a slow weapon is way worse then missing a quick hit every now and then.
 2. You have no aoe threat, you gotta stab around quick to establish threat on each target.

Now, with the threat from the totems and a safety taunt, this can be forgotten. Usually go for a slower one-hander!
Both SS and LS damage scale based on your weapon's damage, meaning that a slower weapon greatly increases your DPS and threat output.

The counter-argument people sometimes have is that any form of enhancement shaman is very crit-reliant, you need many procs to be consistent. But is it true?
Elemental devastation gives the spell hit bonus for 15 seconds (!) after a melee crit.
Clearcasting activates from spell crit and melee crit, too and stays active until you perform 2 damaging spells.
Flurry indeed needs a melee crit but its effect lasts for your 3 next swings, which is a decent time with a slower weapon.
Bloodlust gives a minimal haste buff to the party for 5 sec if you perform a melee crit.

There will be some situations when you lack a buff with the slower weapon which you'd otherwise have with a fast dagger.
But
 * You can proc these reliably enough anyway, specially once geared a bit towards crit.
 * The extra threat from SS and LS makes it worth it.
 * Current flurry is a disgrace.

 **Other things to note:**
 * As an orc, of course prioritize axes.
 * You can do the old shaman trick of using a spellpower weapon -> pulling -> switching to the normal weapon set but our threat is much more reliable now, there is less need for these tricks.
 * You can keep a two-hander in your bag and occasionally switch to 'no shield tanking'. It generates much more threat (SS and LS still scale with weapon power) but do it only to catch up at bosses when taunt is on CD with a big number / the target cannot be taunted or when healer likes to live dangerously. You wear mail after all.

 10. **The party and you**

Honestly, I'm not that familiar with the new builds after CC2, so I might miss some things but wanna give some generic tips.
Let's say that one of the DPS is mage. Ask for water, vendor drink is expensive. Intellect is cool, helps with mana management. And sheep. When to ask for sheep? If the pack is big enough that pulling the whole thing can be risky (+ preferably no known patrol goes there to discover the sheep).
Arcane mage can give you +healing received but but + spell damage taken buff in places where you fight only melee.

Priest works the same way as mage's poly regarding shackle undead. Stam and spirit are super cool. If you're a priest's champion, ask for the + damage and spell damage one if the priest has no problems keeping you alive. Remember, fear ward is base now, if you got feared, raise an eyebrow towards the priest (sure, you have tremor but it's less reliable). Shadow resistance in places like Gilneas, of course.

Palas. Ask for wisdom unless threat is a problem and you don't want to confront DPS. If you're okay with confronting them, ask for wisdom and ask for salvation on the problematic DPS. If you get trapped in a net for example and the inability to move screws you, ask for freedom. Palas underuse freedom in my experience. Ask for devotion aura, specially pre-level 33. A good paladin friend during the early game is a blessing to your run, pun intended. A judgment of wisdom on a boss is a beautiful thing.

Druids. The usual. Druid buff is very cool. Expect to be the brez target if it is needed. Innervate optimally goes to healer, specially if you're confident with mana management and wanna make sure that you give everyone a fast run. I promise you, nothing makes them as happy as a speedrun in a place where they expected a 1-2 hour grind.

Warrior. If you plan to use LOS pulls, discuss with the DPS warrior first. They have that urge to charge at skull at the second your spell hits the target. Many of my ranged pulls became a very weird running all over the place when I hid behind a wall and waited for the mobs to come just to realize that warrior kept all of them far away with a charge. This can be discussed. Utility like shouts are very good (including the damage reduction one if you really need to survive) and sunder armor is situationally super good if they are willing to do it. Just like disarm. After ret palas, the second most vehement protesters if WF totem is not placed.

Rogue. Subtlety definitely does something and I have no idea how that works. Sub can also offer sap without pulling every mob to himself and die or force a vanish right after. Combat rogues are your usual DPS machines. Despite having a strike lowering their threat, combat rogues are the harder ones to keep threat away from.

Warlock. If needed, ask them to keep the imp out. Specially pre-level 33 when survival is a mess. They also have a threat-stone now but honestly, no idea how that works. Fear is super good when LOS-pulling and the area is cleared. But only then. Same goes to the priests' aoe fear of course.

Hunters. They have a tendency to want to dictate the tempo and to select a random mob which they decide to OT with their pet. Latter thing is totally okay, just let them enjoy the game on their terms. But if the hunter keeps pulling when healer has like 10% mana, ask them to stop. Cheetah when running around empty places and well-placed traps are super cool. Extremely high threat though, despite having abilities to reduce it.

Shamans. Honestly, shaman threat is kinda high and enh usually wants to drink after every 1-2 pulls, it's a bit annoying. What's important: if there are other shamans in the party, always discuss who uses what totem. Since you wanna AVOID using totems unless needed, try to ask them to please use this and that (they are perfect for WF totem).

Also:
Don't forget that you can manage poison and disease but you cannot demagic or decurse. Know in advance what to expect in an instance and if it has a very nasty magic or curse, try to gather a team accordingly. Poison and disease you can handle, a bad curse or magic, without a proper team can make whole runs a pain (think about SFK's ghost fest if noone can decurse or SM GY"s healing reduction decurse).

 11. **Which race to choose?**

The main rule is, as cliché as it sounds, is to follow your heart. If you hate playing as a tauren or a troll, you won't enjoy leveling up an 'optimized' tank shaman, so why not go for an orc?
But if you want to clear endgame with your char as a tank, things get more complicated. I usually dislike 'meta-slaves' like guys who couldn't imagine fury warriors not being a human or an orc before the class changes.
But warden shamans, unless patches make them more competitive, are objectively the worst tanks in the game. If you want to earn a place in a not 100% casual guild as a tank with a disadvantaged build, you'll need every possible advantage, including racials.

 **So that out of the way, how do the different races fare?**

 **Orcs** are probably the worst choice. You get some weapon skills, which can indeed be useful. When Kara40 comes out, we'll see what weapons get added to the game. This, plus an ancient war text and some weapon skills from here and there and you have the advantage of greatly reduced glancing blows when using an axe. Even if we don't min-max, if you use an axe as an orc, your glancing blows tend to have a smaller damage penalty.
Warden shaman's can be played as more spell-oriented and more melee-oriented ones (how much you rely on shock and spellpower vs. raw DPS with rockbiter modifier). The latter build can work out for an orc pretty well since all those damage points which are not lost when glancing help generating threat.
Hardiness is nice but it mostly helps in PVP.
Blood fury is a valid DPS and threat increase but the healing debuff is something you want to avoid as a tank.
All in all, you can play an orc warden shaman as a more melee-oriented tank (to benefit the most from the less glancing blow) which has the option to go for a high risk-decent reward threat increase via blood fury.
Orcs also have access to feral spirits, which is a fairly overpriced, slight DPS increase on CD. Currently discussing internally if feral spirit threat gets redirected to you or not.

 **Trolls** are your more offensive option out of the remaining races. Unlike orcs, trolls don't punish you with a healing debuff when pressing the 'more threat' racial (berserking is still good). Honestly, if I disregard the party buff-effect, berserking almost feels like a second bloodlust on a 3 min CD. You also deal more damage to beasts but it is way too situational.
Your health regeneration became better but don't overvalue it!
The closest data I have found for the shaman's spirit-based health regeneration is this:
[https://wowwiki-archive.fandom.com/wiki ... generation](https://wowwiki-archive.fandom.com/wiki/Health_regeneration)

As a reminder, regeneration, as it stands:
'Regeneration now increases health regeneration by 20% instead of 10% and allows 25% of total health regeneration to continue in combat instead of 10%.'
[viewtopic.php?p=102974#p102972](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=102974#p102972)

In other words:
 1.2*(Spirit * 0.11 + 7)/4 -> If I am correct.

Warden shamans are not famous from their over-the-top spirit. Let's say that your spirit is 200, after buffs!

 1.2*(200*0.11 + 7)/4 = 1.2*29/4 = 8.7 HP every 2 seconds with the racial during combat if my math was correct.

I reserve the right to be wrong at maths though.
 *(If you want to abuse the regeneration racial, create a warrior tank with high spirit and extra meditation for health! The warrior's spirit conversion formula is much more interesting.)*

What I wanted to say -> The trolls have one really relevant racial which is extremely good and can give you threat spikes 'on-demand'. Since you mostly cast instant spells during fight, berserking is mostly a melee ability for you.

 **Taurens** are the more defensive iteration of warden shamans. 10 nature resistance is nice but we are talking right now because of three reasons:
 * Endurance gives you 5% more HP (after buffs).
 * War stomp gives you an aoe stun.
 * Totemic slam reduces the target's haste.

Each of these abilities help you to survive. And as a warden shaman you wear mail and have no defensive CD.
The usual thinking regarding tanks is that you have to have just enough survivability and once it is reached, get as much threat as possible. Taurens can be slightly more threat-oriented with their gearing or they can just be a 'safer version of the most fragile tank in the game'.
Also, tauren shamans can 'buy themselves a free lesser healing wave' with war stomp.

 **In short**
Less recommended: orc ( melee-warden build)
More recommended: troll (threat spikes via berserking), tauren (better defensives, allows more offensive gearing)

When leveling a tank, I usually avoid red dungeons but go to orange ones without any problem.
As a shaman tank, I avoid red dungeons and I usually reconsider if it's okay for me going into an orange one.

Main takeaway: be frugal, avoid totems and just auto-attack, unless you are forced to do actions. Saves mana, enables smooth runs.

 12. **PS:**
I almost forgot. Even if you tank many mobs, position yourself in a way that:
 1. Each of them is facing you. Parry, block and dodge won't work if the mob is hitting you from the back.
 2. ...in a way that you turn the mobs around so the DPS instantly sees their backs and can start DPSing right away. Important for melee, since mobs, just like us, can't parry, block or dodge when attacked from behind.
