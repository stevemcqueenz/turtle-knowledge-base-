# Shaman tank and the upcoming tweaks

## Post 143125 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2025-08-04T17:43:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=143125#p143125 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!

Wanted to give my personal take on the topic.
I think that shaman both has OP builds (PVE resto, PVP enh-ele hybrid) which would need nerf and underperformers (mostly focusing on tank for now) which could receive some well-thought about buffs.

But not the way CC3 does it.

 **1. Threat**

Adding a flat threat value for random talents just feels lazy in my opinion.
 *Specially since with some tweaks, threat could be helped much easier imo.*

 **a)** How the elemental shields are implemented here.

Having 9 orbs is nice but it encourages you to pick a shield and keep it for a longer time.
Almost feels like a passive. Changing to LS or ES is just expensive.
 *I think it was an error to make the talent give a bunch of extra orbs instead of making the shields considerably cheaper.*

Let's say you have 3 orbs, proc interval is increased as usual and for the sake of the argument, *lightning strike, instead of consuming an orb, gives one but is only usable if you have an elemental shield active.*

Would result in a much more active gameplay.
As a tank, you can actually switch to lightning shield for threat spikes without feeling bad about the mana bar.
Or switch to earth shield without any regret when the damage is too high.
Lightning strike giving an orb back makes the 'reshielding' less frequent and the added rule, 'it can't be used while you have no shield' adds a minimal skill scaling.
You just gotta pay attention to your shield all the time.

This change would by itself raise shaman tank threat since changing to lightning shield any time would be much more feasible.
 *My point is:* why add random threat buffs to talents if you can instead make it possible for the shaman tanks to use an elemental shield more consistently which is by itself a prime threat build-up tool?

 **b)** More controversial and definitely needs some testing, but being able to use WF totem via rockbiter would solve a lot of problems. As long as threat wouldn't get over the roof. But I doubt it.
Nowadays even bears can proc WF so this should not be a problem.
Playing a melee and never proccing a WF despite having the totem on the bar feels bad.

Honestly, I think these 2 would solve all threat problems, without adding random threat values to talents.
Elemental shield change is kinda big but imo results in a much more fun gameplay.
WF change is small but has a huge effect.
WF would become the threat air totem, grace of air would kinda become a defensive one.

 **2. Mitigation**

I think armor is fine if the tank is properly equipped.
Properly equipped is a keyword, though.
Below T2,5 there are problems, the game needs more mail defensive items.
Other problem is pre-30-35 when leveling, before the talent buffs would really kick in.
 *1-2 boe shields for levelers and more +def items later would be more than welcome.*

 *Special mention:* the fallen prophet set from AQ40. Currently unusable because shares item slots with T2,5.
But T2,5 has 5 slots, the fallen prophet has 3. Just switch those gears to be used on the missing body parts and they would become super cool. Would also help with the tank's defense problems.

Other potential problem is the really lategame. Shaman tanking items kinda stop at T2,5, and T4 is here soon.
Just make sure shamans have tanking gears for higher raid tiers.

About **mitigation**.

Armor is fine if geared well, as I said. But there's a potentially good tweak.
Make the armor talents scale by agility instead of giving a flat percentual armor increase.
 *Why?*
Would reward the tank, reaching somewhat higher armor than now, turning it more into a half-bear *(no defense cap, extra block and parry in exchange of the less HP and somewhat less armor).*
Also, would balance PVP. Ele-enh is usually not that high on agility, nerfing their mitigation.

 *Shield spec:* +5% block is just sad if defense can't be capped anyway. Plus HP would have been much more relevant for that talent.

 *For example:* Instead of shield spec (+block chance, + blocked amount) a talent giving HP and percentual physical damage reduction. This would help with early game mitigation, which is a problem.
In exchange, elemental weapons rockbiter buff could be enabling (7,14,20%) WF proc chance if totem is down, since we moved the damage reduction way earlier.
This change would remove a barely useful talent (shield spec), would help with early game tanking, and would help threat a ton by adding WF procs later.

 *Stoneskin totem:* really needs late game scaling to stay relevant.
 *Healing Stream totem:* it's a strange one. It can be upgraded in deep resto but honestly, only (some) tanks use it at all. Would benefit from being included in the enhancement totem talents, as weird as it sounds.

 **3. Elemental shields**

I would not propose buffing lightning shield.
I'm neutral toward earth shield, not sure how it would affect PVP.
But water shield even with proper AP lightning strikes feels a way worse JoW. I'm not saying we should be as good at everything as other classes but mana can be a real issue, specially outside raids where you don't have all the buffs in the world and even more specially during leveling. Making the water shield procs give more mana would be extremely beneficial.

__________

 **PS:**
I don't wanna turn shaman tanks into OP monsters.
But I wanted to highlight what I think the actual problems are and giving my 2 cents in the topic.

I fully agree with your point that adding flat threat values to talents isn't the most elegant solution for Shaman tanking. Your ideas on reworking the Elemental Shields for more active threat management are genuinely excellent.

However, I want to focus first on the topic of Mitigation and Gear, as I believe this is the most fundamental issue facing Shaman tanks.

 **The Core Problem: Lack of Tank Sets**

You hit the nail on the head: *"Properly equipped is a keyword."* This is where the main problem lies:

 **1. Absence of Pure Tank Sets:**
Existing Shaman gear sets (T1, T2, etc.) primarily cater to Healer, Elemental-DPS, or Enhancement-DPS. We are missing a dedicated Mail Tank Set with adequate scaling for Stamina, Agility, Defense Rating, and Armor.

 **2. Poor Progression:**
The jump between tier sets (e.g., T1 to T2) is an enormous leap in survivability for tanks like the Warrior. For Shaman sets, the jump is often flat, and you sometimes even lose valuable stats like Agility. There is simply not enough Mail armor with sufficient Stamina and Defense to consistently minimize damage and reach defensive caps.

 **3. Addressing Endgame Sets:**
Your note on the "Fallen Prophet" set (AQ40) is brilliant. Adjusting the slot allocation of those pieces to fill gaps in the T2.5 gear would be a great, quick fix for the late-game gearing problem.

 **A Critical Missing Tool: Defensive Cooldowns**

Last but not least, the Shaman tank is also missing a strong, dedicated defensive cooldown. Every established tank class has multiple ways to hit a powerful button—be it a massive damage reduction effect (like Shield Wall), a strong self-heal, or temporary damage immunity—to survive critical phases or mitigate a healing deficit. The Shaman tank completely lacks such a life-saving ability, which severely limits their survivability during boss encounters where a *damage spike* or a *tank swap* is required. Implementing such a cooldown would be crucial to fully establishing the Shaman as a viable tank.

 **The Mitigation Solution: The Talent Dilemma**

To compensate for the missing base stats that the gear doesn't provide, a solution could be found at the talent level, similar to your suggestion but targeted more towards Defense Rating:

Defense Talent:
Paladins and Warriors have talents to increase their Defense Rating. Shaman tanks need a similar option in their tree to close the gear gaps and more reliably hit the Defense Cap.

Before we discuss complex threat mechanics, we need to ensure that the Shaman can survive as a tank in the first place. Without proper gear and a defensive cooldown, any threat improvement becomes irrelevant if the tank dies too quickly in a raid.

What are your thoughts on implementing a Defense increase via a talent and a dedicated CD first, just to offset the missing gear and core utility?

I appreciate your detailed breakdown of the two potential paths for Shaman tanking *(the "Bear Way" vs. "Plate Way").*

While I understand the simplicity of the "Bear Way" (high HP/Armor, always crittable), I must insist that the current design, where the Shaman uses a shield, naturally places it closer to the Warrior/Paladin design camp. The shield implies a reliance on block, parry, and a defense cap, and therefore, our mitigation talents should support this.

The core issue that undermines the Shaman as a viable choice, regardless of the talent path, remains the lack of Gear:

 **Gear Drought:**
There is a definite lack of proper mail tank items. Searching for Mail armor with Stamina, Agility, and Block/Dodge/Parry/Defense is frustrating, and often futile. We need dedicated gear sets and more off-Set pieces.

If you sort the database for "Mail" armor and enter the filters "Stamina 1," "Agility 1," and "Defense 1," you get a mere 5 items displayed (excluding the T2.5 Set). This doesn't change even if you remove Defense and search for Block %, Parry %, or Dodge %. Searching for gear with only Agility, Stamina, and Block/Parry/Dodge yields an equally alarmingly small number of hits.
Only the World Bosses drop one or two good items for the Shaman tank.
If a further tank set with more Stamina, Agility, and Block/Parry/Dodge were added to T1, T2, and T3, the Shaman tank would already be greatly helped in terms of gear.

 **Schaman Tank = Meme Spec?:**
The problem is that the Shaman is currently viewed as a "*meme tank spec*", which is truly a shame. A core part of the Turtle WoW experience is the promise that you can play what you love, and that every specialization is viable. If players want to commit to Shaman tanking, they should be able to find adequate gear and tools to succeed, rather than hitting a brick wall when compiling their BiS lists for different phases.
