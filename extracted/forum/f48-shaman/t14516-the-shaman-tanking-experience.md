# The shaman tanking experience

## Post 99060 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2024-07-10T00:00:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=99060#p99060 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!

First off, I'm not Shockwarden or a highly experienced shaman tank. I don't want to give such false vibes that the things I write about are words from an expert. I'm an average shaman who tried out tanking and giving my impressions about it.
 **The goal is to give some practical advices for those who also wanna give it a shot.**

This article is NOT for experienced shaman tanks who tanked raids. Those guys know this stuff better than I do.

For actual experts, I propose this (very good but refers to Vanilla, TWOW changes are not mentioned there):
<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kKQnV-kpDhU&t=1347s>

 **Note: Some of the things I mentioned are factually incorrect.**
Correction1: Rockbiter gives TPS, a slower weapon gets more threat for the hit (but if hit is missed, that's a larger time window without threat).
Correction2: Rockbiter doesn't stack with WF totem buff. WF is not good for shaman tanks.

After testing out shaman tanking I have to say that it is possible but it requires a different approach than usual tanks. In short, you have to 'outsorce' more, usually to DPS. With proper gear and enchants (this is a must - if you decide to shaman tank, you start from a disadvantage, you have to be okay with farming good items and using enchants like the +2% threat glove one) you are able to reliably keep around 3 mobs. This means that at certain pulls you will need help.

 **In general:**
 * Always check your group composition and know which classes can provide which CC. Do you have hybernate, sap, sheep, fear etc in your arsenal? If the pull seems to be too big to properly handle, ask a party member to CC one of them. Keeping the number of targets at a manageable amount is key.
 * Consider asking a melee DPS to offtank one mob if the pull is too big (even after CC). A hunter pet, a druid, a warrior, a rogue, a pala or an enh shaman should be perfectly able to keep one elite mob focused. Usually the skull. This way by the time the DPS targets 'your mobs', you've built up a high threat on them.
 * Symbols are key, specifically for a shaman tank. Always have the below symbols keybinded: 1st target, 2nd target, CC target, offtank target, a symbol to mark patrol. When skull dies, changes marks accordingly, it is your responsibility as a tank.
 * You are very weak regarding AoE threat. Ask the casters not to AoE unless it is a big pack consisting of many mobs which die fast.
 1. If you are fighting 3 elite mobs and keeping threat properly, a caster doing AoE messes up everything. Mobs will probably go rampant, people will probably die. Never allow this.
 2. If there's a pack with a bunch of non-elite mobs for example, accept your fate - you won't be tanking those properly. Open with a chain lightning, run in and help the work of the caster - use earthgrab and fire nova/magma. Earthgrab makes sure that those mobs die by the time they'd reach the caster while the fire totems help killing them. If the pack consists of many weak minions, you don't tank, you help the kiting/AoE process.
 3. If there are some elites and many trash, focus on those elites. Tank those and help casters clear up the trash via earthbind and fire nova/magma.
 * You don't have a taunt but you have many ways to generate a lot of threat if you are well-geared/enchanted (it is a must). Discuss things with the party. Tell them, that if they pull aggro, they should run to you and stop damaging immediately. Their threat will drop. You take over the threat. As a shaman tank you will never get aggro back if someone pulls and then goes all in on the DPS-meter while the mob is running towards him. Make it clear that if aggro happens, DPS has to stop on the target.
 * Forget stoneskin totem 90% of the time. The prerequisite of the whole shaman tanking thing is to be geared enough to be able to take some hits. You need strength totem much more.
 * Both grace of air and windfury totem are a good choice. Grace of air gives you dodge and crit chance which helps with flurry and clearcasting. However, people underestimate windfury for shaman tanks. It helps melee threat, including you. The thing is that your primary job is to keep the healer alive. If you put down a windfury totem, melee DPS can aggro much more frequently but it is fine as they should be able to take some hits. If you use windfury, you risk losing threat to melee for added DPS and for making sure that casters and healers have a real hard time taking aggro.
 * In very situational cases you can use tranquil air totem. If you don't talent totem radius increase, your totems will have a 20 yard radius. If some casters are really problematic, you can put down a tranquil air in the back and run towards the target. If the caster is within totem range and casting range while you are out of totem range, you reduced caster threat by 20%. It costs your grace of air or windfury totem though.
 * Spamming fire nova and stoneclaw totem right after eachother generates some AoE threat. But it is expensive, has high CD and the generated threat is low. Use only if you really need that AoE threat.

One last thing regarding this part. Again, know your teammates and have a general knowledge about how other classes work. It helps if someone pulls aggro too frequently. If this person is using a specific ability, you might ask them to avoid using that. Have ShaguDPS installed so you see who does what when dealing damage, it helps analyizing why certain aggroes happen.
For the below: any of the below abilities can be fine during a run if they don't cause overaggro. It is only needed to intervene if the below things cause problems during the run.

Some examples:
 * A warlock using searing pain. It's a no-no. Also, warlocks usually have no way to reduce their threat. You might want to ask them to start with curse and corruption so you have time to establish threat before their big hitters come.
 * A shaman opening with a chain lightning is a no. Ask him to hold back a bit so you can establish threat. Also, a shaman using earth shock or having rockbiter weapon on can be a problem.
 * Shadow priest using vampiric embrace. Passive lifegain is good but it has a lot of threat. Advised to use it only at bosses.
 * Warrior spamming heroic strike. Honestly, just let it be, he should be able to tank the mob.
 * Hunter pets growling. Should never happen unless there's an emergency.
 * Pala having righteous fury on. Probably a mistake. Also, if you have threat problems, ask the pala to put salvation on the problematic person (check threatmeter to know who's problematic).
 * Mage or warlock spamming AoE if they see more than 1 mob. That will ruin your tanking (see earlier part).
 * Rogue for any reason using agitating poison.

 **Regarding your toolkit:**

You have earth shock for spell threat every 5 seconds - use at least 3 ranks. Rank 1 for interrupt, mid-rank for threat spike if you don't need to pump threat and max rank if you need as much threat as possible.
You have flame shock which helps you keeping threat on a mob for 12 seconds. Use it only if you don't think you need to 'taunt' within 5 seconds. You can use it on a secondary target to keep threat on it or on your main target to maximize threat. I think max rank alone is enough.
You have elemental shields. For lightning, use at least three ranks again. Max rank for big threat, first rank if you are really low on mana and an inbetween solution. Honestly I mostly advise lightning shield for leveling or when you need max threat. I don't really like it since it is a bit unreliable which target gets the threat.
Water shield is my usual go-to elemental shield when tanking. As long as something is punching you, you get a bunch of mana. Keep it on all the time if possible. Honestly, water shield helped that much that I frequently had close to full mana when the mages asked to stop for a drink. Just don't overuse your abilities and your mana is spared. No matter if you keep threat by 3% or 80%, if you consistently keep it, go cheap, slack some shocks etc and recharge mana for the next pull.
Earth shield is your go to shield for real big hitters, usually certain bosses. Use only if target hits REALLY big.
You also have stormstrike which is a very good tanking tool. Put it to a big button on PFUI so you always see how much time you have left until you can use it again. Whenever I pull, I stormstrike skull first to initiate threat. If you need to maximize threat on a target, just stormstrike, earthshock and...
And bloodlust. As a tank, this ability is for you. Use it when you immediately need a threat spike. 20% haste is really nice, it helps a lot.
And of course rockbiter.

That's your basic tanking tool.
However, consumables/engineering items and racials can further help. If you wanna shaman tank, you have to get engineering. As a D tier tank you have to take every option to make yourself more viable.
Just make sure that you have some target dummies and sappers at your disposal. Don't overuse them. Would zero down your wallet and you'd totally lose motivation to do this. Only use them if you find it extremely important that in this specific situation aggro has to be kept. Don't use it everytime you lose aggro or you go bankrupt. Unless you're mega-rich then do whatever you want.

For racials: each race has something going for it.
Orcs mostly have the weapon skill. If you find a good, fast axe (remember, rockbiter has a static added threat, you need a fast weapon - for a shaman tank a weapon with above 2 sec weapon speed just doesn't exist) that weapon skill can help a lot. Easier itemization etc. However class changes wil in theory change how weapon skill is working. It is possible that orc will be the only suboptimal choice for shaman tanking very soon.
Taurens have added health and war stomp (if only spirit link were castable on yourself...). Added health is always nice on a fragile tank and war stomp can help if you are overwhelmed with mobs.
My personal favourite though is the troll. Berserking. You basically receive a second bloodlust on a 3 minute CD. These two make sure that you can receive extra haste almost any time, so you can consistently rely on it whenever you need more threat.

This is the talent tree I was using:
![Image](https://i.postimg.cc/J0rsJJm8/Talent.png)

It has strengths and weaknesses:
+ Gives both good melee DPS and also a good damage coming from shocks
+ Has all the defensives I need
+ I consider stormstrike and bloodlust totally necessary tanking abilities

 - Mana problems have to be solved by clearcasting and water shield. Totems are expensive and have small radius.
 - Hit chance.

Hit chance is the really big one. I've never raided as a shaman tank and now I'd need a bunch of hit and spell hit to punch reliably. However, taking that talent in the restoration tree takes away too many crucial talents.
Right now I wait and see what class changes bring and if you can get hit+spell hit without sacrificing the core of what you are trying to do, I might try that out.

For items/stats:
Get some intellect but only until you feel comfortable tanking if you don't overspend your resources and water shield is recharging you all the time. Once it's manageable, ignore int.
Agility and stamina every day. Agility helps you dodge (you are quite good at dodging in general), gives some armor and gives melee crit which makes things cheaper via clearcasting and flurry helps with threat. But top prio has to be stamina, you are the fragilest tank after all.
Strength to some degree for bigger hits (to get good threat both from rockbiter and from the damage you deal) and bigger blocked amount. But don't forget you'll usually have a strength totem down, meaning that you'll magically get 88 strength anytime.

Melee crit. Very important.
Haste -> Almost as important. For you melee crit basically gives haste via flurry. But still, the faster you hit the targets, the more rockbiter 'stacks' you apply. As a shaman tank the name of the game is hitting them as fast as possible. Fast weapon, bloodlust, flurry, haste items: you keep aggro by distributing packages of threat super fast.
Dodge is also good, specially via enchants. Get the Kara enchant which gives 2 dodge to a head or leg item. With the 5 dodge from talents, the 2x2% kara enchant dodge and the cloak enchant dodge you'll start off with 10% base dodge. Since you are kinda fragile, the best thing you can do is to avoid hits totally. Unlike bears and warriors, you don't wanna be hit for getting rage.
Before I forget: thorium spikes onto the shield! You need that thorn-effect. Also, don't consider speed enchant on the boot, you need the stamina one. Threat enchant on gloves is a must. Other than that, what you deem the best for yourself. I use agility on my belt but I can easily see someone needing the int more. You really need the enchants to fight the handicap of tanking as a shaman, don't slack it off.

In my opinion:
stam>agi>melee crit>haste> dodge/parry>strength>int

I advise using a weapon swap macro and having at least two (enchanted) weapons. One for threat, one for survival (added armor, parry chance, whatever makes you die harder). If there's a big problem, switch to survival weapon.
Don't slack off consumes. Have health pots available, keybinded.

 **Lastly some tips how different classes can help you:**

 **Druid:** Generic buffs, thorns, innervate. If the guy is a DPS you can ask him anytime to bear-offtank a target. Also roots outdoors and hybernate.
 **Hunter:** Helps with problematic pulls, can handle his threat, pet can OT, hunter can kite, can give trueshot aura (sometimes). Also, freezing trap is a very good CC to take away one mob from the pull.
 **Mage:** Drink machine, intellect buff, sheep and freezes. Don't underestimate slows and freezes, even encourage the mage to use them if you face any trouble with keeping threat.
 **Rogue:** Sap, can offtank,, best stuns, can handle his threat. Interrupts.
 **Paladin:** Very good blessings, also the option to put salvation on an overeager DPS. Can stun a target and has extra utility against undeads. Ask actively for blessing of freedom if you get a lot of movement impairment in a dungeon.
 **Warlock:** Ask for imp buff and healthstone. King of fears, just make sure you cleared up enough space and pull back if fear is needed. Also banish. Never trust enslave demon.
 **Priest:** Shackle undead, priest buffs, fear. Also, if priest is holy, ask for the champion buff which gives attack and spell power. You need every bit of threat you can get. Also you don't use rage, you can be shielded. Ask for shields.
 **Shaman:** Discuss who does which totems, both grace of air and windfury can be huge. King of ranged interrupts.
 **Warrior:** Interrupts, shout, offtanking, fear. Give him a WF and in exchange ask him to harmstring melee targets so even if you guys lose aggro to a caster, mobs are slow and die by the time they get there.
