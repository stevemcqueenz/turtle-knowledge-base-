# Patch 1.18.1 — Itemization Changelog

## Post 168272 by Dragunovi (Turtle WoW Team) [STAFF] — 2026-02-15T17:57:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=168272#p168272 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

![Image](https://turtlecraft.gg/images/IMG_20260215_181058_108.jpg)

Greetings! We're happy to present to you the Itemization Changelog for **Patch 1.18.1:** Nightmares of Ursol.

As always, keep in mind all changes are subject to change.

 **Item Additions:**

We've added new items and new item sets to the following sources and locations:
- Quest Rewards (Moonwhisper Coast)

- Rare Creatures in the New Zone (Moonwhisper Coast)

- New Dungeons (Windhorn Canyon and Frostmane Hollow)

- New Raid (Timbermaw Hold)

- Rare Bind-On-Equip Items from Non-Boss Creatures

- Raid Extension (Onyxia's Lair and Blackwing Lair)

- Player versus Player Combat

- Survival Rework

- Reputations (Earthen Ring and Draenei Exiles)

- Secret Methods
 **New Stat: Fortune**

 **Fortune** is a new stat that reads as the following:
“Increases your chance to trigger chance based item effects by X%.”
This stat is quite unique in the way that it does not function on its own but serves to amplify any other piece of gear that has a chance to trigger an effect, working on both "procs per minute" or a flat percentage chance items.

Due to its compounding power with multiple chance based items, the Fortune stat can only be found on **Necklaces**, **Cloaks** and **Trinkets**.

 **New Stat: Avoidance**

 **Avoidance** is a new stat that reads as the following:
“Reduces the damage you take from area of effect attacks by X%”
First added as an effect provided to all Hunter and Warlock pets for them to survive massive area of effect attacks, its now an effect acquirable by you, albeit in smaller amounts.

Due to the possibility of nullifying most area based effects when stacked, the Avoidance stat can only be found on **Cloaks** and **Rings**.

 **Discipline Alternative Tier Sets + Shadow Tier 2.5 Set**

The rework done to Priest's Discipline specialization to be a Smite slinging damage and support specialization has opened a gap in itemization for the specialization due to the lack of Holy damage oriented gear, we're remedying this by adding tier sets tailored around Discipline's stat requirements and set bonuses that directly interact with its playstyle.

In addition to this, now that all three Priest specializations have their own tier sets, we're also adding a Shadow tier set to the Temple of Ahn'Qiraj. As the Tier 2.5 sets tend to differ from the norm, the Shadow Tier 2.5 focuses on critical strike chance with the set bonuses built around that fact.

 **Crafted Item Upgrades**

We're introducing an experimental new way of keeping crafted equipment relevant. Specific bosses will now have a chance to additionally drop a new upgrade item, such as a sharpening stone. This item will be usable on specific items to upgrade them to the overall power level of the raid its been acquired from, if not slightly higher and include new effects.

The caveat with this is that you need to be proficient in the profession that's required to make that item, such as requiring Swordsmithing if its an upgraded crafted sword, meaning these professions stay relevant for longer with special benefits should you acquire these upgrades.

This will initially be added to only to **Blackwing Lair** with it expanding to later tiers depending on how it is received.

 **Rogue and Warlock Class Weapons**

 **Timbermaw Hold** brings new class weapons with its opening, introducing new quest starters that will make Rogues and Warlocks embark on an adventure, eventually rewarding you with weapons that will last you for a long time, similiarly to **Benediction/Anathema** and **Rhok'delar/Lok'delar**.

How you start these questlines, we'll leave to you to discover. It is half of the fun after all.

Adding two new core statistics at the same time, alongside item revamps, may already create major balance adjustments and community friction. Before introducing additional systemic layers, it may be worth addressing existing gameplay inconsistencies — particularly within Balance Druid.

First, the 16% spell hit requirement is extremely difficult to reach before AQ40/Naxx gear. This creates a progression barrier unique to Balance compared to other casters. From a player perspective, reaching the hit cap should feel like a meaningful performance milestone. However, because the threshold is so high and the spec remains heavily dependent on Eclipse RNG, even achieving 16% hit does not translate into the kind of noticeable power spike one would reasonably expect.

Balance currently relies entirely on Eclipse procs just to remain competitive with other casters at comparable gear levels. It is an extremely proc-dependent specialization with significant RNG influence.
In practice, Wrath is frequently cast not as a true rotational DPS choice, but primarily to fish for Eclipse procs. This creates an internal imbalance between Arcane and Nature within a system that is supposed to represent duality.

Despite being one of the most mechanically demanding caster specs to execute optimally, Balance consistently delivers lower overall results than Mages or Warlocks at comparable gear levels.

With the announced update, the increased focus on Starfire further risks deepening this already existing asymmetry. Strengthening Arcane scaling while Eclipse still alternates between Arcane and Nature may unintentionally create a preferred element within a system built around opposition and rotation.

Additionally, the latest raid content (K40) includes multiple Arcane-immune encounters, meaning that reinforcing Arcane disproportionately may not translate into practical effectiveness in real scenarios where Nature damage is required.

More broadly, a proc-based specialization where both core spells do not equally benefit from and contribute to the central mechanic (Eclipse) weakens the intended cyclical, dual-element identity of Balance. From a design perspective, this asymmetry is a structural concern.
When a specialization requires higher mechanical investment but consistently yields lower output at parity gear, it creates a risk/reward imbalance that discourages mastery.
