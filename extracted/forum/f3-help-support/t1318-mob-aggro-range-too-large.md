## Post 8051 by Roxanneflowers (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2020-10-08T12:52:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=8051#p8051 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Vanilla aggro radius is large by default.
Stealth is basically a "stance change" akin to a Warrior switching between stances (Battle, Defensive, Fury).  Without any supporting talents or equipped items, you'll aggro stuff at the same distance in and out of Stealth.

What causes the aggro radius to "shrink" is the relative levels between your Rogue and the NPC(s).  Without any talent points invested into Master of Deception, it's just a straight your level vs their level comparison *while you're in the frontal arc of a hostile NPC*.  The rear arc is obviously different, but that's the basic formulation.

What Master of Deception does is increase your level relative to the NPCs.
A Level 20 Rogue vs a Level 18 NPC is +2 Levels to the NPC while in Stealth.
 5/5 Master of Deception will increase that to +7 Levels to the NPC.
There are items that can be equipped which will add +1 Level each to your Stealth for this relative levels comparison.

The important thing to remember is that relative levels in your favor will NEVER cause the aggro radius of an NPC to "shrink" all the way down to zero.  That's because the relative levels difference doesn't ***subtract*** from the aggro radius ... it ***multiplies*** it (or divides, when it's in your favor).  So rather than being a case of +/-5 feet of aggro radius per relative level difference, it's more like a x1.1/x0.9 (I don't know the exact numbers, I'm just using these to get the point across) per +/- relative level difference.

With Stealth Rank 1 you basically need to have 10+ relative levels difference before you can approach hostile NPCs from the front close enough to get into melee/pickpocket range without being detected.  In the example you've provided, you're at +9 relative levels (character level plus Master of Deception).  With a high enough relative levels differential, the multiplier/divisor for aggro radius (ala perception) gets "shrunk" enough to allow you to approach close enough to Do Stuff™ with relative impunity ... but if you get "too close" (or let them walk into you) they'll still aggro onto you because while the perception radius to aggro may become quite small, it will never shrink all the way down to zero.

Hope that helps.

## Post 8070 by Roxanneflowers (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2020-10-09T01:16:00Z

Some mobs have modifiers on their own aggro radius.  The tells for these tend to be in the names.  [Starving](https://vanillawowdb.com/?search=starving) is the most obvious one (like Starving Mountain Lion), but there are others that do the same thing.  Mobs with modifiers like [Starving](https://vanillawowdb.com/?search=starving) will actually have an increased aggro radius, as if they were +5 Levels (or more) above their actual combat Level.  Basic idea is they're more desperate for a meal so they're more aggressive towards the PC(s), hence larger aggro radius.

For a lot of +0 relative level mobs, getting within 20 feet of them is enough to aggro them (usually).
Some mobs will aggro at 25 feet of distance ... and some will aggro from even further away than that (as detailed above).
