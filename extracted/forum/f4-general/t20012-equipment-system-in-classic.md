Greetings!

To state this is a mere hypothesis not a truth. Now this being said I think I found a major problem with the classic system to gear up and tiers.

One thing at a time...

Dungeons vs Professions:

If YOU find yourself enjoying the dungeon system then you have a way to gear up. However if you find yourself enjoying to gather stuff grinding for a chill life then you have the option to buy gear from the professions. It's the same as getting gear from pvp by now I'm lvl 30+ and haven't even earned the chance to enter the pvp hall of stormwind, besides you still have to lvl up and grind some reputation in order to buy certain pvp rewards. For that reason you still have to choose: to grind for buying equipment or to grind dungeons to get them. For once you'd say we have a clear point on what the game is about however...

Professions vs Dungeons:

Every profession that creates equipment (armor, accessories and weapons), are secondary. We must be sincere, they are not principal but secondary. Which are the principal professions? They are alchemy (for potions), enchantment (for enchantments of all gears), and even secondary professions are actually principal they are cooking and first aid (I know...). So professions that creates equipment are not an obligation for anybody, nor for any stage of the game. Because you will always find the way to acquire new equipment.

You guys can read all over the place people that states professions to produce equipment as not so important as they should be. In addition it seems they become useless once you hit the late game. Professions can only produce extra objects that will provide benefits or enhancements but their main function is over when you get to raids or pvp final sets.

This is the question I want to present you:

Are you guys willing to recognize that classic has its problems in what it was?

To tell the truth this problems can be fixed, however be honest with yourselves and get to reason if this problems who lacks of all fun are fun because they're classic even if they aren't at all.

There is a solution for this that will change almost nothing, for how you do things, but will give back to professions the role they were intended for.

Turn all gear from pvp and dungeons into cosmetics, and make a system similar to the key hole that comes with your character, to receive points or coins for every cosmetic you unlock in a wide range of leveling. This points can be used to upgrade the gear produced by the professions in game. So every 10 levels you have to buy a new basic gear from professions, and then go do whatever content you want to earn the elements required to upgrade that gear 9 levels: 1-9 / 11-19 / 21-29 / 31-39 / 41-49 / 51-59. So even items dropped by monsters in the world and world bosses and objects from quests can be turned into a content for real, to compete with dungeons, pvp and grinding. That way you can just play the story and for what you get completing the whole world quests there you'll get the points from cosmetics required to upgrade gear from professions, which you will get by defeating elites and world bosses.

This is a real turn of the table it doesn't look like vanilla and that's ok. However I can't agree more because I never felt the interest into leveling up blacksmith, or engineering to produce any equipment for my character or to buy that equipment because it was "necessary".

The moment you understand this kind of stuff one has to be very careful to not ruin other's fun. Because as it was stated above people might find entertaining another aspect of the game. And the final question is that...

Do you really want to make professions truly necessary?

**Hey, thanks for sharing your thoughts! There's a lot here, and I appreciate how deeply you've thought about the topic.**

Let me start with something very real:
 **Have you ever tried crafting the Enchanted Thorium Set?**
It’s a blue armor set that uses **8 Arcanite Bars per piece** — plus a pile of other expensive mats.
And what do you get for that massive investment? A mediocre piece of gear that’s instantly replaced by any decent dungeon drop.

The only reason someone crafts that set is:
- a) by mistake;

- b) because they were trolled;

- c) or because they were trying to roleplay as an NPC blacksmith.
 **That’s not just inefficient — it’s broken design.**

So yes, I 100% agree: **Classic has deep flaws in how it handles crafting professions — especially gear-focused ones.**

Where we differ is in the solution.

You’re suggesting replacing gear progression from dungeons and PvP with crafted gear — and turning dungeon/PvP rewards into cosmetics only.

That’s a bold idea, and I respect the creativity. But from my point of view, it just flips the imbalance instead of solving it.

 **Here’s one of the biggest issues I see with that approach:**
If crafted gear becomes the only real source of power, **everyone will just pick the two professions that benefit their own class/spec the most**.
And that’s a death sentence for player economy, specialization, and cooperation.

 **What happens to professions like Enchanting, Alchemy, or Engineering?**
They don’t make armor or weapons — would they become irrelevant?
Or would you give players access to all professions? If so, why even have professions as a meaningful choice at all?

 **That’s the danger: a system that pushes people into total self-sufficiency and kills interaction.**

My vision is the opposite — to create a system where professions thrive through *connection, interdependence, and identity*, not isolation.

 **What I believe in:**

- **Crafted items are BoE** — so we can trade, specialize, and help each other.

- **Recipes are BoP** — so progression still matters and must be earned.

- **High-end gear requires rare PvE materials** — keeping crafting tied to actual gameplay.

- **Every profession has a role — not just the ones that make gear.**
 **TL;DR:** Professions should be meaningful, not mandatory. And above all, they should bring players together — not push them apart.
