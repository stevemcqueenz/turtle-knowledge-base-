Any duo, by definition, will give adequate service for normal open-world questing since that part of the game's designed to be solo'd.  The balance druid+ resto option will be a little more limited in capability than some other options because no tank, but doesn't have particularly good crowd control, either.

A healer/tank duo gives you the ability to duo many instances, or invite maybe a third and do nearly all of them.  It allows you to build groups very quickly since the "hard to find" jobs are accounted for.  My wife and I have a holy-priest/prot-paladin pair, and it has given excellent service.  As alts we have a pair of druids--resto/feral, for stealth trips--and those also work decently, albeit not quite as strong as the first pair.  The feral druid is however much simpler to operate than the paladin.  Playing the druid after being on the paladin for awhile feels practically like laying off.  That could be important to a new player, as could having the ability to re-spec to a known enjoyed build (balance in your case) if the bear build just doesn't "work" for you.

Hunters are under-rated duo characters.  Their exceptionally strong crowd control and split-pulling capability is a huge asset for dealing with packs of elites.  They do very well with healer support.

Welcome to Turtle!
You are right in that Vanilla balance druid is very-very different than what you probably was in later expansions. While the Turtle team made some nice changes that made it less terrible as it was in true Vanilla, it is still not too good. That does not mean you can't have fun playing one, but be aware you'll have to drink a lot between mobs. The good part is, that you can heal without changing your equipment (or even spec) for a long time, and that works well in a 2-player coop scenario.
Generally a DPS (or tank) class + any other that is capable of healing will make the leveling process really great and you can do almost all elite content as well.
So something like druid(feral)+druid(balance); warrior+any of [priest,druid,shaman,paladin], paladin+any class; shaman+any class; etc
Of course you can also have a great time playing 2 DPS classes, with the small downside of being more vulnerable. But e.g. 2 mages, mage+warlock, hunter+something can be very effective in minimising kill time thus also reducing incoming damage to either of you.
For the professions, I would say it depends on the classes chosen. If you share the same armour type, then I would say it is probably worth to have one appropriate crafter in the duo [Leatherworking,Tailoring,Blacksmithing], as it can help a lot with you two competing for the same armour. If you do not share the same armour type, then it a little less so, yet Tailoring can help all classes with bags and cloaks, and Blacksmithing can help out (thou just a bit) all melee classes with weapons.
Alchemy is a great profession for any and all classes, and definitely helps both of you.
As you both are somewhat new to Vanilla, I would suggest not to take Enchanting, as that can be a frustrating skill to level with you.
Engineering can be also a choice, even though people think of it mostly for the PvP aspects, but it has many nice additions that can help out in PvE.

But, keep in mind, you can't really go wrong in the pairing though, as most of the leveling is soloable for all classes (it's just faster or slower, safer or more hazardous, depending on the class), so having any 2 members in a permanent group will work out for sure. The most important is to have fun and enjoy the journey.
