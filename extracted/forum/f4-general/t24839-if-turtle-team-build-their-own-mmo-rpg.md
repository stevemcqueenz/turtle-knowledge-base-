Legally speaking, the Unreal launcher belongs to them. It just happens to be compatible with Blizzard's data files. Blizzard can sue them for using those files, but if they replace them with completely original ones, then it's a different game. The law is very clear that you can't copyright a look and feel, nor broad concepts. That's where the term "Legally Distinct" comes in. Something can be based on the same ideas, but if it's not a *direct* copy, it's not infringing.

Incidentally, Blizzard themselves have used that method. Both Warcraft and Starcraft originated with Blizzard being unable to get the licenses to Warhammer Fantasy and Warhammer 40k. So they just sort of...did it anyway, with the same basic look and feel (ever wonder why there's so many massive shoulderpads?), but enough differences to not get sued.
