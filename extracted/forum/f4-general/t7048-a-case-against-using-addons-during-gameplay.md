# A case against using addons during gameplay

## Post 44714 by Papapepi — 2023-05-06T17:30:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=44714#p44714 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hi all,

firstly, everybody should play the game they want to play it. Some prefer endgame, some prefer other stuff. Does not matter at all. All written here are my experiences with the game, and you should play it the way you choose to. That is the beauty of this game, after all.

I noticed that every time I started to play WoW again, I got bored with it rather quickly. I usually stopped progressing at level 20-30. It just became boring. And after attempt 5 I started questioning why. And I realized that the reason was that I was not really playing the game. I was following the arrow pointing me in the direction of mobs and other resources. I was not really playing the game. I was playing catch the arrow. And that becomes boring really quickly. I had no idea what the quests really were and the overall size of the world. It was all foreign to me.

So I came to a decision. I removed most of the addons I had installed. All of them are amazing tools. Such as pfQuest. An absolute astounding addon. It just wasn't for me. All of a sudden, I felt naked. I felt like I was truly present in the world. No arrow points me in the correct direction (and yes, I know you can disable that arrow, that's not really the point here). No markers on the map showing me where the mobs are. It felt mysterious again. It felt like I am discovering the game from a completely different perspective.

I started reading the quests. Really reading them. Looking for direction on where to go. All of a sudden, I started exploring. I wasn't chasing levels like that was the primary goal of this game. I don't care about endgame. I was not looking at the right side of my screen where the quests were. The only thing I see now is the world and my character. No distractions.

I also installed AI Voiceover and that pushed it to the limit. The immersion felt real. I read and listed to every thing I am able to find. I know that I am repeating myself, but this is a completely new game to me now. And I love it!

I really became to appreciate the game much more. The world is so much bigger when you are not chasing dots on the screen and min/maxing every step. I realize that some prefer this, and more power to you. But if you are a bit like me and love the story telling, I really advise you to try it out.

I found a crazy cat lady in a house which I have never seen before :). I go into houses and check out things, which I never did before because it was not on the path that an addon said is the most optimized one.

Not only that, but I also stopped listening to podcasts, other music or watching videos in picture in picture mode while playing. Furthermore, I was used to watching Youtube or TV Shows while playing and affectively being AKF to some extent. Which was fine for things like gathering etc. But it also spilled into questing. Because I would need to constantly pause videos. When I now play I am completely present. I listen to the music that is in the game (which is just perfect). I listen to the environment (birds chirping and wind howling). I feel like I am walking through Loch Modan. And I know this sounds silly but this how I feel :)

I still have a couple of addons what I use, but those are mostly small tweaks to the UI. My UI still looks pretty much the same as the core game does.

I liked the game a lot. But now I actually love this game. I am leveling up very slowly, taking my time to level up professions. Not because I need them, or I want to make some coins. But because it adds to the game and the world. I can't wait to log in and see where will I go next.

The only gripe I have with the game are mounts. I would love to be able to get a mount at level 20 like in Wrath, because walking everywhere gets really annoying quickly. I noticed that some people have turtles, but I have no idea what it takes to get that. And traveling with mounts (if these was the case, at getting it at 20 should be noticeably faster). That is the only thing I would change, and I believe it would not take away from the game. It would add to it. Not everything vanilla is the ultimate best thing. Some changes would definitely make the game better.

Enough rambling :). I wanted to share this with you, and maybe you had the same experience, or maybe this will convince you to maybe give it a go.

## Post 44722 by Rat2156 (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2023-05-06T18:14:00Z

Addons as a whole dilute the experience so much that a lot of players are completely unable to accomplish much without them.
It's even worse in retail where people don't know how to follow the already heavily handholdy quest markers without arrows telling them too, are unable to think about gearing without an addon feeding the data of every item to a simcraft bot for them, are unable to play their class without weakauras telling them what to do at any point, and are unable to fight bosses and clear dungeons without DBM screaming at them for every mechanic.

It's to the point where many players will claim that the game is unplayable without addons even though they've never tried it.
In truth, once you get rid of the crutches, you quickly adapt to playing the game without, as it forces you to be more perceptive and to think for yourself, which in turn very quickly helps you improve farther that you could have gotten without addons. I genuinely recommend to people to disable their addons for a bit, it will be rough at the start, but they quickly will end up both enjoying the game more and getting better at it.

In a sense, it's only when you start playing the game without (or with few)  addons and without relying on guides that you get to truly enjoy it. It's a shame so many will never experience it. I used to have fun in mythic raids on retail, but that simply got eclipsed later by the fun we had clearing the mythic raids in a full team raiding completely blind(no use of any outside info) and without addons. The process of figuring things out on our own was incredible, and the satisfaction of getting cutting edge truly on our own felt exceptional
