At its core, AI is not artistic. It MAKES, but it doesn't do so in a way that's expressive or personal.

**Hey Bigsmerf,**

Before anything else, I want to make something clear: I like you. We’ve been part of the same community for a long time, and you’ve always been respectful and polite in discussions. Even though I disagree with you on this topic, that doesn’t change anything.

Regarding energy costs, water usage, or even the insane prices of GPUs and memory hardware — all of this is visible evidence of a **structural shift in society**. These kinds of shifts happen from time to time. They always have.

During the English Industrial Revolution, it was the same story: discomfort, resistance, fear, and adaptation. Professions disappeared, new ones emerged, and the world kept moving forward. The idea of permanent job stability is an illusion. It never truly existed. What exists is constant adaptation, and that’s exactly what we’re experiencing right now.

As for environmental concerns, as harsh as it may sound, they are also self-regulating. Species adapt or go extinct. This applies to every living creature on the planet — intelligent or not. We are not outside of nature; we are part of it. What we’re witnessing is a natural and inevitable evolutionary process of our own species.

When it comes to artists, they are never going to become irrelevant. Creativity has never been more necessary than it is today. What AI is doing is giving people the ability to put their ideas to the test and visualize them. Many highly creative people never had the technical artistic skills to draw, animate, or write well — and now they can actively participate in the industry.

That can create fear or insecurity, which is understandable. But AI will never replace 100% of a truly innovative, original, human-made work.

And for me, this is the most important point:
AI is, at its core, a very advanced form of *autocomplete*. It works by making assumptions and relies on a very limited form of memory. When a person is genuinely committed to making something work, they iterate, rewrite, adjust, and rework the content repeatedly until it becomes what they want.

At that stage, in terms of effort and volume of work, it’s often not very different from doing everything alone. The difference is that AI helps organize ideas and improve clarity — but the intent, direction, and final decisions are still human.

We disagree, and that’s fine. I don’t see this as the death of creativity. I see it as another difficult, uncomfortable, and inevitable transformation of our time.

Because even while using AI, I worked on these ideas, revised the text, and shaped the message. And this, to me, is the core idea that many people here don’t seem to accept.
