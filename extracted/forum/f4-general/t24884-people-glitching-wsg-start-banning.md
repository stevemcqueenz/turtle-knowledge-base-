“**WSG jumps were never intended, so they should be removed**” is a bad argument.

Almost every competitive game in history has high-skill mechanics that started as unintended, but became core parts of gameplay:

- Bunnyhopping & rocket jumping (Quake/TF2)

- Strafe-jumping (CS)

- Wavedashing (Smash Melee)

- Creep pulling & aggro tricks (DotA)

- Mutalisk stacking (StarCraft BW)

- Slope boosting in Titanfall 2

- Tap-strafing in Apex/Titanfall
If developers removed everything unintended, these games would lose the very depth that made them great.

WoW is no different.
Classic/Vanilla are full of emergent mechanics Blizzard never planned but players turned into real skill expression:

- Heroic Strike queuing

- Spell batching tricks (In 1.12 you could "jump" cancel the stun of charges/intercept, you still can vanish incoming CCs like death-coil or rocket-helm, you can still "target dummy" the hunter trap)

- wsg / ab / av jumps

- Countless PvP techs like LoS jukes and timing abuses
These weren’t designed — but they became core to how Vanilla actually plays.

WSG jumps are the same category.
They don’t make you invulnerable, they don’t break the map, and they have counterplay.
What they do is reward:

- mechanical skill

- map knowledge

- movement mastery

- creative flag-carrying routes

- coordinated counterplay
Without jumps, FCing becomes almost entirely a gear/gold check. With jumps, it has a real skill ceiling and enables even green geared players to pick up the flag — just like every other competitive game.

If “it wasn’t intended” was a valid reason to delete things, WoW would lose half of the mechanics that define Vanilla gameplay today.

Unintended ≠ bad.
Unintended is where most of the depth in competitive games comes from.

Was it ever intended that raids leave two-thirds of the trash mobs alive in Molten Core? Even Warcraft Logs—the largest parsing site—requires guilds to kill a minimum number of mobs just to make runs count.

Was it intended that players use Invis Pots to skip entire sections of Blackwing Lair?

Unintended ≠ bad.
Unintended is where most of the depth in competitive games comes from.
