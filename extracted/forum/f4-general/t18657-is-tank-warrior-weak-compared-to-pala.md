There are some topics I want to add.
Atreidon is totally right stating that warrior toolkit is better suited for tanking per se but during raids most available buffs and utilities from the game are at hand.
Raid members with fear ward (specially now that all priests have it) and party members with tremor totem make sure that you are well-versed against fear even as a paladin. The key here is accepting that you lack a tool but counting on your party members to help with it. With that help, besides manaburners, paladin is equally well-versed for tanking raids in my opinion.
Dungeons are a bit different since 4 random team members are not a proof that you'll have a toolkit against fear. But at dungeons fears are 1. rare 2. don't punish you that much (DPSs getting hit for 4-5 seconds is not a wipe per se).

Another topic is dungeon tanking vs raid tanking. Those two things are totally different and people rarely mention this. You need a different set of skills for the two (even for high-end dungeons which you asked about).
Dungeon tanks mostly handle a bigger pack of small-hitter mobs (specially at late game) while the bosses are not that punishing.
Raid tanks usually focus on 1-2 mobs and their job is to produce as much threat as possible while making it possible for the healers to keep them alive.

Palas, as a mostly aoe-designed tanks are far superior for endgame dungeons, because they have the perfect toolkit for a smaller army of small-hitter mobs. Remember: at dungeons, the real challenge is to get over those packs, the bosses are never that hard.
It is still perfectly possible to dungeon tank well as a warrior but as far as I see, warrior tanks tend to have much more issues with packs of trash while prot palas even have a tendency to run around and pull 2-3 of them just to kill all at once.

Threat-wise I'm not really sure, haven't checked logs for a really long time.

In this server prot pala survivability got buffed but even then, I'd trust a warrior tank more during an enraged raid boss fight if (and only if) the tank made sure that his long CDs are all available.

In my opinion, for dungeon tanking you could face two issues as a prot pala:
 1. Mana - but with proper gear and knowing how to play the class, it should not be a problem. It's rather a learning curve. People usually start by spamming max rank consec and eventually learn how to actually use pala tanks.
 2. Range - Your judgement is 10 yards. You'll either need to trust party members at tricky pulls or have some dynamites in the brain, as Charge Man Ken did.
tldr: Getting ranged pulls doesn't come naturally.

One last thing to consider: races.
If you're a pala, you play as a human, a dwarf or a high elf. It can be totally okay.
But if you doubt you want to play Alliance for whatever reason or want to have other specific racials (for whatever reason), warrior is the way to go, much more races can play as a warrior.
Just never go gnome warrior, that is an abomination.
