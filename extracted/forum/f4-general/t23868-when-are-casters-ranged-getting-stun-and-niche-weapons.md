## Post 170825 by Atreidon (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2026-03-14T20:13:00Z

Melees have it, why can't casters. Many people think this way, but there is a very good reason for why melee weapon tend to have more flashy effects.

Opportunity Cost

A ranged attacker can easily spam attacks without risking himself and wear opponents down while maintaining formation in pvp, or by completely kiting the enemy with 0 recourse in pve. A melee on the other hand has to at least risk himself taking some damage in return when using for instance a weapon with chance to stun.
Imagine you run after a mage who is not only constantly slowing you and blinking, but you get stunned on top of everything.

Secondly, a melee draws way more of his classes power out of the weapon he uses. conversely, if a melee uses a crappy weapon with a good proc, it doesn't become overbearing, because he looses power (usually in the form of raw damage) to gain that utility.
As a caster, the difference between using a very strong and very weak weapon for the effect is much less pronounced as with a melee.

Assuming you want your procs to actually feel worthwhile when they proc, you would have to make the procchance be comically low to ensure equal powerbudget between proccing and non proccing weapons.

Little examle. Lets say we want a simple 300 dmg fireball to be shot as a chance to proc on cast and it should be on the powerlevel of one of the bwl onehanded casterweapons ~worth 60-80 spellpower. And for ease of example, lets not give that fireball scaling.

You expect to cast one about every roughly 20 seconds worth of cast to break even between your fireball proc or simply having a 50 spellpower weapon.

Lastly, there is one very big pink and light blue colored elefant in the room. Both mages and paladins due to some classquirks are 2-4 times better than other casters else at abusing any procc that triggers on successful spellcasts. Since the weapons speed doesn't matter, and the different casts have vastly different castspeeds.

In the above example, for an arcane mage, that would be a 5% procchance whenever he hits with an arcane missile, a elemental shaman would need ~10% for his lightning bolts, while a warlock would need a ~15% procchance for it to be equally impactful as just 50 spellpower.

So making a spellbased proc, which feels impactful when triggering, respects the powerbudget of the item it is on, and can not be abused to a ridiculous degree by one or multiple classes is a very difficault thing to balance.

And as cool as it woul be to have some flavorful choices of casterweapons, i prefer not having much variety over subjecting myself to the same slavery for the one good weapons melees have to endure ;)
