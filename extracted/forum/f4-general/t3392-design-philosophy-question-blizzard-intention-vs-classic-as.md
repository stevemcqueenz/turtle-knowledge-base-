# Design Philosophy Question: Blizzard Intention vs. Classic As It Was

## Post 20622 by Hir3 — 2022-07-28T23:59:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=20622#p20622 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I am wondering what the Turtle approach is when it comes to some of the changes to the game.

Is there more of an emphasis on  sticking to Classic "feel" or on attempting to merge that with Concept/Alpha/Beta design vision?

I'll use the example of Hunters:

In the pre-release design, Blizzard intended for hunters to have Focus as a resource .They were given mana after Focus took too many design resources in their tight schedule, and saw no point in changing it until Cataclysm. This was after they wanted to make a change towards heavily customizing each class, seeing the success of Death Knight's custom resource mechanic.

They also intended for hunters to have no dead zone, instead switching to melee only in melee range. Because of internet speeds and latency, this was changed as a mercy to players who would be unable to play the class on equal footing if they suffered from a poor internet connection. There was also a limitation in having no good way of automatically switching to melee and back, which is why in Vanilla clients auto-attack and auto-shot are separate abilities.

Under a strict Classic-feel, we would see no change here. But under a deal of understanding of why the limitations were in place to begin with, one could also argue that a change could be made given that it's 2022 and said limitations are not there.

Anyway, I'm fine with either approach in the context of this post, but I'm curious as to where Turtle's mind is in following original intent or classic feel. Thanks!

## Post 20624 by Redmagejoe (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2022-07-29T00:12:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=20624#p20624 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Staff bases a lot of these decisions on original vision, which means some existing mechanics such as the ones you listed are likely not completely out of hand (deadzone, focus, etc). It comes down to first a decision of whether it fits the feel/theme and "do we want that for our project", then it comes down to how or if it can be implemented, and then it comes down to "what balance/gameplay implications will that have?".

These are generally the steps involved in feature development.
