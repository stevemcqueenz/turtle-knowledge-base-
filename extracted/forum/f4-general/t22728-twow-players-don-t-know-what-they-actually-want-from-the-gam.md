# Twow players don't know what they actually want from the game

## Post 158380 by Troggyboy — 2025-11-04T23:19:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=158380#p158380 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

**DISCLAIMER: rant ahead**

"And you don't want that. You think you do, but you don't."

A now infamous quote from then-executive designer for WoW, J. Allen Brack, in response to the question wether the devs were considering making vanilla servers. He then, funnily enough, mentioned how dungeon finders in retail will just make you a group and teleport you to whatever dungeon you qued up for. And how you didn't need to spam chat to find groups. Like who would want that?

And you know what the worst part is? He was right in a way, although probably not the way he meant it back then.

The people who play turtle wow and to a greater extent also classic wow more generally are literally the sort of people he was describing here. They go to play earlier versions of the game to relive that vanilla experience they had back in 2006 or whatever, only to CONSTANTLY complain about what makes vanilla vanilla and then demand changes to make it more retail-like. Leveling is slow and tedious at times? Don't worry, here's exp boosts from tents and maybe Tel'Abim. Finding a group actually involves the work of socializing with people? No worries, here's a dungeon finder and a world chat for you to spam LFG. Open world pvp is random and imbalanced? Well friend, what about multiple instances completely seperated from the world that are all about PvP? Oh, you liked the oldschool zones and lore and really being immersed in the world. Well good news for you, look at all the cool new zones we made in the vanilla style, there's two new starter zones for goblins and high elves, not to mention Northwind, Balor, Grim R- wait, you're just gonna auto skip all quest texts, have addons literally point you to each and every objective and you'll powerlevel through all of it as fast as possible? Oh ok . . . nevermind.

And the people who play Twow do all this shit, after having every complaint acknowledged and playing the game like it's retail and you know fucking what? They're miserable, they still complain, they still beg for tents and gold rather than actually discovering the world and playing the actual fucking game and they don't know why.

What they just don't get is that if you want classic wow to feel like classic wow, it actually has to be played and designed like classic wow. Imagine that! It's the long-term gratification of grinding out quests and slowly leveling up your character and professions that makes the game feel so meaningful, and the more you speed it up, the more you will take away from it. It's the lack of a global chat, instanced PvP, hyper-efficient dungeon grinding and group-finders that made the world feel so alive and immersive. Use and abuse these tools though, and all you get is instanced content after instanced content and the world simply doesn't matter anymore. You liked the social aspect, finding and making friends because you literally were pushed to do so by the game? If all you do today is just grind out content with an ever-changing array of complete strangers to whom you barely even talk, how do you expect to ever relive that experience?

But no. You don't want that. You want everything to be handed to you on a silver platter without any friction or challenge, without any sense of discovery or surprise. You have solved the game and you will continue to solve it with each expansion, wondering where your enjoyment of it went, thinking going into that direction even further will somehow make it better.

You want to play a game like that? Fine. Go play League, Overwatch, Valorant or fucking Retail. Go, and see what it's like. See how much you will enjoy it, how much other people you play with will and what kind of people play these kinds of games. This is what you want, is it not? You always beg for it after all . . .

MMOs have a lot of community friction nowadays. Back in the days of Vanilla social media wasn't a big thing yet, and technology hadn't advanced enough for us to demand all sorts of systems in the game. That meant everyone was basically on the same page. But, after having experienced modern conveniences, and socializing essentially being outsourced to 3rd party apps, a lot of people now want retail-esque systems in their MMOs. Others want to keep the old school design. We can see this friction in Classic, where a lot of people hated the automated dungeon finder (and Blizzard agreed, at first), but then Blizzard caved in to those that wanted it.

I think it's important the devs have a vision and then stick to it, regardless of the portion of the community that disagrees with it. Alternatively, they could create multiple versions of the game, like an "Old school" version and a "Modernized" version for the different crowds, but that would require a decent playerbase to begin with.

I don't think it's a linear thing, where you move between very Vanilla and very Retail.
I agree that TWOW should never be retail-like, there is a reason many people play here (also, this server is free but for the sake of the argument, let's ignore the players who play here because of monetary reasons).

But the thing is that things are not linear.
Things in Vanilla can change without turning stuff more retail-like.
Vanilla had many annoying aspects (like class balance being broken beyond comprehension).
The trick is not to solve those problems by repeating what retail did.
Sure, if something worked really well, it can be employed, but only scarcely.
For the other problems, you can be creative, while respecting the lore and the gameplay.
F.ex. dungeon scarcity at certain levels.
Was introducing Hateforge a bad thing?
Or Crescent Grove to give leather users a place to gear up at that level range?
Those additions made the experience better and didn't turn the place more retail-like.
Making tier set alternatives for the different builds, again, only helped the gameplay.
Class changes is a mixxed bag, sure, but here a raid comp doesn't mean warrior tanks-only.
You can like playing as a bear or a paladin tank and wouldn't become a joke.

That being said, certain aspects have to be kept to keep the game grounded in Vanilla.
It should never become unrecognizable.
Your character should always remain a noname weirdo who eventually gears up but never becomes the legend of Azeroth. Just like no player character compares to NPCs like Rexxar.
You should experience the vastness of the world and to a degree, be limited by it.
The outside world should pose challenges, forcing you to cooperate with others.

But if the basics are respected and kept properly (and faithfully to the lore), I see no reason why annoying aspects of Vanilla can't be addressed.

Also, TWOW was never truly Vanilla, there's a reason behind that + sign.
Devs, from the very beginning, said yes to cross-faction PVE and no to world buff-raiding.
That + sign gives them the right to change things AS LONG AS changes keep the world recognizeable, are fun and don't break the lore (pre-TBC lore of course).
