# The viability of PvP

## Post 157398 by Dracarusggotham (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2025-10-27T21:54:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=157398#p157398 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

Well, this is more of a discussion than suggestions or anything else.
Is PvP really viable? Is it a good idea? Is it necessary?

First of all, PvP in Turtle is practically garbage, I don't need to say more, many will have some reasons, others will have different reasons, but it all converges to one point, PvP needs A LOT of work.

Several situations have crossed my mind. I've gone from hating it to accepting that PvP needs to be refined on its own.

Many thoughts, the first:

Is PvP really necessary?
WoW has had PvP from the beginning, although there was no incentive, no rewards, no honor. It was simply impossible to socialize with the opposing faction. Each faction went its own way, its own way. If they met, they could fight, but only for fun. There was no competition. Even without the ability to socialize, they could help each other without saying a word. This changed in later patches, implementing the poor system we have today, which generated an industry that can sometimes be irritating and abusive.

The second:
It's necessary, but I don't like the way it's implemented.

Warcraft HAS LORE. The whole world, the story, the characters, the factions are born of war, races that hate each other because of a violent past, Orcs versus Humans, Elves versus Trolls, Alliance versus Horde. Everything has an active conflict behind it. The battlefields have conflicts, they have a history, a reason for being there.

Lately I've seen a lot of people complaining that PvP is bad because of RPGs and lore, which, honestly, I find stupid. You're ignoring the foundation of the world you supposedly love. This isn't a Battle Royale where you fight characters with no background, no, my friend. There has to be a reason for YOU choosing to be Alliance or Horde.

The last straw is the recent Crossfaction in PvP, which I dislike beyond belief, since it ultimately breaks that constructed world and you just go in to fight each other for the hell of it, which ultimately breaks everything I liked. I hope you enjoy it. I won't be doing PvP anymore, which I rarely did before.

Possible future solutions:

The main problem has never been faction balance, or the lack of Paladins and Shamans in factions that don't have them. The problem has always been imbalance and people's lack of real interest in PvP.

This server has been PvE since its inception, from its very conception as Vanilla. Slow and Steady has been its motto. Little has been done to change PvP, which is understandable in the end. The point is Classic+, not to make a generic PvP server like the thousands out there.

However, I think there should be a patch entirely dedicated to fine-tuning PvP.

The classes are unbalanced because they are designed to be efficient in PvE, not PvP. Mobs are not players, and they don't function the same.

Classes are the root of everything and the lack of interest of the majority of the population in doing PvP because it is simply not their thing, forcing them to do PvP is not the solution either, we have already seen that of the 3 European realms, the most unpopular is Tel Abim.

So, here are three main things I've thought about. I'm not saying this as a PvP expert, nor as someone who really cares about numbers or efficiency.

 1. A new talent tree for all classes: PvP and PvE are impossible to balance because all three branches try to be used for both purposes, which, as I've already said, is absurdly difficult to balance. Therefore, there should be a fourth branch dedicated entirely to PvP. If there are three, why not four? Ultimately, it's up to the player to find the most viable way to spend their talent points for their own goals.

 2. PvP gear should be better distributed across ranks.

There are 14 ranks, and you only start getting gear up to rank 7 or 8, I can't remember exactly. This should be changed; gear should start being earned at rank 4 and go from there. Likewise, equipment can be obtained based on levels. For example, the Sergeant's Cloak, which comes in three versions, each increasing the required level. This way, you avoid overpowering or everything being reduced to level 60.
Improving appearances is also an incentive; several models are reused, which is tacky and shabby.

 3. The Honor System:
As I mentioned before, equipment should be distributed by rank, but the honor system also needs improvements. The main problem with this is that it rewards consistency more than real effort. Therefore, I believe it is necessary to implement objectives for the honor received, as well as emulate or directly copy the tradable honor from later expansions. This is for something I will explain later.

 4. PvP Tiers:
For each PvE tier released, a version of it, only obtainable by having the highest rank, must be implemented. To achieve this, group effort must be rewarded, objectives must be implemented, and rewards must be exchangeable honor. This ensures that the effort is necessary to obtain that boost to the next tier. Otherwise, PvE players would now be at a disadvantage if they want to do PvP.

It is necessary to balance the power of PvE with that of PvP so that there are no unfair imbalances, as is the case now. PvP gear was never updated to the latest tier 3.0 or 3.5, which is already a tremendous disparity.

 5. BG every 5 levels:
I find it absurd that there's a 9-level difference if you decide to enter at level 10, 20, 30, 40, or 50.
And the worst is undoubtedly at level 50, when you face off against full Tier 1, 2, or 3 players.

Also, at those levels, you're worthless if the other person is at the maximum BG level. You're a nuisance, you gain nothing, and you have to wait at least 5 or 6 levels to even tickle.

All of this leads to my final thought:
PvP just for fun?
A common problem: people get frustrated by the competition, and coming to seek it out on this server right now is shooting yourself in the foot. If you're not Horde, you'll lose 90% of the time.
At first, it was just for fun; there was no honor system or rewards. Implementing it created countless problems, which leads me to think that if Turtle can't balance it, it would be better to remove the honor system and everything that goes with it, returning us to a place where everything is just for fun.

Is it balanceable? Maybe not 100%. There will always be whiners who object, and don't believe that Crossfaction will magically fix PvP's problems. It's simply a band-aid on a huge cut Blizzard inflicted on the game that they've never managed to heal to this day.

Races and their racials were designed to roleplay how they worked on paper, not for fair combat, and so were classes.

WoW wasn't designed for PvP, it was designed for roleplaying and combat for fun. If you think otherwise, you're wrong. Just go back to 1.0 and you'll see that's how it was.

I don't agree that pvp should be implemented *only* because lore reasons.

I think main reason to implement PvP is because it provide almost endless content for players.
If you play random matches, you see different people. (In ideal situation) They have different classes and might do different strategies.
Single warsong gulch is enough for people to have fun across all leveling journey. Dungeons are opposite - if you outlevel them, you are not interested in going there.
Also, what people doing when they get their BiSes t3? They go battlegrounds and stomp people.
It might not feel good for stomped, but it is good for server - people still playing when they cleared out all available content.
Here is another problem - there are not enough people to be stomped. There should be others - dedicated pvp players who have gearing goal exclusively in pvp. So they spend all their time playing bgs and won't give up when they got stomped by t3 guys.

So I think that playing as premade should be limited and additional shuffle should be implemented. At least against random groups. It literally kills the point of content endlessness.

Content endlessness is the reason why overpowered classes should be nerfed. If all enemies are of the same class it kills endlesness.

Nerfing high level enchants on low level gear is the same reason too. If people lock themselves at bracket by enchants - you will see same faces over and over in battlegrounds.

I think best approach in leveling and gearing is to open additional battleground every bracket.
So people stop at 19, grind exalted with warsong. Then proceed to 29, grind exalted with arathi. Then proceed to some new battleground (eye of the storm?) and grind exalted reputation there.
It will stop people for some time at the bracket to collect critical mass of players. But it will be limited - this particular goal will be reached soon and player will move on further.
Ideally, gear rewards should be rebalanced in a way to give reasons to play battleground on each bracket. Maybe like all weapons and gear on 19 available at honored, but for 39 you need revered and for 59 - exalted. Similar for arathi - for 29 - honored, 49- revered, 59-exalted.

There might be an achievements like lunatics have to grant titles. Warsong outrider title by getting exalted with wsg on 19, Defiler - get exalted with arathi on 29. And some final achievement for completing all x9 achievements.

I don't ask to change anything at 60. It is beyoud saving. Gear power creep is too huge. Let people enjoy their epics.

> > >
> > >  Is PvP really necessary?
> > >  WoW has had PvP from the beginning, although there was no incentive, no rewards, no honor. It was simply impossible to socialize with the opposing faction. Each faction went its own way, its own way. If they met, they could fight, but only for fun. There was no competition. Even without the ability to socialize, they could help each other without saying a word. This changed in later patches, implementing the poor system we have today, which generated an industry that can sometimes be irritating and abusive.
> > >
> > >  The second:
> > >  It's necessary, but I don't like the way it's implemented.
> > >
> > >  Warcraft HAS LORE. The whole world, the story, the characters, the factions are born of war, races that hate each other because of a violent past, Orcs versus Humans, Elves versus Trolls, Alliance versus Horde. Everything has an active conflict behind it. The battlefields have conflicts, they have a history, a reason for being there.
> > >
> > >  Lately I've seen a lot of people complaining that PvP is bad because of RPGs and lore, which, honestly, I find stupid. You're ignoring the foundation of the world you supposedly love. This isn't a Battle Royale where you fight characters with no background, no, my friend. There has to be a reason for YOU choosing to be Alliance or Horde.
> > >
> > >  The last straw is the recent Crossfaction in PvP, which I dislike beyond belief, since it ultimately breaks that constructed world and you just go in to fight each other for the hell of it, which ultimately breaks everything I liked. I hope you enjoy it. I won't be doing PvP anymore, which I rarely did before.
> > >
> > >  Possible future solutions:
> > >
> > >  The main problem has never been faction balance, or the lack of Paladins and Shamans in factions that don't have them. The problem has always been imbalance and people's lack of real interest in PvP.
> > >
> > >  This server has been PvE since its inception, from its very conception as Vanilla. Slow and Steady has been its motto. Little has been done to change PvP, which is understandable in the end. The point is Classic+, not to make a generic PvP server like the thousands out there.
> > >
> > >  However, I think there should be a patch entirely dedicated to fine-tuning PvP.
> > >
> > >  The classes are unbalanced because they are designed to be efficient in PvE, not PvP. Mobs are not players, and they don't function the same.
> > >
> > >  Classes are the root of everything and the lack of interest of the majority of the population in doing PvP because it is simply not their thing, forcing them to do PvP is not the solution either, we have already seen that of the 3 European realms, the most unpopular is Tel Abim.
> > >
> > >  So, here are three main things I've thought about. I'm not saying this as a PvP expert, nor as someone who really cares about numbers or efficiency.
> > >
> > >  1. A new talent tree for all classes: PvP and PvE are impossible to balance because all three branches try to be used for both purposes, which, as I've already said, is absurdly difficult to balance. Therefore, there should be a fourth branch dedicated entirely to PvP. If there are three, why not four? Ultimately, it's up to the player to find the most viable way to spend their talent points for their own goals.
> > >
> > >  2. PvP gear should be better distributed across ranks.
> > >
> > >  There are 14 ranks, and you only start getting gear up to rank 7 or 8, I can't remember exactly. This should be changed; gear should start being earned at rank 4 and go from there. Likewise, equipment can be obtained based on levels. For example, the Sergeant's Cloak, which comes in three versions, each increasing the required level. This way, you avoid overpowering or everything being reduced to level 60.
> > >  Improving appearances is also an incentive; several models are reused, which is tacky and shabby.
> > >
> > >  3. The Honor System:
> > >  As I mentioned before, equipment should be distributed by rank, but the honor system also needs improvements. The main problem with this is that it rewards consistency more than real effort. Therefore, I believe it is necessary to implement objectives for the honor received, as well as emulate or directly copy the tradable honor from later expansions. This is for something I will explain later.
> > >
> > >  4. PvP Tiers:
> > >  For each PvE tier released, a version of it, only obtainable by having the highest rank, must be implemented. To achieve this, group effort must be rewarded, objectives must be implemented, and rewards must be exchangeable honor. This ensures that the effort is necessary to obtain that boost to the next tier. Otherwise, PvE players would now be at a disadvantage if they want to do PvP.
> > >
> > >  It is necessary to balance the power of PvE with that of PvP so that there are no unfair imbalances, as is the case now. PvP gear was never updated to the latest tier 3.0 or 3.5, which is already a tremendous disparity.
> > >
> > >  5. BG every 5 levels:
> > >  I find it absurd that there's a 9-level difference if you decide to enter at level 10, 20, 30, 40, or 50.
> > >  And the worst is undoubtedly at level 50, when you face off against full Tier 1, 2, or 3 players.
> > >
> > >  Also, at those levels, you're worthless if the other person is at the maximum BG level. You're a nuisance, you gain nothing, and you have to wait at least 5 or 6 levels to even tickle.
> > >
> > >
> > >  All of this leads to my final thought:
> > >  PvP just for fun?
> > >  A common problem: people get frustrated by the competition, and coming to seek it out on this server right now is shooting yourself in the foot. If you're not Horde, you'll lose 90% of the time.
> > >  At first, it was just for fun; there was no honor system or rewards. Implementing it created countless problems, which leads me to think that if Turtle can't balance it, it would be better to remove the honor system and everything that goes with it, returning us to a place where everything is just for fun.
> > >
> > >  Is it balanceable? Maybe not 100%. There will always be whiners who object, and don't believe that Crossfaction will magically fix PvP's problems. It's simply a band-aid on a huge cut Blizzard inflicted on the game that they've never managed to heal to this day.
> > >
> > >  Races and their racials were designed to roleplay how they worked on paper, not for fair combat, and so were classes.
> > >
> > >  WoW wasn't designed for PvP, it was designed for roleplaying and combat for fun. If you think otherwise, you're wrong. Just go back to 1.0 and you'll see that's how it was.
