# Treatise on the responsibilities of roles in instances

## Post 149864 by Risingphoenix — 2025-09-02T11:22:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=149864#p149864 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

When doing dungeons in World of Warcraft it is critical to know and understand your assigned role not only to better the group’s chances of success but importantly also to ensure that it will be a pleasant experience for everyone involved – it is, after all, just a game, and you should have fun while playing.

 **Being a part of a group**
First things first: When queuing for an instance or LFG in the chat, you should remind yourself that you are interacting with other people, not NPCs. You have to understand what is required of you as a part of a team with a shared goal. This means respecting other people and their time. Be mindful when you write in /p and write as if to a colleague or friend – you are after all going to spend a while together, why not make it pleasant for both them and you. Also please travel to the instance as fast as possible and don’t waste time with tasks you could do on your own. Of course it’s fine doing a quick de-tour to pick up a quest for the dungeon, but maybe ask in chat, if someone can share the quest first, so you don’t waste time, but don’t take 10 min. doing your crafting profession in town or go out of your way to farm every mining node on your way to the dungeon – respect that other people have taken time out of their lives to play a game with you.

Also make sure you have the time to do the whole run. Of course, if you unexpectedly wipe ten times, anyone might run out of time, but don’t sign up for a run you know or expect not to be able to finish – that’s bad form.

Come prepared for a long run. Have food and drinks available (whether bought, made or conjured) and don’t expect there to always be a mage to help you out.

 **Tanking a dungeon**
The role of the tank is one of focus: Keeping the focus of the group (“where are we heading and which mob are we killing”). While some tasks might be delegated to others (a hunter might pull and a rogue might decide kill order) your role is to keep the group focussed, ea. by communicating with the group either in chat, using icons/marks or even voice chat. In a sense you are the group’s leader – not in a Captain America-way, but by the very nature that you should be the first thing every mob hits (baring the odd off-tank encounter).

In every fight you should keep aggro on every mob (again with a few exceptions). If you loose aggro or a patrol comes along, you have to grab that mob and make sure to build threat as fast as possible. If everyone trusts you to do this, the healer won’t run around when they aggro a mob, but will instead stand still and keep healing, knowing you will come and save them – just as they save you almost every single pull.

It almost goes without saying that you should be up to this task. Both personally and with your character. Nobody can reasonably expect you to be tanking specced (unless raiding), but make sure you have a shield on hand and tanking abilities of the current highest level. I’m not doubting that some of you are players of incredible skill who can easily tank with a two-hander – but do the healer and the rest of the group a favour and take the role seriously. There’s no need to flex, it’s a team effort.

 **Healing an instance**
While the other members of your group are working on “winning”, your job is to make sure, you all don’t lose. Keep everyone alive and if that isn’t possible then quickly and smartly decide who to keep alive. If a DPS draws aggro and is about to die, you have to react to the situation and ascertain whether to step in and save them or conserve mana for the tank. This is never an easy dilemma as saving the DPS ensures higher dps which may end up winning the fight in the end. It’s a gut reaction made on the fly with a million and one factors to consider: Does the DPS have healing abilities of their own or utility to get out of the situation (e.g. feign death or vanish)? Is the fight almost over or just begun? As well as a whole host of other considerations.

No one should expect of you that you are specced for healing (unless raiding), but you should come prepared if you sign up as a healer. Make sure you have the highest ranks of your healing spells – even the ones you don’t use that often; you never know when you’ll need them and leaving home without every available tool is just plain silly. Bring your own drinks – don’t assume there’s always a mage in the party ready to help you out. And if there is a mage, you have the drinks for another time. Also make sure you know where your abilities are and what they do – especially if you haven’t healed with the class before. Perhaps even make another skill bar only for healing so you don’t get confused with all your other abilities.

 **DPS’ing**
Whether you are ranged or melee, magic or physical, you have an important job to do. Your mission is to win each encounter, while the tank keeps focus and the healer makes sure, you don’t all die. But to win you must strike a balance: push your dps as high as possible without sacrificing the whole group to the “dps gods”. That means being very careful not to pull without a go (and preferably not at all), not aggroing mobs or pull new ones into the fight – this also includes being in control of your pet. If you pull aggro, which you will, it is your job to help the tank reacquire aggro by lowering your dps or even stopping your attack altogether and if necessary, run towards the tank – not away from them.

Every character still levelling has damage talents, so don’t worry about your spec. No one expects a particular spec from you and outside of raids, your actual dps doesn’t really matter. What matters is that you participate as a member of a team and understand your role as DPS. You should also be mindful of the flow of the run – i.e. don’t use all your mana, if half is enough. Using too many resources costs unnecessary downtime. Neither the tank nor the healer has the means to dictate the flow of battle, so this task is up to you – focus targets down, keep dots on the right targets etc.

Also help out with utility during fights if you have access to it – de-curse, dispel magic, cure disease or poison etc. and even off-heal or off-tank, if it’s necessary (this usually requires agreement with the group in advance to avoid mistakes, but it could of course also happen inadvertently). If you have AOE-abilities, make sure to make your intention to use these clear for the whole group: First of all because AOE often breaks cc’s or pulls adds but also because the tank can position the mobs better to get full effect of the AOE. Also understand that the tank must have a decent amount of threat built on each target, before you AOE – otherwise you will pull aggro.

 **Dungeon utility**
If you play a class which possess some relevant and useful utility, make it known to the group, but also make sure that it is ok, before you use it. If you have buffs always keeps these up on all party members – it is your job to keep track of who’s buffs have run out and then reapply (this also goes if you are the tank or healer!). If you have a cc ability discuss and agree with the group when and how to use it, e.g. let the tank communicate it to you via a dungeon icon. Also, you should make any utility available to the group, whether it be conjuring food and drinks, summoning teammates to the dungeon, healthstones and soulstones. This also applies for profession utility, although to a lesser extent. You are, of course, not required or even expected to enchant every party member’s gear, but if you have spare potions or armour kits, don’t hesitate to offer these up – it will be appreciated, and it will help towards a speedy and pleasant dungeon experience, and who knows you might even make a friend.
