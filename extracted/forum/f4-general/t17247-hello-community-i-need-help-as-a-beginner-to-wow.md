Welcome ! Turtle WoW is a great place to experience Vanilla WoW pretty close to what it was back in the day, so if you like community, exploration, and a rich, vibrant, hostile world, with stuff to discover everywhere, I think you'll like it here.

My biggest advice about race is to play the one you want. The performance difference between races only really matters to sweat lords and you will be able to do well with any race you want. If you end up changing your mind and want the high-perf one you can always buy a race change token, but in my opinion, picking a race for its numbers goes against the spirit of Vanilla, so you'll already be getting off on the wrong foot and exposing yourself to toxic mentalities that distract from what makes Vanilla so great.

As for classes it depends on what you want to do !

If what you're most interested is flavor and a general rundown of mechanics, here's my take :

The most versatile class is Druid, as it combines little bits of healing, DPS casting, and the gameplay of a Rogue and a Warrior by morphing into different animals, so it's a good way to get accustomed to WoW's mechanics and figure out what you really like. They're great for both solo and group content, and have a way to respond to just about any situation.

Shortly behind them in terms of versatility is the Paladin, who, like the Druid, can play any role. Paladins can solo quite well but especially shine in group content thanks to their myriad of buffs and, again, their ability to fill any role that a group may need: you'll never be in a full group waiting for a healer/tank/DPS since you can do any of those.

A little lower still in terms of versatility is the Shaman, who can take on roles of a melee DPS, a caster DPS, or a healer. You can also tank to a small extent, but it's not their strong suit. Shamen are especially good in group contents thanks to the wide variety of utility their totems can bring. They're also very fun in PvP !

If you prefer to play solo, Hunter offers a very complete experience where your pet is a tank and DPS, you are a DPS and healer for your pet. It's a difficult class to bring to max performance, but their performance in going slow and steady and keeping things simple is extremely high too while you're getting accustomed to the game. Hunters can be a little challenging to itemize well though, and poor control of your pet can put your group in danger. Having one fewer bag slot, which is occupied by your quiver, is also a drag early game where you're short on inventory space.

Warlock is the other pet class. I don't know too much about them so I'll keep it brief: warlocks have access to 4 pets whom, individually, aren't as versatile as the hunters, but are more fine tuned for each situation. They have a tank pet, a DPS-and-crowd-control pet, an anti-caster pet, and a ranged buffing pet. The warlock themself uses their own health as a resource since they can sacrifice it to regain mana, and get it back with life-draining spells. They have excellent crowd control but can quickly get overwhelmed. Importantly, they're also quite boring to play in a raid as you'll mostly be spamming shadowbolt, but you don't need to raid if you're not interested in it.

Rogues have great utility and class fantasy, their gameplay is very unique. They're great for both group and solo content: they can crowd control, offtank, scout ahead, unlock doors and chests ... And as solo, thanks to stealth, you can skip straight to the end of busy areas if you just have one mob to kill at the end ! They're also great at escaping bad situations thanks to their multiple powerful defensive cooldowns.

Priest seems to have been mostly designed as a team player, with excellent supporting spells and heals. Leveling as a priest can be pretty boring early on until you get powerful Discipline or Shadow talents, but they're extremely convenient to have in a group. They're one of the better classes in PvP too, with great crowd control and the ability to attack other mana users very effectively.

Mage is a class that excels at soloing thanks to its incredible crowd control and ability to deal immense group damage. They also have great utility as they can teleport themselves and others across the world, and create food and drinks, as well as interesting buffs. A frost mage can pull a dozen same-level mobs and kill them safely from afar with Improved Blizzard.

Warrior, lastly, is a difficult class to play solo but perhaps the one that scales the best with support and gear. This is a double-edged sword: if you're not finding good gear, your performance is gonna suffer, and RNG might not be on your side. Their flavor is very cool though: all warriors want to have a one-hander, a shield, and a DPS option (either another one-hander or a two-hander) in their bags to answer each situation the best. Good warriors will swap equipment during combat to best respond to the situation. They also have 3 stances ; in WOTLK, you kinda picked one stance based on your spec and basicaly always stayed in it, but in Vanilla (and TBC for that matter), you'll do a lot of "stance dancing" in combat to have access to your entire arsenal.

My best advice to you is, like races, to play what you want to play ! Which class mechanics and flavor seem to appeal the most to you ?

There are some minor considerations but mostly for those who wanna min+max a build (usually people playing this for several years). As a fresh experience, just choose the race which you like the most, those really minor differences are not worth mentioning.
Two things to look out for:
 1. Check what classes do and make sure the race you like can be the class you like.
 2. Faction. Most things are crossfaction meaning anyone can play with anyone but being Horde or Alliance determines things. How your capital looks like, initial zones, zeppelin vs boat for example.

Have fun for WOW but consider this carefully. I like the game but spent way more time of my life on it than I care to admit. Can kill free time.
