Shapeshift removes certain CC Debuffs like Roots and Snares. That is unique to the Class and any similar powers present in other Classes are just that - - similar; Mages, for instance, have Blink, but it is on a Cooldown and is far too important to blow on something like a Root, but rather save it for a Stun. Engineers can benefit from a Rocket Helmet.

Regarding PvE, in Raids, it highly depends on the Class composition and what CCs are available to use on a given non-Immune Mob: most that are, actually happen to be fragile (Sap, Hibernate, Polymorph, Shackle, more), whereas Roots are not. They instead have a threshold, though it's been a while for me and I've forgotten whether it's based on raw Damage, or Damage relative to the Target's Max HP or something similar. I wanted to voice my strong opposition to a suggestion that’s been circulating recently — the idea of allowing Entangling Roots to work indoors.

Let me say this plainly: this is a terrible idea that goes directly against the core design principles of World of Warcraft 1.12, and implementing it would further erode class identity, game balance, and the authenticity that makes Turtle WoW so special.

Allow me to lay out several key reasons why Entangling Roots must remain unusable indoors, just as it was in the original game.

In vanilla WoW, Entangling Roots not working indoors wasn’t a bug — it was intentional design. Blizzard made it this way to balance the spell's incredible utility in outdoor PvP and world content. Giving Druids powerful CC outdoors while denying them the same in dungeons created a clear gameplay identity: the Druid excels in nature — not in crypts, caves, or castles.

If we change this, we undermine that deliberate class design choice.

Druids already bring an enormous toolkit: HoTs, emergency tanking, emergency healing, travel forms, stealth, and some of the strongest world PvP mobility in the game. Let’s not forget Hibernate, Cyclone (on Turtle), and even powerful movement slows via Nature’s Grasp.

Now imagine adding indoor roots to that mix. They’d have reliable crowd control in every environment, putting them on par or above Mages or Warlocks — without the same trade-offs. This is power creep, plain and simple.

If you open the door to indoor roots, what’s next? Hibernate on mechanicals? Regrowth while silenced?

Entangling Roots is a spell that channels nature’s grasp — vines and brambles erupting from the ground. It makes perfect thematic sense that this spell only works outdoors, in soil and sunlight, not on stone floors of ancient ruins or dungeons deep underground.

You’re telling me vines are going to burst out of the granite floors of Blackrock Depths?

This isn’t just game mechanics — it’s worldbuilding. Removing this restriction would break immersion and reduce the class’s connection to nature.

Let’s be honest — Druids were never the go-to dungeon CC class in Vanilla. That was the role of Mages (Polymorph), Rogues (Sap), and Hunters (traps). Druids weren’t designed to bring reliable, repeatable crowd control in tight indoor spaces. That was the price they paid for being the most versatile class in the game.

Giving them indoor roots is not fixing a flaw — it’s patching over an intentional limitation that preserves class diversity.

Turtle WoW has stood out among private servers for how well it preserves the spirit of 1.12 while gently adding new content. This balance is delicate.

Adding indoor roots crosses the line into "custom server convenience" rather than "faithful vanilla+" design. It’s a quality-of-life buff that sacrifices depth for accessibility — and we’ve all seen where that leads on other projects.

I understand the desire to smooth out class limitations. But limitations are what make classes unique. Entangling Roots not working indoors forces Druids to think differently in dungeons. It encourages creativity, cooperation, and adapts to the environment — just like the class’s fantasy.

Please don’t flatten the class by removing one of the most flavorful restrictions in the game. Leave roots outside, where they belong.
