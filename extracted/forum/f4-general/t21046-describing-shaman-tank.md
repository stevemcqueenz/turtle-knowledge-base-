# Describing Shaman Tank

## Post 146363 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2025-08-17T06:23:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=146363#p146363 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!

My goal here is to describe the **strengths** and **weaknesses** of a shaman during **dungeon** tanking.
I wanna do this because many people are not familiar with the build, what they can and cannot expect from them etc.

Shaman tanks are really decent at that role.
Not primethough: I firmly believe that prot pala is the goat of dungeon tanking, but shammies are more than capable for providing smooth runs.
Below their strengths and weaknesses.

 **Strengths:**

 * Extremely high snap threat (specially once SS is learned).
Many tanks tell things like please wait for some sec before DPSing.
Shamans however can dish out an ungodly amount of threat in a short spam of time, specially for 1-2 mobs. They can usually allow you to do the zug from the beginning.
The reason behind this is is ES+SS+LS with lightning shield plus some passive orb procs.
For not so extreme threat expectations they usually go water shield at pull for better mana management though.

 * Locking out casters.
 *5/6 second CD interrupt, locking out for 2 seconds from a school of magic and a now 18/20 sec CD grounding for targeted spell redirection. Shamans can almost fully lock out caster mobs and dungeon bosses.*

 * The ability to kite.
Not as good as a frost mage but it's a pretty underrated aspect of a shaman tank at dungeons.
You focus down the casters. Let's say 2 melee remains and things are looking grim, HPs are low everywhere.
The tank can just cast an earthbind and people can run around the mobs, pinching them down from distance.
If done cleverly, melee mobs should never cause wipe in a tank shaman run.

 * Range pull variety.
Shocks are basically pala judgments with twice the range and coming with extra threat/slow or dot.
Lightning bolt is good for high single-target threat, f.ex. when another target is getting sheeped at the same time.
Rank 1 lightning bolt is basically warrior arrow for barely any mana.
Chain lightning is a ranged, multitarget threat generator.
The sheer variety of range pulls can be awesome, specially if they are combined with earthbind to turn those approaching melees into a shooting range for casters.

 * Good AoE threat.
Not prime. Don't confuse it with consecration. You can definitely steal a mob from the tank.
But shamans have the second best AoE threat from tanks, better than the druid or warrior counterparts.

 * Passive threat.
 *A shaman tank can switch to lightning shield before a boss stun and generate threat while being stunned. Can be useful at certain dungeon bosses. F.ex. I like using full lightning shield on Rotmaw. Even if I'm consumed, lightning shield keeps the threat on me much longer.*

 * High utility.
 *Imagine the tank casually party-cleansing while tanking, taking care of fear, adding resistances or party-buffing.*

 * Ankh and rezz:
 *A wipe doesn't equal a corpse run.*

 **Weaknesses:**

 * Initially super squishy.
 *Feels like healing a melee DPS, not a tank. It gets much better later but until mid-leveling they either need a really alert tank or they use armor kits, enchants and consumes where other tanks don't. Just keep in mind that if you're a healer and you go to a dungeon with a shaman tank, let's say before level 40, you should almost imagine it as raid healing.*

 * No AoE taunt.
They can generate some cool AoE threat, yes. But taking over many mobs can be a problem.
There is one taunt and other than that, some single-target abilities for high threat. If you run forward, pull three, fight them a bit and run back, expecting your targets to be instantly taken over: maybe 1 or 2 will be.

 * Mana.
 *They have depleting resources like palas but shaman mana regeneration is inferior compared to pala. If the shaman tank is pushed to spam additional totems and go really high threat, expect a lot of drinking, specially initially.*

 * Fire or nature resistance.
There are places and mobs where the shaman suddenly starts being worse.
Fire rez mobs mean that AoE threat gets reduced to one earth totem on a CD.
Nature rez mobs mean a way lesser threat ceiling.
Just be aware of this and be careful how you DPS in a high fire rez environment f.ex.

 * Weapon imbue + weapon totems
Flametongue can basically be forgotten, if a shaman tank uses a fire totem, it is mostly for threat.
Windfury can be a source of a conflict. It costs mana (shaman tank's weakpoint until really lategame), raises DPS threat while not affecting him because rockbiter can't proc WF. So WF totem basically forces them to spend mana just to spend more abilities to keep threat and burn even more mana as the worst resource management tank.
Many shammie tanks will still do it.
Just know that if a shaman tank hesitates on WF in a dungeon, they might be anxious about their mana bar.

 * Lack of defensive CD.
Most tanks can press a button and reduce the incoming damage by a lot temporarily. In this sense, shaman tanks have no defensive CD.
They can of course use the Hakkar trinket but realistically they have two things similar to a defensive CD but not a defensive CD:
 1. Earth shield + LS + agility totem + stoneskin totem + life totem (possibly including a resistence totem).
This is the absolute defensive setup. Earth shield restores health after using an orb and LS during it adds further health. Agi totem adds armor (agix2), dodge and crit (at level 60, 4-5%, depending if talented), stoneskin adds some flat damage reduction and life totem adds some HPS.
These together make the shaman survive better and for dungeon-level damage, they are quite effective.
But they are nowhere close to a percentual damage reduction.
 2. Kiting. I'm not kidding. If casters are done, the situation is dire, the shaman can drop an earthbind totem and jump out. This is not a traditional defensive CD and def not a strategy in raids but at dungeons it fulfills its job: totally negates melee damage if moving correctly.
Personally used it at Gilneas with the guards many times. Can be used even against casters if you can lose LOS asap.

 * Loots
 *Shaman is the ultimate hybrid now. Can play any roles and beside plates, can wear anything. Many shammie tanks are normal. But every now and then, you'll see tanks rolling for everything, saying, 'I need for OS'.*

 * Taunt and movement
Unlike bears and warriors, shamans can't rush to you if you pull a mob away. And their taunt is short distance (5 yards).
It means that a mob walking away from them can be a problem. Many shaman tanks start to walk toward the mob with the same speed and eventually taunting it back, maybe losing some other mobs in the process.

 *There are* **workarounds**, *though.*
 1. You can just run to the tank, knowing that taunt has 5 yards.
 2. ES can be tried but it can miss. It also just generates threat. If you REALLY pulled that mob, an ES won't pull it back.
 3. Shamans can't be faster but can make the mobs slower. Clever shaman tanks drop earthbind to be faster than the mob and reach+taunt it.
I mentioned this among the weaknesses because it is lesser known and many shaman tanks won't do this.

_____________________

 **Buffs.**

A shaman tank usually loves a **pala**, if:
 * Real pumpers get salv. The shammie could probably keep up with the threat, but the salvation on the pumper means more spared mana for them. Mana management is super important for them.
 * BoW on tank. I mean might and kings are fine, too but personally I ask for BoW.
 * Retri aura is sometimes nice but Devotion aura, spec until midgame is a game changer. Shamans even forget they wear leather until level 40.

 **Priests** are also super cool:
 * Stam, spec before midgame? Yes.
 * Spirit is minor but hey, mana regen is mana regen.
 * Keep shielding the shammie tank, specially pre-level 40.

 **Druid**:
 * I don't say that prio innervate on the tank instead of the healer but if healer manages, shammie tanks really like being innervated.
