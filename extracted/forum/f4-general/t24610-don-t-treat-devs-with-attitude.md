Yes—people who play free-to-play (F2P) games like Counter-Strike: Global Offensive (now evolved into Counter-Strike 2) are still considered customers, just in a slightly different sense than traditional buyers.

Here’s how it works:

 1. They’re customers even if they don’t pay

Even if someone never spends money, they still:

Use the product
Contribute to the player base (which keeps matchmaking alive)
Increase the game’s popularity and visibility

From a business perspective, they’re often called users, but they still have customer value.

 2. They generate indirect value

Free players help in ways like:

Making the game feel active and competitive
Attracting paying players (no one wants an empty game)
Watching ads or engaging with content (in some F2P games)
 3. Paying players are “premium customers”

In F2P models, companies like Valve Corporation rely on:

Skins, cosmetics, cases
Battle passes or upgrades

So:

Non-paying players = users / potential customers
Paying players = direct revenue-generating customers
 4. The key idea: you’re the product and the customer

F2P games often operate on a model where:

A small % of players (sometimes called “whales”) fund the game
Everyone else contributes by keeping the ecosystem alive

Short answer:
Yes—F2P players are customers in a broader sense, even if they don’t spend money. They’re part of the value system the game relies on.

AI:

Q: is someone who plays free to play games liek csgo, lol, dota a customer?

In the world of games like Dota 2, League of Legends, and CS:GO, whether a free player is a "customer" depends on how you look at the business:

Technically, No: In a strict business sense, a "customer" is someone who makes a financial transaction. If you have never spent money in the cash shop, you are officially a User or Player.
Strategically, Yes: Most modern game developers treat free players as Potential Customers. You are using a service they provide, and their goal is to eventually "convert" you into a paying customer by offering a skin or battle pass you can't resist.
The "Product" Perspective: There is a famous saying in tech: "If you aren't paying for the product, you are the product". Free players provide the massive community and short queue times that make the game attractive to the big spenders (the "Supporters"). Without a million free players to play against, the paying customers would have no one to show their fancy skins to.

Q: and if they paid for skins or other store items?

Once they spend even a single dollar, their status changes. In the eyes of the game developer, they officially transition ***from a Player to a Customer.***
