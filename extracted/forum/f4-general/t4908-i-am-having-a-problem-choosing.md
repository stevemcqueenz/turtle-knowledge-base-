You worry too much, mate. Just play what you like  smiling_turtle_head  .

Keep in mind that "not viable" only sometimes means that a spec or setup is utterly useless and simply will not work.
Example:
Warrior tank: good idea.
Warrior healer (with bandages and maxed first aid, ofc): not viable.

However, there are many cases in which "not viable" can be translated to things like "Speedrunners don't do it like that",  "I tried to play this way but I wasn't good enough, now I don't want to feel bad for seeing other people succeed where I failed misearably" or "I haven't found a guide that told me to do it like this and I'm too lazy to try new stuff by myself". You never know unless you try.

Still, being considerate about not holding a raid back is a good thing. This means everybody should put a reasonable effort into maintaining their gear, bringing consumables, and being prepared and on time for the raid without having to walk the dog between pulls and being sober and awake enough to move out of the glowy stuff on the ground.

But please don't let anyone push you into playing not the class or spec that you like. Just play what you want to play and then sink time into collecting good gear and enchants while becoming good at whatever you want to play (by playing and having fun instead of absorbing guides). Figuring stuff out on your own can be hard but it's fun.

The only "drawback" to happily pooping on the meta (which I've done for many years, and loved every second of it  smiling_turtle_head  ) is that you might not find a place in a sweaty minmaxer-zug-zug-raiding guild, but personally I don't see that as a great loss.

Yeah, I wouldn't argue that paladins are great dps, either.

However, just consider how often people die due to bad luck or bad playing. I think we can agree that a retadin might not be a great dps, but a skillfully played retadin is still a better addition to a raid than some guy who stands in the fire and dies.

If a raid can afford to completely lose people during an encounter and still kill a boss, they can also afford to take along a few meme-specs. Of course, this leaves less tolerance for mistakes from everybody.

I know that many people like to play it safe and minimize risks, but I personally think that locking specs out of a raid setup completely for the purpose of risk minimization goes too far.
