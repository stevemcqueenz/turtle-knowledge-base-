Hello, turtles

I was just wondering what the general consensus is regarding soft-reserving an item which isn't for your main spec? Here's an example: a shadow priest doing Kara 10 competing with healers for Sliver of Hope on soft-reserve. In the example given above, I've seen people leaving raids, stating soft-reserving healing gear as dps, is not OK. On the other hand, I've seen people defending such soft-reserving if a tank does the same, for example soft-reserving Vis'kag or Deathbringer from Ony.

So: Does SR trump MS, or should people only SR for their MS? Can or should I expect people to accept me soft-reserving Sliver of Hope as a druid tank for instance? I feel like I shouldn't be doing it, but on the other hand I don't need much else from that particular dungeon atm.

I really don't know what the "general" rule of thumb is here, so just interesting to hear other opinions on this   smiling_turtle

I main a hybrid and if I pug I usually ask the raid lead if it's okay if I SR x.

Some examples:
Warrior is not confident in his tanking gear but wants to tank in the long run. Comes as a fury, SRs *generic tanking item*

Overgeared guy wants to do MC/AQ20 but also wans to feel rewarded. Goes for an item for a spec he sometimes uses.

In general I think it's fair. Many 'MS' you see in those pugs are alts anyways.

## Post 98987 by Geojak (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2024-07-09T12:57:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=98987#p98987 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

During progression raiding I think you should not sr outside of ms, especially on highly sought after key items that are hard to replace. If it's a raid on farm, there is no reason to deny it as the raid is clearing it anyway, even if it's a shaman that wants styleen.

Imo, it's best, to couple sr with some kinda bonus point system so that if you try consistently for an item, you shoild be able to build up a roll advanatge over others than haven't grinded for ages and earn it fairly. This is especially important for those super rare or competed for items. Nobody wants to sr an item for 4 months only, to lose the roll to a newcomer. A fair system can prevent a lot of drama.

Some guilds opt for hard reserves = loot cousil as a solution which just causes even more drama since now people are forced to argue why they should be on the list instead of someone else.

Conclusion:
 1. Don't allow os sr on key items during progression
 2. Allow os sr in raids on farms
 3. Use a fair points system on sr rolls to reward loyal consistent members without direct partial interference by officers

A fair system could be: each raid you gain 1 bonus point for every 10 minutes raiding. On any sr rolls your points are added on top, if you win your sr due to the bonus points, then your points get halfed.
