# How I imagined CC2

## Post 115279 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2025-01-03T00:44:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=115279#p115279 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!

This is not a 'what should have been done' post. I am one person and I don't state that I know better than a whole crew working on this for months.
Just wanna show what my take was on CC2, what core ideas I followed and what I wanted to see.

The most important core idea was: 1 talent tree = 1 valid and interesting build for PVE. TWOW is primarily PVE and while PVP happens, from design pov my PVP focus would have been reduced only to avoid monstrosities to emerge. Fine-tuning would have happened mostly from PVE pov.
This idea means that fun specs which exist by taking talents all over the place could exist but would not be balanced for PVE. F.ex. a shockadin or a spellhance, while possible, wouldn't be planned to be nearly as good as a ret or enh which focus mainly on their tree. Core idea is 1 talent tree = 1 way to play. In exchange, that 1 way is made fun and viable.
This also means a unique approach towards the druid's feral tree. More about it later.
Other core idea is: the less utility you give to the team, the better your output is. Something for something.

So, how the builds would have looked like?
I won't go into details, just sharing the core concept behind each.

 **Hunters**
BM hunter - A pet-based DPS where the pet is responsible for the majority of the damage. I like to call this role 'the puppeteer'. If it oppresses PVP, some potential nerfs like the hunter also receiving n% of the damage which the pet receives. Not sure. All I wanted was a BM hunter which brings a pet to raid and relies heavily on it for DPS.
MM hunter - Done well, a ranged, non-caster DPS, just like now.
Survival hunter - Melee DPS, I agree to the direction. Maybe a bit differently, like hiding some additional traps behind the survival tree instead of things like carve. A mostly single-target focused melee DPS with middle of the pack numbers who can bring a bunch of utility by cleverly using a high variety of traps (instead of spamming fire trap on CD).

 **Mages**
Arcane - Mage already has two damage-focused caster DPS. I would have stayed where we were pre-CC2 and would have fleshed out the 'mana battery' archetype. Damage is not outstanding but acceptable, mana groups love them.
Fire - Mostly the way they were. A possible tweak to overcome fire resistant places (let's say you really enjoy your fire mage but also wanna run BWL) and - agreeing with TWOW - reconsidering how ignite works. Core would have remained the same, though.
Frost - Would have weakened their kiting toolkit a bit for PVP and would have moved them a bit more towards multi targets. Fire mage to nuke down that one boss, frost mage to make big trash packs disappear - for the price of limited DPS for single targets.

 **Warriors**
Fury - Slight nerf to DPS output but making the build a bit less gear-dependent. Still the biggets melee DPS in the game from a generic pov. No fury warrior - more precisely, no competitive fury warrior.
Arms - Better with bigger packs but overall considerably less damage than fury. In exchange providing unique debuffs on targets (for example added crit chance after they crit on someone).
Prot -  'The' tanking tree for warriors. Some aoe threat for them, mostly for dungeoning but nothing too strong. Making sure that their threat is decent.

 **Paladins**
Ret - Middle of the pack melee DPS with excellent utility.
Holy - Either the 'never oom but single target only' version of priest or we really go the melee healer way. I honestly liked their idea and was thinking about a melee healer earlier on but not with the current execution. Holy paladin, the melee healer would be very interesting if things like silence, cleave, area-based CCs etc would be proiperly handled in their talent tree.
Prot - The best aoe-tank in the game with an upgraded ardent defender talent, giving them a proper defensive CD. Worse at survivability than warriors but they are perfectly valid and can handle larger packs like noone else.

 **Shamans**
Elemental - Middle of the pack caster DPS with a unique spell damage totem. Mostly single-target focused with the occasional spell cleaves. No realistic way to do aoe in raids.
Enhancement - This one hurts a bit because I love shaman tanking. But more people love enh DPS. So they are a 'middle of the pack' melee DPS build focusing on burst damage.
Resto - Mostly the way they are. Maybe something motivating people to use a bigger variety of healing spells.

 **Druids**
Balance - Again, middle of the pack casters with great team utility.
Resto - More HoT toolkit, like some which were added after CC2. And a normal rez for dungeoning.
Feral - This can be controversial but I'd mostly merge cat and bear into one build, making most talents do something in either of those forms and making stat conversion from items smoother so both form can work properly. I'd have imagined feral as 'the' offtank build. Bear would have been a valid tank even for raids but the worst of the 3 while cat would have been a middle of the pack melee DPS with no cleave or aoe and less utility than a usual 'middle of the pack' build. In exchange, they would be the best choice when your tanking numbers are fluctuate during a raid, bears outperforming non-prot warriors grabbing a shield and performing pretty decent but not top DPS as cats when they are not needed to offtank.

 **Rogues**
Combat - Would make them the higfhest single-target melee DPS (or DPS in general) in the game. Very narrow utility, very narrow PVP gameplay, suffering with muliple targets. In exchange we could have made them the best when there is only one target to kill.
Sub - Moving improved exposed armor to their tree immediately. A support melee class but with better DPS numbers than the thing they have now and a much lesser reliance on reagents to play the game. Also, leaving the PVP talents as intact as possible for the rogue PVP enjoyers.
Assassination - A bit of a mixture of these two builds. A rogue tree granting some utility via poisons (adding some interesting poisons only for assassination rogues) while having high single target DPS. Definitely below combat's output though.

 **Priests**
Disc - I would have made disc priests into proactive healers, heavily relying on shields and HoTs instead of direct healing while making their damage prevention toolkit bigger.
Holy - Direct healing specialist. Many big heals, each having a casting time.
Shadow - Really considering a talent which removed threat modifiers from mind blast. Other than that mostly what we had pre-CC2. A somewhat gear-dependent 'life+mana sucker'. With better itemization options, for sure.

 **Warlocks**
Affliction - Dot and channel-based build, agreeing with TWOW. But with better output. Gameplay-wise a bit more single target-focused than destruction and the slower an enemy die, the better they get. Can be top DPS for bosses but lags behind a bit with trash.
Destruction - Huge burst options but a very mana-consuming gamestyle. Might need to skip many GCDs for tapping during a long fight. Optimal for trash and speedruns.
Demonology - The other puppeteer class. Honestly, I don't care if they need to use imp or a felguard, all I want is a competitive pet-based DPS build for raiding.

And this is highly problematic because the drastic changes of CC2 (pandora's box) are a slippery slope for the rest of the server the logical conclusion of which is something so far removed from VANILLA plus that it does not deserve the name. I am not saying turtle is there yet, but as the historic vanilla base feels alienated more and more, you will just be left with players who don't really understand or appreciate vanilla design philosophy and just want the fast-paced commercialized bloat feel of SoD.

Re: turtle wow's new content, I am much more accepting and lenient. It's not perfect, but a lot of it is genuinely really great. New content is their strong point and I would bet it is the real reason that people come to this server, not the class changes. The thing about content is: if you don't like a certain new zone or dungeon, etc. it doesn't have to impact your entire gameplay experience when there is so many alternative areas to explore. And new content is much easier to tweak if there are any issues (e.g. changes to ES or Hyjal).

CLASS CHANGES on the other hand? You can't avoid their effects. How your class plays is core to the game, so if there are problems with that as we see with CC2, it impacts the entire gameplay experience. And class design is so much more complex than content, which is why focusing on *tweaks* rather than *revamps* is so important. Brazenly thinking you can just come in and design classes better than the original vanilla devs and not understand the delicate ecosystem in which classes are connected will inevitably break that ecosystem and require a never-ending stream of fixes as we see now with CC2 damage control.

They were set to launch Kara40 probably in Nov and then got moved to Dec (as per in game message). It still has not been released, largely because of CC2 backlash. It is sad to see new content rollout essentially being held hostage by the turmoil over class changes, which has even resulted in a dev allegedly leaving. It feels like a well-managed team would not have given the green-light to 5000 hours worth of class changes that were never needed. And players have suggested rolling back CC2 and testing changes on a separate test realm as the most sustainable solution for the direction this server is going, but the dev team does not seem open to this (CC2 sunk cost fallacy and all that).
