# Tents feel like a "low quality private server" addition that doesn't match the quality of TWOW. Please rework them. (1)

## Post 158641 by Oldenwisp — 2025-11-06T16:26:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=158641#p158641 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

*This is the best version of WOW I have ever played. The quality of 95% of the additions is mindblowing. I feel there are some details however that are giving a bad name and look to that massive work done and I will make a series of threads to address them and hopefully help the team iron them out.*

(1) **Tents.**

Tents, as they currently exist, feel like an awkward “private server” addition— not integrated naturally into the game’s world or systems. They stand out both thematically and mechanically in ways that don’t align with the world’s logic or overall game balance.

 **1. Thematic and World Logic Problems**

- Seeing tents lined up outside every major city looks out of place and ugly.

- It makes little sense that characters would camp at the gates instead of using proper inns designed for rest.

- From a worldbuilding perspective, the idea that a tent offers better rest than an actual inn breaks immersion.

 **2. Gameplay and Balance Concerns**

- Tents significantly overpower the base game’s resting mechanic.

- A profession item that can double rest benefits and reduce rest time from days to minutes becomes mandatory for efficient leveling.

- Players who level with tents experience a completely different pace and rhythm than those who don’t — making it almost a requirement, not an optional bonus.

- Inns lose their meaning.

- A player cannot even evade tents if they want to not receive this not classic bonus often.

 **3. Out of Scope for a Profession Item**

- A single craftable item shouldn’t redefine core progression systems.

- When an item changes how players approach leveling or resting entirely, it steps outside the intended balance and purpose of professions. **Note that this is even a secondary profession.**
 **Conclusion/TLDR:**
Tents could be an interesting concept if rebalanced or reframed, but as they stand, they distort both the theme and pacing of the game. A rework that aligns them more closely with immersion and intended progression would make the experience feel fair and authentic again.
Tents could simply act as inns, without having to walk to an inn, and be instead easy to make and last shorter, but the devs can decide what the rework should be.

Sorry for my English. As the saying goes: put up or shut up.

So:
 **Our first goal** is to make the resting process fit into the game without breaking the "slow and steady" principles. We are keeping the rest bonus exclusively for taverns and cities, where the full 150% bonus accumulates over 1.5 hours. This will allow players to leave their characters AFK in taverns, contributing to the world's liveliness, while also preventing the abuse of the bonus experience system.
This means Player 1, who plays for a couple of hours in the evening, can safely log out in a tavern and have the full 150% rest bonus by the next day. Player 2, who plays for 5-6 hours, will use up their bonus XP and face a choice: continue leveling without the bonus or take a 1.5-hour break to go touch grass in real life (or hang out in the tavern with other players).

 **Our second goal** is to rework tents, making them useful but not mandatory. Setting up a tent grants a 10% movement speed buff (and perhaps buffs to other stats?) that accumulates at a rate of 1 minute of buff duration for every second spent under the tent (up to a maximum of 60 minutes). This won't give us any significant advantages but will be a nice bonus.
It would also be good to change the campfire buff from the Cooking skill. It works on the same principle as the tent: for every second spent by the campfire, you get +1 minute to the buff duration (max 60 min.), granting +10% Spirit, which allows the character to recover faster between battles.

Example: You set up a tent and a campfire, chill for a minute, and get buffs that make you feel a bit more comfortable while leveling.
