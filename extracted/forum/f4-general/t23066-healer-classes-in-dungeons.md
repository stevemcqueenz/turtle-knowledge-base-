# Healer Classes in Dungeons

## Post 160991 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2025-11-28T13:03:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=160991#p160991 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

Hey!

This will be a **really long** post, but I wanna share my experience and remarks regarding dungeon healing using the four classes capable of doing that.
 **I am by no means an ultimate expert on the topic.**
I healed up each four classes via dungeon spamming but still, I can be incorrect or might forget to mention crucial details, keep this in mind.
I focused solely on dungeon healing, no PVP, raiding, questing was taken into consideration.

 **Part 1: Basic Description**

 **Druid**
 *Disclaimer: I leveled a tree, will focus on that build.*

Drus are frequently looked down during dungeon healing. The lack of normal rez can indeed cause annoyance sometimes if the team lacks anyone who can properly rez, but otherwise I think the bad reputation is **not justified**.
 *At least from the moment Improved Regrowth is taken.*
Before taking that talent, I also think they are *kinda bad*.

That talent allows drus to consistently downgrade a healing spell, expecting frequent crits, saving bunch of mana.
Turning to tree further helps with this as HoTs get cheaper, and this leads to one of the main advantages of dru: *they are extremely mana efficient.*
You have a 20% HoT discount, a cheap Rejuvenation, a Regrowth with 50+ spell crit chance, ready to be downgraded, a dirt cheap Swiftmend and the option to Innervate every 6 minutes.
This leads to the other big strength of dru: several HoTs mean that they can turn the big damage spikes into manageable ones.
There are optimally two ticks happening on the tank during the spike and your Swiftmend can turn into a really big Holy Shock on a 15 sec CD, all you need is to make sure the target still has some HoT ticks.
In short, the big damage spikes which can be really annoying to other healers are easily manageable to the druids, and those trees can keep up that pump heal *for a really long time.*

There is somewhat of a misunderstanding about the tree in dungeons.
People say Regrowth is not optimal since you get a relatively small heal with a HoT component which you keep overwriting if you spam heal on a single target.
 *This is half true.*
First, look at what regrowth really is: it is a 20% discounted, 50+% spell crit spell greatly benefiting from your extra healing provided by the tree form.

<https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=9858/regrowth>
[https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=2 ... aling-wave](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=25357/healing-wave)

Used the Vanilla spells, I had links for that but they are perfect for the demonstration.
Used shammie Healing Wave as comparison.
Is Regrowth underperforming?
It heals less immediately and is slightly more expensive while having a shorter cast even if full Healing Wave cast reduction is talented.

 *But.*
Add the 20% price reduction and the fact it kinda reliably crits and the numbers are not that bad.
If we are very strict, trees indeed have a slight disadvantage at single target spamming but it is way less worse then people make it out to be.
Also, if you consider it, we want to spam big heals at spikes to prevent the tank dying.
But if you have 2 HoT effects going on, you reach the same results just *via different means*.
The only annoyance is your Regrowth HoT being overwritten all the time.
You can also leave tree form and start using Healing Touch but would you spend mana to re-tree and lose your extra healing of tree form for that?

 *Specially since keeping up HoT ticks here and there is the biggest trick of dru.*
As I said earlier, Swiftmend is the dirt cheap holy shock which requires a HoT ticking on the target.
Stop thinking about HoT ticks as wasted healing, think about them as *investment*.
For example, put a Rejuvenation on the DPS you know will pull.
When the HP starts going down, you can just Swiftmend him back in no time.
So while single target heal spamming is indeed not ideal, *it is far from being bad.*
And the HoT ticks being kept up mean you can always emergency instant heal the tank.
It is pretty nice, considering you can also instant heal via Nature's Swiftness.

 **Strengths:**
 * Damage spikes are easy to handle.
 * Extremely good mana management.
 * Instant emergency heals (Swiftmend, Nature's Swiftness).
 * If confident, can Innervate starved mana users like shammies or hunters.
 * Some help to tank at keeping threat via Thorns + a generic buff (ignoring E. Blessing as it rarely happens in a dung).
 * Melee utility with Fairy Fire.
 * Battle rez.
 * Occasional Hibernate or Roots.
 * Being able to decurse can be huge. Poison cleanse is ok.
 * Vile trolls can't hex you. Actually big help in ZF or LBRS.
 * The option to self HoT then emergency offtank.

 **Weaknesses:**
 * Single-target heal spam has some disadvantage.
 * AoE emergency heal is limited by big CD on Tranquility.
 * No normal rez can be annoying.
 * If targeted by mobs, can't reach 100% pushback prevention or fade/bubble.
 * Really low offensive contribution.
 * Buffs are kinda underwhelming, specially compared to shamans and paladins.
 * Movement can be iffy because of tree speed reduction. Being decent at speed costs mana via shapeshift.
 * Lack of temporary armor increase (think about shammy's Ancestral Healing or priest's Inspiration).
 * Reaching Moonglow means having to take a bunch of irrelevant talents.

 **Verdict**:
Tree is way better at dungeons than people give it credit for. If the team is inexperienced and there is no other healer, grave runs will be super annoying though. My biggest nitpick is the lack of things to do when you don't need to heal. A priest can wand, curse etc. A pala pulls up decent DPS via melee. A shaman, by nature of WF and strength totem, gives a huge melee buff and he can add some shocks every time he wants. In contrast, a tree punching mobs is just lame. It is only somewhat cool if the target has been judged with wisdom.

 **Proposals:**
 * Give dru a normal rez. I don't care, let it be a talent in Resto tree, let it require a reagent, whatever. It just makes some random runs way less annoying. People saying it leads to class homogenization are wrong in my opinion. Tree being unique by failing to rez the rogue and having to wait five minutes for a graverun is just bad. The tree is unique because of the several HoTs and Innervate. Those things give the tree its *identity*.
 * Give dru a talent which provides some kind of benefit if you overwrite Regrowth HoT with another Regrowth. Maybe mana back or some amount of instant heal. Would turn the feel bad part of single target healing into an interesting mechanic.
 * Give dru something to do during fights when no healing is currently needed. Maybe a better tree punch or a second debuff which can be spammed to help the team (maybe allow Insect Swarm). Dru's offensive contribution is really lacking.
 * Give dru a worse form of Innervate available much earlier, so that dru healers before the late 30s suck less.
 * Either remove the healing spell cost reduction from Moonglow and integrate that into resto talents or make some earlier balance talents more interesting for healers so picking them don't feel useless.

 **Shaman**

Another class which starts bad but scales beautifully.
Raiders know shamans as chain heal machines but it is, for the majority of leveling, not the case. Shammies need to get both Stable Shields and Improved Water Shield to get their mana rolling, which means being 8 talent points late from late restoration talents. Improved Chain Heal is at row six, and honestly, the 3 second cast base version sucks.
So we are at level 47 the earliest when the shaman has full cast time-reduced chain heal, level 44-45 being the area where chain heal has a comparable cast time to Vanilla.

 *So, why does shaman suck initially?*
Awful at mana. No meditation talent for mana regen, no dirt cheap spells to spam.
Also, the *totems*.
Totems are buffs which have to be spammed basically every time the team moves and initially they take down quite the chunk of the shaman's mana bar.
Imagine being a healer with your mana bar being capped at 80% most of the time unless the party waits for you to drink after you arrive at the new spot and you summoned the totems. Totemic Recall helps *but not that much*
.
This will change radically with Stable Shields and Improved Water Shield.
 9 mp5 + 18% meditation is better than the other classes receive.
 *But to get to this combo, you need to be level 35 at least.*

Things get somewhat better from level 31 though.
Healing Wave is a big help:
'Your Healing Wave and Lesser Healing Wave spells have a 33/66/100% chance to increase the healing of your next Healing Wave or Lesser Healing Wave on that target by 6% for 15 sec. Stacks up to 3 times.'

It basically allows you to get away with downranks during focus healing.
Time to put r1 healing wave on the keyboard to cheaply trigger 18% healing bonus on the tank.
So shaman healers initially suck but from the mid 30s they get great at mana management.
At the early 30s they also get air totems, completing their totemic element set that gives them a really unique niche: *melee party healer.*
The more melee you see the more OP you'll be.

Shamans from that point can give the party WF, Strength totem and Flametongue totems at once, making clears much smoother.
Until the lategame this becomes the shaman identity: reliable single-target heals helped by Healing Way, Tidal Surge and Undertow, the ability to emergency heal via whatever Nature's Swiftness is called these days and giving power fantasy to a melee party.
As time goes, party-healing also becomes much better, making shamans exceptionally strong.
To be honest, what I like (and also dislike) at resto shaman leveling is that it's a true zero to hero experience.

 **Strengths:**
 * Excellent later leveling experience (gets good from the 30s).
 * Excellent melee buffs.
 * Great additional utility (resistances, tremor, grounding).
 * With enough spell crit, can spam armor buff left and right via Ancestral Healing.
 * Self-rez as wipe-protection.
 * Great at interrupting or even preventing a spell being cast (Grounding Totem).
 * Usually somewhat bulky. Can also emergency taunt if has a shield.
 * If Healing Focus talent is taken and Earth shield is learnt, can reach 100% spell pushback prevention.
 * Party-wide, super efficient poison and disease cleansing.
 * Spirit Link can be a life saver in case of huge incoming damage on tank.

 **Weaknesses:**
 * Early game mana.
 * Lack of quick, wide heal until lategame.
 * The more caster-focused the group is, the worse their utility is.
 * Relatively rudimental heal toolkit (normal heal, fast heal, chain heal).
 * Magic and Curse effects can be the most annoying. A shaman can't deal with any of those.
 * Healing Stream totem is useless even with talents.
 * No Atiesh for you.

 **Verdict:**
Shaman starts off as a weak healer, being not particularly mana efficient, not having ways to passively regain mana and being forced to give up around 20% of the mana bar for totem support. They end up as one of the strongest healers lategame, specially if the party has 3 or more melees.
Even though they can only cleanse the 2 more 'boring' debuff types, they can do it extremely efficiently, adding more kinds of cleanse to their scope would be unfair.
Also, excellent fear negation.
Just don't expect great party healing from them before their mid 40s.

 **Proposals:**
 * Honestly, only the curve. The end product is really good. Maybe put improved water shield earlier in the tree and increase the mana regain from totemic recall. That way shaman becomes valid earlier and it sucks less to give totem support early game. Everything else is all right.

 **Paladin**

Palas are extremely strong but people tend to overestimate them based on raid logs. Don't get me wrong: *they are good.* But raid logs don't include mitigated damage due to armor increase which shamans and priests trigger left and right. A paladin can only do it via Lay on Hands. Sure, it is a bit bigger and lasts 2 minutes but it's on a 40 min CD and makes you lose your mana. As a result, holy palas are still excellent, but they are not as dominant as raid logs make you believe. *Mitigated damage is just not shown fully.*

Holy palas start off at an interesting spot. They have really cool buffs both via auras and blessings (and hands).
They have a dirt cheap spell but initially it heals so little you need +healing asap to make any use of it.
Holy pala has a weak initial phase when the Flash of Light is still useless and they gotta rely on Holy Light, becoming oom quick.
Still, blessing of wisdom and judgment helps once learned.
The most interesting part of holy pala in my opinion is that it requires you to know the dungeons more than the other healers. For optimal gameplay you have to get melee range for strikes and maybe for a stun or two.
Which mobs cleave? Which ones have aoe silence? Where is the back of the mob so I can add more DPS while hitting?
The game expects you to know more than other healers to do your job optimally. *But this is why it is so unique and fun.*

As you get enough +healing, things become a cakewalk if you don't run into silence or fear.
Flash of Light becomes an almost free spell which can be spammed to keep the tank alive while wisdom (blessing, seal, judgment) and holy strike makes sure your mana bar stays full.
AoE healing can be a *bit more problematic* though.
Holy Shock has a CD which you can to a degree circumvent with Crusader Strike.
Holy Strike does some group healing but not enough to heal the party in case of big group damage.
In my opinion this is the weak spot of holy pala leveling. You can spam your fast heals and use emergency shocks, even give some aoe heal, but unless you are super-geared, big party-aoe becomes a problem really quick.
Fortunately it is really rare in dungeons.

The utility... While shamans have an OP melee utility kit, palas have an excellent, *personalized* utility kit.
Good options for everyone.
Also, as a holy pala, you have a get out of jail card from the two biggest dungeon problems:
 * Tank can't keep threat -> Salv the DPS.
 * You are too undergeared to keep the tank alive via Flash of Light spam -> Blessing of Light.
Sure, the latter option comes at level 40.

In short, you are a utility powerhouse with excellent single-target heal, some emergency heals and some AoE heal with a great cleanse kit. Only curses you cannot touch. But in exchange of variety, your efficiency is meh.
You can't abolish those debuffs or can't mass cleanse. But still, you can cleanse 3 out of the 4 debuffs.
Also, your judgment of wisdom makes you best friends with mana using melee.
Enh shamans, melee hunters, they all love you.

 **Strengths:**
 * Mana management.
 * Personalized, super strong buffs.
 * Great damage as a healer.
 * Can make tanks extremely consistent via Salvation and Retri (or even Devotion) Aura.
 * Can heal tanks beyond their gear/level via Blessing of Light.
 * Really strong stun.
 * Decent AoE damage even without spellpower gear.
 * Wide range of cleanses.
 * Extreme armor for a healer, can easily emergency taunt.
 * High upside-high cost emergency button via Lay on Hands.
 * Can just save someone (probably a caster) every 5 minutes via Hand of Protection.
 * Negates movement impairment on tank.
 * Enables mana using melees.
 * Learning Spiritual Focus and swapping to Concentration Aura can give them 100% spell pushback prevention.
 * Great self- save via bubble.

 **Weaknesses:**
 * Needs +healing gear before becoming okay.
 * Less reliable on healing up aoe damage.
 * High upside-high cost emergency button via Lay on Hands. Yes, both a strength and a weakness.
 * Cleanses need to be spammed one by one.
 * For their full toolkit, they require melee range, which can be a problem, depending on the mob.
 * Daybreak can be considered a preventive healing buff but it cannot be consistently applied, needs crit.
 * Holy Strike needs a deep talent (Blessed Strikes) before feeling like a relevant healing spell.
 * Buff duration is still short, rebuffing can be annoying.
 * No Atiesh for you.
 * Judgement of Light is underwhelming.

 **Verdict:**
An outstanding support option with a huge variety of buffs. Tank healing will be excellent from mid-game. Big AoE heal is a weak spot. Arguably scales too well into late raids but logs are misleading as they don't show the mitigated damage via shammie and priest armor increase.

 **Proposals:**
 * If there's one buff I'd consider for Holy Palas, it is giving them a weaker Blessing of Light earlier, maybe even for Deadmines. That way they can already play they rotation even if their Flash of Light is still trash without the proper gear. *This buff wouldn't affect endgame performance in any way.*

 **Priest**
 *Disclaimer: I have leveled a Proclaim Champion build, not an Enlighten build.*

Priest is in a super weird spot. I mentioned that some builds go from zero to hero. Priests go from hero to okay.
Initially, they are super good. In general, they manage mana better than the others and have a huge healing toolkit basically for any situation.
But their weak point is that none of their (non-champion) buffs are offensive per say. Their offensive contribution is limited to some Shadow Word: Pains (usually without +sp gear), and wanding, which gets less and less relevant as time goes on.
At a later level you'll stand in a dungeon easily healing up everyone, doing 2-4% percent damage and feeling weird because as a pala or shammie the group could be clearing the mobs considerably faster.
Priest just doesn't have that offensive 'umphf' later.
Initially, it does as wands start off being really good.

The spirit buff, later Inner Focus and the meditation talent ensures you'll always have the mana you need.
Fade ensures you are always safe.
And while champion spell helps with utility, it is limited to one person. The 5% damage buff can be used to make the tank more consistent but it is nowhere near a salvation f.ex. And no one else benefited from your champion buff.

Priests can't cleanse poison but importantly, they can cleanse magic, which is super important.
Arguably priests are worse cleansers than pala but at least they can also abolish the disease.
 *Fearward is a huge utility, though.*

 **Strengths:**
 * Biggest healing toolkit in the game. A heal for every situation.
 * Buffs helping people stay alive (stam, even spirit and shadow resistance).
 * Access to magic removal.
 * Fearward is huge.
 * Early game damage contribution.
 * Mana management in general.
 * Can proc extra armor via Inspiration talent.
 * Can stay safe via Fade.
 * Occassional utility and hilarious scenarios via Mind Control.
 * Great AoE spike for a shorter amount of time via Holy Nova spam, if talented.
 * Current Proclaim Champion is better than nothing.
 * Emergency AoE fear.

 **Weaknesses:**
 * Damage contribution falls off.
 * Raid performance (not the topic of this article).
 * Most limited access to healing items (cloth only).
 * People not knowing when to use AoE fear will wipe the party.

 **Verdict:**
Priest is the golden boy of early game healing and while (in a dungeon environment), their healing stays awesome, but the lack of offensive contribution/utility makes them less interesting as time goes on. From a dungeon PoV, all they need is a better Proclaim Champion talent.
But it's not all gloomy. If your party has a priest healer, and the priest is not a total noob, you guys are *safe*.
And that's primarily what a healer is supposed to ensure.

 **Proposals:**
 * Buff the Proclaim Champion talent. Either make it a really big buff, or turn it into a party buff. A priest giving a 5% damage upgrade to every party member? Already more interesting. And still way worse then a WF totem alone.
 * Maybe have a wand-scaling talent based on healing power at deep holy. Not to have caster DPS at all but to keep the priest's damage contribution relevant when pinging even at level 60.
 * Figure out how to make priests not suck at raids (not the topic of this writing).

 **Part 2: Talent and Spell Change Ideas**

This part is all about theorycrafting. *Ignore it if you want actual info about the builds.*
This is me ranting about how I think different builds could be optimized.

 **Currently unfinished.**

 **Druid**

 1. The Moonglow Problem: Currently the only talent truly benefiting resto while going down until Moonglow/Nature's Grace is Nature's Reach.
To make this more rewarding for resto druids, I made a custom version of the Balance tree and highlighted the changes.
I respected the ferals' need to reach key talents.
I disregarded problems with Boomkins regarding deep talents as this post is about healers.

![Image](https://i.postimg.cc/QdCzr2RP/1.png)

 *The changes:*
 * Swapped Moonfury and Moonglow, making Moonglow easier to reach. I think it's fair, now spells get cheaper before they hit harder. This also meant moving Nature's Grace to the 4th row (a potential pain point).
 * Swapped Nature's Reach and Guidance of the Dream (forgot to highlight that one). You get a bit of added range before getting 70% spell pushback prevention. Gives resto druids relevant talents for row 2.
 * Changed the effect of Improved Nature's Grasp.
 *Old:*
Increases the chance for your Nature's Grasp to entangle an enemy by 15/30/45/65%.
 *New:*
Increases the chance for your Nature's Grasp to entangle an enemy by 15/30/45/65%.
Also, being physically struck by an enemy grants you 7/15/23/30% chance to avoid interruption caused by damage while casting for 5 seconds.

Why? This way druids can reach 100% spell pushback prevention if they spend enough talent points (Nature's Focus for resto, Guidance of the Dream for balance).
But they are limited by the types of spells they receive full pushback prevention on.
The effect is also temporary and only activates at melee.
From resto PoV: this allows them to cast without any pushback if they aggro in a dungeon without relying on Concentration Aura. This is something a paladin and a shaman already can do so I'd consider this fair.

With these changes, resto druids only have 3 dead talent points trying to reach Moonglow, instead of 13(!).
Including Nature's Grace, we talk about 5 dead points vs 15.

In the Restoration Tree my goal was to differentiate better from the tree and non-tree gameplay plus added some tweaks with focus on the early game.

![Image](https://i.postimg.cc/RFp32nfg/2.png)

 * Removed Furor from the tree as it is clearly a feral talent.
 * Added a new first row talent which gives you 10% spirit for 5 talent points.
 * Swapped Nature's Focus and Reflection. That way the leveling druid can have an early game spirit increase and can access mana regeneration while casting between levels 15-17, turning it ready for Wailing Caverns.
 * To turn non-tree healers into a unique utility healer build, added a new talent under Aessina's Bloom. With that talent, casting Healing Touch on a target affected by Rejuvenation or Regrowth increases the target's haste for 10 seconds, equal to the rank of Healing Touch which was used. This would turn non-tree healers into an interesting utility option, hunting down HoT targets and deciding which rank of Healing Touch to use to optimize both healing and support.
 * Added a secondary effect to Preservation.
 *Old:*
Increases the periodic healing of Regrowth by 10/20/30% if the friendly target is affected by Rejuvenation.
 *New:*
Increases the periodic healing of Regrowth by 10/20/30% if the friendly target is affected by Rejuvenation.
If you cast Regrowth on a target which already has Regrowth on it, the target gets instantly healed for 20/40/60% of the spell's remaining heal over time effect.

The goal was to make tree single target heal smoother by not letting overwritten Regrowth stacks go to waste.

 **What I wanted to tackle with these changes:**
 * Made early game healing better.
 * Forced the druid to waste less talent points in the balance tree.
 * Reinforced the utility of the non-tree heal spec.
 * Improved tree single target heal spam.

 **What I didn't tackle:**
 * Tree's close to 0 offensive contribution.
 * Out of combat rezzes. However, it could be a 1TP talent in row 1 or 2 anytime.

 **Pain points I might have introduced:**
 * Nature's Grace being available 1 row earlier might have unexpected PVP effects in lower brackets.
 * Pushed 5 more talent points to feral (but they have currently the fewest amount of talent points in the tree).
 * Nature's Grace and Tree of Life Form would be pickable together, might be abused by trees with 20% cheaper regrowth. If it causes any kind of problem, Nature's Grace can be pushed back one row where it was.
