To calculate the probability that in 20 rolls of the three 10-sided dice (one red and two blue), none of the blues result in 1 and the red results in 1 or 2, we can break down the problem into smaller parts and use conditional probability.

 1.	Probability of a blue die not resulting in 1: Each die has 9 sides other than 1, so the probability of not getting 1 on a roll is 9/10.

 2.	Probability of both blue dice not resulting in 1 in one roll: Since the dice rolls are independent, the probability of neither blue die resulting in 1 in one roll is (9/10)x(9/10).

 3.	Probability of the red die not resulting in 1 or 2: Each die has 8 sides other than 1 or 2, so the probability of not getting 1 or 2 on a roll is 8/10.

 4.	Probability of none of the dice resulting in 1 or 2 in one roll: Similar to the case of the blue dice, since rolls are independent, the probability of the red die not resulting in 1 or 2 is (8/10)x(8/10).

 5.	Probability of none of the dice resulting in 1 or 2 in 20 rolls: Since rolls are independent, we simply raise the probability of each roll to the number of rolls, i.e., (8/10)x(8/10)^20.

 6.	Final probability of the desired event occurring: Subtract the calculated probability from the total possible events, which is 1, to find the probability of the desired event occurring. Therefore, the final probability is 1−(810×810)201−(108×108)20.

Now, let's calculate:

 1−((8/10)×(8/10))^20 ≈ 1−(0.64)^20 ≈ 1−0.0012 ≈ ≈0.9988

Thus, the probability of rolling the three dice 20 times and not getting 1 on any of the blues or 1 or 2 on the red is approximately 0.9988, or 99.88%.
