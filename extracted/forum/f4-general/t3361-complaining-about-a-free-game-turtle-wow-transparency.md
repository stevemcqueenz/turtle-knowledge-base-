# Complaining About a Free Game / Turtle WoW Transparency

## Post 20475 by Jolikmc (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2022-07-26T22:28:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=20475#p20475 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Recently, *Turtle WoW* dropped its v1.16.1 patch which was supposed to radically change the game in a lot of good ways.  Tons of bugs were to be fixed, classes were to be rebalanced, new Talents were added, and new content was, too.  This patch had been in the works since the end of 2021 and people had been eager to experience all the new stuff.  There's just one problem.

 **The patch broke more things than it fixed.**

People complained and that understandable.  They wanna play their game.  I know *I* do.  What's *less* understandable, to me, is the idea that other people instantly *shot down* said complaints because "this is a free game, you have no right to complain".  Folks, that's not how that works.  Just because it's free doesn't mean you can't complain.  But on top of that… a little more information about what's being done to *resolve* some of these issues would be nice.

Jo Li KMC's Patented Wall-o'-Text Spoiler Block On Day 1, the server suffered major and repeated crashing.  On Day 2, much the same.  This makes it very difficult, if not impossible, to play.  Everyone's Class Talents got reset and, in doing so, some people seemingly *lost* their "free respec tokens" – or so I've heard.  On top of that, a lot of the new Talents don't work even work *as intended*.  Many quests are broken and, for a while, spells-casting classes **could not gain experience points** because, for whatever reason, only *physical attacks* (melee, missiles) were "tagging" mobs to players.  The Health bar on targets no longer displays how much health a mob has, some AddOns seem to act more erratically under this patch, and overall, it's been something of a disaster trying to get things working the way they're supposed to.  It's almost like the shell-heads on Turtle Team just monkeyed about with the code without actually testing it.  Basically, the server is currently in the same state as most release commercial games: "early access" without being called "early access".  I guess it's just easier to toss up a buggy, sloppy, half-finished mess of code up, call it "done", then have to sort through [dozens of bug reports](https://github.com/slowtorta/turtlewow-bug-tracker/issues) and fix things later on?

I understand that Turtle Team consists of, like, three main coders who have full-time jobs, and a handful of people who don't fully understand how the emulation software (Vanilla MaNGOS, I think?) works with the v1.12.x code.  Frankly, it's absolutely *gobsmacking* what the Turtle Team has done without reverse-engineering the game more thoroughly.  They added new race / class combos, two new playable races, about *fifty* new regions for players to poke around…  They implemented hundreds of new quests, revamped old content, balanced the game somewhat better, and they continue to have a server that supports no less than 600 online at any given time… *most* of the time.  To say that this is just some "slacker server" running out of some grandma's basement would be the understatement of the year.  *Turtle WoW* is a major undertaking and it proves it has chops with everything from its new items to its all new dungeons, created from scratch!  The community is *booming*, they have official artists and musicians, maybe a couple of modelers, and all and all, this is a server that simply blows the likes of Valkyrie / Elysium and Nostalrius *out of the water*!!  However… there is one major problem that I have seen with this server.  It's a pretty common one that happens with both commercial companies and independent crews like Turtle Team.

 **Turtle Team lacks transparency and competency.**

No, no, I'm not saying they sell your personal information to the highest builder or that they intentionally make broken content.  As I understand it, it's pretty hard to implement new stuff without some major hack-jobs.  No, what I mean by a lack of transparency and competency is simply… no one on the team seems all that keen to keep people updated on the goings on.
It's pretty normal for a commercial company to announce "big changes" to build hype for a new patch or new content.  That's nothing new.  The thing is, when things go wrong – or *horribly* wrong, in this case – it's generally good for public relations to, you know, tell the community what's going on.  Instead?  This whole situation has been handled with simple explanations of "Server's down" or "We're looking into it".  *Behind closed doors*.  Simply, if you aren't on the *Discord* server, **you're not gonna know anything**.

Since the patch dropped yesterday, I've seen maybe *one* GM post on the forums addressing all the issues, and that post was made after the server had been *sorted* (for the time).  The in-game Message of the Day or "Breaking News" box has had the exact same message in it for the last few months: "disable your anti-virus software if you can't patch the game", and there has been no attempt made to keep people in-the-know on the official forums.  Why?  Surely, one-or-two GMs can pop in with a little knowledge.  Better still, maybe one of the coders who are "looking into the matter" could say a word or two.  Not everyone goes on "the New *Skype-IRC*™".  Why not just say something, once-in-a-while, on the forums *for your own game*?  Even a little *Tweet* would be better than **absolutely nothing**.
As for "competency", I've seen a lot of GMs do a lot of… things I don't agree with… in the name of keeping civility.  Mostly, it boils down to deleting posts, erasing chat logs, and outright ignoring – and in some cases, *banning* – individuals who may have had a point to whatever argument they were providing. (No, not me.)  If I might wax poetic for a mo', how many times will the words of the "unwashed masses" fail to touch those they've desperately reached out toward?  How long will the privileged few reside in their ivory towers, untouchable by the common man?  And how frequently will there be others who *defend* those "towers" with their very *lives*, blind and deaf to everything but the will of their "superiors"?  And that leads me right into my next point – which is actually my *previous* point.

I occasionally see people actually complain about the game.  Not just new patches and the like, but existing stuff, too.  Nine-out-of-ten times, someone comes back with comments like "It's a free game, stop complaining" or "you're just adding to the toxicity of the community".  I find that to be ignorant in a number of ways.
To the first point, it's as I said at the start of this post: just because something – *Turtle WoW*, in this case – only costs you *time lost* does not mean you automatically *waive your right* to complain about it.  It can't all be positivity and sunshine, folks.  Yes, absolutely tell Turtle Team they're amazing and you love the things that they do!  But, don't be afraid to let them know when something they've done is *disagreeable* with you!  If no one ever complained, how would the people who run the game know what they've done is *wrong* by you?
As to the second point, "toxicity", like life, is what you make of it.  I'm sure a lot of people are going to find *this* post "toxic" and "hostile" if only because I didn't pat every single member of Turtle Team on the back.  But more to the point, there's such a thing as "*toxic positivity*", too.  As I said in the previous paragraph, how would anyone ever know they're doing something "bad" when all they ever hear is "good job"?  There's also a level of "toxicity" that comes when you *blindly defend* someone and stay *willfully ignorant* of whatever the "aggressor" has to say… but I digress.  I think I've made my point and then some.

Y'all enjoy your game and your "social interactions".
Me?  I'm gonna go back to keeping to myself.
Just as some people prefer~  (;

The thing to try to keep in mind is that none of these bugs should have happened, and Staff is still trying to determine the cause. Everything was fullly functional and extensively tested on PTR. There was a branch that all the functional things were pushed to that didn't included in-development stuff on PTR for future content. Everything was good to go and only needed to be ported. Then when the branch was pushed to live, all these things that had been working were not.

The reason staff delayed the release for so long is precisely because they DID NOT want to release an unfinished or broken project. Everyone who has been patient and understanding is appreciated, but can you imagine the amount of stress and frustration after 8 months of delays staff is already experiencing even BEFORE people start slinging shit and being generally unconstructive? Yes, you are completely entitled to complain, free or not. No one, however, is entitled to stamp their feet like a toddler because everything didn't go perfectly.

To put it into perspective, imagine that a single parent has been working two jobs with overtime doing their best to provide for their child, and save up for a really nice toy the child has had their eyes on. The parent couldn't get the toy when it came out due to funds, and the child became resigned and wistful, but the parent kept trying their best and working harder and losing sleep just to save up those funds. Finally, after months, the parent was able to buy the toy, brought it home secretly, and revealed it to the child in the morning. The child was overjoyed and thankful and happy, but the toy did not have the features that the producers promised, or fell short in some respects. This was not the fault of the parent or the child, yet it is what it is.

This analogy perfectly encapsulates, from my exposure to the personalities behind Turtle WoW and those players who know them since the server's beginning, the staff and all their work. The toy producer in this case would be the compiler/middle-man software that made the merge go not without a hitch. The child in this scenario can do one of two things: Be thankful to the parent and happy for what they got, or throw a tantrum and get angry, breaking the parent's heart in the process.
