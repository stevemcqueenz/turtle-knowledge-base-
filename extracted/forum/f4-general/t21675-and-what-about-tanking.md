I tanked with each but spent way more time with some than others.
My take:

Warriors require a lot of experience to work as dungeon tanks.
Their niche is to have a bunch of buttons which help you survive which are cool in raid.
But.
Their threat and specially their aoe threat can be a problem.
A good warrior tank solves it but even then you often see 1-2 mobs starting to focus a DPS every now and then, despite symbol usage.
I don't recommend them unless you want a real challenge because they are probably the most skill-heavy ones, expecting you to do much more for the same result (in dungeons).

Paladins are probably the kings of dungeon tanking, specially once spell damage gear becomes available.
They don't have an AoE taunt but they have a really strong aoe DoT (consecration), gluing the mobs onto them.
You can be decently bulky with devotion aura or get even more help to keep threat via retri aura.
If a DPS still out-threats you, you can just use salvation on him.
Make sure judgment of wisdom is on some mobs and mana will also be okay.
The real drawback I saw is the lack of range.
You like range pulls? Get ez throw dynamites or ask a DPS to do so.
Maybe the hardest part of pala (dungeon) tanking is body pulling.
Learning how close you need to go to a mob to aggro it and then run back, breaking los (as a form of ranged pull).

Druids are interesting. Super bulky, from level 40 can be a healer's best friend by recharging their mana via innervate.
Huge single-target threat but cleave threat eventually falls off.
At bears fighting a bigger pack, I usually see 1-2 mobs getting loose and attacking a DPS.
You have armor and HP numbers no one else will have.
But you'll never block or parry and never see the new gear you just got while tanking since you look like a bear.
At druids, race and gender matters because those determine character size.
My bear was a tauren male and in some dungeons like Scarlet Monastery I had to shapeshift back to tauren before some corners to see anything. Camera can be a b*tch at narrow places.

Shaman is a bit weird.
You start off with extremely high threat and low mitigation.
You almost feel like paper, healer really has to react quick.
Initially mana will also be really bad but at shaman the trick is not really a rotation (at dungeons) but to know how much buttons you can skip to save mana and keep threat.
Later mitigation becomes okay. Threat stays consistently high (but not as absurd as initially).
Great utility.
You use both nature damage and fire (for some aoe threat). Mobs with resistances can really hurt your output.
A paladin makes wonders with you because a judgment of wisdom on the target mob makes your mana much better.
At raiding, probably still the weakest choice.
No def CD.

I had always the most fun as Warrior while tanking in the leveling phase. You need a bit of experience, with the class. I like to change stances and always get Tactical Mastery to keep some of Rage when changing stances. You have to know when you can tank without a Shield and when you need the block.

I leveled my Druid to 55 now and had to tank often, but in comparison to warrior its way less buttons and therefore sometimes a bit boring for me. I felt strong everytime. You are able to pre-HoT yourself before a pull (Heal over Time) so you get a bit of safety :)

My Paladin is on a bit of a hiatus and only 40. Maybe I did something wrong but the last run I remember (couple of months ago) I had problems with people focusing out single enemies. Could be my fault probably played a bit sloppy. My mob grinding experience out of the dungeon was great.

If you like Paladin as a class I would say it is a solid choice for beginners, read some guides how to play so you don't run out of mana and you should be good. I have Engineering as Profession so I could throw Dynamite to pull.
