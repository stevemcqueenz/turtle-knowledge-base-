# Raiding tier for specs

## Post 98573 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2024-07-05T10:44:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=98573#p98573 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!

My goal is to rank different builds strictly at PVE raiding environment. I try to be as objective as possible and reducing personal bias to the minimum.
Not an official tier list of course but trying my best to be as exact as possible.

 **Note1:** Many builds have a different effectiveness based on the raid tier (for example fury warriors get really good with high-tier gear and start off at a moderate position). To tackle this, I will associate two ranks with each build: early raids -> late raids (for example B->S).
 **Note2:** I will only consider raiding. Being extremely good at PVP or at leveling will not affect these ratings whatsoever.
 **Note3:** At ranking I will define some tiers from the perspective of min-maxer speedrun guilds. I do not propose a playstyle here, play whatever you like. However, speedrun guilds try to run raids as optimally as possible, hence I chose to include their PoV in the tier definitions. I do not embrace elitism in any form.
 **Note4:** For each build, will consider the other builds that class can bring to the table. I'll rank arms that low for example because there is an objectively better choice for the DPS warrior build. So a build's ranking always takes into account the other builds the related class can play.
 **Note5:** Class changes come soon. Everything I write here is regarding the current way of things.

 **The meaning of tiers:**
 * S: Extremely good class. The best in an important aspect of raiding.
 * A: A very good build which finds place in most speedrun guild comps or a build having a crucial utility unique to them.
 * B: A good build which has no problems finding raid spots but speedrun guilds would consider not using them.
 * C: A build with serious drawbacks. They find place at laid-back guilds but sometimes peopole ask why they raid with that specific build.
 * D: Like C but harsher. Builds having close to zero redeeming qualities at raiding.

 **Mages**

Frost: S->B
At early raid levels, frost mages are extremely good both damage-wise and utility-wise. They are overtaken by fire mages at around AQ40 though. I gave them B instead of C to the late-game raids because I believe that, despite being out-DPS-d by fire, they are still a good spec which can show promise in the new custom raids.

Fire: C->S
That C is mostly due to the high fire resistance in several early raids. Once fire mages receive high-raid items, they become the best caster DPS in the game.

Arcane: B->B
They do the same thing throughout raiding which is a bit lackluster damage-wise (but not as bad as people usually think) but in exchange gives the party mana (specially if they are innervate targets). Since they have to rely on druids to give that huge party utility to the fullest, I didn't consider them higher than B. Important to note that with full T2 they experience a sudden damage spike by going slightly into fire to spam free pyroblasts after all of those arcane missile procs. That almost made them an A in my book. Almost.

 **Warlocks**

SM/Ruin: A->B
They are almost the same as DS/Ruins but they give up some shadow spell damage for the imp stamina buff. Sometimes used in Vanilla for tank parties - same can be done here at earlier raid levels if needed. However, in TWOW, highly-geared shadow priests provide much better tanking team utility with their passive healing, meaning that the imp buff because of which they give up roughly 5% shadow damage is barely relevant at those later raids.

DS/Ruin: S->S

They start off as MS/Ruins but with higher damage instead of a situational utility. Warlocks arguably have a utility peak at MC progression with banish but still, curses, SS and ports make them a must have and honestly speaking, this is the more effective build out of the two warlock raiding specs.

 **Hunters**

Marksmanship: C-S->C-S
Here I have to explain many things. First, hunters in raiding are marksmanship. Some goes deeper into survival, some goes for some beast mastery talents beside marksmanship but at the core they are the same build for raiding.
For the ratings: by default I'd consider the hunters B tier based on their power level. However they bring some utility which are absolute musts at raiding, meaning that speedrun builds would also bring them (aspect, tranquilizing shot, kiting), which makes them A tier following the tier definitions I've made.
Regarding those weird number ranges I made: there is a huge difference between a good hunter and a bad hunter, maybe more than at any other class. A bad hunter forgets to dismiss pet before jumps, spends 90% of the time auto-attacking and pulls all the time. They are frequent, this is why I gave C, specifically for those hunters.
However, hunter works in a way that if you actually learn an intricate rotation, which you can perform while doing auto-attack, you can basically double your damage output. These hunters are rare but I've met some. They never ásspull, kite perfectly, prepare with the best bullets, tranquilize perfectly, make party movement faster with aspect switching... Those few are S tier. Others fall into A because of the utility.

 **Shamans**

Elemental: C->C
They are strong in PVP but have real problems at raiding. They don't bring any build-specific utility. Their threat is awful. They have some mana problems. Nature resist griefs them.
Just a word regarding why ele threat is that bad. They have a threat reduction totem which is unfortunatelly an air totem. In a raid they are usually in a melee group, giving windfury or twisting wf and agility, meaning that they usually don't have access to their own threat reduction. Besides that their main spell is kinda fast (2 sec casting time), they have a whopping 11% spell crit chance only from talents (!) and they can spam shocks every 5 sec. This usually results in overaggroing the tank.

Enhancement: D->D
The only time you can play enhancement in a more serious guild is when you are the 'nighfall guy'. They have very low damage and the extra utility they can bring can also be brought by BL/resto which is a healing spec. They go to raids to do 30-40% of the fury's damage output.

Resto: A->S
Including both deep resto and BL/resto. Healing shamans start raiding with serious mana problems which hold them back initially. However, after getting a certain amount of +healing and mana regen, they quickly become the best group healers in the game. They can bring all the shaman utilities you need while also having arguably the best raid heal. Once they are geared, they are extremely good. Until that point -> mana problems.
I gave them A to early raids because usually they provide the shaman utility to the party.

 **Paladins**

Holy: A->S
Same as resto shamans but in a different way. Instead of mana problems, they initially have problems with the amount of healing flash of light does. They need to gear up, get +healing and +spell crit. Once done, they are the best spot healers in the game and are absolutely needed in any guild. However, at the early phases their healing output will be low because flash of light will do smaller heals than for example a flash heal from an equally geared priest.
I gave them A for early raids because in an optimized setting holy palas would give the pala buffs at raids.

Retri: B->B
Before biting my head off: I only consider raiding here, PVP is not related. Most people seriously overestimate retri pala DPS at raids. It is respectable but it's nowhere near to a fury for example. Let's face it, each pala build learns blessing of kings, they can't bring a unique utility. Don't get me wrong, most guilds would happily bring a retri pala but speedrun guilds would not, except for 'the nightfall guy'. I have a pala friend at Doom Turtles who attended many speedruns. During those speedruns he is either a heal or 'the nightfall guy' because of this.
I won't make a distinction between shockadin and more melee-oriented retri, I think B tier is suitable for both of them.

Prot: B+->B+
I know I cheeze the ratings a bit here but I think it's justified. Prot palas bring a very good utility to the raids in the form of tanking a lot of trash at once and they are capable of tanking even Naxx in TWOW but as tanks they still fall short to fury-prots, are kind of fragile and can face mana problems in really long fights. Most guilds bring a prot pala for aoe tanking but speedrun guilds either don't bring them or someone respecs quickly for places like the bug tunnel. The reason I gave them B+ is people respeccing to prot in certain situations in the minmaxing raids.

 **Druids**

Bear: A->A
The 'almost fury prot'. High damage, extremely high stamina and armor, some great utility, including Leader of the Pack. Still, not being able to parry or block and not having as good defensives as the warriors make them fall off slightly. I've put them above prot palas because they are much less fragile than them and leader of the pack can grant them a 1-of raid slot even at speedruns.

Cat: D->D
They have the enhancement shaman problem. Bad damage and despite having cool utilities, they can't bring anything unique to the table. They'd need some push to be viable.

Moonkin: A->A
They have relatively low damage and some mana problems. Just like hunters, I'd have put them into B tier but they have moonkin aura. That party buff alone (which only moonkins can bring) makes 1 of them worth it. You want those spell crits and this weird animal will provide those spell crits.

Tree: A->A
Again, a 1-of from speedrun PoV. Trees are arguably the worst healers in a vacuum (no out of combat rez, 90% of what they do is spamming HoTs) but as a druid spec they bring a very good party buff, which can only be provided by trees. Since their healings don't stack, one of them is usually enough but that one can find a place in any raid comp, that heal party buff is that good.

 **Warriors**

Deep prot: B->D
They are initially okay, since they survive pretty well. But as the DPS gets more and more geared, problems start to arise. Bad threat, DPS aggroes all the time. Deep prot is perfectly fine to start raiding and making sure you survive as a tank but at later stages bringing a deep prot means that DPS has to stay really low to avoid aggroing. They fall from grace really hard.

Fury-prot: S->S
Arguably the best tanks in the game. At first glance they are a bit squishy but every fury-prot who worths a penny has a shield swap macro. Shield wall, last stand, very good survivability if needed. At lower damage income they use 2 weapons and generate a bunch of threat, allowing the DPS to really go off. Most raid comp is centered around mostly fury prot tanks.

Arms warrior: D->D
They wouldn't be as bad, their damage is far better than enh shaman damage for example but fury warriors exist. Going to a raid as arms means picking a far weaker DPS and people will probably ask you to change spec.

Fury: B->S
They need some time to gear up, initially they are not that good. But once geared, they are the best melee DPS in the game from raiding PoV.

 **Rogues**

Combat: S->A
Dagger or sword, their talent core is similar, using backstab or not. They start off pretty well because they are not as gear dependent as fury warriors but as the guild gears up, they start to fall behind. Having problems at damaging multiple targets, damage falling short compared to fury warriors, poisons effectively 'nerfed' by windfury, the only real saving grace they have is expose armor. However, expose armor is good.

 **Priests**

Holy: S->B
They are in a weird spot. Initially they are the best healers you can find. Tree HPS is never that big, shamans will struggle with mana and paladins have only small heals at the early raid levels. However, when the priest reaches a certain amount of +healing, disc just becomes better. Other healers catch up and utility becomes more important than some more added healing. Proclaim champion doesn't hold its ground against Power Infusion too well.

Discipline: C->S
As described above. Once healing get good enough, focus shifts to PI.

Shadow: A->S
They start off a bit sketchy because of their low damage but with good gear they become beasts. I've considered putting them below A tier for early raids but shadow weaving would guarantee a spot for one shadow priest at any raids. Their other big utility, the passive party healing/mana reacharge is linked to the damage they make. At late raids that talent really kicks off, making them an extremely good utility build.

Not mentioned builds like smite priest, melee priest, shaman tank, ranged warrior spamming the crossbow etc all go to D because I won't be bothered to create an E tier and distinguish it from D.
