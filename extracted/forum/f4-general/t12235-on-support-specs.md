# On "Support" specs

## Post 81378 by Jstansberry (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2023-12-16T07:38:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=81378#p81378 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Warning: BITTER WALL OF TEXT AHEAD

I've been tied up in a few debates related to the concept of "support" specs - mostly Retribution and Enhancement, sometimes Discipline. My stance is that "support" is a completely irrelevant archetype in vanilla WoW and that it does not exist in this game.

This is a debate that irks me personally because it is a stance taken by stubborn people who don't want to see changes from baseline vanilla gameplay while playing a vanilla+ server. It's used as an argument as to why enhancement must have bad DPS, or why it is unacceptable that ret paladins can now do competitive dps.

Holy Strike may be overtuned for PvP but there is nothing inherently wrong with a ret paladin being able to occasionally top the meters in a raid. Enhancement is just awful, the only reason you take an enhancement shaman is because you can't find an ele or, best case scenario, resto shaman to provide Tremor and WF totems.

The arguments generally made by "support spec" believers:

 1. A spec is a support because they provide unique utility to their party/raid
 2. They have talents that make their buffs/debuffs stronger
 3. They aren't good at anything in particular but have unique utility in their kit so they are a support
 4. It's unfair to pure DPS specs that a hybrid class can do almost as well as them in parses

I intend to dismantle these arguments

All classes in the game provide utility in the form of strong debuffs and buffs unique to their class and/or spec.
Warriors, rogues, hunters, mages, and warlocks - the pure DPS specs - all provide unique utility to their group. You want at least one player of each class in every raid because Blizzard ensured that.

Warriors are not even a pure DPS spec in my mind as they are also the best tanks in the game, but generally players consider them to be a mono-role class like the other classes listed. Warriors have Sunder which increases the physical damage of the raid significantly, Battle Shout, Disarm - which can make some fights such as 4H significantly easier, Thunderclap, Demoralizing Shout, and Pummel.

Rogues have a lot of overlap with warriors, having their own Disarm in the form of Dismantle (shorter duration and cooldown), several stuns, Blind, two incaps, Disarm Trap which is required in BWL in the most painful way, Expose Armor which can have more armor reduction than Sunder if a talent is taken, and Kick.

Hunters, while having uncharacteristically poorly scaling DPS for a mono-role pure DPS class, are required for many fights due to enrage mechanics that require Tranquilizing Shot. They've also been given Trueshot Aura in Turtle WoW - a +100 AP aura. Mana drains such as Viper Sting are required for a few encounters.

Mages have Counterspell, Detect Magic, Polymorph, conjured food and water (Oranges in Turtle WoW), Amplify/Dampen Magic, Remove Curse, and Arcane Intellect

Warlocks can provide Blood Boil from their imp, Healthstones for the whole raid, Curse of Elements/Shadow which are huge damage increasing debuffs for all caster DPS (except for ele shamans who deal only nature damage in their basic rotation), Curse of Recklessness which provides armor reduction, Banish, and Drain Mana

I basically just listed a bunch of spells, but specifically all of these spells are used to strengthen the raid significantly or are required to be used in certain encounters.
Being able to provide a buff like Blessing of Kings is not a good reason to cry about the ret spamming Crusader Strike doing nearly as much damage as you. It's not a good reason to deny enhancement shamans actual buffs.

Windfury Totem (which isn't even unique to the enhancement tree) is not a good reason to say enhancement is a support spec and shouldn't be able to do anything effectively except place totems and pretend to do damage.

Having talents that makes buffs/debuffs stronger in your talent tree does not make that spec a support spec. Almost every spec in the game has talents that make buff/debuffs stronger. Enhancement has 3 talents which affect totems, two of them are nearly useless. Improved Weapon Totems provides a flat ~1.5 DPS to every party member utilizing Windfury Totem at best, Guardian Totems would only be useful for encounters where you want the reduced Grounding Totem cooldown and it's not really worth the talent points even in those scenarios. Every other talent in enhancement increases the shaman's ability to either take or deal damage.

Warriors have the same amount of "support" talents in their Fury talent tree: Booming Voice, Improved Demoralizing Shout, and Improved Battle Shout. Fury is not a support spec. The enhancement tree simply does not provide the tools required for a shaman to be a viable melee DPS, that is why people see it as a "support" spec - it's bad at its intended purpose so all people see is the baseline shaman utility provided: totem buffs.

Turtle's iteration of Bloodlust is a mistake - playing into the "support" role that does not exist in vanilla WoW - something like Unleashed Rage would have made a lot more sense - providing a benefit to both the shaman and their raid that scales with gear progression.

Discipline's capstone talent is Power Infusion, what many would call a "support" skill. Discipline priests are healers, not "supports". They use healing gear and heal people. Enhancement shamans and ret paladins use DPS gear and hit people. Yes, they have "support" skills, every class in the game does. Discipline still gets to heal just as effectively as other healing specs despite being more buff-oriented - and that makes sense. There is no stat in the game that improves your "support" or "utility" except maybe int/mana so you can excrete raid buffs faster without drinking. Your character scales via acquiring more damage, healing, and mitigation stats. Tanks, healers, and damage dealers. Yes, I am aware that warrior tanks for the most part don't really care about mitigation stats very much.

The last point would be that it is unfair to DPS specs if these hybrid DPS that provide strong buffs can do nearly as much damage as them. I get it, you made a warrior or mage likely because you wanted to be on the top of the meter. You still will be. 10% less dps is the difference between 360 and 400. That is a significant difference that can possibly be bridged by consuming/not consuming and/or a large mechanical skill difference. Hybrid specs are doing well below 90% the DPS of a warrior, mage, or rogue. The screenshots and Legacy-players parses of rets being top DPS in naxx are due to them winning the Windfury/Seal of Command proc lottery on top of using every consume available - it's just as likely they do incredibly poorly due to their reliance on RNG. Average ret DPS is not problematic and is quite a bit lower than what most people have gotten worked up over.

Even if they DID do 95% the DPS of a warrior or mage, it'd still be more optimal to bring only as many of these buffing classes as necessary to provide the higher performing classes all the buffs they need. That'd be as many shamans as there are melee groups, 5 paladins (most likely not all ret) to cover all the optimal blessing for all specs, a boomkin for each caster DPS group, ferals for each physical DPS group, SPriests maybe with healer groups due to the mana regen they provide, and the rest you can fill with warriors, mages, rogues, warlocks, and hunters (who deserve a buff, by the way). That is pretty damn close to equal representation, still a bit slanted in favor of meta mono-role DPS classes.

Providing strong buffs or unique utility is not a justification for any spec to be awful at what they do. Enhancement shamans and ret paladins are very clearly supposed to be bonking things and there is no good reason they should not be able to bonk effectively.

I see your point but I disagree. Maining a shaman, would answer via this point of view.
I agree that each class has some forms of utility, but their importance and the amount of them varies.
Following the class utility lists you made for classes considered as pure DPS, I create one for shamans now (will only include the somewhat relevant ones, things like windwall totem or that life totem exist but noone ever uses them):

 * melee extra attack (wf totem)
 * added melee strength (strength totem)
 * reduced received melee damage (stoneskin totem)
 * handling fear/charm/sleep (tremor totem)
 * kiting (earthbind totem)
 * very good poison/disease cleansing (both totems and ability)
 * party mana regen (small amounts unless deep resto then it's considerable eevry 5 min)
 * 60 resistance on fire, nature or frost (totems)
 * 77 (or 88) agility for the party for dodges and weapon crits
 * spell redirection (grounding totem)
 * interrupt (earth shock)
 * purge
 * party threat reduction totem for a high DPS caster group (tranquial air totem)
 * heals and chain heals, regardless if the shaman is specced as healer, rdps or melee
 * bloodlust (if enh DPS or enh-rest hybrid)

If you check guild invitations at world chat, they are almost always looking for shamans. To be fair, they wanna have a resto and I'll react to that a bit later but not in this segment. During raids most raid leaders want a shaman in each melee group. They also prefer one in a hunter/druid group and would consider one in caster groups, too, despite (I agree with that) shaman builds perform overall worse as healers, rdps or melee than other classes like priests, mages or rogues. There are cases when, during a pug, raid leader starts to specifically look for more melee to join because a comp is very caster-heavy, but I haven't seen cases where the raid leader decided to specifically LF rogues. With mages and locks it happens but to a lesser degree than shamans - you want port, HS, the most important curses, oranges, decurse, in some places poly + the raw damage they have (mostly the mages). My point is that this utility we are talking about exists. In a world where elemental and resto would suddenly disappear by some reason, guilds and pugs would invite a bunch of enhancement shamans for the things the resto guy used to do. Not 1-2 of them or not any kind of melee because the comp is too caster-heavy. 40 man raids would have at least 4-5 enhancement shamans despite their laughable DPS for those totems, cleansing, purging and occassional healing. Probably equipped with nightfalls. This utility definitely exists but it exists in a bad way in my opinion.

Since shamans are mostly kept for the above things (most importantly WF, chain heal, bloodlust, cleansing, tremor, grounding) and not because of their individual numbers, guilds are just taking the build which can provide most of these utilities. It cuts off options from other shaman builds and promotes a build as 'desirable' which is really bad in my opinion (the bloodlust resto). People basically say that you healer go 31 talents deep into enhancement getting things like improved shields (you don't even have shields) and bigger melee (you never melee) just to get stronger strength totem, WF (minimal difference) and the bloodlust ability, giving up more than half of the resto talent tree in the process - we think about you as a passive buff taking up a raid slot. Don't worry about healing efficiency, just spam your chain heals which are there from the beginning for each build and bloodlust the rogue or fury warrior at bosses.
I think the problem is that shamans are defined way too much by their utility and not by their performance, and it has two main reasons:

 * shaman builds lag too much behind 'true caster/melee/healer builds' - (resto is the nearest thing to a normal build so guilds take as much resto as possible and ask non-restos to switch to resto (or usually bloodlust-resto)
 * most utilities can be used by whatever build, main exception is bloodlust - you don't need to be an enhancement for windfury or a resto to chain heal

For the 1st point: I agree that shaman individual efficiency should be increased. It should never reach the efficiency of a rogue or a mage at damage dealing but it should be somewhere between 80-90% (unsure about the number). This way different shaman builds would be kinda considered for their individual worth, too.

For the 2nd point and it is very important: shaman has too much utility accessible via each build! Separate most of them into 3 chunks and distribute them between the 3 builds. Would allow each build to be stronger since they could provide a smaller chunk of the above utiliy list. If we do that, shaman builds become strong and shamans will be considered more based on their performance and not as a worldbuff + different builds would be recruited by guilds for the different situations. For example: make base WF considerably weaker and only enh would access its true form. In exchange resto and ele are buffed in the talent tree to compensate for the missing good WF totem. Same with chain heal going to resto etc. We'd end up with strong individual classes with a number (but much more limited number) of ways to help the party.
