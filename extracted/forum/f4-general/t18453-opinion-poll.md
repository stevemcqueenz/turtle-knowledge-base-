## Post 126567 by Sleeplust (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2025-04-13T03:50:00Z

A bit off-topic, but Hunter in WOW has always been a weird class that functions with weird inclination and unique role-playing flavour. They are archer-like characters with poor melee capability and a beast companion in original versions.

The problem is that this class is designed with the notion of combining the stereotypical archer figure in fantasy works, such as Legolas in The Lord of the Rings, and a beast master role that wanders in the wild. If we only like to play a role like Legolas, who is more like a soldier without an indispensible beast helping him or dependence on traps, we can't find an option in WOW's available classes. On the other hand, when we feel like playing a beast master like Rexxar, who dual-wields and throws projectiles, again, no option like him in original WoW is available. (Making Rexxar use a bow as his primary weapon in Hearthsthone is too over the top.)

In other words, the hunter class in WOW can be only seen in its own universe and this class never meets the role-playing requirement of those who want to play a role like Legolas or Rexxar. Making hunter a class available for mimicking Legolas (no pet or trap reliance) would feel like making hunter a ranged warrior or a outlaw rogue using a bow.

Way too many questions all at once imo. But I'd like to throw my hat in the ring and say that passive threat reduction is bad for the game, and that it would likely be better to have no threat reduction at all than to have it be a non-interactive buff.

It is one of the few mechanics that every role in PvE needs to be aware of, and allowing buffs to shirk the shared responsibility of the tanks, healers, and dps is antithetical to the teamwork inherent in classic design and is a mark of a retail-like approach to gameplay.

If there is to be threat reduction, and especially for DPS, it should be in the form of a CD or non-rotation spells
