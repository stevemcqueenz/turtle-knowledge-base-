## Post 57764 by Elesion (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2023-08-17T15:04:00Z

I think they are indeed generally a little on the strong side, but for a multitude of factors:
 1) Most Turtle-custom items make very good use of the stat budget. They are clearly designed from the player perspective. For example, very few items have just randomly added spirit that eats up budget. These items are generally made with a task or spec in mind and optimized for them, while Vanilla items are often not. They also don't hold back strong stats until the endgame (a lot of Turtle items give you early access to +heal or +spell damage for instance).
 2) Some stat weights have actually changed. That's not particularly important for Crescent Grove but for many higher-level items. Turtle devs think mp5 and parry chance should be cheaper on the item budget for instance.
 3) They fill gaps in Vanilla itemization. They appear strong because Vanilla has been lacking. A good example are feral druids - Thornroot Maul is an amazing feral dps weapon particularly because there are no real alternatives for many levels. The same with the tanking staff that was added to Wailing Caverns. Vanilla didn't have these specs in mind and didn't provide items for them, but Turtle tries to. Crescent Grove drops stuff for dagger rogues, shadow priests, etc. It spreads the love to those that Vanilla has perhaps neglected a little bit.
 4) Some items are indeed flat out overpowered or even broken. Many tend to get adjusted after some time, some don't. For a dungeon around level 34 I don't really see the need to adjust the balance with a microscope to be honest. It's all just temporary anyway, just enjoy these items while they last for your character   turtle_tongue_head
