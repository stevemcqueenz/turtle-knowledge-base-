## Post 81868 by Forthehorde — 2023-12-20T04:36:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=81868#p81868 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

There are 9 classes to choose from, but the main question is whether you prefer melee or ranged.  Next is whether you prefer to tank, dps, or heal.   Here's a basic breakdown:

 **Melee:**
Warrior (tank, dps)
Rogue (dps)
Paladin (heal, tank, buff)
Druid (tank, heal, dps)

 **Ranged:**
Mage (dps)
Hunter (dps + pet)
Warlock dps + pet)
Priest (heal, dps)
Shaman (heal, buff, dps, tank)

Warriors/tanks are hardest to solo level but also in short supply, so finding groups will be easy.

Healers are surprisinly easy to level.  It's not fast, but not slow either.  Just steady.  And of course every dungeon needs a healer, and raids need several of them.

Hybrid classes like the druid and shaman are very interesting and fun to play, but their versitility could be a challenge for first-time players.  I put the shaman in the ranged category, since it's their their comfort zone, but they can also melee and tank with limited ability.  By contast, druids make great tanks in dungeons, but not nearly as good as a warrior, due to the warrior's aoe threat abilities.

DPS wise, pet classes like the hunter and warlock are the easist/fastest to level due to their pets, and have good dps for dungeons and raids, however their aoe damage is limited.  Warlocks are the least-played class on Turtle Wow, so you'll have nearly zero competition when rolling for warlock gear.

But if you're looking for all-out simplicity, easy solo, and dungeon/raid desireability, then mages are a top tier choice.  Their ability to do massive single-target and aoe damage makes them excellent solo/group players, and every mana user will be begging you for your summoned water in every dungeon and raid. Just don't get hit and be prepared to drink for mana often.

## Post 81912 by Jstansberry (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2023-12-20T09:22:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=81912#p81912 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Paladin or druid are great at soloing and are very relevant in endgame content. You may be pigeon-holed into healing if you want to raid - their healing talent trees are much more viable than their tanking/damage dealing ones in high level content  - but they are fun to level and are quite good at soloing content. They are "hybrid" classes, meaning they can fulfill multiple roles depending on their specialization. Paladin and druid DPS are not the best, but not the worst either. Some paladins and druids have some extremely high parses on Turtle WoW. Paladin tanks struggle in higher tiers of raiding and require a lot of effort to make work, while druid tanks are actually preferred for some encounters. Paladins and druids both are very strong PvP classes, druids have a very high skill cap though. Paladins can be the best single-target healers in the game while druids specialize in either healing-over-time spells or long cast-time but high value single target burst healing.

Alternatively, you could play a hunter or warlock. They are not hybrids, both are dedicated damage dealing classes. They have pets and other tools that can make soloing leveling content relatively trivial. Both are fun to play over-all (though warlock can be quite boring in raids) and would be pretty safe "first character" classes. Warlock is a bit more complicated than hunter while leveling, but that flips around once you get into endgame content. Both hunters and warlocks are desired in raiding guilds as they are mandatory for some encounters. Both are quite good at PvP - with warlocks generally being considered the strongest PvP class in the game. Hunters have more in-depth "pet" gameplay, with you taming beasts found in the world and getting to learn talents and abilities for not only your character but the pets you tame as well. Both classes are easy to use and hard to master. Hunters are by far the most common "noob" class.

 1h with a shield is the prot paladin archetype, though it's worth noting that in absolute endgame content paladin tanks often use 2h weapons to generate enough threat to ensure the boss stays on them. While leveling, there is no harm in keeping a level-relevant 2h in your bags if you want to kill individual mobs faster or just want to switch things up.

Personally, I wouldn't recommend exhaustion *and* slow & steady if you're a first-time player - it's going to take a loooong time for you to level if you don't know where quest objectives are, where the questing hubs for each level range are, et cetera.

That said, having multiple challenges will not "cancel out", it's just a bit masochistic. As long as you didn't make your character hardcore you'll be fine.
