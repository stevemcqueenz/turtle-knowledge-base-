# Interesting thought process behind class changes

## Post 107622 by Abiozan — 2024-11-12T06:56:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=107622#p107622 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Good whatever time of the day it is for you while reading this.
CC2 was a patch people have been waiting for a while, whenever in a negative light or in a good light, and while for the majority of the playerbase the class changes either really didn't matter or didn't really change much class gameplay, a lot of people especially in the raiding scene are quite unhappy with a lot of changes, especially warrior changes (and a few others, but I am mainly focusing on warrior here since warrior is the staple class of vanilla).

While fury warriors have been dominating the raiding scene in WoW Classic and on TWoW, I think the devs really misunderstood WHY warrior dominates the scene and I'd like to get into it in this thread, and compare it also to a class that I think got a really good redesign that's really close to vanilla standards.

So, as it stands, fury warrior is still currently considered the highest DPS from all classes judging by **logs**.
Logs for people that genuinely don't raid as much or never bothered to look are basically the way people check dps, healing, dispelling, damage taken and etc in the raid, albeit limited compared to what World of Warcraft Classic.
However the main concern of the new fury warrior (and by extension, warrior in general) is that current warrior doesn't feel *vanilla* anymore. By all means you still pull high dps, but the current gameplay especially for fury is just not fun. It feels like all skill expression was sucked out of it and now to be high on the meters they made you gamble an rng dps increase for 8 seconds, increased your rage gain to be obsecenely high since without it current warrior just doesn't have the rage to literally do anything, and made cooldowns go from: "I should use this when the boss is close to dying so that the burst damage at 20% is higher" to "I should wait for good rng with enrage and press everything at once hoping it all hits so I get the maximum amount of damage in" and that just makes warrior feel like your average post-classic blizzard rebalancing cope to make warrior "better" by removing the whole reason why warrior was fun in Classic.
Most of the complaints about new enrage self-harm is that it's a sour form of RNG. This is a gamefeel issue that some people, especially parsers and those mages who like to see 5k pyroblast crits, hate.

Sour RNG generally comes from two things: feast & famine gameplay loops (generally caused by lower proc rates with massive impacts when it procs) and a lack of agency in when it procs and/or agency in when you can use the proc.

Now, for a class that I think is well-designed in CC2 is... surprisingly, paladin. I think the devs behind paladin changes (especially for ret paladin) made ret actually fun. Ret gameplay became somehow more vanilla-like than warrior is, because they made paladin go from gambling with crits to having interesting mechanics and abilities to use and the whole spec as a whole is really engaging to play now while no longer being broken in PVP with weird scaling of abilities, and on top of that paladin got good QoL changes that actually do make sense to be made (like 30 min big blessings and the change of blessing of protection, freedom and etc to work separately from the assign paladin blessing). So it just if anything proves that the developers CAN make a class vanilla-like and engaging, they just specifically chose to screw over a beloved spec of many for CC2 and make it completely unfun, on top of it adding gambling into it (and arms in its current state works only because of a bug with an ability, otherwise it barely performs above fury even though the spec requires much more skill arguably).
Paladin is no longer an RNG fest like the old ret with holy strike, meanwhile fury is the opposite of what it was being reworked to such a degree to depend on RNG to feel like the old class and kind of sucked dry of the old timing your cooldowns for the death of the boss part, as well as having the issue of depending on rng a lot more now.

#revertfury

Most of the complaints about new enrage self-harm is that it's a sour form of RNG. This is a gamefeel issue that some people, especially parsers and those mages who like to see 5k pyroblast crits, hate.

Sour RNG generally comes from two things: feast & famine gameplay loops (generally caused by lower proc rates with massive impacts when it procs) and a lack of agency in when it procs and/or agency in when you can use the proc. The Enrage proc on Bloodthirst is sour because it has a somewhat low proc rate/uptime, especially at lower gear levels, and when it procs you must react and decide if you're going all-out on this one in that 1.5s GCD. As you get closer and closer to crit cap, you have an additional layer of RNG on top of it where you have pretty decent uptime on it thanks to being at 40% crit but if you can't trigger it 2-3 times in a row (when Death Wish is rolling) you will have a much worse feel during the burst window.

The new fire mage Hot Streak is a good example of well-designed RNG since the fire mage has a clear indicator of how close they are to needing to mix up their rotation and they can plan ahead accordingly. If they don't have the ability to use the proc immediately, they can hold it for a few seconds until better conditions exist.

I'm not sure if a proper solution exists with the way the self-enrage works, but I would encourage the devs to look at some combination of changes like giving warriors an enrage proc when they press Death Wish and/or Recklessness instead of Bloodrage, a reduced effectiveness/duration Enrage every time they press Bloodthirst (instead of just when they self-crit), or a means of storing an enrage proc from Bloodthirst until a more opportune time.
