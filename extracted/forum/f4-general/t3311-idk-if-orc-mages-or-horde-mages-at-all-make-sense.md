# IDK if Orc Mages, or Horde Mages at all, make sense

## Post 20158 by Hir3 — 2022-07-21T06:22:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=20158#p20158 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

I was discussing this with a friend today:

Mages make sense as an Alliance class. The Kirin Tor is quite organized, Alliance-centric, and authoritative in its membership and regulation - Compared to more general classes such as Warlocks. Now I understand the Kirin Tor does not run the Ley Lines of Azeroth, but the mages in WoW are not generic and neutral, such as rogues or warriors - All would have you believe they have strong ties to this organization.

It is my belief that this reflects similarly to Paladin lore, in that it is very specific to the Silver Hand; If the Horde had a light-tradition race, it would still not make sense for them to be paladins.

Troll and Orc mages do NOT make sense, because they use the same lore justification as(And I know Blizzard implemented or encouraged this dillemma in a game-wide way even in Vanilla) Cataclysm's Tauren Paladins and Priests. That is, mages are an in-game representation of some unrelated practice.

Forsaken are a little tricky - They do have the mage history as humans, but ultimately due to the Kirin Tor's influence and the complete decay into Undeath, Forsaken should not have the Arcane connection that Blizzard wrote for them. They should inherently have a "fel" or "shadow" connection to the Arcane in the way of the many fallen Human warlocks and necromancers.

This goes into a follow-up point: Warlocks should not be on the Alliance. The Alliance does not have the fel or shadow tradition of the Horde at all. The begrudging tolerance of Thrall in the Horde, or the complete disregard by Sylvanas, has zero place in the Alliance. The Night Elves and Light organizations of the humans are fiercely against and antagonistic towards this type of magic. (Yes I know this is supplemented with basement dwellings and secret chambers, but this is really nonsensical considering how organized and policed Alliance society is.)

How this all ties into the game: Mages should be limited to Humans, Dwarves, Gnomes, and High Elves. Warlocks should be limited to Orcs, Forsaken, and Trolls. Warlocks, being practitioners of this corrupted form of Arcane, should have some overlap with mages: Teleportation, Conjuration, "Khadgar's Unlocking", etc. Mages should have some overlap with Warlocks: Summoning of Arcane familiars and Water elementals, Life Tap/Sacrifice(Remember that the Kirin Tor is modeled on popular conceptions of Hermetic and Occult societies of IRL history, also reflected in some Mage quests. They also do partake in rituals of magic ceremony and incantation, such as imbuing the Guardian of Tirisfal with essences of their power).

With the proposed class redesigns, this can be taken into consideration. At the very least, warlocks should be limited to humans, and mages to Forsaken, in their respective factions.

And since I brought up Priests -

I fully understand their themes seem to be at odds with my post, or general race lore. The issue here is that fixing the Priest problem would require a MASSIVE overhaul of the class, and additions of multiple new racial classes. Examples being the creation of tribal priests/witchdoctors, priestesses of Elune, and the Arcano-Light tradition of Dwarven priests. This would create more issues than it would solve in wild proportion rather than the changing of a few abilities and combos, and is therefore not justified.
