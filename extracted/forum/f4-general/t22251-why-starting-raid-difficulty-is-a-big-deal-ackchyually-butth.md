# Why starting raid difficulty is a big deal ackchyually [butthurt longpost]

## Post 154424 by Ctgrl — 2025-10-05T10:39:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=154424#p154424 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

When I was starting out here, I was under impression, that I finally found a game ran by people, who do understand, how their game works. Now after MC was thoroughly fucked up in a latest patch and ONY/BWL are on a track to be just as fucked up in a next one, I feel baited and switched. After reading arguments on a forum, I also noticed that a lot of people are still stuck in 2k08 discourse and genuinely don't get, why it's such a harmful change, even if they see it as one. So, I guess, we have a fuckton of discourse to catch up here. No easy way around it, I have to explain it from the very very beginning.

Ye olde blizz mishandled player motivation ackchyually

In multiplayer games there are two kinds of player motivation: internal and external. Internal is something along the "Cool! I am now better at the game than I was before". External motivation is tied to how player sees himself relative to the rest of a playerbase: "I am better at the game than them unworthy losers". Since blizz was mostly making cybersport titles before, they understood externally motivated player very well, but didn't really get, how the rest of their playerbase think. After vanilla, blizz set onto a direction to separate playerbase into "casual", which is basically a corpospeak for a drooling retard, paying for match3 mobile games, and "hardcore" audience - the actual players. In this paradigm casuals are averse to any challenge, not capable of a meaningful play at all, just want to swing a big ass sword and see shit exploding. The "hardcore" players here are all competitive externally motivated players, they know well from their previous competitive titles. The catch here is: majority of players doing endgame PvE are neither of this. They are mostly internally motivated but not "casual". This flawed model at foundation eventually led to how things are now. Lemme explain.

Filtering is what kills fun ackchyually

So, what happens now, when entry raids were made unpuggable. The value of an organized group goes to the moon, the value of an individual new player is firmly negative. So, to get a group new players must offer something to offset their negative value. This turns a non-competitive game mode into a competitive one. In retail it devolved into the system, you now have to compete with other players for a group spot by doctoring your parse. In vanilla you will have to put up with all the bullshit rules ego-driven RL will subject you to, while LC all the useful shit to himself and his buddies. If you don't like it, the door is right here, good luck out there. Bring in the next one.
Externally motivated players love filtering, because it gives them an affirmation on how special they are relative to others, while not requiring anything but time and ability to tolerate bullshit. Also if they ever get a hold on being in charge of the process, they get such an ego boost, they will fight tooth an nail to keep it. Everyone else hates it because it is not fun by itself and it sucks all the fun out of the game. Similar to how real life job market works, those filters not necessarily filtering those who are good at the game. Once any metric becomes a KPI, it stops being useful. Filters always favor people, who are good (or more motivated) at passing filters.
Here comes the catch 22. Externally motivated players always have to better relative to someone. Once they get their way and squeezed them filthy casuals out of THEIR game mode, they will try find "casuals" among the rest. There always has to be someone at the bottom to kick. This puts a game into a death spiral of endless bullshit filters. Imagine telling someone in 2006, that in the end, they will come up with fuckin spyware credit score addon to record your every action, so you have to compete on that for your spot. Now that sounds fun af.

Tricking sweats into carrying noobs is a good thing ackchyually

Gradual difficulty progression is why vanilla works. After that, it pivots towards a set difficulty for every raid tier, that is tuned for players, that have played the game before. After blizz took a course on separating "hardcore" an "casual" audience by artificially widening gap between endgame raiding and everything else, which was maintained as a filter. At the latest patches of wotlk, when "The Retail" took its' final form, it looked like "link achiev min gs 6.5". It became like that because they finally completely replaced doing older raids as a catch-up mechanic with retarded daily grind (Good for retention metrics, shareholders were probably so happy they pissed their pants). The ladder was pulled up forever. Now every new player has to break out of a loop of negative self-value, when you can't raid because you have no experience, and can't get experience, because you can't raid. Soon after that player numbers peaked and started to decline.
In vanilla you don't hit the same wall after you ding 60. Raids and loot are designed to mix noobs with experience players, so knowledge organically gets passed down the playerbase. The whole legendary farm mechanic is entirely about this. Raids from previous phases were there to teach skills, not to filter someone out. Externally motivated players perceive it as an "unfair" shortcut. An encroachment on their exclusive status, because they had to climb that wall once. They will say, you have to git gud, you have to "find a real guild" (and put up with all my bullshit to boost my ego).

You can never make it challenging enough ackchyually

Locking out every player in a raid from a loot for a week for a fuckup of a few is a very harsh punishment, especially on progression. If given their way, players will go to ridiculous lengths to avoid it. Like requiring to collect every single world buff, go full consume, fully enchanted, overgeared and farm everything farmable. Because there are inevitably some people willing to do all that, they will squeeze everyone else out of a competition. As the bottleneck, which is organization requirement and some key roles like tanks, becomes narrower, this becomes a requirement. And anyone who played wow past vanilla knows from experience, that it becomes unfun way before it becomes truly challenging. All real challenge comes from social bullshit.
To give this sacred cow one more kick. Endgame raiding is NOT skill-intensive. The only reason majority of people are not making it there is the insane amount of gatekeeping, enabled by economy of scarcity, harsh punishments and based on noting but pure bullshit. ALMOST EVERY AVERAGE PLAYER CAN DO ENGAME RAIDS with enough experience (and it's a good thing). You are now cutting the easy route for new players to get it. Only relevant skills required to pass a filter are tolerating bullshit and competing with others on how hard you can no-life the game. Counterintuitevely, if sweats want more challenging gameplay, they have to give up on gatekeeping and harsh punishments enabling it (Making consumes last on death an banning world buffs from raids was a nice step in that direction btw). But that's not the crux of their motivation and not what they are after. Competitive and challenging are sometimes the opposite things.

 - Treat effort, organization difficulty and player skill as a part of progression to balance. Puggability and skill requirements should be slowly and gradually increasing. The end goal is to teach the most amount of players, not filter them out. Fill in the gaps in that progression (MC ==> BWL, BWL =========> AQ). Add new layers on top. MC should be puggable by a raid with 1/3 of players raiding for the first time in their life, all raid in blue gear. BWL an ONY should be somewhat puggable by mostly noobs, who have completed MC several times. Never ever EVER move a goalpost because of how average player skill has changed.
 - Raids from previous phases are a catch-up mechanic. Anything about them should be designed to GET AS MANY PEOPLE AS POSSIBLE to be ready (gear and experience) TO do ENGAME RAIDING, not to filter people out. The funnel should be made as wide as it can get. It's fine if some people reach their skill cap somewhere in the middle an stay there. It's not fine to create artificial wide gaps to separate playerbase into "real" and "casual". It has been tried and it sucks.
 - Consider every change on how it will impact economy of raiding spots. Will there be more or less people doing raids? If the answer is less, then why the fuck are you doing it?

Yes, sweats will be kicking and screaming and biting. You have to take this candy back from them now to set a precedent. If you don't want power creep because of new gear (nobody seems to buy that bs about classes being too stronk now btw) - ffs just ban kara40+ gear from MC. A more dev intensive solution is to implement gear downscaling based on instance tier similar to how heirlooms were.

Adding a harder boss to an easy raid means, that minimal requirements for that raid are now to match that boss. Nobody will carry noobs there. And carrying noobs is the main purpose of previous raid tiers. Adding a second boss to a single boss raid means, every player is now risking his lockout => more bs filtering => not fun. If you want to do a tightly-tuned encounter, remove the punishment for trying it. Like single boss instance or a weekly quest in an infinite lockout 5-man.

The OP is right.  Trying to turn entry level raids into sweat content shuts the door to new players.

It's fine to add harder content NEXT to those raids, or even in a separate room within those raids, so to speak.  However, that first step into raid gear so that a player can go on to participate in the harder raids needs to remain at an entry level tuning or the entire game falls apart over time.

I disagree that PuGs should be the endgame at any point for any raid content.  Finding a PuG for ZG or K10 or something is fine, but players PuGing into MC/BWL/AQ/Naxx/K40 isn't healthy.  The guild system exists for a reason, and players should be using it.  Find a guild, join a guild.

The vanilla content structure itself incentivizes running all raid tiers every week for any player.  Even a "finished" character wants to be played.  For guilds to continue recruiting and gearing players, or for new guilds to form, that initial two tiers of MC/BWL cannot be tuned to anything remotely close to an AQ level.  You'll only shut down the front end of TWoW's endgame while catering to players who don't need the extra incentive.

It's also worth noting that there is no upper limit to difficulty tuning.  It is ONLY a slippery slope.  Every little tweak to make a piece of content harder will be adapted to until it's easy.  You can see this in the existing raid tiers themselves.  MC is maniacally easier than BWL which is easier than AQ which is easier than Naxx.  Yet, players who struggled with MC and go on to clear BWL then AQ then Naxx will look back on that struggle in each tier and feel that the content is now easy.

Players always adapt to extra challenge, and constantly catering to that adaptation creates an unapproachable mess of a game over time.  This is thoroughly proven by retail WoW's raiding content.  Chasing this dragon is guaranteed to kill the game.
