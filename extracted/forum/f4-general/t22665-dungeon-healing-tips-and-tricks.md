# Dungeon Healing Tips and Tricks

## Post 157823 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2025-10-30T13:12:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=157823#p157823 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

Hey!

Wanted to write an entry about dungeon healing.
Core audience consists of players wanting to try out healing but are not familiar with the role or healers who are not satisfied with they own performance during dungeon healing (let's say, too many corpse runs).

This article is NOT for raid healing. It relies on a fundamentally different principle.
Dungeon healing is about resource management: you have to keep everyone healthy by using as few resources as possible.
Raid healing is mostly about output, using consumes to keep up the increased healing.
Of course, some resource management is also needed there but at raid healing, the focus is on the output itself.
This also leads to specialisation amongst healers (a tree spamming HoT), shammie spamming chain heal etc.
Raid heal, is in a way, simplified.
 *Dungeon healing is not.*

I wanna focus on your full toolkit and how to make the best of it in dungeons.
Will provide generic tips addressing every healing build and then go into specifics for **shammie** and **priest**.
I haven't played healer **pala** for a really long time and I dislike **druid** healing, so didn't play it enough to be able to give tips.

 **Generic Tips and Tricks**

 **1. Heal Downgrading**

Some people say you only need heal downscales at later levels but I disagree.
As soon as you have rank 2 of a heal, have both that one AND rank 1 on your action bars.
Your main goal is to keep people alive while wasting as few mana as possible.
If a rank 1 healing wave does the job, using rank 2 healing wave just wastes mana.
I usually advise having 3 ranks of each heals on your action bar. One mana-efficient, one big and one in-between.
Usually assigning them to a certain key while shift + key and control + key give me the alternatives.

 *So, why do you want to waste as few mana as possible?*
Two reasons:
 1. You'll have more mana for emergencies. During the fight someone ass-pulled another pack? Don't worry, you saved up enough mana to keep the guys alive.
 2. Smoother dungeon runs. The more drinking brakes you can skip the faster and smoother the run is for everyone.

 **2. Group Unit Frames can be Tricky**

Specially if you check how much of the HP bar is lost instead of the numbers.
Some classes just have less HP.
A mage with half HP can probably still be fully healed with a downranked heal, no need to waste mana.
A bear at half HP? You *need* the big heal.

When you evaluate what to use when healing a target, don't only check the size of HP bar remaining but also the class in question.
 *Or just have the missing HP data displayed.*

A shammie example: You see the bear at 70% HP, a rogue at 55% HP and another one at 90% HP. You cast chain heal. Many shammies would cast it on the rogue with 55% HP because its HP bar is the smallest.
But the correct decision is usually to target the bear.
 *The tank's 30% missing HP is probably more than the rogue's 45% missing HP.*

 **3. Look at Damage Intervals**

In short: a character, usually the tank, being damaged more frequently needs more focus.
A DPS randomly getting damaged once means a lower prio at healing.
Unless the DPS consistently pulls threat. In this case you can anticipate the guy getting incoming hits over and over again and jumps in healing priority to keep them alive.
Or you go educational and let the guy die. Depends on how passive-aggressive you wanna be.
God knows, how many arcane explosion-spammer mages I've let die when the tank was a prot warrior.

Main takeaway is: based on roles and threat patterns you can anticipate who gets hit and it helps with heal prio.
Usually tank > big parse DPS > other DPS.
Count in the DPS' health pool (point 2) but don't expect them to focus on their own survival.
Your fury won't swap in a shield and go def stance or your rogue won't go for feint to lose aggro.

 **4. Know How Much Mana You Need**
There is no need to slow down the run by wanting to be at 100% mana before pulls.
With time and experience, you get to know your mana bar.
You know how much heal you can do with the current mana you have.
If you feel comfortable, don't slow down everyone's fun, many times even 20% is enough if you know what you're doing.
Just have 1-2 mana pots as emergency in case you miscalculate or ass-pull happens.
But if others are drinking and the tank stops to wait for them, *always drink*.
In that case you don't slow down anyone but still recharge your mana.

 **Shammie tips:**

 **1. You'll Struggle with Mana Until You Don't**
Rush stable shields and improved water shield combo. Until that point, mana is a problem.
From that point, mana is easy.

 **2. Healing Focus + Earth Shield**
Those two give you 100% spell pushback reduction. So if there's an emergency, you go all out and pull mobs, don't panic. Swap to earth shield, spam heals and trust the team. If you had to heal so much you pulled, keeping up those heals is essential for the team to survive.

 **3. Forget Healing Stream Totem**
Never worths it. Waste of mana.

 **4. Know Which Healing to Use**
Is it urgent? Lesser healing wave.
Normal situation? Healing waves.
Multitarget and you wanna get over it in 1 GCD? Chain heal.

Healing wave is more mana efficient than chain heal. Many cases, it is enough to slowly go through the targets with healing wave one by one. Over-relying on chain heal is an error, wastes a bunch of mana.
Also, downgrade accordingly.

 **5. Your Resistance Totems are Basically Heals**
If the elemental damage in question is high. Sure, using WF is kinda obligatory, forget about nature resistance totem.
But frost resistance? Fire resistance? They make the tam resist a bunch of damage which you then don't have to heal.
Handle those totems as situational healing totems.
Same applies to cleansing totems. A cleansed poison does no damage, for example.

 **6. Nature's Swiftness**
Or whatever name it has now. The emergency button. Always use it alongside a max rank healing wave (tank saving option) or max rank chain heal (biggest multitarget heal you can make).
You don't need the biggest rank of heal at the moment? Then you probably don't need to use nature's swiftness, there is no emergency. Don't waste it. You might need it soon.

 **7. Judgment of Wisdom**
There's a pala in the group? A prot pala even?
If there's no need for urgent heals and the moöbs don't cleave, jump into melee and hit the judgement of wisdom target. Free mana combos really well with your goal of mana management.

 **8. Tidal Surge + Undertow**
Kinda meh at raids but at dungeons help with your no waste principle.
Another reason to prioritize the downranked, single-target heals and enjoy all your mana you have left.

 **9. Rank 1 Healing Wave**
At later levels, it is basically free. If the tank is kinda squishy, spam it on him before big pulls or bosses.
Proccing ancestral fortitude on him turns the tank super bulky.

 **10. Mana Regeneration**
You gotta place those expensive totems and I just talked about spamming rank 1 healing wave.
But you also have to pay attention to the 5 sec full spirit mana regen rule, makes your mana recharge faster.

 *What it means in practice:*
 1. If you drink before the pull, spam the totems before the drinking. Saves you a GCD with casting and leaves you with more mana.
 2. Rank 1 healing wave only if needed. Otherwise just let your spirit recharge your mana.
 3. Be lazy when you can. Why heal up someone instantly when the guy is at 90% HP if your downgraded heal heals 20% his HP bar? Do nothing, wait until the guy gets to 80% HP and in the meantime, let the 5 second kick in and watch your mana grow.

 **Priest tips:**

 **1. Inner Focus Usage**
It makes your next heal free. So:
 1. Spend the most expensive heal you need at the moment. Usually prayer of healing, greater heal or even just a max rank renew or shield.
 2. Use it strategically. Free heal means no interruption in full spirit mana regen. Unlike nature's swiftness, inner focus is not necessarily an emergency button. You can use it just to save your full spirit mana regen going and enjoy your free mana.
 3. You can also use it to do those expensive rezzes or buffs. Prayer of fortitude? Done. No mana lost.

 **2. Which Heal to Use**
It is much more complex with priests with a bunch of recommendations, *since they have much more ways to heal.*

 *Lesser heal/heal/downgraded greater heal:* Mana-efficient heal, I'd consider it the default way of healing.
Big greater heal only becomes relevant in case of a single-target 'full restore', usually done alongside inner focus.
These heals are used if there is no urgency, otherwise you need faster options.

 *Flash heal:* Your fast response. Don't spam it as it is expensive. But you can still downgrade it. A mage need a fast heal? Don't waste your max rank flash heal if a lesser version is enough.
Used in case of urgency.

 *Renew:* It's a bit complex, has 4 uses in my opinion.
 1. You expect big incoming damage on the tank. Keeping up renew makes it feel like the party has two healers.
 2. Low urgency heal on DPS. Let's say rogue ripped threat and lost 20% of HP. You wanna heal him up but don't wanna waste mana (flash heal) or time (heal/downgraded greater heal). A downgraded renew does the trick.
Works well if there is no urgency on the target but there is some urgency on the tank (you wanna get back healing the tank asap).
Don't rely on renew on a DPS ripping the threat all the time since renew slowly builds up. Guy might die. For such DPS, shield or direct heal is better.
 3. On the move heals. You don't wanna stand like an idiot and watch the mates run away as you try to heal between pulls. Just renew and run with them.
 4. Warlocks. Just keep a downgraded renew on them. It basically allows them to lifetap whenever they want and go all out.

 *Shield:* Again, a complex one.
 1. Don't shield rage users unless it is an emergency. In that case, *shield them*. PW: Shield is your instant damage absorption. Everything else either requires a cast time or has a HoT buildup. So if the tank is on the brink of dying, shield him, even if he's using rage. This is your instant 'I save ya' button which has to be followed up by emergency heals.
 2. Keep up shield on mana-using tanks before big pulls and bosses. Kinda like renew, point 1. You can even use both, just be aware that shield and HoT on a full HP tank will just result in wasted HoT ticks on a full HP target, and the tank will start losing HP later because of the shield.
 3. I sometimes use downgraded pw shield on threat-ripper DPS. *You know the guy will pull threat.* But the shield buys you time until you have to save his arse again when that happens. Good if you really wanna focus on the tank during that specific pull.
Don't use it on rage-using DPS threat-rippers though.
 4. Self-shield for spell pushback mitigation if fade isn't enough or is on CD.

 *Prayer of fortitude:* Expensive, slow but heals everyone. You see everyone at half HP? Can use it if healing up everyone one by one eats up too many GCDs. Usually used alongside inner focus. Usually happens if a mob does a big cleave/aoe.
DON'T use automatically prayer of healing if many targets need heal or you can kiss your mana bar goodbye even if you downgrade the spell. Usually you can slowly renew/heal the targets one by one. Use this spell if you need to patch the guys up in 1 GCD or wanna burn an inner focus.

 *Lightwell:* Just put it down before some bosses. No one will use it anyway. Looks cool, though.

 *Holy nova:* Goes against your mana management principle but can be super fun. I usually advise using it on big, low-health trash if wanna speed things up. More fun than useful though from a healer pov, because of how fast it burns mana.

 **3. Proclaim Champion**
Only go for the extra heal on target if the tank is underleveled or undergeared.
Otherwise you should be able to patch him up.
Go for the 5% extra damage. Helps the tank keep threat, dungeon is smoother. Everyone is happy.

 **4. See a Pala? Go Melee**
Same as with the shamans. If no immediate heal is needed and mobs don't cleave or aoe, eat up that free mana from judgment of wisdom.

 **5. Shadow Protection**
Same as with the shamans. That buff is basically a preemptive heal if mobs deal shadow damage.

 **6. Inspiration**
Same as the shammie's ancestral fortitude. You can fish for it with r1 spells.
BUT
You need it way less. You have renew and shields in your toolkit to help the tank survive.
Still, for mana-efficiency, before a big pull or boss, you can go for it. 25% more armor is huge.

 **7. Cleansing Magic**
It can be super heavy on mana. Always consider if the effects needs to be dispelled or not. Sometimes the mob just reapplies the effect instantly and you just oom yourself.
As a general rule:
Dispel a magic effect, if it is indeed harmful, not reapplied instantly and specially, if it is on a tank.
Casters always get a priority if the magical effect does silence.
