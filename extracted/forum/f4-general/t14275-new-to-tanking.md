Depends. If you like tanking, do it and practice it.
Honestly, if you are kicked so often, it probably means that you are currently bad at tanking. But it doesn't mean that you can't become good if you practice - as long as the added hours of practice worths it for you because you like tanking.
Some more ideas:
Keybind target symbols. Always use skull and moon for CC (if needed). As you get the hang of it, start using x for second target.
Usually go with dual-wield but have a good shield in your bag and have a weapon swap macro to have the shield for big incoming damage.
Learn the dungeons as a tank. Know which mobs to kill first in a pack to put up skull correctly (like ghost in Scholo).
Don't be too slow, usually overthinking leads to people getting annoyed. Have mana users enough time to get some mana (60-70 percent I'd say normally, full before big packs or bosses). If healer has issues, wait until he has more mana before pulls.
If threat is a problem, ask for some sec of no DPS when you run in to have initial threat.
Learn when to rush in, when to range pull and how and when to LOS pull.
Run some lower dungs like Mara to practice your pulling and rotation. Once comfortable, try the same at high dungeons, preferably with friends or guildies.
But tanking can be stressful, people can be retards and at the end of the day, your gameplay is being punched in the face while you farm a LOT to have enough consumes for virtually any raid. If you still wanna do it, I'd advise these steps.

## Post 97367 by Forthehorde — 2024-06-19T22:14:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=97367#p97367 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Here's my experience as a warrior tank.  Not saying this is correct or best, just saying it's what I've observed and learned.  Note this mostly applies to tanking 5-10 man groups (not raid tanking, which is a different thing).

Aggro
As a tank, your primary objective is to obtain and keep aggro. I realize that sounds obvious, but when I first started tanking, I was so worried about defense, armor, and shields, then realized that my ability to absorb damage was meaningless if I wasn't the one taking damage.  Mobs aren't going to attack you just because you're "the tank".

DPS
The #1 way you obtain and keep aggro is through DPS. This means your weapon becomes your #1 tool to keeping aggro; always invest in the best possible weapon dps.  If you have to spend money on enchants that push your dps higher, do it.  If you're trying to decide between armor that gives more defense or dps, choose dps.  Can't decide on talents?  Answer: dps.  Also, while the "defensive stance" helps to keep you at the top of the aggro list, don't rely on it. Always make dps output your #1 priority.

But not just single target DPS, but DPSing as many targets at the same time as possible.  If you're not hitting all mobs, you're not getting aggro, which means someone else is.

Also remember there's 3 other primary dps in the group going all-out dps on every group.  Some are trying to be the #1 dps'er in the group.  Others are playing a mini-game to see if they can steal aggro from the main tank.  Keep that in mind.  Being a 5-man tank is as much about fighting your own group's dps as it is fighting the mobs.

Rage
The more rage you have, the more you can use that rage to maximze dps across multiple targets, which increases your aggro across more targets, which increases you getting hit, which gives you more rage. This is the cycle you're looking for, and you want to keep it going throughout the dungon; even between mob groups.

At the beginning of a dungeon, you'll have 0 rage, so ask the group to let you build some up before they start.  Then you want to use the mobs to fill your rage meter as much as possible, using rage sparingly only either to a) keep the aggro of as many mobs as possible or 2) dumping aggro because your rage bar is full.   IMPORTANT:  At the end of the fight, your rage bar should be as full as possible, so you have plenty of rage to start the next fight. This is why you see so many tanks rush through mobs.  Partially because they're bored and/or speed-run freaks, but also because they're trying not to let their rage tick down between fights.

Shields
Unless you're taking so much damage that your healer can't keep up, don't bother with a shield.  Instead, use a 2H or dual wielding to maximize your dps (therefore keeping aggro and taking damage as a tank should do).

Talents
Ironically, the "protection" tree doesn't protect 5-10 man groups; it's more designed for single-target raid bosess.  Instead, use the fury and arms tree.  Select talents that will maximize 1) your dps 2) your rage so that you can do more dps.

Target rotation
Unlike your group, who is primarily focused on 1 target (and occassionally doing aoe to others), your strategy will be the opposite; you want to simultaneously dps as many taragets as possible  so they all focus on you.  This is where "target marking" using hot-keyed raid markers comes in handy.  Eveyone knows skull is first.  You hit skull while also doing aoe damage to everyone else, constantly switching targets to make sure all mobs are getting hit with aoe and/or direct damage.  By the time skull is 50% health, you can forget about it and start building aggro on X.  Because even if you lose aggro on skull; who cares.  By that time it's so low on health that one of the dps is more than happy to finish it off.

Target priority
 99% of the time: Mobs that ignore aggro tables > Casters > elites > non elites.   Learn which mobs ignore aggro tables and target them first.  A single lingering caster can wipe an otherwise good group.

Aggro/dps meters
Invest in an aggro meter that shows your aggro and that of your group. This gives you real-time feedback on what weapons and tactics are keeping you at the top of the aggro meter, and which ones aren't.  Yes, it's yet another thing you have to visually juggle being a tank, but like learning to drive a car with all the craziness passing by, it's just something you have to get used to.

Taunt
Save this for your healer.  If your healer can't heal, everyone is dead.  But if something goes after a dps, fuck'em.  (Although mages are kinda an exception to this unless they're oom).  DPS players will learn to back off their insane dps, and while they might get mad at you publicly, secretly they're smiling inside knowing their dps rocks.  They're playing the dps mini-game while pretending to be offended.  Recognize the game and let them feel the heat as needed.   Unless of course, your dps sucks and you're losing aggro to anything and everthying, which brings us back to the top.

Mindset
Last thing I'll mention is that tanking is not just a skill but a mindset, and it seems to require a certain personality type.  Some thrive on the fast-paced chaos of tanking while others are stressed out by it, no matter how good their skills get.  The same goes for other roles like healing.  Some find it stressfull or even mindless boredom, while for others, it's a tranquility better than therapy.
