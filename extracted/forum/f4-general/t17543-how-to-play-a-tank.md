Hmm...
I'm giving some personal experiences. It's only about dungeon tanking, not raid tanking.

Warrior is among the harder ones for dungeon. To understand this, your role as someone who tanks is different in raids and dungeons. This is something people tend to overlook.

In dungeons as a tank you manage pulls and make sure you never take more than you can handle. Most of the time you have to tank many low-damage mobs and no opponent, including bosses has extreme damage.
In raids you get a symbol assigned and try to stay alive despite receiving disgusting amounts of damage damage while keeping threat above everyone else.

These are two different things. Warrior toolkit is perfect for the latter but in dungeons the warrior's biggest weakness comes out. While it can tank big packs, it is much more complicated and less effective for them.
I don't propose warrior as a beginner tank because of this. It's kinda complicated, you have to get hit to get resources to use skills and most importantly, most dungeons' main feature (big packs of small hitters) is something warriors do relatively badly.
I've seen a lot of warriors grow insecure and losing mobs left and right in dungeons.

Paladin is a good choice. Scaling is relatively high, it can get complicated. But hitting the bare minimum is easy. You get righteous fury and some ways to deal holy damage, including holy aoe. You gotta trust the others for range pull or get some throwable explosives, though. Imo paladin tanks' biggest pain point is range but it's something the others can do for you. You just glue every small mob onto you and have fun. And as you keep playing, you can get better, use downgrades, seals etc properly. At first you'll probably drink all the time because you'll waste a bunch of mana. But even then, pala will work and will only become better.
And you can always salvation the DPS which is a BIG advantage.

I don't really propose druid. Big armor, lots of HP, big threat after mauling, sure. But swipe and thorns are not that reliable for the big packs. Since imo the biggest part of dungeons are those big trash packs, I won't recommend a tank which - if inexperienced - can have serious problems with them.
Also, bear form makes you almost blind in some narrow places.

Shaman is weird. Basically you get a paladin, give it range and in exchange take away survivability. I don't propose it because initially it is very hard to stay alive. But honestly, if we talk about dungeons, after certain talents and gear, I think shaman is the 2nd best dungeon tank. The name of the game is trash aoe and shamans are the 2nd best at it. At places where you have problems at a warrior, you just put down totems and mobs magically glue to you. Just make sure you stay alive.

tldr: I'd propose pala (high ceiling, but easy to be 'good enough', excellent aoe threat, can decrease DPS threat with blessing).
Warrior is overcomplicated and worse with aoe.
Bear makes you sometimes hard to see and worse with aoe.
Shaman is good with aoe but it dies very easily initially.

I'd def propose getting enginnering for cheap grenades so if you really wanna range pull, you can do it yourself.
Or dungeon with a friend whom you trust with the pulls. It's important that pulls are timed right so you gotta have trust. Otherwise some dumbo will pull 3 packs while pulling a pat at the worst possible second.

Some base concepts:

 * LOS pull - You hit the targets with something and hide from line of sight. Targets will run to you to fight you. Good to force mobs to come to a cleared place and avoid patrols. Also very good to deal with casters who 'come here for the smack'.

 * Run in pull - You just run to the pack. Riskier but faster. As a pala, if geared enough, you can usually run to a pack, consec and then run to another one and tank each of them at once.

 * Symbols - Keybind some symbols for (very) quick access. Always have a skull up to 'direct' DPS. Consider moon if you wanna rely on someone's utility, like a mage's sheep.

 * Mana - Only pull if you have enough resources. Same with healer. Healer with 10% mana will let you die and it's your fault. DPS... I usually let them drink up and skip a pull because it's faster.

 * Have a threatmeter.

 * Tab between hit targets to distribute threat but also make sure that skull gets most of the love. I usually tab one, hit a guy, turn back to skull, hit, turn to the guy next to the latest non-skull victim, hit, turn back to skull etc. One to skull, one for someone else. But with consec, things are easier.

 * No build has everything, feel free to ask party members to use things to make your life easier. Poly, shackle, you name it.

 * You have the responsibility to make the run smooth (more or less) but it doesn't give you the right to be a jerk with the others. They are not NPCs to serve you.
