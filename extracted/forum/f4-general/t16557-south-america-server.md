# South America Server

## Post 112928 by Wilsonsds (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2024-12-16T16:39:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=112928#p112928 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

After hearing about the development of the Latin America server for Turtle WoW and watching the promotional video, I was initially excited about the idea of a dedicated server for our region. However, upon deeper reflection, I believe this initiative faces significant challenges that could ultimately make it unviable.

The primary issue lies in the vast linguistic and cultural divide between Brazilian Portuguese (PT-BR) and South American Spanish. While Spanish speakers across Latin America share a degree of linguistic and cultural cohesion, Brazilians are distinct in both language and culture. Historically, efforts to unite these communities in online gaming have rarely succeeded. Brazilians and Spanish speakers often struggle to coexist on the same platform, and when both languages are allowed without a neutral intermediary like English, the result is frequently conflict and toxicity. Expecting these groups to interact harmoniously under a bilingual framework, without clear strategies to address these tensions, seems overly optimistic.

Adding to this complexity, the decision to launch the server with Spanish support only, while phasing in PT-BR later, is a critical misstep. Brazilians represent the largest internet user base in Latin America and account for a significant portion of the region's gaming community. Launching without full support for their language risks alienating the single largest demographic the server could attract. By prioritizing Spanish and relegating PT-BR to a later phase, the project sends an unfortunate message to Brazilian players: that they are secondary to the server’s initial vision. This phased approach also raises practical concerns: how will Brazilian players communicate in the interim? Relying on Spanish or even English—which is not the focus of this server—will only exacerbate feelings of exclusion and frustration. First impressions are crucial, and failing to fully include Brazilians from the start risks solidifying the perception that the server is primarily for Spanish speakers, making it less appealing to Brazilians—even after localization is added.

The branding of the server as “South America” further complicates matters. While it ostensibly reflects the region's geography, it inadvertently excludes Latin American players from Central America and Mexico, who might feel disconnected from the “South America” identity. Rebranding to “Latin America” could be more inclusive, but this still doesn’t address the fundamental issues caused by the language and cultural divide.

Additionally, it’s worth noting that many Brazilian players are already settled on Nordrassil, where the dominant language (English) provides a neutral space. The idea of migrating to a server where Spanish takes precedence and PT-BR is an afterthought is unlikely to appeal to them. Moreover, the success of this new server would heavily depend on bilingual support from Game Masters (GMs) and the moderation team. If GMs are primarily Spanish-speaking at launch, Brazilian players are likely to feel unsupported. Providing robust and equitable support in both languages is essential but also resource-intensive and challenging to implement.

History provides valuable lessons here. Blizzard’s experience with Brazilian servers in retail World of Warcraft is a cautionary tale. Despite Brazil's vast player base, those servers were largely unsuccessful due to poor management, toxic interactions, and the unique challenges of accommodating the dynamics of the Brazilian gaming community. If Blizzard, with its substantial resources, struggled to cater to this audience, the risks for this project are even greater.

Ultimately, these factors lead me to believe that this server, as envisioned, is unlikely to succeed. Forcing two communities with such stark differences to share the same space, without a shared language or clear strategies to bridge these divides, risks creating an environment of division, hostility, and frustration. Instead of uniting players, the server could fracture the community further and fail to establish a healthy, sustainable player base.

A better approach might be to create separate servers or clear divisions for Portuguese and Spanish speakers, allowing each group to enjoy the game in their own language and cultural context. Without such measures, the server risks alienating the largest gaming demographic in the region and failing to provide the inclusive and harmonious experience it aims to achieve.
