# Why “they’re not in the US” might not save TurtleWoW

## Post 175054 by HerTiSo — 2026-04-13T12:15:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=175054#p175054 | page 1 | era: post-1.18.1 -->

Reading through the comments on most settlement posts/threads, I get the feeling that a lot of people are underestimating how serious this actually is. Once it gets to an actual court decision and a settlement, it is not just internet drama anymore, it is legally binding. Projects like this have always been on shaky ground, but as soon as money and visibility are involved, they become a much bigger target.

The whole argument that “they are not in the US so they are untouchable” does not really hold up either. Companies like Blizzard operate internationally and have multiple ways to enforce their rights across borders. Even if direct enforcement is messy, they can still apply pressure through hosting providers, payment processors, platforms, or future legal action. Being outside the US might slow things down, but it does not make you immune.

Another thing I noticed (I have not followed the case closely for a while) is the domain change from turtlewow to turtlecraft. Realistically, changing names or domains is not going to solve the underlying issue. If the legal pressure is there, it follows the project, not just the label.

I get why people want to believe it will somehow just continue or come back unchanged, but that is not how these situations usually play out when there is real legal pressure behind it. At best you might see something different emerge later, but expecting things to carry on as they are now feels pretty unrealistic. I am not saying everything disappears overnight, but acting like this will have no real consequences might be setting expectations a bit too high for how this will play out.
