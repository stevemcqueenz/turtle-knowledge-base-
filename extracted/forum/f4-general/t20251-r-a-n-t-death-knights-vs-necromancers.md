I hate to break it to you, but "Right of Conquest" was historically a real thing up until around WWI / WWII.  The entire concept is that the conquering force is stronger than any legal governance of that territory, by the fact that they conquered them, and is more likely to secure peace and stability afterwards.  The victor in a war had the right to impose their rule over the defeated territory and its population.  Read up on the Scramble for Africa for a fairly modern example of this, which is as recent as the 19th and 20th centuries; the colonization of Europe was another great example of this.

As for the right of conquest in Warcraft, the most recent example of this was in WC3:TFT just before the the Horde was officially formed between orc, tauren, and the Darkspear Tribe as they settled in what is now known as Durotar and conquered the land, displacing the centaur and quilboar.  Prior to that, a great example would be to look at how the humans and elves seized control of territory from the Amani Empire via war and conquest.  In fact, the primary way a kingdom expands is through the right of conquest by invading another land and seizing it.
