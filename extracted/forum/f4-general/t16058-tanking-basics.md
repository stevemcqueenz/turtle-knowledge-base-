# Tanking basics

## Post 109364 by Akos1896 (Grandmaster of Forum PvP) — 2024-11-22T13:33:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=109364#p109364 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hey!

I want to write a short article about basic concepts and tips for tanking.
It is created for those who wanna try out tanking but are afraid to do so or for those who recently started tanking and feel that the whole thing is a mess.
This writing is about basic things only. Also, differences between the tank builds and rotations are not touched.

So.
You wanna tank.
Some advices for you.

 **Raid symbols**
Use them. Keybind them. At a bare minimum, use skull consistently so DPS knows what to focus on. Otherwise they'll zug 3 different targets and you'll find yourself chasing down mobs which randomly start rushing towards your frends.
I use F1....F9 buttons for them but whatever you feel like using.
Always use skull for the primary target.
Use X if you wanna highlight a secondary target / the guy to kill after skull is dead.
Use moon on sheep target. I use it as a universal CC symbol. In a dungeon you won't need several CCs like a trap and a sheep at the same time anyway.
Whenever there's a downtime, like healer is drinking, observe the packs in front of you. Look for patroling mobs. During those downtimes I suggest marking the patrols, so you see VERY CLEARLY where they are. Prevents a bunch of multi-pulls and wipes.
I repeat, at least skull has to be very easily accessed. During fight, if your target dies, you can just reassign skull dynamically as the fight is going on, making sure that you direct the damage the desired way.

 **Types of pulls**
LOS Pull
Your best friend if the enemy pack has some casters. You range pull and hide to lose LOS. It will force the enemy to run to you, including the casters who come to have LOS and by doing so, they come so close that they can be meleed down reliably. Practice it a bit (hide properly, regain LOS at the right time). Super useful.
Just really make sure that you have really lost the LOS. Range pulling then standing next to a wall like a moron while eating up shadow bolts and dying looks lame. Speaking of experience.
LOS pull usually is a back-pull, meaning that you pull the mobs towards an already cleared direction (where you came from). So fight happens in a safe place where you won't need to worry about pulling additional packs.

 **Charge pull/Run in**
I mean you either just run in if you're a pala or a shaman or you're actually charging in.
All in all, a faster method. But you won't pull the mobs to a safe place. Be sure you won't pull additional guys.
Has its own advantages. By running in, you create space between you and the casters which can be important. Think about the aoe silence mobs from Maraudon or Stratholme! By running in you make sure casters won't get silenced.
If you're confident, you can combo with it, too, rushing to a pack and running to other ones, pulling several packs at the same time, far from the casters. Palas are specially good at that.

There are other ways of pulling like charge in + aoe taunt or asking a hunter's pet but it's for the basics.
You wanna play safe: LOS pull+pulling back.
You wanna play fast or you wanna create distance between the casters and the mob: charge.

Tab
Tab
An absolutely tabulous thing.
Okay, so as a tank you create a lot of threat usually when hitting the target. While each tanks have a multi-target attack option, they are usually not enough by themselves to keep threat. Spamming thunderclap will make ghouls run to frends, making frends sad. You can use your multitarget options to COMPLEMENT your rotation not to REPLACE it!
Your best button on the keyboard is tab. Hit a guy, tab, hit another guy, rinse and repeat. Make sure all of them get the big threat.
BUT
Pay special attention to skull since it will soak up most of the damage. You have to experiement the rhytm for yourself but skull needs more hits. Just tab regularly so every monster receives the love of your enchanted blade.
Forget this if the pack is just too big and contains like 120 larvae which just instantly die. But up to 4-5 targets pay really good attention on proper tabbing.

 **What to mark as skull?**
Usually check two things:
 * Which is the nastiest mob?
 * Which mob dies the fastest?

If there's a mob which can really hurt yall by staying alive, start with that one.
Otherwise skull a low HP first, probably a non-elite. It means DPS kills it super fast so you have to worry about 1 less target very soon. Exterminating the non-elites fast enables you to have a really easy time with 2-3 elites after the job is done.

Get a threatmeter and put it to a really visible place. Helps a lot.

Save as much resource as possible. If it's enough to spend 10 rage to keep the mob you shouldn't spend 20. This thinking makes sure you'll always have enough resource to react if needed. This one requires a lot of practice but it's maybe the most important one, specially if you are a mana user.

 **Also:**
Tanks are kinda expected to also be dungeon guides since they lead the group. There's no shame in having no idea where to go though, just ask a DPS to lead the way. This doesn't mean that you shouldn't memorize the place sooner or later - a tank eventually has to lead. But there's no shame in asking for directions.

 **Most importantly:**
More or less know what the other builds do. Good tanks are friendly but bark orders sometimes. If you know what your team can do, what buffs are available, what CCs can be done etc, you can guide them much better. Asking for specific CCs, whatever you need. Even if it's fighting with the enhancement shaman to accept the pala healer's salvation instead of might.

 **Mostest importantly:**
Don't take it too seriously. Ásspulls, bad teams, wipes will happen. You have to accept the possibility of failure. Those tanks who become dictators after a wipe because their pride was hurt are the worst.
