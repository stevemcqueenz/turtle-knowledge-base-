# Beginners Guide on Roleplay

## Post 141021 by Kyern — 2025-07-23T18:23:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=141021#p141021 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Hello everyone, this is my first post. I wanted to make a quick guide on how to Roleplay for Beginners.

You just entered the world. Congratulations. Depending on what race, class, or faction you chose, that has its own lore. You are going to want to read up on the background of whatever you selected. For example, let’s say you picked a Human Paladin. You will want to learn about Human history, Paladin tradition, and the power known as the Light.

 **Human Lore**

Humans are one of the youngest races on Azeroth, but they have made a massive impact. Their kingdoms once spanned large regions of the Eastern Kingdoms, with Stormwind being one of the last great strongholds. Known for their adaptability, ambition, and determination, humans often find themselves on the front lines of conflict. From the ancient empire of Arathor to the tragedies of the Scourge and the fall of Lordaeron, human history is full of war, sacrifice, and resilience.

 **Paladin Lore**

Paladins are holy warriors trained to wield both sword and spirit. They draw power from the Light to protect the innocent, heal the wounded, and smite the wicked. First seen in the Order of the Silver Hand during the Second War, paladins were created to fight the growing threat of the Horde and the undead. A paladin’s strength is not only in armor and hammer but in their unwavering belief that the Light will guide them. They are champions of justice and mercy, often placed in morally complex situations that test their resolve.

 **The Light**

The Light is a powerful and mysterious force in the universe, associated with healing, protection, and righteousness. Those who believe in the Light can harness its power through faith, discipline, and training. For paladins and priests, it is a source of strength that comes from within, fueled by conviction. However, the Light is not always black and white. It requires belief and a clear sense of purpose. Doubt weakens it, while true devotion empowers it. Characters who follow the Light often have a strong moral compass, but that path is not always easy.

 **IC = In Character**

“In Character” means you are speaking and acting as your character would within the world of Azeroth. Everything you say or do IC is part of the roleplay. If you are a grizzled dwarf warrior, you might say, “Aye, lad, them trolls won’t know what hit ’em.” If you are a priest, you might offer blessings or quote scripture of the Light. Think of it as being in a fantasy movie and staying in your role the entire time you are around others who are roleplaying.

 **OOC = Out of Character**

“Out of Character” means you are speaking as yourself, the player, not your character. This is used to clarify something, ask a question, or handle something unrelated to the game world. In World of Warcraft, people usually use parentheses or brackets to show OOC chat. For example, you might say: (Hey, brb real quick, grabbing dinner) or [OOC: Does anyone know where the RP is happening tonight?]. It helps keep things respectful and smooth between players.

 **Metagaming**

Metagaming is when you take information that your character would not logically know and use it during roleplay. For example, let’s say you see someone post in General Chat that they are an undercover spy. Your character does not know that. If you suddenly confront them IC and say, “You are a traitor,” that would be metagaming. Always keep in-game knowledge and player knowledge separate.

 **Godmoding**

Godmoding is when your character is unrealistically powerful or does things that break the rules of fair roleplay. For example, if you say, “I swing my sword and instantly kill you without a chance to dodge,” that is godmoding. Roleplay should be cooperative, not forced. A better way to handle it would be: “He swings his sword in a wide arc, aiming for your side,” and let the other player respond. Always give people a chance to react and keep things balanced.

 **Joining a Roleplay Guild**

Joining a roleplay guild can be a great way to immerse yourself in the world. Many guilds welcome new roleplayers and can help guide your journey. The best way to join is often through roleplay itself. Attend an event, interact with their members, and show genuine interest. You can also whisper someone OOC and say, “Hi, I saw your guild and I’m new to RP. Would it be okay to join or learn more about what you do?” Guilds are always looking for people who are respectful, eager, and ready to have fun.

Roleplay can be fun because it lets you step into someone else’s shoes and live out stories in a shared world, almost like being the writer and actor of your own fantasy movie. It sharpens your creativity, helps you think on your feet, and encourages collaboration with others. Beyond improving language skills, it can boost your confidence, improve your writing, and even reduce social anxiety by helping you practice communication in a safe and imaginative setting. You get to make friends, explore your character’s emotions, and laugh at ridiculous moments together. "Look at the lad go! 'Es on the roof again, aye!" *Slowly raises his mug to his lips and sips on his ale, the fat Dwarf smiled as he drunkenly stared up at his friend standing on the roof, wiggling his rear down at the angry Guardsmen.*
