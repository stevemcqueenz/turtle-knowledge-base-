## Post 114321 by Neechy (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2024-12-25T20:05:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=114321#p114321 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Multiple things can be true at once. The current issues are:

 1. Class changes have been too severe. CC2 is too massive of an overhaul that has led to to design scope creep and power creep. CC1 was more reasonable as it was focused on touching up subpar vanilla specs such as ret pal and enh sham. Given that the devs cannot reign in the chaos caused by CC2 and only continue to double down with endless sets of arbitrary revisions, the best course of action is to end the [CC2 sunk cost fallacy](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?f=14&t=16377) and roll back changes. This may require rolling back to CC1 as it was a more stable baseline to work from, and just focus on the subpar specs without touching the solid classes like warriors and mages. In the meantime any big talent/ability changes should be tested on a separate test realm so as not to disrupt the live server and hold back the release of new content any longer.

 2. Stats, items, consumables, etc. should be compressed. There was already concern in vanilla about the widening power increments between tier sets, so that T3 was significantly more power than T2 compared to T2 vs T1. In my opinion, the value of rewards to the difficulty level of raids/dungeons should not be some 1:1 correlation or even close to. Meaning, just because Naxxramas is widely considered more difficult than AQ40, it doesn't mean that Tier 3 should be significantly better than T2.5. As such, when Kara40 is out, the devs could design it to be, say, 25% more difficult than Naxxramas but T3.5 to be like max 10% stat increase, and likewise for later tiers. This can also avoid having to emphasize weird stats on tier sets like agility for paladin T3/T3.5 (why would a paladin be agile?). And I think this can also be done without having to remove flavour from the game as turtle has done in some cases. For example, you can compress the power levels of items without removing the ability for shadow oil to scale with spellpower.

 3. Boss encounters can be buffed. I'd say I'm against buffing enemy stats across the board, because if the above two points are addressed, then it is unnecessary. Compression rather than expansion is generally better, because it reigns in number creep. That said, I have heard discussion about how some raid encounters, particularly for end bosses like C'thun feel way too easy, and I have seen myself how these bosses just melt. A boss like C'thun in particular could have its values reverted to the vanilla pre-nerf which would ensure the fight stays more challenging and requires more player coordination to defeat.

 4. Vanilla wasn't necessarily designed to be some super challenging game  or have all the complex bloat of class abilities and raid encounters that came in later expansions.. Being casual friendly was always part of its appeal, and I hope turtle keeps that in mind as well. Not everything needs to be some sweaty neckbeard grind that shuts out a large block of players from experiencing content. I think it's a good idea for Naxx or Kara40 to be challenging while allowing the possibility for 2 or 3 filthy casuals to tag along for the ride. Perhaps by the final raid (e.g. [Fighting LK for T5?](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=111662#p110858)) this level of flexibility will be diminished, reserved only for the most sweatiest gamers.

P.S. Some of the racial changes also remain weird and are even contributing to power creep. For example, humans are the most played race and yet they received a 2% crit CD ability - if you have a raid with 50% humans you don't think this is gonna further trivialize fights? It also just feels out of place, because why would normie ass humans have some racial superpower that enhances their perception and deal more damage than magical beings like elves, trolls, etc.
