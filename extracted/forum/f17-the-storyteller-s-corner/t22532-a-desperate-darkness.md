# A Desperate Darkness

## Post 156664 by Daromath — 2025-10-23T03:31:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=156664#p156664 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

*I hate losing control of everything.*

The thought danced in circles around Daromath’s addled mind. For the first time in his life, not only was his future a mystery – but his present was, as well. He evaded the grasp of Amaraline’s manifold shadows, just long enough to realize that the solution lay within her presence in his life all along. It was ironic, really – for a sorcerer so sworn against the dark, Daro found himself desperately seeking it.

It had been weeks now since he’d left town – but in his isolation, weeks began to twist into months within his head. It wasn’t the greatest idea to venture out and find this shadow caster alone – what with the fractured state of his mind, any harm intended to befall him could prove lethal, and cut his journey short.

But he couldn’t give up on this mission – this quest. Daro had a handful of items on his mental bucket list: exacting revenge against his father; dispelling the Crusade, and its branches; protecting Calistah from himself; and most importantly, saving an old friend, Vendric.

The woods encircling the sorcerer had begun to decay a few hundred feet back, but it was only this far into this neck of the forest that Daro actually noticed, too enveloped by his rampant thoughts swallowing his consciousness. It appeared Daro was closer than ever, finding himself here, at the foot of the Deadwind Pass.

A lone crow cried out in the distance. *Not unlike myself*, Daro thought to himself, audibly scoffing. *A lone creature, void of any flock, any family – just himself, and his tools.* Daro trudged along the weathered path, clearly bothered by the lack of vibrance in the immediate vicinity. Deadwind truly lived up to its name – the only force of nature being the howling gusts that ensured Daro was grateful for his pyromancy.

Daro snapped his fingers, instinctively, to try and summon a flame. Several embers flickered within his palm momentarily… and extinguished themselves, almost instantly. He frowned at his failed spell. Daro knew that giving into this darkness that lay ahead, this shadow magic, would reduce his arcane mastery to that of a lowly apprentice, but it was a required sacrifice.

One for the greater good… a greater good that Daro was never a stranger to.

The crow from before flew overhead, as if… watching him? Daro narrowed his eyes at the creature as it hovered above the treeline over his head, casting a faint shadow as it gracefully soared in and out of view–

A rustle in the shrubbery to his left. Daro snapped his attention to the source of the noise… spotting nothing, but his instincts told him otherwise. This wouldn’t be the first time he was stalked by bandits, and it likely wouldn’t be the last.

“I know you’re there,” Daro growled. It was evidence from his tone alone that the sorcerer was exhausted, both from the thousands of steps he’d walked and the debilitating weight of the encroaching shadow within his vessel. “If you’re intelligent in any capacity, you’d know that your best odds are to strike–”

A whizzing noise, as if something flying through the air, cut off Daro’s sentence. Daro staggered back, as if hit by an arrow… which, judging from the arrow that had sunken into his shoulder, would be exactly that.He winced as he instinctively clutched at the arrowhead. Daro could feel the blood seeping from the wound, the pain flaring up and down his entire arm… but it wasn’t time for assessing the damage.

Daro tried to lift both arms to cast a spell properly, but after realizing he was unable to lift both due to his wound, resorted to summoning his conjured blade to his open hand. “Come out and face me, coward!” he yells into the night, frantically looking to search where his assailant was.

“You should know better, Daromath,” a smooth, sly voice chuckled from over his shoulder. “A noble, traveling alone, and leaving the King’s Lands? What could you possibly be after out here in Deadwind?” The voice was taunting, a lion playing with its food.

Daro twisted behind him, narrowing his eyes at the path he traveled from – no one was there. Whoever this was attacking him, they were clearly experienced.

“That is none of your business,” Daro coldly remarked. His hand gripped the blade’s hilt tightly, anxiously expecting another arrow to arrive shortly.

“Oh, but it is precisely my business.” The voice seemed to be in a different direction now – somewhere away from the road once more. “I was sent here for you, you know – your name is a rather valuable one in some circles… heh. ‘Cindero’.”

Daro narrowed his eyes – who could be after him, or more importantly, know that he was this far from Stormwind? There wasn’t a chance that the Scarlet Crusade had kept tabs on him for this long – no, he’d managed to escape into hiding again. But if it wasn’t them…

Daro heard the whisper-like creaking of the bow, this time to his right again, but he insisted on remaining still to bait the attack. “My name?” he shouted back. “If it’s noble’s business you’re after, you should be looking for my father.”

Silence.

Another arrow pierced through the air – this time, Daro was able to spin around, ducking out of the way. “Dead or alive?” Daro asked the mysterious stranger, glancing back at his shoulder injury. Awaiting the response, he brought his sword to the arrow embedded in his arm, chopping off the majority of its shaft.

“Dead.”

A third arrow landed at Daro’s feet – before he could react, it exploded into a cloud of dense fog. *Some sort of Gnomish smoke arrow*, he thought to himself, annoyed by the maneuver as he coughed and tried to fan away the smoke.

What Daro assumed to be the figure leapt into the fog, forcing the sorcerer to take a step back to regain composure and footing. The assailant unsheathed two blades, ever obscured by the mist, swiping at Daro. The double strike was calculated, and clearly reflected a good deal of training – both the assailant’s longer and shorter swords were aimed for Daro’s vitals.

Daro sidestepped, parrying the longsword and avoiding the shortsword. Strike after strike from his attacker was parried, although it was obvious that Daro would only last so long in a martial combat before suffering a life-threatening wound. He’d have to even the playing field.

Daro spun as he opened his left hand. While unable to bring it to eye level, he whisked arcane energies in an arc between himself and his opponent, before forcing it at their legs – but the assailant simply dodged it, performing a corkscrew flip, landing deftly on their heels.

Daro was stunned – who would send such a professional assassin after him? If the assassin was this well trained, surely they cost a fortune – which would mean whoever targeted Daromath wanted more than just gold.

The fog finally began to part as Daro had a much clearer look at whoever this was… the person was clad in black leather armor, sporting a gray mask. The shortsword seemed to be etched in strange runes, which even in the blink of an eye Daro recognized as some sort of anti-arcane magic enchantment. *Great. Someone did their homework.*

But the one thing that struck Daro as odd was this mercenary’s cloak. Some sort of symbol on the inside of it… he couldn’t tell exactly what it was, but the symbol itself shimmered in and out of view.

“Alright, Cindero. If it’s a fight you want…” the mercenary flipped his blades. “It’s a fight you’re gonna get.”

Daro sighed haggardly, remaining silent in response. He was too drained to deal with this – the quicker this all ended, the better. He brought his blade up to eye-level, wincing as his other shoulder still had an arrowhead lodged in it.

The man lunged at Daro, feinting his shortsword before striking with his longsword. Daro blocked the shortsword, but felt his clothes tear at the longsword as he narrowly dodged it. Daro growled in frustration before bringing his blade toward the mercenary’s neck.

The fight continued for a while, with neither side relenting in the slightest. Every time the mercenary would find an opening, it was just too late, resulting in a near-miss that could have ended the battle right then and there. Daro would try to weave his spellcasting with his swordsmanship, but to no avail – if not for his wounded arm, then for his waning magical abilities. The mercenary’s shortsword proved effective as well – any arcane spell Daro would cast would be entirely nullified by a parrying magical blade.

The mercenary inched closer and closer, until finally catching Daro’s blade with both of his weapons. He twisted the blade out of Daro’s hand, tossing it to the side to leave Daro completely open before stabbing Daro with the shortsword in the gut. Daro sputtered, a stream of blood escaping his mouth involuntarily. He had lost.

The mercenary seemed out of breath, but otherwise unharmed from the duel. “Huh. Guess you put up a good fight after all,” he commented snarkily. “I’m gonna get one hell of a payday from your head.” The mercenary flipped his longsword, before bringing it to Daro’s neck. Blood began to trickle down the blade – whatever time Daro had left on Azeroth was growing atomically smaller by the second.

“Guess it doesn’t matter if you hear my name, since you’re dying anyway,” the mercenary chuckled sadistically. “My contractors call me Hunt. You can guess why.”

 *I… failed…* Daro thought to himself, struggling to piece together even the most basic of words in his head. He stared helplessly into the mercenary’s eyes, knowing in his heart that this would be his final view. He began to accept death…

But oddly, Daro couldn’t bring himself to close his eyes. Something in him was holding on. Something deep within him cried out… but it wasn’t him.

Daro began to hear voices – initially, quiet and gentle, as if serenading him into whatever place he’d find himself after life. Voices he recognized. Vendric, his mother, Ithris, Calistah… Amaraline. But the voices multiplied tenfold, their voices echoing, their volume rising, engulfing his entire mind. His vision began to grow dark… but it wasn’t the darkness of death.

 **It was a desperate darkness.**

Dozens of tiny, shadowy tendrils burst forth from Daro’s body, writhing around mindlessly. The sorcerer’s eyes darkened to a near pitch-black, the arrow head in his shoulder beginning to vibrate.

Hunt tilted his head silently in confusion before continuing with his attempt to take Daro’s life, slashing the blade against the sorcerer’s throat. The arrow stopped vibrating, clattering against the ground – mysteriously clean of any blood.

But the blade sounded as if it scraped against metal, only to reveal zero damage to Daromath as a result. “What the fuck?” Hunt said, stepping backward, astonished by Daro’s survivability.

But Daro didn’t stop there. His body began to float, hovering a few inches above the ground outstretched as the tendrils continued to grow, merging with one another and becoming sharper along their edges. Hunt looked as astounded as he was pissed off, unsure of how to deal with such an ability that he didn’t come prepared for. Daro seemed to be in a state beyond consciousness – whatever his body was doing, it was acting on its own in a fight-or-flight manner.

Hunt quickly sheathed his blades, drawing his bow again to fire at Daro – but the arrow was smacked away by a tendril. As he started to turn around to gain distance, the tendrils exponentially lengthened, with one of them ensnaring him by the foot. Hunt grunted in pain as he was dragged back toward Daro, the lacerating tendril digging into his ankle and calf.

The mercenary retrieved his shortsword again, trying to chop at the grasping shadows – it took more swings than it should have, but eventually he cut free of it. But before he could even stand on his own two feet, two more grabbed ahold of his arms, rigging him to stand above even Daro’s floating eye level.

“Let me go, damnit!” Hunt beckoned – but there was nothing behind Daro’s eyes. Not even a speck of emotion, or life.

The tendril wrapped tighter around Hunt’s arms, with two more bursting forward from Daro’s body. The free tendrils began to slash at Hunt’s body and legs, leaving them dotted head to toe in small scratches of varying sizes and lengths.

Hunt screamed in agony as at least thirty cuts and slashes were spread across his body, unable to break free. One of Daro’s tendrils swirled around, before thrusting itself into Hunt’s eye – blood spurting out onto Daro, and his tendrils, both of which seemed to not care in the slightest.

The tendril removed itself from Hunt’s now-bloodied eye socket, before performing a flicking motion in tandem with the others keeping him in place – the mercenary was flung at least twenty feet away, leaving a blood trail under the ground he covered during the distance.

The tendrils slowly began to seep back into Daro, finally disappearing as his eyes closed. His body collapsed to the ground, leaving him unconscious.

 -     -

Daro snapped awake – although in moderate pain. *My throat–!* He instinctively grabbed at his neck – he didn’t feel any blood… it was as if the blade had never made contact with him. He looked down to his shoulder, to see that the arrowhead was on the ground next to him – but the wound was still there, albeit oddly, much smaller than before. The moon above cast a different light, indicative of the hours that had passed while he was out cold. He looked back to check if Hunt was after him – there wasn’t a trace of the mercenary in sight. He sighed an unsteady breath of relief, before slowly returning to his feet.

Whatever just happened, Daro knew he didn’t survive it on his own. He would need to find Amaraline, and soon – before Hunt would make him his prey once again.

 *Sometimes*, *Daro thought, losing control isn’t the worst thing in the world.*
