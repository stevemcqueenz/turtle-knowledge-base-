# For Honor

## Post 4587 by Kazgrim (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2019-12-07T21:51:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=4587#p4587 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Kazog lowered his hood as he entered the dimly lit inn, moving towards his usual spot. He sat down and gestured towards the patron for a drink. Not soon after a cup of grog was placed in front of him, of which he soon downed with no time to spare. The glaring Barrens sun had worn down on him, and it had been a few days since his journey that he had something to drink. There had been some hiccups in his travels that he had not anticipated, which Kazog could now ponder upon and bury.

Thunder clashed as clouds gathered far over the horizon. A storm was brewing, and so Kazog sought shelter from the lightning. The amber landscape soon became grey with shadow as the tempest grew near, the thunderbolts roaring like the stampede of a kodo herd. As Kazog searched for a place to hide, he saw ahead of him an abandoned orc burrow. He entered the wreckage that was once someone's home, crouching in the corner where the ceiling had not caved in. It was dark, and debris laid everywhere. It was wet too, but Kazog could not see what from. As he sat there contemplating the journey ahead, he reached for his water flask, prepared to take a sip. As his lips touched the flask he heard a soft voice that began to fill the tiny room. Startled, Kazog stood to his feet, grasping at the hilt of his axe as he searched for where the whispers came from. He took a step forward as the voice grew nearer, and looked down at a pile of dust and rubble. Between the void space of that debris he saw a hand reaching out to him, its tiny fingers clawing at air. Eyes stared back at Kazog, shining beneath the shadows. Kazog hesitated before throwing away the rubble ontop of the child, clearing the debris that had somehow not crushed the infant. He stood there, looking down in disbelief. The child playfully snorted and huffed air, looking up at Kazog with bewildered eyes. It gestured towards Kazog's flask, which Kazog gripped against his chest. Kazog knelt down and lowered the flask to the child's snout, watching in apprehension as the child thirstly consumed all that was left.

Kazog sighed. He knew that the child would not remember him, or what he had done. He knew that it would one day grow from a child into a warrior, and become his enemy. Kazog knew what he had to do.

Kazog left the burrow, leaving behind his hesitations and second thoughts. He turned from the direction he had headed and now treaded in his own footsteps. The soft murmurs of a child carried on his back was all that needed to remind him of where he was now headed. In the distance great thorns rose from the plains, enveloping a place in shadow darker than those tempest born.

Kazog crouched hidden in tall blades of grass, waiting for the patrol to turn its back. As the guardsmen disappeared from view, Kazog left the cover of the savannah and approached the campfire. Night had fallen, and the tribe stirred silently in their sleep. Kazog reached over his shoulder to gently place the child from his back onto the ground by the fire. He wrapped the blanket tightly over the infant, saying his goodbyes. He stared down at the child, looking into its eyes, hoping it would one day remember. He then darted back towards the safety of the grass, not before turning to look for one last time. As he hesitated, the piercing sound of an arrow cut through the air and plunged into Kazog's shoulder. Kazog turned from the force of the blow, blood dripping down his arm. He sprinted towards the direction he had came, away from the sound of shouts and snarls that now stalked him in the night.

Kazog placed a hand on his shoulder, but soon dropped it to the table. There was nothing to be said, no words to explain. He took another swig of grog and fell asleep, waking up the next day only to have forgotten.
