**Chapter I: The Legacy of Ash**

In the final years of the golden age, Varent lived a life of quiet privilege within the bustling trade hub of Andorhal. As the fourteen-year-old son of a high-ranking officer, he was a youth caught between two worlds: his nights were spent by the fireplace, studying ancient maps and strategy, while his days were consumed by the first rigorous drills with a practice blade. His father, a Knight-Commander of the Silver Hand who bore the honored name "of Andorhal," saw to it that his only heir possessed both a scholar’s mind and the budding strength of a soldier. From the high windows of their estate, Varent watched the rhythmic glow of the city's forges, believing he had a lifetime to master the iron he would one day command.
The first whispers of unease arrived with Prince Arthas and Jaina Proudmoore. They came to Andorhal hunting the source of a mysterious ailment, and it was here that they uncovered the horrific truth hidden within the northern grain. Varent remembered the sound of their horses on the cobblestones and the sight of the young Prince’s shining armor as they purged the infected granaries. For a moment, the city believed the danger had been contained. The Prince departed in pursuit of the mastermind Kel'Thuzad, leaving the people of the Western Vale shaken but hopeful. They did not yet know that the victory they celebrated was merely the opening act of a tragedy.
As the Prince vanished into the north, the silent rot began to fester. The bustle of trade died behind boarded-up windows as the true scale of the plague became apparent. Magistrate Marduke struggled with a collapsing bureaucracy, while the wealthy Barov family saw opportunity in the rising darkness, hoarding clean grain while the poor suffered. Streets were flooded with military patrols, and Varent watched as Paladins exhausted themselves in the city squares, their Light failing against a sickness that defied divine grace. Massive pyres burned day and night on the outskirts to consume the dead, their black smoke mingling with an unnatural fog that smelled of sweet decay.
The final end arrived in a roar of bone and frost when Arthas returned, no longer a savior but a harbinger of death. As the dead breached the inner gates, Varent’s father gripped his shoulder one last time, his golden armor reflecting the flickering torchlight. There was no time for long farewells, only a desperate command: "The Light will not fail us, Varent! Stand tall and carry our flame!" Varent hesitated, his hand trembling on the hilt of the sword he was still learning to use, paralyzed by the sight of his home becoming a slaughterhouse. From his hiding place, he watched in horror as Lord Blackwood and his father made their final stand. He saw them fall under the relentless surge of the dead, and only then did he find the strength to run, fleeing into the night as the Western Vale burned.
The years that followed were a blur of dust and exhaustion. Varent wandered south, a displaced heir with nothing but the clothes on his back and the bitter memory of his hesitation. He eventually found sanctuary among the dwarves, where he spent years as a lowly apprentice to a stern blacksmith. It was there, amidst the roar of the furnaces and the scent of coal, that he learned to trade his scholar’s mind for a worker’s hands. He found a grim solace in the honesty of the anvil, realizing that his father’s legacy was not a title, but the strength to endure. He emerged from those dark years no longer a lord, but a man who understood that in this world, only what you forge with your own hands truly belongs to you.

Chapter II: The Weight of Copper

Ten years after the embers of Andorhal cooled, rumors of rich veins led Varent to the shadows of the Northshire Abbey mines. No longer a youth in silk, he arrived as a man who had traded his books for a pickaxe, moving with the heavy, deliberate pace of a laborer who knows the true cost of survival. He spent weeks in Goldshire, undertaking menial tasks for the local marshals to earn enough coin for coal and supplies. Step by step, his craft evolved; from the simple copper plates he hammered out in the heat of the town’s forge to the intricate etching of his first Runed Copper pants and gloves. Varent was reclaiming his purpose, crafting a new skin of metal to replace the heritage he had lost in the fire, proving that his hands remained steady even when the road ahead seemed endless.
At the Goldshire anvil, Varent encountered a fellow traveler just beginning his journey, ill-equipped and unprepared for the dangers of the wilds. In a moment of professional kinship, Varent forged a sturdy copper dagger for the youth, accepting only scraps of leather and patches in return. These meager materials were exactly what he needed to finish the binding of his first true weapon—a heavy two-handed copper axe. Soon after, his growing reputation brought him together with a dwarf hunter, a fellow wayfarer who carried himself with the disciplined air of a seasoned expert. They formed a grim contract to hunt the notorious gnoll Hogger, prepared to test their combined skill and the strength of their reddish metal against the beast that had terrorized the woods.
The confrontation with the gnoll proved to be an unexpected lesson in the shifting powers of Azeroth. As Varent and the dwarf prepared to engage the monster, a powerful mage suddenly intervened, wielding arcane forces with such terrifying precision that the battle was over before it truly began. Varent watched in silence, his heavy axe still unblooded, as the creature fell to spells that defied the laws of iron and sweat. The mage, draped in an aura of overwhelming might, asked for no share of the bounty and departed as quickly as he had appeared. Standing in the quiet woods, Varent realized that while his copper was solid, the world was full of mysteries that his hammer could not solve. With his new runic gear glinting in the sun, he turned his gaze toward the dusty borders of Westfall.

Chapter III: Dust and Red Masks

Before venturing into the western lands, Varent arrived at the gates of Stormwind for the first time. The sprawling stone city felt alien to a man of the north, its air filled with a dialect that sounded hurried and transactional compared to the measured, formal tones of Lordaeron. To Varent, the people of the south spoke as if they were always in a rush to sell a soul or buy a favor, their words lacking the heavy resonance he remembered from the halls of the Vale. He stood before the Cathedral of Light, watching the sun glint off its stained glass, but felt no warmth in its shadow. To him, the divine radiance his father worshipped now seemed as hollow as the city's frantic chatter—a flickering promise that had failed when the world needed it most. He realized that the brittle copper in his hand held more truth than the grandest cathedral; at least the metal did not pretend to be something it was not.
With his tools packed, Varent arrived at Sentinel Hill to find the People's Militia—desperate farmers abandoned by the Crown, forced to take up arms while the city guards remained behind their high white walls. He set up his anvil near the old mill, offering his hammer to those left alone with the dry dust. He looked at the fallow fields and wondered why the capital had fallen silent, leaving its people to face the darkness without aid. In a place where the distance between a nobleman’s feast and a peasant’s hunger was a gap that authority seemed content to ignore, he found that hope was a far more difficult thing to forge than even the most stubborn alloy.
The shadows of these lands belonged to the Defias Brotherhood. Varent remembered his father’s lessons about the Masons' Riot—how he had spoken of the senseless chaos that led to the tragic death of Queen Tiffin. His father used it as a warning: a brutal reminder of the darkness that takes root when oaths are broken and obligations are cast aside. Seeing the red masks, Varent realized these were the same men, driven to madness by that old debt. He saw no righteousness in their robbery of the dispossessed, yet he felt no pride in striking down those who had been so bitterly betrayed. In a land where contracts were worth less than the dust under his boots, Varent of Andorhal tightened his grip on his copper axe, knowing that the only thing he could truly rely on was the weight of his own weapon.
