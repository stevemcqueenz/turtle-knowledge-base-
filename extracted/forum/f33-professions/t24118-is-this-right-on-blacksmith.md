<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=169637#p169637 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

If you take a step back and look at the Runic Plate set as a whole, the pattern isn't just clear — it’s honestly a bit baffling.

Every single piece follows the same punishing logic: they’re all Bind on Pickup, they all require 300 Blacksmithing, and they only drop from high-level elites or rares tucked away in some of the most inconvenient corners of the map.

 **The "Out of the Way" Problem**

Think about where you actually have to go to get these.

 **Alcaz Island:** You’re trekking out to Dustwallow Marsh to farm Strashaz mobs for the helm and shoulders. Unless you're deep into the Scarab Lord grind, there is zero reason to be there. It’s a ghost town for a reason.

 **Hearthglen:** For the leggings, you’re diving into a level 59 rare elite inside a fortified Scarlet Crusade stronghold.

 **The World:** The boots are another elite drop in a hostile zone.

This isn't gear you "stumble upon" while leveling. This is gear you have to go out of your way to target-farm, which usually means dragging a group along to help you kill elites just so you can get a BoP recipe.

 **The Math Doesn't Add Up**

Then you look at the crafting cost. We’re talking two Arcanite Bars per piece. In any version of the game where these materials matter, that is a massive investment. And for what? A green item.

The comparison to Blackrock Depths (BRD) is where the argument for Runic Plate completely falls apart:

 **Accessibility:** Most players are running BRD anyway for pre-raid BiS or attunements. You get gear there just by playing the game.

 **Specialization:** The Runic set tries to split its budget between Fire and Nature resistance. That’s just bad design. You almost never need both at the same time. If you need Fire res, you go Dark Iron. If you need Nature, you go Ironvine. Both of those sets blow Runic Plate out of the water.

 **The Verdict**

It’s a classic case of "High Effort, Low Reward." It unlocks too late to be useful for progression, costs too much to be a "budget" option, and the stats are too diluted to be optimal for raiding.

At the end of the day, it’s not just that the set is hard to get — it’s that it has no home. It’s a relic of early design that doesn't have a clear purpose in the modern game flow.
