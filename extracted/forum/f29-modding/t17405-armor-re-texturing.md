# Armor Re-texturing

## Post 118802 by RetroGaming7373 — 2025-02-03T10:32:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=118802#p118802 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

So a few months ago I was working on re-drawing each and every armor and clothing texture by hand in gimp, converting them to PNG, then to BLP, then copying them into MPQs. I lost all my work sadly so I'm hoping someone else can take up the task. I was redrawing most things to have a more skimpy or revealing look, while not being overtly so. I also avoided anything that wasn't specified as Female since it had an effect on the male characters as well and looked seriously out of place. (some texture names will have an M or F on the end of the name. U is unisex)

Best way to handle all this is to find the MPQ that has every texture, it's usually the texture MPQ, I can't check anymore I don't have the MPQ reader now. I then converted a mass amount into PNG's with a converter, then opened them in GIMP, erased or redrew what I wanted, then converted it to a PNG again, and then back to a BLP. Keep your work organized since it's easy to mix things up. There are some settings you can adjust with the PNG to BLP convertor and I'm sorry to say I don't remember what settings were necessary, there are some forums in here that bring it up. This is a long and tedious process, I'm pretty sure I only actually did maybe 50 outfits since I was doing it casually and testing them all in game and making adjustments.

Also in the Turtle HD patch, there should be skin textures for wearing armor and for underwear, they're separate. You can do a little pixel art and draw on some details onto the bare skin of the under-armor texture for some more realistic nudity. Up to you. Although you'd have to do this for every single skin color of each race you want to retexture, which is another couple dozen textures per race. And don't screw around with the shading too much unless you're really

I might pick this back up myself sometime in the future if I ever care enough again, but for now I'm just seeing if anyone wants to try it themselves and maybe provide some guidance for those who were curious.

Summary:  >Open texture MPQ with MPQ Editor
>Individually select and copy all textures you want to work on.
>You could just copy it all but it takes more space and time. But at least you'd be able to see everything.
>Run all BLP's you just copied through a BLP->PNG converter. One can be found somewhere in the forums here.
>Edit Images in GIMP or your preferred program. It must support transparency.
>Save your altered work by converting it from GIMP to PNG.
>Convert your new PNGs to BLPs, using some settings specified in a helpful forum (I'm so sorry I don't remember)
>Make a new MPQ Archive and name it similar to other patches. ie. patch_G, make sure it is the last-most letter of all other patches so it will overwrite previous patches.
>Make sure your MPQ is formatted **EXACTLY** the same as other MPQs. All folder names leading to your new textures should be verbatim to the texture.MPQ
>Done! Put your new MPQ into the Data folder of turtle wow and test your hard work in the game!
