# Let's Discuss the Molten Core Rework & The Soul of Vanilla WoW

## Post 148757 by Zmarotrix — 2025-08-27T20:22:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=148757#p148757 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

**Hello everyone,**

I'm starting this discussion because I believe the recent changes to Molten Core touch on something deeper than just boss mechanics—they touch on the very identity of this server and its commitment to the "vanilla feel." I want to share my thoughts, not just as a raider, but as someone who deeply values the design philosophy that made this game a classic.

To be clear, my perspective is rooted in the vanilla experience. Balance should be considered from the point of view of a guild in pre-raid blues, and custom features like Dual Specialization shouldn't be a prerequisite for clearing the game's first-ever raid.

---
 **The Unseen Pillar of Vanilla: The Feeling of Progression**
Before diving into specific bosses, we need to talk about *why* Molten Core matters so much. In Vanilla, progression isn't just about loot; it's a carefully crafted journey. Molten Core is the first step. It’s designed to be simple, to teach 20-40 people how to coordinate, how to learn the basics of threat, positioning, and endurance. From there, BWL adds complexity, AQ40 adds chaos, and Naxx demands mastery.

Molten Core *should* be clearable by a dedicated group in dungeon blues. If that foundation is broken, the entire progression ladder of the game is severed at its root.

This leads to a more profound, emotional pillar of the game—the reward for that progression. I think this is the most important part of the entire discussion:

> **My Core Belief wrote:**
> One of the most defining features of Vanilla World of Warcraft is its ability to make you truly feel like you are getting stronger. Returning to these older raids you once struggled to clear just to obliterate them feels unbelievably rewarding. It's one of the primary draws of Vanilla World of Warcraft and it's incredibly important for preserving the vanilla feel of the game.

This feeling isn't just a minor feature; it's woven into the very fabric of Vanilla's identity. It's the silent reward for your effort. When we alter that fundamental journey, the change might not feel jarring in the moment, but it subtly erodes the long-term memories and experiences that draw us to this game. The view from the mountaintop is less breathtaking if the path up the first hill is a sheer cliff face.

---
 **What's Good: A Tip of the Hat**
The new wing, new bosses, and the loot they provide are fantastic additions. They successfully address gearing gaps created by Turtle's unique class and spec changes and add fresh content, which is always welcome. The problem isn't the new content, but the revision of the old.

---
 **Where The Rework Deviates From The Path**
While some bosses feel fine, several key encounters have been changed in ways that feel contrary to Molten Core’s original design philosophy.
---
 **Magmadar**

The issue here isn’t just increased difficulty, but a change in the nature of the fight. By spawning so many pools of fire over a much longer fight, the encounter becomes a slow burn of attrition. Healers run out of mana and the room fills with fire, not because of a mistake, but simply because the fight is tuned to last that long. It's a test of endurance that feels more appropriate for a later raid tier.
---
 **Garr**

While the reduced initial damage on the pull is a great quality-of-life change, the new damage reduction mechanic feels like bloat. Garr was already one of the most strategically challenging fights in the instance, requiring coordination and precise execution. Adding more layers here doesn't feel necessary and overcomplicates an already effective encounter.
---
 **Baron Geddon**

Let's call this what it is: this is the Season of Discovery mechanic. It makes an already punishing mechanic even more so without adding creative depth. Baron’s purpose was to be a simple, brutal lesson: "manage this debuff or you will wipe your raid." The new fire patches don't enhance that lesson; they just add another layer of punishment.
---
 **Golemagg**

This is, by far, the most destructive change to the raid's structure. The Core Hounds' new fixate mechanic, combined with the longer fight duration that now necessitates a tank swap on the boss, creates an absurd tanking requirement of at least 6 tanks. In a 20-person raid, this means nearly a third of your raid force is dedicated to tanking. The resulting loss of DPS is catastrophic, not just for this fight, but for the entire instance. With other bosses having increased health pools, that DPS is not a luxury—it's a necessity. This single encounter's change fundamentally breaks the raid's composition and jeopardizes the viability of the entire run. It's a requirement that belongs in Naxxramas, not Molten Core.
---
 **Majordomo Executus**

The original Majordomo encounter was far from simple; it was a masterclass in strategic complexity. It was a tightly choreographed dance requiring perfect CC assignments, precise positioning, and flawless execution from every single player. The challenge was not in complex mechanics, but in perfect coordination. Buffing health pools and adding more disruptive teleports doesn't enhance this intricate strategic puzzle. Instead, it just layers raw punishment on top of an already demanding encounter, making it needlessly harder without adding any meaningful depth. The fight's original strategic elegance has been muddied.

---
 **A Warning: The Pandora's Box of Rewriting Vanilla**
Let me be absolutely clear: this is not a post against change. New content, new quests, and new zones are what make Turtle WoW special. I applaud those efforts.

But there is a world of difference between **adding** to the world and **rewriting** its history.

The Molten Core rework is, in my opinion, the single biggest deviation from the vanilla spirit this server has ever made. This isn't "Vanilla+." Adding a new dungeon is Vanilla+. This is different. This is going back and fundamentally changing the original, core experience. It alters the foundational identity of the game we came here to play.

This sets a dangerous precedent. It opens a Pandora's Box that, once opened, is very difficult to close. It asks a very serious question about the future of this server: Are we preserving a classic experience while adding to its world, or are we beginning to create a custom re-imagining?

Both are valid paths for a server to take, but we must be honest about which one we are on, and what that means for the soul of the game.

Thank you for reading, and I genuinely want to hear what you all think.
