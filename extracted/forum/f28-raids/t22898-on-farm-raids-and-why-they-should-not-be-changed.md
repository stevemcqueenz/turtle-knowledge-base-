# On Farm Raids (and why they should not be changed)

## Post 159848 by Elesion (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2025-11-16T20:13:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=159848#p159848 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

**The Concept of Farm Raids**
There is a time period in gameplay where you tackle new challenges. You discover a new instance, you develop new strategies, you put your time and effort into the exhausting task of beating an actual challenge. That time period is called "progression" and usually lasts a few weeks.
After progression you enter the "optimization" phase, where you polish your strategies so they become solid and repeatable. Everything should be stable and reliable. This is not very fun, but quite rewarding as your clear-times improve. That can take some weeks to a few months.
And lastly you enter the final phase of the raid lifecycle - "farm". That phase is of infinite length (especially on a forever-Vanilla server like TWoW). Nothing new happens, you just repeat the same ol' week after week.

 **The Draw of Farm Raids**
If it's that rote, why do people continue to run farm raids?
1. To get rare items. Vanilla is a game of grinds. With how scarce and RNG-heavy loot is, it's routine that people wait many months to win particular items.
2. To enjoy brain-less gameplay. There's an entire genre of games where you grind repetitive tasks (something like a Stardew Valley) and people love them. And it's actually pretty sweet if an entire raid team enters a "flow" state and the raid runs itself on muscle memory without a raid leader having to give directions.
3. To sozialize. When you don't have to focus on the gameplay, you can focus on the people.
4. To bask in nostalgia. Can be general nostalgia for a 20yo game, or particular nostalgia for past achievements with your raid group.
5. To help out other people in your group.
6. To train and gear new people. Raid groups need fresh blood. Nothing better to give people their first important assignments than a raid where very little can go wrong.
 **Why it's a Bad Idea to change them**
Take note that "discovering new things", "having to learn new skills", or "being challenged with hard encounters" is not among the draws of a farm raid. Because that's what progression is about. You don't sign up to MC to be surprised. You sign up for MC because it's a breezy optimized clear and you want to enjoy the company of people and help the guild.
Farm raids are not grand adventures where people are sad because of the boredom after a while. Farm raids are entries on a weekly schedule, something that "has to be done" to keep the raider pipeline in your guild going. Geared and experienced people largely join to help out. They've long concluded their progression phase, long filled their need for items, and moved their focus onto higher tiers.

So if you make the experience unpleasant - by overtuned requirements, by needlessly introducing new mechanics, by drastically altering established routing - there might be a few people who enjoy the novelty. But by and large you spread misery to and steal time from the thousands of players who run those instances as farm raids. Their weekly schedule just got longer for no appreciable reward. Raids are less approachable for weaker or newer players and cannot serve as training grounds anymore. The established familiarity is disrupted. The nostalgia is disturbed. The years of muscle memory and flow state are broken.
And suddenly the former farm raid has now become a chore. All the effort of progression and optimization with no pay-off, because you've been through it already. And people who previously joined to help out will think twice about turning up because of the annoyance and additional time commitment. And guess what happens when the geared veterans lose interest? The raid becomes even slower and even more annoying to run.

Yes, MC and BWL are stale. But that's their point at this time in the server's lifecycle. They're farm raids. They're somewhere between nostalgic comfort food and weekly basic routine to keep up the pipeline from fresh 60 into the endgame. They don't need an overhaul. You can tune their numbers carefully (to compensate for class change powercreep), but please don't add or remove bosses or mechanics.
Direct your effort towards new content. TWoW is Vanilla+. Keep the Vanilla content unchanged, add the + on top. I enjoy the new content and I think a smaller MC spin-off raid would have been a lot more well-received than what has been done to beloved Molten Core with 1.18 (and what might be done to BWL and Onyxia with the next patch).

Related post from someone else: [Let's Discuss the Molten Core Rework & The Soul of Vanilla WoW](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=158970) - Molten Core was iconic and it's supposed to be easy.

The problem is not the difficulty. I even called for slight numbers adjustments to compensate for powercreep myself.
The problem is that it was changed without need, making it longer and more annoying when the vast majority of players was not looking for MC to become longer or more annoying. The fact that it was changed at all should require justification on a Vanilla-based server, regardless of the tuning question. And I don't see a good reason for it when they could have made Old Shadowforge a separate raid while leaving MC untouched.

The role that MC fills on the weekly raid schedule is not to be "slightly less boring". It's to be fully boring while you breeze through it for the third year without having to explain fight mechanics because you have it on muscle-memory-tier farm.
