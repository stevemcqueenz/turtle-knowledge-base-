# This is What Druids really need

## Post 164802 by Wilsonsds (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2026-01-10T02:17:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=164802#p164802 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

I’ve been thinking about a way to give druids a clearer sense of visual progression without any heavy changes to the game, and without trying to match your form to every single gear piece. The idea is to make Bear and Cat forms feel like they “grow up” with your character in a few clean steps, kind of like Diablo, where hitting certain milestones gives you that instant “I’m stronger now” feeling.

The concept would be **purely cosmetic** and tied to broad stat thresholds. Bear form would evolve based on **Armor**, since that’s the clearest indicator of tank progression and fits the fantasy of becoming tougher and heavier over time. Cat form would evolve based on **Attack Power**, since it directly represents feral damage progression and fits the class fantasy naturally. The important part is that this wouldn’t change constantly—only when you reach meaningful milestones.

Visually, it doesn’t need a brand-new model every time. It could be **4–5 tiers max**, with changes that are subtle but instantly readable. For Bear, think a slightly bulkier silhouette, thicker fur, a more pronounced mane, maybe a few scars or druidic markings, and small primitive details like charms or runes that still feel “Vanilla” instead of flashy. For Cat, same idea: over time it looks more seasoned and more dangerous, but it’s still clearly a cat form, still grounded, and still in line with Turtle’s vibe.

On the technical side, this can be kept straightforward. When you shapeshift, the server checks your effective **Armor in Bear** or your effective **Attack Power in Cat**, then picks the cosmetic tier for that form. Because the tiers are broad, it won’t flip back and forth from small gear swaps. And to avoid forcing anyone into it, it should be **optional** via a simple toggle (NPC option, glyph-style toggle, or setting), so players who prefer the classic look can keep it permanently.

I think this fits Turtle’s direction because it’s a **flavor win with zero balance impact**. It gives druids a visible “I’ve advanced” moment that isn’t just numbers, without dragging the game toward a fashion system. Turtle already experiments with form cosmetics, so this would feel like a natural extension rather than something out of place.

The main concerns also feel manageable. If some players don’t want extra readability in PvP, keep it opt-in or easy to disable. If breakpoints are a worry, keep the number of tiers low and the thresholds wide. And if anyone’s afraid it could look too flashy, that’s just art direction—keep it primal and druidic, more “seasoned beast” than “glowing armor cat.”

If people like the idea, it could expand to other forms later, but even just Bear and Cat would already add a lot of identity and progression for druids.
