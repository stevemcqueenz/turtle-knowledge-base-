Depends on what you find fun to play.  I'm fairly new to Turtle WoW with no level 60's yet, but here is my groove as a casual player that plays for fun/versatility more than anything else.

Prior to the big change patch this last month I recently leveled my NE Druid up to 51 via Balance spec (after purchasing the donation store Glyph to change Moonkin form into Astral form instead, since I detest Moonkin form).

After the patch, I decided to continue solo/questing as a "Moonglow" (non-Moonkin use) hybrid Balance/Resto spec instead, since the new talent layout makes it more viable & appealing, & going in & out of Moonkin form to heal/top-off while solo is super annoying & clunky.  I still have 1 point for Moonkin, for use in dungeons/grouping.

My basic grind strategy is to usually pull with 2-3 Wrath casts, Moonfire, Insect Swarm & then melee it (the Druid version of "wanding") while the DOTs finish it off.  A yellow mob dies right before our updated 18 sec DOTs finish their duration (all green gear, no significant +damage gear).  I prioritize Spirit stat so I have no downtime or need to drink.  The Genesis talent in Resto has great synergy for a Moonglow build since it works with both DOTs & HOTs.  A random Rejuv here & there keeps me topped off as I move about.

My spec works for me & I have fun with it solo/questing.  I am also be able to DPS or heal dungeons interchangeably just fine.  I've been healing in WoW with various hybrid builds for Druids & Priests since the original Vanilla WoW days decades ago (leveling & endgame), & that was with the sad, somewhat-broken original-vanilla talent trees.

I don't see balance druid being good at AOE like mage or paladin. You simply lack the right spells for it. Currently leveling a druid and switching back and forth between balance and feral. Overall feral still seems a bit stronger at my level (40+) but balance is still viable.

The best balance spec for leveling is pretty much going to fill out the entire balance tree minus:
- gale winds (hurricane slows attack speed)

- improved nature's grasp (just the 1 in initial point is enough)

- only 2/3 into natural shape shifter
This leave you with 5 points which could well go into Furor from Resto tree. The instant rage upon entering bear from is very useful for emergency stuns/charges.

Going deeper into restoration tree seems like a trap, you'd be investing way too many points before you get to the good stuff (genesis for increased damage over time). Sylvan blessing is amazing, when chain killing enemies it has near 100% up time. Don't know why you want to skip that. So you don't really benefit much from meditation (15% mana regen in combat) either.

This is how I would allocate points for the first 40 levels:
![Image](https://i.imgur.com/KaW3sjb.png)

My rotation is usually:
- (for tough enemies:) open with wrath from max range and before the projectile hits another wrath or starfire

- insect swarm, move backward during global cool down

- moonfire, move backwards during global cool down

- Starfire while enemy runs toward you

- for tough foes or quick hitting enemies like cats: entangling roots to prevent spell push back, move back a little

- another star fire

- finish off the enemy with wrath and punching; remember owl punches can also trigger omen of clarity
For mana efficiency the enemy ideally dies when your dots run out. Remember to synchronize your spells with the enemy attack speed for less chance of spell push back.

I almost never have to drink, instead I go into cat form a bit when I'm low on mana. It helps if you have an add-on for quickly equipping a feral gear set while your in it. Thanks to omen of clarity you get a lot of free abilities in (best used on rip as it costs the most energy). I also level up 1st aid anyways, using bandages conserves mana and let's me stay in form.
