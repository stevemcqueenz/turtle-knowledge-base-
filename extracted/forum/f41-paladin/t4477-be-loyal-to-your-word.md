Except the OP is right in the fact that in the lore, before Blizzard had to "twist the lore enough", Wildhammer Dwarves had shamans.  In Warcraft 2.  The Gryphon Riders were 100% shaman.  Not allowing Dwarf Shamans is itself twisting the lore to justify an artificial restriction.

As to the OP, I'd say lorewise before it was "twisted" like Thol posits, Trolls would be the best since we know Loa are personification of the different spheres of magic.  Worshiping the idea of "The Light" isn't any different than worshiping the Loa/God of "The Light".

Then Tauren I guess because they do worship a sun god in An'she, and while calling them sun druids was pretty dumb, a "warpriest" springing from the likes of the Spiritwalkers or other religious sects among the Tauren isn't quite the stretch some people want to make it out to be.

Orcs would def require some work in justifying, though we do know roughly a generation of them were raised in internment camps amongst the humans and adopting the religion of one's oppressors has a historical basis in our own world which can give credence to the idea.

As for Forsaken, the whole point of their lore is that they turned away from the Light, IE they are the Forsaken.  I know this seems to be a fan favorite put forward for the idea, but this one really does require the most "twisting" of the lore and mental gymnastics to justify.  In Vanilla/Classic there is only one undead NPC capable of wielding the light, Sir Zeliek, and his wiki intro sums up his unique position pretty well: *"Sir Zeliek was one of the Four Horsemen of Naxxramas. He was a paladin in life, so strong in his faith, that even in undeath the power of the Light still heeded his call, smiting his foes in battle.[1] Though his body was forced to obey Kel'Thuzad's commands, Sir Zeliek managed to retain his sanity and morals despite his corruption. This makes him a tragic character, forced to slay what he once held dear in his heart against his will."*  That doesn't lend well to the lore of The Forsaken turning their back on The Light which forsook them, as the major theme for them is that all their actions are directly the result of them REGAINING their Will.  The Will of the Forsaken.

Defininitely Undeads, because of the following:

 4) According to the lore, the Forsaken can use the holy light, it just causes physical pain and anguish to them.
 5) The Forsaken can be Warriors and Holy Priests, so it makes sense to continue to be Paladins, even in undeath

Tauren paladin was activisions nonsense retcon, and trolls don't even need explanation why they shouldn't be paladins. The Forsaken on the other hand are, as mentioned earlier, by far the best candidate for a horde paladin.
