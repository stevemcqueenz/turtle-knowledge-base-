# Suggestion: Seals & Blessings Are Too Easily Dispelled (Need a Talent-Based Solution)

## Post 161159 by Maxe71 — 2025-12-01T08:48:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=161159#p161159 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

Hi fellow Paladins and Turtle WoW team,

I’d like to bring up an issue that many Retribution and Holy Paladins feel very strongly about, especially in PvP:
our Seals and Blessings are extremely easy to dispel, and this leaves the class unable to function during combat.
Paladins are heavily dependent on both Blessings and Seals to do anything — pressure, utility, mobility, damage, healing flow, you name it.

But right now:
One single dispel can remove Blessing of Freedom, Blessing of Protection, or Blessing of Sacrifice instantly.
Seals are removed just as easily, shutting down core abilities like Judgement, burst windows, or defensive play.
Classes with strong dispel tools can lock a Paladin out of almost everything with very little effort.
Because of this, Paladins often end up in situations where they simply cannot play their class, despite using their tools correctly.

Proposed Solution: A Talent That Protects Seals & Blessings
My suggestion is to introduce a new talent or position in one of the Paladin trees that:
Makes the Paladin’s own Seals and Blessings immune to dispel,
OR
Gives them a very high chance to resist dispels (e.g., 50–75%),
OR
Applies a buff that temporarily prevents dispel effects after casting a Seal or Blessing.
This would give Paladins a fair chance to actually use their toolkit without being shut down completely by a single dispel spam.
It wouldn’t make us overpowered — it would simply let us function.

Final Thoughts

Just my two cents to improve class balance and gameplay feel.
I love the server and appreciate all the work the creators have done.
Thanks for reading, and I’d be glad to hear the thoughts of other Paladins and the dev team.

I understand the point about dispels being the counter to buffs, just like kicks counter casts.
But there’s an important difference here:

A Paladin without seals or blessings doesn’t just get “countered”—he loses his entire kit.
Other classes don’t become “unplayable” when you remove a single component of their rotation.

A Priest doesn’t just stand still spamming dispel—he applies DoTs, Mana Burn, SW:D pressure, so while he dispels my seal, I’m also rotting at the same time.

Same with Shamans: even if purge now has a CD, they still keep massive burst windows (WF + shocks + CL) that a Paladin has no tools to smooth out, except bubble—which is purely defensive, and cuts our offense in half while it’s active.

My point was never that “counters shouldn’t exist,” but that no other class becomes completely neutered by a dispel the way Paladins do. Something that gives partial resistance, or reduced dispel success, or a talent that stabilizes at least one core seal, would prevent the class from collapsing the moment a dispeller is present.

Just my 2 cents, fellow Paladins—and devs.

I see your point, and I agree that dispellers should counter buff-based classes — but the situation for Paladins is not exactly equal when compared to other classes being unbuffed.

A Priest doesn’t just sit there spamming Dispel: while he removes your Seal, he also loads you with DoTs, so you’re forced to spend your GCDs refreshing Seals and cleansing, while he’s still dealing damage in the background.
Same for Shamans: yes, Purge now has a CD, but their burst window is extremely high and the Paladin has no real way to stabilize it except Bubble — which is a purely defensive trade and completely shuts down your own offense.

Other classes unbuffed don’t lose their entire kit or identity. A Warrior without Battle Shout is still a Warrior. A Rogue without Slice and Dice is still fully lethal. A Mage without Arcane Intellect still casts.

A Paladin without his Seal literally stops functioning, because both pressure and utility are tied to buffs that can be removed instantly.

I’m not asking for uncounterable Blessings or Freedoms — those should absolutely remain dispellable.
But Seals getting removed so easily creates a situation where the Paladin loses access to his core rotation entirely.

A talent that gives partial Seal resistance (not immunity) or a chance to resist dispels could help keep the class functional without removing its intended counters.

Just my condensed thoughts — respect for your points, fellow paladin.

I understand the counterplay argument, but there are a few practical issues worth noting from the Retribution Paladin perspective.

First, refreshing seals while also dispelling DoTs, slows, poisons or magic debuffs quickly becomes unsustainable, especially considering that a Ret paladin’s mana pool is much smaller than most other mana users. Trading GCDs with a priest isn’t equal when the priest can also pressure you with DoTs while you burn mana simply trying to stay operational.

Second, if you try to actually play the class as intended — applying a seal, using Judgment for its effect, and re-applying the seal — against constant dispel spam this becomes almost impossible. On top of that, with a 2H swing timer of 3.5+ seconds, losing even one window because a seal was stripped at the wrong moment can literally delete your entire damage cycle.

I don’t think this is about removing counters or giving paladins “no downsides”, but about acknowledging that when almost many classes can strip seals instantly while still dealing full pressure, Ret ends up spending most of the fight not dealing damage, but trying to function.

Just my perspective as someone actually playing it in PvP.
