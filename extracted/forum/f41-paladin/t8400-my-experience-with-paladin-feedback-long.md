The goal of this feedback post is to do a detailed breakdown of where I see Paladin currently within the sandbox of TWoW, - primarily Retribution as that is what I played the most here - do a detailed breakdown of the mechanics that make and break it, and share my suggestion as to where it could be improved without sacrificing it's intended design. I will attempt to the best of my ability to present objective reality and accurate information and draw an unbiased conclusion, however, I will make readers aware when I am delving into the subjective territory of my feedback to avoid confusion. With all that being said, I don't expect the entirety of the post to be read (or at all), as it is a massive behemoth and I don't include a tl;dr simply because there is so much to talk about and I do not wish information to be compressed to the point of it possibly losing it's value (however, I have tried my best to be conscise).

To give some context about myself: I've been playing WoW non-stop since 2008 in it's many forms, and have always mained Paladin. I've always played every spec and sub-spec, have always done my best to push it to the limit and I primarily come from a background of endgame raiding and PvP (although I don't do very much PvP on Vanilla and TWoW by proxy, as I find that it aged poorly). Here on TWoW, I've been playing non stop for the last 4 months on this character at level60 (given I leveled my current toon last Autumn), primarily playing Retribution and its three variations (Spelladin, Hybrid and Full AP), although currently I am playing Hybrid. I've also put a fair amount of time into Holy and Protection. Currently, I'm raiding BWL with my guild, and I'm close to finishing up my BiS pre AQ40 - only needing UTB, Scroll and the cloak from the Silithus Abyssal Council bosses. I've also gone out of my way to pick up my D1 set.

My feedback here is purely based on endgame raiding, with only occassionally reaching into leveling and PvP content. I will skip a larger introduction to Paladin, as most of us are already well aware of the role of the class, and instead will jump into the nitty-gritty of TWoW. I will also be using a lot of abbreviations as the post goes on to make writing this easier.

 **1. How Paladin changed from Vanilla to TWoW**

Fundamentally speaking, Paladin remains mostly the same, but it has seen significant combat improvements. First of all, it is important to note for TWoW that Paladins and Shamans can play together, which means both sides get access to some new buffs, primarily Windfury. World Buffs have also been banned from raids, and there are a bunch of itemization changes and new items to be seen.

The core combat gameplay still heavily relies on auto attacks, which Holy Strike doubles down on, but we also see the issues with itemization being made up for with Crusader Strike adding significant amounts of spell power as a debuff to the enemy. Now, while on the TWoW Class pages, it is claimed that CS can't proc damage based procs, however, they absolutely can at the moment. Whether or not this is a bug, this makes the base Paladin combat rotation much more involved, as we have access to Windfury Totem, and a core mechanic of a Windfury proc is that it resets the swing timer, creating a spicier rotation where you want to time your CS-es and Judgments just after you swing with your weapon.

 **And as a small sidenote, I would argue that this should not be fixed as it makes the base gameplay much more involved.** However, you also have to account for the lack of information that the Windfury spells provide, as it doesn't state anywhere that it resets your swing timer, which it really should. While spamming CS in hopes of getting WF procs is still a DPS gain, it is a larger DPS gain to time a CS right after an auto attack, as if your swing timer isn't ready when you proc WF, you don't actually do an auto, you just do your WF hits.

Some SP coefficient buffs mean that sustain damage has been slightly increased (Consecration, Seal of Righteousness), and SP geared Rets become a significant threat boon with Retribution Aura for some Tanks.

Seal of the Crusader has been changed up more than half a year ago. The base Seal is still not strong enough to be our main seal (mostly because SoR's damage proc is just stupid strong mechanically), but the Judgment effect received a confusing at best rework, making the new Judge effect have a 30 times higher chance to proc weapon effects and enchants. This Judgment effect in fact has proven to be so disrupting and broken that certain weapons have received special conditions to either not or barely benefit from the new Judgment. Which makes me ask, what is the point of this Judgment effect? Information regarding this spell is highly obscured, one could easily think "Oh I'll just go and craft a Dark Iron Pulverizer and dunk on people in WSG" then it turns out that this weapon has received a specific nerf because of the JotC. Myself, I personally have ran 86 runs of UBRS in hopes of getting the Blackblade of Shahram, which, if my maths line up, would be about an infinitely stronger weapon than Might of Menethil with this new Judgment as it lets you swim in +50 stacking stat buffs and 30% haste procs. And I don't even know if it would work or not, because no one knows if it works with JotC.

New items with on hit damage effects added to TWoW on top of the fact that SoR double procs those effects and Windfury existing has made Judgment of Wisdom generate infinite mana, completely eliminating mana issues for both Prot and Ret. Even Holy Paladins on a lot of bossfights could go in and swing their weapons if they are willing to sacrifice some healing to engage in a riskier playstyle that gives them infinite mana without having to specifically build for spellcrit. **Personally speaking, the combination of having access to near infinite mana and Windfury is what makes Paladin combat truly enjoyable on TWoW, however, it also presents some problems that I will delve into a bit later.**

Perhaps most significantly, and something that can easily slip by people's attention, Holy Shock, aside from benefitting from a healing and damage buff (on top of Healing Light being extended to both portion of Holy Shock) has had it's cooldown lowered from 30 seconds to 20 seconds. This makes deep Holy Spelladin builds much more appealing, and offers some meaningful gameplay variety, especially going into Naxx where you can get extra benefit on the 5% spellcrit of Holy Power.

Precision having been extended to also cover Spellhit% has also made it so that Paladins are no longer at the risk of innate holy resistance removing all of their damage on some of their spellcasts, and perhaps by accident has also helped to create the strongest taunt in the game. With Improved Seal of Justice, the Judgment effect now acts as a Taunt on top of it's base mechanic, and because of Precision 3/3, and given that most Tank builds are hitcapped, this taunt is impossible to miss or resist (note that Judgment isn't subject to parry or dodge). This power comes at a cost though - many people argue that the taunt competing for Judgment is bad design, but I actually don't think so. You should always let some other Tank generate better threat, which you can then make up for with this absolutely overpowered Taunt. And if there is no threat, either on pull or a threat wipe, you JoR anyways to establish threat, which, remember, is also a spell that always lands. What I think conflicts with SoJ however is the fact that Crusader Strike exists, which can GCD lock a Tankadin, causing a delay in taunting that other classes don't have. Warrior and Bear taunts are OGCD (excluding Mocking Blow, but the strenght of this spell is that Warriors have 2 taunts), and so is Judgment, but SoJ is not, which can delay your taunts especially if someone suddenly critted a Shadow Bolt and ripped threat. The other small annoying side of it is that if you judged Wisdom beforehand, Justice will remove the Wisdom effect, making Pally tanking without a Ret in tow (especially one who has the CS libram and PvP gloves) a bit awful.

To go off of that, a recent fix has also made it so that rage/energy/mana procs generate threat, apparently as intended. This opens up a lot of threat generation for Prot Paladin - currently working on a Hand of Edward set with 2/3 incendosaur, and I am already generating miles more threat than my guild's Paladin tank. Having to judge Justice as the sole melee Paladin can be quite damaging to sustained threat generation, assuming you are supposed to keep threat for a long period of time after taunting.

While there have also been other various talent reworks, the main takeaways are that SoC can no longer be used together with Holy Shock in PvP (a good change), Prot had Redoubt and Shield Spec significantly improved, and you don't have to go down till the last row to get Holy Shield, another giant buff as this allows for more threat focused talent builds. Ardent Defender unfortunately is more of a leveling / AoE farming talent than a raiding talent, as it's rare to benefit from the mitigation component when you are more than liable to be oneshot when you are below 35% MT-ing a boss. However, keeping in mind the much better itemization on TWoW, Prot is finally a good tanking option in raids, which is always a good thing.

In fact, all three roles of Paladin are good, however, Prot and Ret don't come without their quirks. While I have delved a little bit into it, I'd like to dedicate a new section to some of the design issues I've encountered.

 **2. Obscured information**

There are a lot of different mechanics in this game that are simply never communicated to players, even from level1 onwards - and I am going to list them all.

The game doesn't tell you what your main stats translate to. STR, AGI, STAM, INT and SPI are never precisely communicated to the player based on the class you play.

The game never explains the hit table, melee and spellhit, weaponskills, parry hasting and the importance of standing behind a mob when hitting it.

The game never explains the swing timer, spell pushback and what causes the swing timer to reset.

 **The game never explains threat generation, nor is there a natural way to track it.**

Edit: The game never explains how Rage generation is calculated, the energy tickrate and mana regen mechanics.

The game never explains that Seal of Righteousness can trigger all damage based procs.

The game never explains that you can use on-hit items to generate extra procs of Judgment of Wisdom. In fact, it also doesn't explain why Judgment of Wisdom isn't PPM based but Judgment of Light is, and also why on-hit items only really work with JoW but not JoL.

The game never explains Windfury's mechanics.

The game never explains which spells of Paladin are based on spellhit, and which are based on weapon skills and melee hit. In fact, as a sidenote, since the last patch on 07/18, HoW seems to have shifted to spellhit% table, causing roughly 75% of all HoWs to miss. On that tangent, it is also never explained which spells benefit from spellcrit% and which benefit from meleecrit%.

The game never explains the buff and debuff cap.

The game never explains which buffs and consumables stack with each other, and which don't.

The game never explains which weapon procs work with JotC and which don't.

The game never explains which weapon procs scale with spell power, and which don't.

Sometimes, the game even lies to you, telling you that items like Ragehammer and Sword of Zeal raise your raw damage dealt by some amount, only for it to not actually increase anything (because it actually raises attack power by 20/10, barely a DPS gain).

The issue is that knowing all these small details is what is going to truly improve a person's gameplay. A lot of this is the fault of Blizzard of course, as they are the ones that made the game, but I would argue that TWoW should have made an effort to improve on explaining the base mechanics of the game. In Cataclysm, they actually had a really good solution for the unintentional lack of information - they developed a new option that allowed you to have simplified or advanced tooltips. Basically, your Crusader Strike would either say "Strike with your weapon and generate a Holy Power." or "Deal 135% physical weapon damage (with the tooltip updating based on your talents and such) to your opponent and generate Holy Power if the attack is successful". It is also important to note here that on your character sheet on Cataclysm, it was easy to tell that you only needed 5% hit to always connect against a level 85 target. I would suggest that this should be worked on over time - there are Warriors that don't even know that if they use Heroic Strike or Cleave while dual wielding, their main hand weapon only needs 9% hit and not 24%, which is what makes those spells so strong, not their raw damage dealt.

 **3. Retribution is unplayable**

Unfortunately, pushing Ret to it's limits also comes with a massive downside. Once you figure out a build that works for you, get your on hit items, windfury, consumes, all the buffs and such, you are going to see something horrifying. This issue has been present for a long time, which was then skyrocketed by the new change on 07/11 that caused mana procs to generate threat. **Ret Paladin is threat capped.**

From a first glance, the core suspicion would be around Windfury procs with a strong 2 hander. And yes, it does generate significant threat and damage, however, it doesn't account for what pushes Ret over the top.

First, Judgment of Wisdom. As it procs a lot due to SoR and on hits, especially as Spelladin with faster weapons (and also having access to the on-hit offhand Lantern from Survival), sustained threat generation has skyrocketed significantly, when you can generate anywhere between 300 to 2.5k mana per auto attack. While this significantly helps Prot Paladins in threat gen, it also significantly hurts Ret Paladin.

Second, and while I can't confirm this as extracting information from Vanilla is borderline impossible, but I've always had a massive suspicion - I'm convinced that Holy Strike is generating threat twice. It just simply generates absurd amounts of threat, even when it doesn't crit. What I'm guessing is happening is that the game thinks you swung twice - once for Holy Strike, and once as just a normal auto attack. Or maybe Holy Strike isn't subject to Blessing of Salvation and other threat reduction effects. I don't know because I can't tell because the Vanilla client is terrible.

The point is, Ret both generates too much sustain threat and has insane threat spikes. It has to be one or the other, it can't be both. If you generate too much sustain threat, you can adjust your build to generate less mana and focus more on regenning mana through consumes, Innervates, Shadow Priests or Arcane Mages. If threat is too spiky, you can adjust your rotation so that you don't CS after every auto, but instead use Consecration or help with cleansing and such, or just hold back a little bit. **I would argue what makes Ret extremely fun in raids is both the absurd mana economy and Windfury, and I'd like to see that element retained in some way.** I could get rid of all my on hits and massively reduce my mana economy, but it just feels shallow.

However, at the moment, I can rip threat off of Naxx geared Bears, if I get (un)lucky enough. Testing post JoW change, I generated 16k threat in 2 swings on the lvl63 target dummy. And while that was an insane RNG chain, it kind of proves the point. Perhaps a better example would be raiding MC with my guild post-change, and while on Golemagg, I suddenly spiked 7k threat, overtaking our otherwise good Fury Prot. After bubbling to drop threat while Golemagg was 50% hp, and AFKing for 15 more seconds afterwards, I started attacking again. By the time the boss was 6% HP, I overtook threat again. **This shouldn't happen, ever.** Oh, and I wasn't flasked for this attempt, in fact, I didn't even use any consumable other than 2 GFPP-s.

 **4. When tanking, threat is the name of the game**

First of all, Prot has been massively improved in the areas it needed improving. Mitigation is much better with Redoubt and Shield Spec, mana economy is much better due to Shield Spec and JoW on hits, spike threat gen is significantly better with unmissable Judgments and Holy Strike, and sustain threat gen has been greatly improved with CS-es spellpower debuff and how frequently you can block now due to Redoubt - and of course, since the mana proc change, this aspect improved again. And of course, we have a Taunt. While not specifically tied to the class, Tea with Sugar is a fantastic consumable that especially Prots gain a massive benefit from. However, there are a couple things that stand out to me as large issues.

Despite making up a significant portion of your threat, Holy Strike is subject to insane variance with dodging and parrying. It is extremely punishing on pulls to have your HS parried, because establishing long term threat depends quite a bit on Holy Strike. While the mana proc change helped to mitigate this, and the fact that you can also tank with Hand of Edward giving you free Holy Light procs to generate threat with (which also scales with Righteous Fury, because remember, it's a Holy spell), Prot builds that put a larger emphasis on mitigation, which inevitably will happen on some bossfights will lack behind on threat much more so than they should.

It is also worth to note just what differentiates a mitigation vs threat build when it comes to Prot, because for the most part, it's mostly based on your itemization. The thing is, talenting anything below Holy Shield in Prot is a bit of a meme. One-Handed Weapon Spec is still 5 points for barely any gain, Reckoning is a PvP talent and Ardent Defender is terribly useless when main tanking due to the mitigation effect being worthless when you're that low HP - which basically means that for the most part, you can only really differentiate mitigation builds from threat ones is if the Paladin in question talented 5/5 in Toughness and Anticipation, because after you got Holy Shield, most Paladins just simply go down Ret to pick up threat and efficiency tools, or Holy if they have a Hand of Edward. Ultimately it will come down to itemization moreso than anything, which isn't a bad thing in itself, but it does mean mitigation builds don't really have a greater identity.

 **5. Holy is as awesome as ever**

Honestly, not much to say here. I really have no feedback on Holy other than it just simply continuing to be an amazing and fun healer. Perhaps the only thing that might be worth looking into is that due to the lack of World Buffs, early Holy Light builds are not as viable due to lacking ~20 spellcrit - 3% from Savvy, 10% from Dragonslayer, 5% from Songflower, and the remaining 1-2% from main stat buffs that scale your intellect. I've been finding myself needing on more DPS gear than usual because it has spellcrit%.

 **6. Change without sacrifice**

So, with all that being said, how would I go about making Prot and Ret better? First of all, it is important to establish a couple things.
 1. Changes have to adhere to the core gameplay and style of Paladin.
 2. Changes that take away have to replace with something that is at the very least equivalent to what was before, but preferably better.
 3. Changes have to create interesting and nuanced gameplay.
 4. Changes have to be efficient when it comes to managing game development resources.
 5. Changes have to be easy to pick up and understand.
 6. Changes have to have long term value.
 7. Changes have to maintain balance across the wider game.

The very first change I'd make is to revert the change on mana/energy/rage procs generating threat. This just creates excess unwanted threat for DPS without Tanks being able to keep up, and looking into Holy Strike's threat calculation to make sure that it is actually generating 100% and not 200% threat (again, I might be on copium for this one, but I long suspected this to be a possible issue).

Now, we get to how Prots can generate better threat despite Holy Strike's variance, and I think that Seal of the Crusader is the perfect tool for this. Change the Haste to be replaced with making your auto attacks (and possibly Crusader Strikes, if SoR would continue generating more overall threat) deal 100% holy damage. This would play into Righteous Fury significantly, but would also create some degree of choice when it comes to JoW, as SoR of course can proc JoW and also double procs on hit items. You can either choose to dish out max rank spells with a Hand of Edward to do Holy Lights with SoR, or you can choose to play it safe and be able to build mitigation without losing significant chunks of threat. Deep Prot could also see some improvements - One Handed Weapon Spec should be a 2-3 pointer talent at most and give the same benefit, but also extend it to Crusader Strike damage. Excess talent points can then be funneled to Toughness and Anticipation to create a true mitigation build.

Ardent Defender can go in some directions.
 1: Extend it to Wrath's variation that gives a small cheat death effect, which would make the passive much more viable when main tanking raids - although I would argue that cheat deaths are extremely lame.
 2: Make it so that that damage that reduces you below the HP treshold is also mitigated. For example, if you are sitting at 50% hp, and you get hit by something that takes 50% of your hp, Ardent Defender would activate, causing that effect to only take 37.5% of your hp.
 3: Make it so that Ardent Defender instead of reducing damage taken below a set HP value would instead allow for spell damage to be blocked, which would make up for the lack of health compared against Warriors and Bears.

Either way, a solution has to be found to create a true mitigation build. Choice matters a lot when it comes to RPGs, and this option is sorely lacking.

I haven't adressed Judgment of the Crusader yet. A spell that causes players to always second guess themselves is not good design (especially with a lack of a PTR, disallowing Paladins to test certain weapons beforehand). And here I am at a bit of a loss. We can't move Imp. SoJ's effect to just be the base effect of JotC, because having to talent into a taunt is what defines a Paladin Tank. You can't have JotC be some sort of a damage Judgment, because then there is nothing to differentiate it from JoR. You also can't have it tied to resource restoration, as JoL and JoW already do that. So, perhaps, some form of mitigation, or a personal buff would be the way to go to distinguish it from the rest. Maybe increasing block chance by 10%? Here I am totally unsure what the solution is, but a solution has to be found. Because if not, we will continue to wander around blind as to which proc weapon is the best and why it's Blackblade of Shahram for both Prot and Ret, because who needs a shield when your Stamina and Agility keeps going higher and higher and your threat skyrockets with the insane haste and Strength values. Flame Wrath AoE farming will become a victim, but it has to be done to make the game better.

Beyond that, I would love to see one change made for Sanctity Aura. At the moment, it is a completely selfish Aura - it is beyond rare to have another Paladin or Smite Priest benefit from it. Every other aura, the group can benefit from. I've always wanted to see Sanctiy Aura increase (R)AP and SP by some value, as not only does Ret benefit from both, but it would be an actual *aura*. Sanctified Command's mana restoring portion is also kinda useless given how little it restores. With SoC being a primarily PvP seal, I'd argue that the resistance to purge effect should be extended to all Seals and Blessings (with Blessing of Freedom and Protection being a likely exception).

Oh, and, do everything I listed in point 2.

 **7. Conclusion**

The reason why I've written such extensive feedback and criticism is because I - despite everything I just went over, and how much I shit over some design decisions and the palpable lack of QA testing at times - love WoW and like playing on TWoW, and I'd like to see this project do better. Discussions around Paladin are wide and varied, however I found that a lot of topics weren't really touched on by a lot of those who do engage in discussions, and so I wished to bring forward what I had on my mind, because the current state of Ret has almost made me quit the game.

I hope that this post was a comprehensible and enjoyable read. Thank you for taking the time to get this far.

To explain, the reason why I used the word "compete" despite SoR eclipsing SotC and SoC - as you correctly put it - is because SoR, SotC and SoC are all, without exception, damaging seals, therefore they compete to be used.
