The most important thing as a tank, no matter the class you play, keybind the raidmarks. Especially the "Skull" icon.

Put it on a mob (preferably a healer or caster mob)
When you pull everything, concentrate on tanking all other mobs. Dps will kill skull first and you can focus on grabbing all the other mobs with ease.

Low level dungeons have a bit of a paradox, they are quite hard to tank because you lack some tools in your kit. But the mobs are weak enough where a dps can deal with ripping threat just fine. Id say just go deadmines. The role just requires some practice to get used to. Nothing you can learn in a yt video ;)

One trick is often overlooked and helps all tanks.

Make a macro or a keybinding to mark a target with skull (optional a 2nd one marking X). Unless clueless or trolling, most damage dealers will go to marked targets, making your tanking job easier and giving you enough time to grab the entire pull.

You can use it to prio dangerous targets, or when the order isn't relevant to ensure focus to one single mob.

Best decision i've made for tanking in general.

On a more specific case, paladins can either body pull, use dynamites or ask any ranged to do the pull for them. If someone pulls for you, make sure you drop a consecration to intercept, and always hide behind a corner if the pull includes ranged mobs

Paladin tanking is the easiest of all tanks, in my opinion. Consecration is your bread and butter ability and does 80% of the work for you. Everything basically revolves around that one ability. Obviously keep your seal of wisdom up so you don't run out of mana. Place Blessing of Salvation on party members like shamans to lower their threat generation and help you maintain aggro. > Spellpower is most important for pally tank right? But how far do you need to go for pure mitigation?

You build sets based on caps:

A set that guarantees full avoidance (every attack is dodged parried or blocked)

A set of plate gear and as much spellpower as possible to maximize threat.

And you change accordingly to fight. Usually, trash and weak hitting boses you go threat set while unknown bosses or hard hitting ones you go avoidance.

Enemies that need tanking but no one is dpsing them are also fit for avoidance set

If you don't reach the cap it's ok, just be as close as possible

I'm not a pala expert but had a prot pala.
Trying to give some tricks.
 1. Make sure righteous fury is always on. If you died, first thing is to reapply it.
 2. Keybind raid icons as others said.
 3. For blessing, go for the best for the party, unless you are undergeared. You can salv them, it's okay. Specially salv big damage DPS unless they are totally ok with pulling the mob from you.
 4. You see a pack. Put up skull. Run in (won't talk about range pulls and body pulls here). Prepare the seal and judge your main target with it (usually wisdom so you have a passive mana battery on the battlefield). Then strike (if I remember, holy strike was usually better). Ensure holy shield is always on. You can keep threat by tabbing between the mobs. If there are 3+ mobs, consec makes wonders.
 5. Go for ret aura for more consistent threat unless you feel super squishy, then devotion aura it is.
 6. As you have problems with range, always be mindful what you run into. Accidentall multipulls can happen. Worst case: have some ez-throw grenades for range pull or ask someone to range pull. Body pull comes with experience.
 7. Don't be afraid of failing. All tanks have a bit of learning curve, but pala is an easier and very rewarding one. Just remember: blessings are up on everyone, aura is up, righteous fury is up, seal is always up, holy shield always up, judgment at pull followed up by strike and keep tabbing, consec anytime but mostly in case of 3+ mobs.
 8. With SP gear you can eventually use different ranks of consec, considering spell downgrades.

Have fun.

A good habit is when you pull mobs, to always position them so that your dps is attacking them from behind. This helps achieve 2 things:

 1- Your physical dps won't lose on damage, as mobs and players can't parry, dodge or block when attacked from behind.

 2 - Mobs that run away when low on HP always run in the opposite direction of their last aggro target, which in this case would be you, the tank. Doing so will prevent the mob from accidentally pulling other packs by accident.
