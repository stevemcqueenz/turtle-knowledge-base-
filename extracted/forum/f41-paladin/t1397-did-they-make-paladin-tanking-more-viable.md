I "tank" in raids, and I use emphasis on tank because I can't main tank or off tank for every encounter in the game for various reasons; as long as that is true you can't commit a paladin to a tanking role seriously on the level of a druid or warrior. I do play a tank oriented paladin which means that at best I am an offtank for trash, certain boss encounters; Holy shield paladin if you have good healers is actually a decent maintank in bis, and I have thunderfury so I can build decent threat because of windfury + sor.

I think really the gamechanger is giving paladin windfury because it does go along way to fixing the threat and mana issues, but the lack of a gear progression for most of raiding until you hit aq40, and the fact that you have no taunt means that for the most part, paladin tanking is roughly in the same spot as classic; That is to say that paladins can tank for alot of fights in raids, but outside of Viscidus it's just more trouble than using a warrior or a druid, and you will always be compared to a feral druid or a warrior because apart from aoe tanking they do everything better.

The problem I and pretty much everyone else has is, what protection paladin offers is not enough justification to actually have it replace a dps warrior fully as an offtank. That being said, as the game currently works, what is viable is playing a tank oriented paladin that is willing to heal or dps when they aren't tanking. It's a very flexible role and something that won't suit everyone as most people are expecting to just play as a tank.

Here are the strengths of this playstyle, I dub "the healer-tank":

You can heal if you can't tank
You are the best aoe tank on trash that lives longer than 6 seconds or needs to be killed more than once every 10 minutes.
You can do nightfall if you don't need to heal and tank

Protection paladin niche in vanilla is they are the best tank for aoe; but considering how infrequent aoe situations are in raids, it is not enough justification to bring them for this. They need other advantages, in my case I over compensate a bit because of thunderfury so I have decent threat to tank alot of bosses, though the raid still prefers a warrior due to def stance -10% dmg and shield wall/last stand as well as their lifegiving gem, but if I'm not needed to tank I can fill other roles as needed.

I usually run a 20/31/0 spec, as this allows me to heal almost as effectively as an actual holy paladin, and tank raids almost as effectively as a conventional prot paladin, all by just swapping gear. If you want to play a protection paladin and your raid is not centered around you being the "main tank", then I suggest this playstyle as the viable route to be a "tank paladin" in vanilla raiding. Essentially you seek to capitalize on all of the strengths of paladin and cover the weaknesses, and if you can convince a raid to let you do this you should have a solid spot, though your actual tanking mileage may vary. This does depend of course on the priorities of the raid group, but if you have a decent amount of healers, depending on the raid it does offer alot more advantages than a traditional holy paladin.

In some ways you can consider it a support oriented paladin as you are not solely dedicated to any one role and you adjust your role depending on what is best for a fight. It is definitely viable to play this way and I think this is closer to the intended design that blizzard had for the class. A dps alternative that is also able to tank trash well is 21/0/30, which is a ret paladin that keeps an eye on tank health to give a heal when they are low, and is also able to act as healer with a gear swap.

To answer your question: did they make paladin tanking more viable? Yes somewhat, you will see the most change when you get a thunderfury (lol though). Otherwise it's still got a whole host of issues that prevent you from being a dedicated offtank, and alot of these issues are both by the design of the class and the design of vanilla content; the latter is unavoidable unless they actually go out of their way to change raid content, which will probably not happen. Nor is there yet much of a compelling need in vanilla raiding to require a paladin tank, so much as tanks who are able to taunt.

As an addendum, I do want to point out that as 1 of the 2 tanks in a 20 man (zg/aq20) paladin is more than capable of tanking it with the gear required of those instances; Molten core is not an issue and a tank paladin is actually on par with warrior/druid in terms of resistance fights since the gear is common, so there's a strong reason to bring them there.

Blackwing Lair has the most amount of paladin tank unfriendly mechanics in vanilla. Apart from Razorgore, Broodlord Lashlayer, and Nefarian it's not a fun time for you.

Aq40 does not require taunt to complete the raid; This raid has most of your bis and it's also not a bad idea to bring a tank paladin as there is a fair amount of aoe trash in the instance, as well as Blizzard threw paladins a bone by making the spell immune twin emperor not immune to holy spells. They must've realised what they did in BWL.

Naxxramas does not hard require a taunt for any fight, as the 4 Horsemen fight has an aggro drop mechanic that allows you to swap targets if you can do enough threat quickly, but that being said the most you'd want to bring for tank paladins is 1 for the back horses. All of the other fights are taunt immune and many of them a paladin cannot tank either due to dmg taken (patchwerk is fine in bis though) or mana burns/silences.
