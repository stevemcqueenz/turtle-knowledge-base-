# Extra Threat for Seal/Judge of Righteousness

## Post 45315 by Ravenstone (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2023-05-10T10:44:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=45315#p45315 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

One of the problems Paladin's have when tanking is smooth threat gen, particularly when beginning a fight. Having your first Holy Strike and Judgement miss/resist can really mess things up. I've [suggested it before briefly](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?t=6294), but my proposal is to give the Seal and Judgement on Righteousness an additional threat component, akin to Shield Slam or the plethora of other Warrior abilities with inbuilt "causes a high amount of threat". This harkens back to the pre 1.9 Seal of Fury that Paladin's had as Darg727 and Bob022 elaborated on [here and further on in the thread](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=42651#p42651).

> **Darg727 wrote: Sat Apr 22, 2023 11:08 pm**
> Seal of Fury was the original iteration of a tanking seal for paladin. It added 90 base TPS per swing along with 4 phantom swings to proc weapon procs and judgements. The judgement gave 337 base threat every time it was hit with a holy damage effect: SoR, holy shield, JoR, holy shock, consecration, smite, etc.

I believe part of the problem with the Paladin design is that you are reliant on Holy Damage to achieve your threat. Now inherently there is nothing too problematic about this if there were properly statted items for this style of play, but currently these do not exist to the extent that Paladin's would need them. I also think it would be harder to balance since I feel that Paladins need far more from their gear stats wise than Warriors do and could introduce more problems. Giving Paladins extra inbuilt threat on SoR/JoR would lessen the impact of lack of proper itemisation (though this should definitely still be looked at).

This change could also serve as the start to diversifying the Seals a bit more as currently SoR is better for DPS than both SoC and SotC. Having SoR as the Seal of choice 95% of the time is just not good from a design perspective. SotC and SoC can't be brought back into play unless SoR is toned down.

I should say that this would probably have a knock on impact to the current SoR MCP spellret DPS builds that are out there. Now, I'm not too familiar with the actual threat they output, but in my opinion this style of play isn't particularly appealing from either a gameplay or thematic point of views and I'd prefer Retadins to DPS without having to rely on such a gimmicky style, and therefore would be a necessary casuality for the sake of a better Paladin.

Thank you for reading, please let me know what you think.

Paladin dungeon threat is just fine. I think the biggest point people have a hard time wrapping their heads around is that threat is like a pool. You only need enough to swim. Too little and you trudge extra slow, too much and you might not have enough in other pools when people jump in. A mistake I see a lot is paladins not using  BoSalvation. This is our D-stance or bear form. In layman's terms it, for all tense and purposes, gives ~43% more threat on everything we do. Righteous fury is like the extra threat on heroic strike, it's just extra. It's the reason shaman aren't designed to be tanks. They don't have an d-stance equivalent of their own. Though, like any class, they can make use of BoSalv on turtle.

If a target has 5000 hp, your group is the standard 1/3/1 comp, you should only need to do ~700 threat to keep aggro until it's dead. At 20,000 hp you should only need to do about 3,500 threat. Of course, these are minimum values based on evenly distributed threat between the dps in the group. If there is only one target to beat up on, you'll be able to bring the full force of your single target threat generation to bare which makes 1 dps doing the work of 2 not much of a problem.

One thing I haven't checked is whether seal buff threat is a thing on this server (equal to the level at which you trained it × RF bonus)

Where paladin threat gets wonky is raiding. The poor scaling and cooldown/mana constraints means that while warrior and druid threat generation grows by leaps and bounds, paladin threat generation practically stagnates without greater blessing usage. That said, it is a paladin unique way to scale threat generation. Though I think I can speak for all paladins in this regard, the spell component cost is quite annoying. Though much less than farming MCPs.

As for why you specifically aren't holding threat there are a multitude of reasons beyond just what your party member is wearing. For RF to actually be considered equivalent, the threat multiplier needs to be increased, ignoring consecration. And it's not about the gear, you get new ranks increasing your damage and threat as you level. Sunder doesn't scale with AP or weapon damage for example.

Paladin was never designed to be a raid tank in vanilla. It's as simple as that. BoSalv not counting as a self only d-stance in large groups was the point from the beginning. It wasn't any different from the beta Seal of Salvation spell which was castable on others.
