# Repost: Necromancer is a kind of warlock...

## Post 149814 by Sleeplust (Patch Note Conspiracy Theorist) — 2025-09-02T02:06:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=149814#p149814 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

Blizzard's original warlock specs are more inclined to three different styles of the class as a demon summoner, which in fact is a system varying only on the extent of demon's participation in combat. In other words, the affliction warlock is merely a lesser demon summoner than demonological warlocks. In my opinion, that sounds like a controvercial identity of affliction warlocks regarding whether they are dark spell casters who can also summon demons or demon summoners who use weak demons (or a shadow priest who uses demons).

My suggestion is transmogging affliction to necromance by replacing demon minions with undead creatures. It doesn't feel odd that a warlock (dark caster) can control skeletons or ghouls. They can only operate in shadow, away from the public, and necromance has been extant in Warcraft prior to the founding of the Cult of the Damned, so the change would cause no contradiction to the basic plot.

Destrucion spec, just like Affliction, could be revamped. Into a Old God-affiliated summoner maybe.

Overall, I suggest that warlocks exist in game as a light armored summoner of diversified evils.

I understand your question. My opinion on warlock changes/redesign is simply from the rigid original design of this class, which feels like it was split into three specs to merely fit into the trichotomous talent system. That isn't the problem itself, but the character's flavour and orientation in RP is demoted in the original design. The Affliction and Desruction warlocks feel too much like a shadow priest with siphoning and DOT skills and a Fire mage who can cast a bit shadow magic, especially when they sacrifice the demon. I believe that making this class more inclined to summoning evil minions rather than diversifying their own styles without demons is definitely helpful to flavour and RP activities.

Back to your question, there are two measures/suggestions. The more simple one is just using different scripts, models and creature categories for Affliction and Destruction specs, and this wouldn't force players to rebuild their skill bars or refamiliarise with the mechanism. The more complex one is like reforging the whole class, at least two specs, by rearranging quests and adding spec-related skills inlined to necromancy and old gods. The latter is obviously less probable, I'm afraid.
