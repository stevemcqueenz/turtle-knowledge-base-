"Okay, let’s set aside the numbers and focus purely on warlock pet design—specifically, the role of demon pets.
Back in Vanilla WoW (Classic 60), my experience with warlock demons was this: They were powerful companions for solo adventures across Azeroth, filling crucial gaps in a warlock’s survival, damage, and utility. But in raids or dungeons, other players naturally replaced those roles, so demon pets are sacrificed—and that was fine. I loved this design.
However, both modern Blizzard and Turtle WoW have strayed from that philosophy. Focusing on Turtle’s 1.17.2 Demonology tree: It over-tunes demon pets for group content (hit, crit, raw damage, etc.), making players feel like a buff-bots for their pets. No matter how strong demons get, their clunky AI and fragility make raiding frustrating. Worse, even if a pet pumps insane DPS, what’s the fun for players—especially those who crave visceral combat? WoW’s combat thrives on impact, yet Demonology locks barely feel it.
Here’s the core issue: Demon pets should act as a solo warlock’s trusted partner, reinforcing the class fantasy. In group play, warlocks should rely on allies—not all-in demons. I enjoy that the true spirit of Vanilla warlocks.
