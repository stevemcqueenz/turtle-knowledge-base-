> Defensive Tactics: While wearing a shield, you retain 180% of the increased threat generation from Defensive Stance in other stances.
>  <Developer Comment> The point of this talent is to allow tanks wearing shields to sacrifice mitigation for more threat by swapping in and out of Defensive Stance. With this buff to this talent, players should experience noticeably higher threat done, outside than inside Defensive Stance.

Am I the only one that thinks [Defensive Tactics] is conceptually flawed? Why do we have a talent in the deep protection tree that is incentivizing tanks to *leave* defensive stance? How does a talent that adds viability to other stances appeal to a sword and board playstyle? Wouldn't it make more sense to give warriors increased threat viability inside of defensive stance, as opposed to using the stance as a lesser version of Shield Wall? Buffing the [Improved Thunder Clap] talent by allowing it to hit more targets (up to 6 targets) and having the base ability scale with AP would have been a simpler solution to help warrior AoE threat. [Defensive Tactics] seems like a poorly thought out talent that should have been heavily revised before release, in my opinion. I think the talent either needs to be reworked or replaced entirely. In it's current state it's effectively just a waste of a talent tree slot.

Are there any seasoned warrior tanks that can explain how to accurately utilize [Defensive Tactics]? If I'm misunderstanding the usefulness of the talent then feel free to inform me.

## Post 115882 by Ellifta (Barrens Chat Casualty) — 2025-01-08T14:04:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=115882#p115882 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

It's a very good talent that gives every warrior basically a reason to use any stance while tanking. In short summary, here are what each stance does if you invest 3 points into defensive tactics and 5 points in defiance. Battle stance is very similar to using deathwish or using dual wield from old fury/prot tanking in the sense that it gives you more threat but less mitigation.

Battle Stance:
+7-8% more threat gained per point of damage dealt compared to defensive stance, 10% more damage compared to defensive stance, which roughly ends up to 15-20% more threat overall
Ability to use overpower and execute, especially good if you use impale/prot spec.
No crit or crush immunity because you don't have shield block, 10% more damage taken compared to defensive stance.

This stance is good when you want to maximize threat, such as Vaelstratz or Maexna. Very satisfying to play with overpower, but because you only get 1 shield block every 4.5 seconds you are open to crits and crushing blows. With 370 defence in Naxx, in my experience this leads to 1 crit per boss fight and usually 4-5 crushing blows.

Defensive Stance:
The classic stance, crit and crush immunity if using shield block and shield slam on CD and 10% less damage taken. Good for bosses that deal a lot of damage and you want to give up some threat for mitigation like twin emps, Chromaggus or Patchwerk.

Berserker Stance: 3% more crit, 10% more damage taken but access to Whirlwind for AoE threat and fear immunity.

This talent is an excellent way to choose between more mitigation or more threat for deep prot tank without having them dual wield. You always stick to your shield.

While leveling my HC warr, I've been looking at this talent in every way I can, with my 20 years of WoW experience, a lot of it with warrior. These are my conclusions:

 - In Battle Stance, you can use Overpower and Execute. The problem is Overpower is proc-based and therefore unreliable. Also, the value of Improved Overpower and Deep Wounds is very low if you're using a fast weapon. Furthermore, even if you were to stance-dance for Overpower procs, this means you're burning rage that you could've just used to queue up a Heroic Strike. Being able to use Execute while maintaining high threat is nice, but at that point of the fight threat is not an issue anyway. Healer mana is.
 - In Berserker Stance, you can use Whirlwind, Berserker Rage and, again, Execute. Whirlwind for AoE threat is OK, although far from ideal, especially if you're using a fast one-hander. Stance-dancing for Berserker Rage is something good tanks already know how to do, so the value here is actually pretty low.

In summary, using Defensive Tactics and sitting permanently in Battle Stance results in a gameplay that's very similar to vanilla Arms, swapping Mortal Strike for Shield Slam. You use Sunder Armor as a filler and lose access to Revenge, which also deals more threat than SA (similar with Defensive Tactics in Battle Stance) and is far more rage-efficient. You lose flat 10% mitigation and Shield Block for more threat. It is worth noting that, without a doubt, opening with Concussion Blow + Shield Slam would result in significantly more threat with Defensive Tactics and outside of Defensive Stance.

All in all, it's a cool talent, but I don't want to lose 10% mitigation for more threat. Move it up in order for it to be accessible by other builds and instead change One-Handed Weapon Specialization to Sword and Board, with the talent text reading: "Increases all damage you deal when a shield is equipped by 2/4/6/8/10%. Also increases the threat generated by Thunder Clap by XYZ". This would make the talent actually affect more than just your autos and would make it equal to Prot Pala's equivalent. I don't know why we're talking so much about Prot while ignoring the elephant in the room - this talent, situated very deep in the Prot tree, only affects our auto-attacks (and HS) and ignores Shield Slam and Revenge.

> Actually, no. Vanilla tanking was built around stance dancing. Turtle reduced need to switch stances dramatically.

Not really.  The most stance dancing happened in dungeon pulls with charge engages.  Stance dancing for berserker rage doesn't take any real skill.  If anything, vanilla most harshly punished moving through stances or trying to use cross stance abilities.

The problem with Defensive Tactics is that it's a pseudo Warbringer talent while being obtuse to use.  It doesn't incentivize stance dancing because you're going to lose up to 75 rage every time you change a stance, so it's really opening avenues to tank in battle or berserker stance.  When you break down the benefits and drawbacks of either stance, all that's really being accomplished is tanking in battle stance so that Execute can be spammed at the end of the fight.  At the end of a fight is when you can swap to battle stance even without this talent and hit execute without any real possibility of losing threat.

If the idea is to open up which abilities a prot warrior can use, just put Warbringer as a replacement.  If the idea is that tank warriors need to retain threat generation to spam execute safely, then this talent is wildly missing the mark.
