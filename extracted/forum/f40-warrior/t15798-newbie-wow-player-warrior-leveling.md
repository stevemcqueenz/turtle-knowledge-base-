Here a classic armsbuild for leveling:
[https://talents.turtlecraft.gg/warrior? ... AAAAAAA%3D](https://talents.turtlecraft.gg/warrior?points=DQFARYDQpYAABABoAoBYAoAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAA%3D)

"Rotation": charge -> sunder amor (which is often better than heroic strike) -> rend and sunder armor again. Execute is situational because starting a new fight (after killing an enemy with execute) with 0 rage and charge on CD is lame. While leveling Overpower is very OP and it proccs really often. Counterattack ist also very usefull while leveling (thats why both are skilled/upgraded in the guide above). And keep your Battle Shout up.

Your stats are stamina + strengh an you play with a 2handweapon. Try to keep your weapon up2date because they are everything for warrior. Make sure your weapons are as slow as possible. Also have a shield and a fast 1handweapon (speed 1,x) rdy because when you go in dungeons you often have to tank (or to off-tank) or you need it for outside elite grp quests.

Try to do quests where the enemys are 2-3 levels below your own level and you can't really die. You also only have to eat every 4-6 mob this way. Quests with mobs with the same level are often a pain in the ass. And try to fight mobs 1 vs 1. Warriors hate fighting outnumbered (for example you can use Overpower and Counterattack only vs. mobs who parried you - but you don't know which mob it was and so you have to tab through the mobs)

Macro

For my basic abilities I use a simple startattack macro like

#showtooltip Rend
/startattack
/cast Rend

#showtooltip sunder armor
/startattack
/cast sunder armor

They are other usefull macros (for example for switching your 2handweapon into 1hand + shield) but I don't use them atm. You find them here or with google.

When you omit the spellrank in a macro the macro always choose the highest rank available.

Art of arms leveling is in hamstring kiting.
You charge+hamstring, deal melee hit and run from slowed mob while your attack is recharging.
Then you run to enemy and deal melee attack.
Benefit of this is ~30% reduction of incoming damage compared to standing still.

It looks like slow weapon is more profitable, but I think it is better to check damage per hit. The higher the better.

People were already talking about it 20 years ago*. Hamstring kiting is great against higher level opponents but completely unsuitable for normal leveling, in my personal opinion.

 1) When you start a fight, you'll get at least two attacks from enemies before you're out of range (due to latency alone). Most enemies (especially animals) have a much higher attack speed than you do. And by the time you are out of range, the time is almost up and you have to approach the mob again.

 2) Unnecessarily complicated (especially for beginners like the TE).
 - You have to practise running sideways away from opponents and not pummeling other opponents. In the worst case, you also pull the enemy away from the group of quest mobs and then have to run back (you have to kite several times during a fight; or you kite back but then you get even more attacks)
 - For this to really work you have to play with a swing timer addon and really look at it all the time. I find that _much_ too stressful for simple leveling.

 3) Overpower (and now Counterattack) do not benefit from this playstil.

 4) Due to the reduced rage generation (due to the lack of incoming damage from enemy attacks) you often have only autoattacks.

Playing with hamstring is like combat duration x 3 but incoming damage - 20% (both values estimated)
