I have no idea if this was changed by an amendment hidden in another thread, but the [consolidated Server Rules thread](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=12794#p12794) says this:
> Selling or buying characters or boosting services for real money, in-game currencies, or donation tokens is strictly forbidden.
>
>  GDKP raids, where groups auction bind-on-pickup or bind-on-equip items for gold, real money, or similar in-game currencies, may NOT be created, promoted or attended. For instance, farming a dungeon and inviting players to wait in the corner for items to drop, with the expectation of payment for the service, is not allowed.
>
>  However, acting as a mercenary to sell tanking or healing services (not DPS services) for in-game gold is permitted, but not for items or drops, only to be compensated for time and repair costs to assist others with 5-man instances. Mercenaries are not allowed to offer services for raids.

So it's a bit murky. Dragging low-levels through dungeons for BoP loot while charging money seems to be banned (both under the "boosting" clause and the "waiting in the corner for items to drop" clause). You can however sell your time as a tank or healer in 5 person instances. Presumably the group has to be roughly the same level as you to avoid the other clauses? And it seems to me your payment has to be your full compensation. You probably can't say "give me all left-over blues as payment" since you're not allowed to do it for items, only for gold. And you also can't charge money while hard-reserving drops obviously.

So there is clearly a way to legally offer tanking/healing services since there's a special rule for it. I have considered offering healing services for BRD Arena runs since there's nothing for healers to farm and Arena groups always lack them. I'm unsure personally if such a run would allow me to roll greed or if that would be "acting as a mercenary for items or drops".

If there's any doubt in your mind about he legality you should probably do it for free to stay in the clear though. Sometimes low-levels ask to be dragged through dungeons for sizeable amounts of gold - I would stay far away from those.

That is a really interesting way of looking at things, I haven't thought of that.
"LF4M <instance>, <item> HR"
"Offering <role> mercenary services for <instance>, demanding <item> as payment"
Not 100% convinced they're exactly the same (in the first you have to assemble and lead the group as well), but there are undeniable and strong parallels.

On the other hand you have a Server Rule that basically says whatever the group wants is a binding loot agreement that may even be enforced by GMs ("If you are in a group or raid, and the loot rules are clearly defined in game, prior to the raid beginning, and those rules are not followed when the time comes to receive loot. You may open a ticket for investigation.")
It's one of those issues where it's clear what kind of bad stuff they're trying to prevent, but how they do it is overly broad, fairly vague and potentially hanging out players to dry. Typical Terms of Service I guess :-)

Like usual, there's also the second issue of enforcement. We have no idea how vigilantly these issues are policed for average players who only ever build consenting groups with clear loot arrangements. If there's no harmed victim, at what point do the GMs step in? There's an infamous rule on the books that I suspect most players have broken at some point: "HC mode characters are not permitted to get assistance from players outside of their group." You can't cooperate with an HC character (I guess they may assist you, but not in return), you can't help them in a pinch. Purely by the letter of the law you can't even buff them or cleanse them (Rogues on the poison quest usually ask to be dispelled in SW). Sure, this is *aimed* at the HC tagging mobs and a non-HC (potentially lvl 60) on follow kills them. But the way it's *written* it's super broad and easy to violate. Even for HC characters themselves if they're helping outside of a party.
