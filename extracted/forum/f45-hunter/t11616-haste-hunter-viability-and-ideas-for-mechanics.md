# Haste Hunter Viability and Ideas for Mechanics

## Post 76855 by Nurincarinun — 2023-11-10T23:41:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=76855#p76855 | page 1 | era: pre-1.18.1 -->

tldr: Going for a more haste oriented hunter setup does not come with any benefit in terms of game mechanics atm, but does come with drawbacks. To add a viable haste oriented hunter playstyle without taking away those already present, introducing time window related mechanics, prbly into the talent trees, seems like the only option.

I created an other post where I consider some of the viability of haste in comparison to raw ap stats, but only for autoshots:
[viewtopic.php?t=11230](https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?t=11230)

First and foremost, only using auto shot as yet in the calculations is not realistic. It is rather a model to build upon for more realistic scenarios, mainly the usage of ability shots. Considering these, the classic vanilla mantra, as far as I always understood it, was foremost maximizing usage of aimed shot - with a fixed cast of 3sec independent of weapon speed or haste; and thus, the slowest weapons (with relatively higher upend dmg and avg dmg per shot compared to their faster colleagues of same itemlvl) would always prevail. Since we are still with a fixed casting time for aimed shot (and trueshot afaik), this mantra is kinda unchanged. One could look at how much haste is needed to get an extra shot between ability shots (for, theoretical "incomplete" shots will, here again, never be fired and the haste totally without effect if too small). For an already quite fast weapon speed of 2.0, which is the gcd on trueshot, we would need an attackspeed hastened to 1.0 for an extra arrow to go through. H=As/Ash-100% gives us a needed haste of H=2.0/1.0-100%=100%. That is insane to bring together, certainly not possible with only gear / as passive. More so, a meagerly 5% haste from enchants will not have a big enough impact between rapid fire, quick shots, troll berserking, or kiss of the spider to make this more than a cd.

So I wondered, how could quicker attacks with less ap boost prevail in this classic vanilla situation? Procs won't help, cause Ppm. Not sure enough whether higher chance to land any crits would help, since these crits would multiply on shots with lower dmg. Doesn't look too good for speedy hunter.

I had one idea though: hastened attack speed will lead to more shots in a (in relation to the amount of haste big enough) time window. So why not use that?

We could have a stacking selfbuff, eg in marksmanship tree; or a stacking debuff. Maybe synergize with Hunter's Mark (to make Improved talent more viable), so you can only debuff or get the buff from a mob with your own hunter's mark on it; and maybe the buff/debuff modifies the hunter's mark effect. Actually, it should be via a buff because debuff limit.

Or why not try to synergize fast shooting with BM? Like: If you land a crit, a short buff is procced and if your pet lands a crit during that first buff, both you and your pet get a dps increasing buff. We could even say: if your pet crits first, and you land a crit soon enough after that, a different dps buff is applied. This dependance on the order of who crits first would even make the Devilsaur Tooth Trinket more interesting because it could be used to guarantee the pet lands a crit before you (maybe) do, or that your pet crits soon enough after you did. In the end, one could even have a third buff that is created if one obtains both dps buffs at the same time (independent of sequence, we have to cut this off somewhere). These buffs definitely would have to be balanced well to not escalate enormously.

And maybe in the survival tree (which I consider the "get me out of melee" and other utilities tree, not a melee hunter tree) we could have a chance to retrieve back an arrow that was shot (mechanic-wise prbly rather: chance for a shot to not use up an arrow); I think gathering back your arrows can be considered a survival strategy while not straightforeward: it is dangerous to do on a field of ongoing battle and thus needs skill. This could be made to greatly help with one elephant in this room, which is: a quickly emptied quiver.

All of these should happen much more often with higher speed than with lower, so prbly the BM scenario is good to think about how to make them not too easy to bring about. If the MM version would happen just on crits, there might be no need for haste and it'd just be a buff to the old slowness mantra. Maybe the MM version could work when multiple crits within a time window that gets started by an aimed shot crit are needed to produce a patch of the stacking buff. [edit: One could also include arcane shot here: Eg a patch of the stacking buff is applied by landing an arcane shot on the target with your own hunter's mark on it. You have to use arcane shot on cooldown to stack up more patches. Improved arcane shot talent could come into play here, where the cd reduction allows to fire one aimed shot after building up a whole stack of the buff and an arcane shot soon enough after that to keep the stack whole; though this would still have to be with numbers not to boost a slowness playstile too much at the same time. This would branch from an aimed shot rotation, since they share cds. Also, arcane shot being instant wouldn't reset auto shot swing timers, and thus haste comes into play more fully. RP wise, arcane shot could be a "marking shot" (- it does glow in the end -) to show your groupmates(/raid) where there is a vulnerable spot at an enemy.]

Basically: keeping the timewindows narrow somehow (eg trigger condition on cd) will change whether these mechanics would be of too much benefit for the standard slowness approach while making quickness viable.

Hm so... First of all, I would split this question in two:

Why would I want to consider this?
And if I would want it to be implemented, why so?

I wanted to check how viable a stat haste is for a hunter. Since I came to the conclusion it isn't very viable atm, I asked myself: On what does this depend? What would have to change for it to become viable? And my answer to that is my original post in this thread. By answering this question, one can then evaluate whether one thinks it would be worth implementing. Without considering, one could not evaluate this, and one would not have an idea whether haste is a stat worth including in your gear setup.

I would like to see haste become a more viable stat for a hunter in some way, because I think it could be fun, and because I think RP wise it doesn't make sense that an extremely fast shooting hunter should not be an other viable extreme to the current slowness mantra. Is this a good reason? Well, I think having fun is good. I also don't want the fun of other players ruined. So I tried to find a method to do this while leaving melee weaving untouched, and this is what I came up with.

Also, I have the impression there are some custom twow mail armor items with haste that seem catered towards hunter more than shammy, while others are much more obviously for shammys. If the devs thus wanted to experiment with haste for hunter, I think this investigation of mine can be of help to say: We are not there yet. There are also quite some obsolete ranged weapons due to their speed, that don't even give good stats for melee physical dps classes; these could become more than wasted boss drop slots. And there are some talents in the hunter trees that quite frankly count as obsolete; these could become more interesting to actually open up different specs for hunters. As I understand it, end-game PvE only has one core rotation with maybe a BM button switched around between specs. [edit: Sure, micromanaging your pet is a different playstyle than keeping a wolf next to you for the Furious Howl buff. But:] Other classes have at least 2 specs with different rotations >and< gearing. My ideas of mechanics here are not the exclusive answer to this; and yes, giving pet scaling from the hunter's gear stats (if I got you correctly) would also be an option here. I found it interesting, though, that with introducing these haste oriented features one could answer multiple quirks at once.
