# Proposed Improvements for the Marksmanship Hunter

## Post 161088 by Kullez — 2025-11-30T09:42:00Z
<!-- url: https://forum.turtlecraft.gg/viewtopic.php?p=161088#p161088 | page 1 | era: 1.18.1-announced-pre-release -->

**Good day!**
I play a Hunter on our server and greatly enjoy the changes you’ve made to the Beast Mastery and Survival specializations. Although the recent rebalance of Marksmanship—my long-time favorite—has been positive, I feel that these adjustments don’t fully reflect the “core identity” of the MM specialization, which I will explain below. With that in mind, I would like to propose several ideas that could improve the gameplay experience for Marksmanship Hunters. These are merely suggestions, but I would be genuinely thrilled to see them implemented:

 **TALENT REWORKS:**

 1. (First option — depending on the balancing of the new talent *Ensnaring Net*, described below)
Change the talent *Improved Concussive Shot* with the following:
(x/5 points) Extends the duration of *Concussive Shot* by 0.4 seconds per point and adds a 2% chance per point (up to 10%) to entangle the target in a net for 5 seconds, preventing movement.

 2. (Second option — depending on the balancing of the new talent *Ensnaring Net*, described below)
Change the talent *Improved Concussive Shot* with the following:
(x/5 points) Extends the duration of *Concussive Shot* by 0.4 seconds per point.

 3. (If introducing 1/2 or 2/2 variants proposed below)
Change the talent *Endless Quiver* with the following:
(x/1 point) Your *Auto Shot*, *Stready Shot*, *Arcane Shot* and *Multi-Shot* have a 5% chance to fire an additional shot that does not consume ammunition and deals 100% weapon damage.

 4. (If introducing 1/2 or 2/2 variants proposed below)
Change the talent *Improved Steady Shot* with the following:
(x/1 point) Increases the damage of *Steady Shot* by 12.5%.

 **NEW TALENTS:**

 1. (First option) *Lone Wolf*
x/1 talent point, available after spending 30 points in the Marksmanship tree.
Effect: If you do not have a pet summoned, you gain the *Lone Wolf* buff, increasing all damage dealt by 10/12.5/15% (depending on the rank of the ability).

 2. (Second option) *Lone Wolf*
x/1 talent point, available after spending 30 points in the Marksmanship tree.
Effect: You can no longer summon a pet, permanently gaining the *Lone Wolf* buff, which increases all damage dealt by 10/12.5/15% (depending on the rank of the ability).

 3. (First option) *Ensnaring Net*
Available after spending 30 points in the Marksmanship tree, x/1 talent point.
Deals no damage. Cooldown: 30 seconds. Requires the *Lone Wolf* buff to use.
(Optional: Add a consumable for its activation — *Hunter’s Net*, purchased at the same vendors as arrows, bullets, and water. Cost: 50 copper, stack size: 100.)
PvE Effect (vs mobs or adds): 100% chance to entangle any creature except dragonkin and giants, preventing movement for 10 seconds. Against giants: 50% chance; against dragonkin: 75%. Does not work on elite mobs or bosses.
PvP Effect (vs players): 100% chance to immobilize players for 5 seconds. Counts as a root effect and can be removed.

 **WHY?**

Hunters have three specializations:
 *Beast Mastery* is structured so that the Hunter primarily empowers the pet—roughly a 70/30 distribution, where the pet deals the majority of the impact.
 *Survival* focuses on close-range combat with heavy use of traps, while the pet provides meaningful assistance—roughly 65/35, with the Hunter leading the damage.
 *Marksmanship*, described as masters of precision, ranged combat, and powerful shots, still relies considerably on the pet—around 75/25, even though the Hunter is intended to be the main source of damage.

Across various servers and expansions (except TBC), up to Legion, my main character has always been a Marksmanship Hunter. Because of this, I would love to see a version of the Legion-style talent that prevents pet summoning entirely. I enjoy using a pet, but sometimes I want to play as a ranger or archer similar to elven bowmen—and they traditionally do not use animal companions, yet are still recognized as archers.

With the addition of new control options, an increased duration for *Concussive Shot*, and overall higher outgoing damage, Marksmanship Hunters would find it far more manageable to operate without a pet. I haven’t yet participated in raids on this server, so I cannot confidently judge the exact percentage values for *Lone Wolf* or related effects, but I sincerely ask you to consider my proposal. I hope that with one of the upcoming patches, I will be able to dismiss my pet entirely and fully embrace a playstyle centered on the Hunter’s own capabilities.

Have a nice day!

Hello!
The whole point of *Steady Shot* is that the requirement to cast it is rewarded with higher damage. I support the idea of a small buff to *Arcane Shot*, but I think its damage should not exceed 80% of the total damage dealt by *Steady Shot* over a full rotation. As for PvP—the proposed control improvements together with the *Arcane Shot* buff (through talents) are already quite significant. I don’t think that with a 5-second cooldown there’s any need for a damage split or a 50/50 mechanic.

Regarding *Aimed Shot*, I agree, though tying it to a DoT is debatable. It might be better to consider a “balancing” approach: make its base cast time 4 seconds, but allow it to fire as a guaranteed critical (with half the cast time—2 seconds) after *Piercing Shot* procs. It’s also a DoT, but tied to the MM specialization. If the goal is to make this work for “all” hunters, then the Serpent Sting DoT proc chance should be no higher than two percent because of the constant ticking damage on the target.

As for *Trueshot Aura*, I don’t see much reason to increase melee damage for the MM tree. Instead, perhaps it could give a one/two percent temporary buff when traps trigger on an enemy (as a temporary bonus for Survival), though I won’t speak for the Survival spec.

Regarding *Lone Wolf*, I believe that playing with or without a pet should offer equally valuable rewards. Running Heroics as MM, I simply wouldn’t have managed without a pet. Based on that, I proposed the control adjustments—so there would be a chance to survive on your own while dealing damage from range.

So the ideas are very good; they’re definitely worth considering.
